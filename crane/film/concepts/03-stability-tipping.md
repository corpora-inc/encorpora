# Stability: why capacity falls with radius

Skills: `loadchart.structural-vs-stability`, `loadchart.configuration`, `loadchart.quadrants`, `loadchart.on-rubber`, `site.outriggers`, `site.leveling`. Exam domains: Technical Knowledge, Load Charts, Site.

## The idea

A crane on outriggers tips about a line through the two floats nearest the load: the tipping axis. The crane's own weight, acting at its center of gravity behind that line, holds it down. The load, acting at the hook out beyond the line, pulls it over. Each pull is a weight times its distance from the axis (a moment). The crane is at the edge of tipping when the two moments are equal.

So as the radius grows, the load's lever arm grows and the load that balances shrinks. Double the arm and the tipping load halves. A stability-limited chart cell is that tipping load cut to a margin: on our chart, 85% of tipping on outriggers, 75% on rubber.

## The moment

A seesaw made of a crane. The load slides out along the boom's reach; its lever arm stretches on screen, the moment bars stay level only if the load shrinks, and the readout falls. Past balance, the whole crane rotates up about the float.

## Cases (three)

1. **Balance.** Crane 60,000 lb with its weight 6 ft behind the axis: 360,000 ft-lb. Load arm 18 ft: tipping at 20,000 lb. Rated (85%): 17,000 lb.
2. **Twice the arm, half the load.** Load arm 36 ft: tipping at 10,000 lb.
3. **Outriggers at mid extension.** The axis moves in toward the crane. The load's arm gets longer and the crane's gets shorter, both at once, so capacity drops sharply. That is why each outrigger setup has its own chart page, and why the swing direction (over the side, over the front) can change the rating.

## Close

Tipping axis through the nearest floats. Load × arm against crane × arm. Longer radius, smaller load. Use the page that matches the outriggers you set.

## Note

The film's picture is a simplified balance (the boom's own weight, which also moves out with the radius, is left out and named once). The chart already did this arithmetic; the film shows why its numbers fall.
