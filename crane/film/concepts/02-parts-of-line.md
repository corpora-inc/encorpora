# Parts of line and line pull

Skills: `loadchart.parts-of-line`, `wire-rope.parts-of-line`, `wire-rope.reeving`, `wire-rope.line-pull-vs-swl`. Exam domains: Load Charts, Technical Knowledge. Chart numbers: HT-50 line pull, 10,000 lb per part (fictional).

## The idea

The hoist rope can pull only so hard: its permissible line pull (one part). Reeve the rope back and forth between the boom-tip sheaves and the block sheaves, and the block hangs on several parts of the same rope. Each part carries the same tension, so n parts hold n times the line pull. That is why heavy loads are reeved with more parts, and why the hoist is slower: the drum pulls in n feet of rope to raise the hook one foot.

Count the parts that hold the block. The lead line going off to the drum does not hold the block. A rope dead-ended on the block adds a part, which is how an odd count happens.

## The moment

The block hangs on one part, then the rope is re-reeved to four. Each part lights as it is counted, its tension label shows a quarter of the load, and the line capacity readout climbs from 10,000 to 40,000 lb.

## Cases (three)

1. **Why parts multiply.** 4 parts × 10,000 lb = 40,000 lb on the line.
2. **How many parts a load needs.** Load 34,000 + block 1,100 + rigging 400 = 35,500 lb on the hook. 35,500 ÷ 10,000 = 3.55: always round up, 4 parts. Three parts would hold only 30,000.
3. **Parts never raise the chart.** More parts let the rope carry more; they do not make the crane stronger or more stable. The lift is limited by the smaller of the chart's net capacity and the line capacity.

## Close

Total load includes the block. Parts = total ÷ line pull, rounded up. The smaller limit governs.
