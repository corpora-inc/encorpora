## Chapter 4 — The Cooking Ways

Three ways of cooking carry most of soul food: frying, slow stewing, and smoking.

Frying happens in a heavy pan called a cast iron skillet. Cast iron holds heat for a long time. A good skillet is older than the cook. It is passed from mother to daughter.

Slow stewing happens in a big pot on the back of the stove. Greens go in the pot for two or three hours. A piece of smoked pork goes in for flavor. The water becomes a dark, rich broth. People call this broth "pot likker," and they pour it over cornbread.

Smoking happens outside, in a smokehouse or a barrel. Wood burns slowly under the meat. Hickory wood is the favorite. The smoke colors the meat brown and gives it a deep flavor. Pork shoulder smokes for many hours, sometimes for a whole day.

A good Southern kitchen has fire, time, and care.
