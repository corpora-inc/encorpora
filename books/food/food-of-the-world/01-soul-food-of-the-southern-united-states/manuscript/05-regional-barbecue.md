## Chapter 5 — Regional Barbecue

In the South, "barbecue" means slow-cooked meat over wood smoke. Each region has its own way.

In the Carolinas, barbecue is pork. In the eastern part of North Carolina, the sauce is made from vinegar and pepper. It is thin and sharp. In South Carolina, the sauce is yellow, made from mustard. It is a little sweet.

In Memphis, in the state of Tennessee, the meat is pork ribs. The ribs are rubbed with a dry mix of salt, pepper, and many spices. The meat is the star, and the sauce is small.

In Texas, barbecue is beef. The cut is called brisket. It is smoked for twelve to fourteen hours over oak wood. When it is right, you can pull it apart with your fingers.

In Kansas City, barbecue is many meats: pork, beef, chicken, even sausage. The sauce is thick, sweet, and red, made from tomato and molasses.
