## Chapter 2 — The Ingredients

Soul food has two big roots. One root is West Africa. The other root is the Americas.

From West Africa came okra. Okra is a green pod. It grows on a tall plant. When you cook it, it becomes soft and a little slippery. It is in gumbo, and it is fried in cornmeal.

Black-eyed peas came from West Africa too. So did watermelon. So did sesame seeds. The old Southern cooks called sesame "benne." They put benne in cookies and on bread.

Rice came across the ocean as well. African farmers had grown rice for a thousand years. In South Carolina, that knowledge built whole towns.

From the Americas came corn. Corn is native to Mexico. It traveled north to the Cherokee and the Choctaw long before the Spanish came.

From the Americas also came sweet potatoes, peanuts, and many kinds of beans.

Soul food is what happened when these two pantries met in one kitchen.
