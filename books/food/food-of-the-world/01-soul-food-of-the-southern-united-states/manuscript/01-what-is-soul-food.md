## Chapter 1 — What Is Soul Food

Soul food is the everyday food of Black families in the American South.

The name "soul food" became common in the nineteen sixties. The word "soul" came from music and from church. It meant something deep and true. It meant something Black and proud.

But the food itself is much older. It comes from the plantation kitchens of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Enslaved African cooks took the plants and the animals they had, and they made dinner.

The cooks were brilliant. They worked with little, and they fed a lot. They saved every part of the pig. They made the greens taste sweet. They turned cornmeal into bread, into porridge, into a hundred small things.

When Black families moved north in the twentieth century, they took the food with them. Now soul food is in Chicago, in New York, in Detroit, and in every Southern town. But the center of it is still in the South.
