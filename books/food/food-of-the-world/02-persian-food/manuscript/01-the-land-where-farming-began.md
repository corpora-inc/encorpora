## Chapter 1 — The Land Where Farming Began

Persia is a high, dry land. It sits between the Caspian Sea in the north and the Persian Gulf in the south. To the west is Mesopotamia. To the east is India. In the middle are mountains and deserts.

Right next door, about ten thousand years ago, people first grew wheat and barley on purpose. Farming was born there. Persia was one of the first places to learn it.

That long head start is on every Persian plate. The peach is from Persia. The Romans called it Persica, "the Persian fruit." Our word "peach" comes from that name.

The apricot is from there too. So is the pomegranate. So is the walnut. So is the sour cherry. So is one of the world's first salad greens, spinach. The English word "spinach" comes from the Persian word esfanaj.

Even the word "paradise" is a Persian word. It comes from pardis, a walled garden. The first paradise was a Persian orchard, full of pomegranate trees.

Persia did not just eat well. Persia taught the world how to eat well.
