# Set Pieces

Set pieces are the moments when play restarts from a dead ball. Corner kicks, free kicks, and goal kicks all follow specific patterns. As the sweeper, you have a role in every set piece, on defense and on attack.

## Defending Corner Kicks

When the other team takes a corner kick, you guard the back post area. The back post is the far goalpost, the post farthest from where the corner is being taken. Many corner kicks curve toward the back post. You stand near that post, ready to clear any ball that arrives in your zone.

Keep your eyes on the ball as the kick is taken. Judge where the ball will land. If the ball comes toward the back post, attack the ball with your head or your foot. Clear the ball out to the sideline or out toward the halfway line.

If the ball goes to the near post or the center of the box, stay alert. A deflection can redirect the ball toward the back post. Be ready to react to the second ball.

## Defending Free Kicks

When the other team has a free kick in a dangerous position, position yourself behind the defensive wall to sweep up any ball that gets through.

For free kicks from deeper positions, treat the free kick like a long pass. Read the flight of the ball. Position yourself to intercept any ball that drops into the space behind your defenders.

Communicate with your goalkeeper during free kicks. Listen to the goalkeeper and adjust your position.

## Goal Kicks for Your Team

When your goalkeeper takes a goal kick, you are the closest safe passing option. Stand in a position where the goalkeeper can play a short pass directly to your feet.

Receiving the goal kick from the goalkeeper gives your team a calm start to the attack. You collect the ball, look up, and find the next pass. A short goal kick to the sweeper is safer than a long goal kick that the other team can contest in the air.

Call for the ball if you are open. Check your surroundings before the ball arrives. Know where the pressure is coming from. Receive the ball on your back foot so you can turn away from pressure.

If the opposing team presses high and takes away the short option, wave the goalkeeper off. The goalkeeper can play a longer ball instead.

## Attacking Corner Kicks

When your team takes a corner kick, you have a chance to score. Move forward to the back post area. Stand at the far edge of the box. The same position you defend on defensive corners is the position you attack on offensive corners.

A corner kick that sails past the crowd at the near post often arrives at the back post with space. You are standing there, ready to head the ball or volley the ball toward the goal.

Time your run. Start a few yards outside the box and run toward the back post as the kick is taken. A moving player is harder to mark than a standing player.

## Attacking Free Kicks

When your team has a free kick in the opposing half, move toward the back post if the kick is close to the box. Position yourself for a header or a deflection.

If the free kick is deeper, stay near the center circle. Be ready for the second ball. A free kick that is cleared by the defense often lands in the midfield area. You collect the clearance and restart the attack.

## Restarts Are Opportunities

Many goals in youth soccer come from set pieces. Knowing your role on every set piece gives your team an edge. Know exactly where to stand on every type of restart. Set pieces reward preparation, and you are always prepared.
