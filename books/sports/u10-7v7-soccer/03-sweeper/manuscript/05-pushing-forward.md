# Pushing Forward

Defense is your first job. But you are not chained to the back of the field. When your team has the ball and the play moves forward, you push forward with the team. The sweeper who joins the attack makes the whole team more dangerous.

## Moving With the Team

When your team has possession, the entire formation shifts up the field. The attackers press high. The defenders push into the attacking half. You advance past the midfield line.

Pushing forward keeps the team compact. A compact team has shorter passing distances. A compact team can press the other team and win the ball back quickly if possession is lost. You push forward because the team needs you close, not fifty yards behind the play.

## Be a Passing Option

When you push forward, you become an extra passing option in the center of the field. Your teammates can pass the ball back to you when the forward options are blocked. You receive the ball, look up, and recycle possession to the other side of the field.

Find pockets of space in the center. Stand between opposing players where you can receive a pass without pressure. Call for the ball. Let your teammates know you are open.

The extra player in the center changes the math. Your presence pulls a defender out of position. That creates space for your attackers.

## Overlapping Runs

Sometimes the moment calls for a bold run. Your defender has the ball on the right side. You sprint past the defender on the outside. The defender plays the ball into the space ahead of you. You are running into the attacking third with the ball at your feet.

The overlapping run surprises the other team. The confusion creates space and time for a cross or a shot. Use the overlapping run sparingly. Save the overlap for the right moment. The surprise is what makes the run work.

## Scoring Goals

You can score goals. When the play develops near the opposing goal, look for a chance to arrive late in the box. A late run is hard for defenders to track because the defenders are focused on the attackers.

You arrive at the edge of the box. A teammate crosses the ball. You strike the ball cleanly. The ball flies into the net. The sweeper just scored.

Scoring goals is a bonus, not your main job. Enjoy the moment when a goal comes. But remember that your primary value is controlling the game from the back.

## The Sprint Back

Here is the trade-off. When you push forward and the other team wins the ball, you are out of position. The space behind the defenders is open. You must sprint back at full speed.

The sprint back is non-negotiable. The moment your team loses the ball, you turn and run. Do not jog. Do not watch the play develop. Sprint. Get between the ball and your own goal as fast as you can.

Great sweepers have a sixth sense for when possession is about to change. You start drifting back a half-second before the ball is lost. You read the pressure on your teammate. By the time the other team wins the ball, you are already moving toward your defensive position.

## Knowing When to Stay

Not every attack requires you to push forward. If the ball is bouncing around in a crowded area near the opposing goal, stay in your position. Staying back gives your team insurance against a fast counter-attack.

Read the game. If your team has clean possession, push up and join the attack. If the ball is contested, hold your position and protect the back.

## The Balance

The sweeper lives in balance. Push forward when the team needs you. Sprint back when the defense needs you. Attack and defend in the same game, sometimes in the same minute. Trust your instincts and keep learning. The balance between attack and defense is what makes the sweeper position the most exciting role on the field.
