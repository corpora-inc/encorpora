# Winning the Ball

You are the last field player before the goalkeeper. Winning the ball is your most important defensive skill. When an attacker gets past the two defenders, you are the player who stops the attack.

## Stepping Up

When you see a through ball heading toward an attacker, step up to meet the ball. Do not wait for the attacker to collect the ball and turn. Step up early and intercept the pass before the attacker controls the ball.

Stepping up takes courage. You are leaving the space behind you open for a moment. But stepping up early means you win the ball cleanly and start the counter-attack. Waiting too long gives the attacker time to make a decision.

Judge the speed of the ball and the speed of the attacker. If you can reach the ball first, step up with confidence and go.

## Intercepting Passes

The best way to win the ball is to intercept a pass. An interception means you take the ball without making a tackle. You read the passing lane, step into the path of the ball, and collect the ball with a clean first touch.

Interceptions come from reading the game. You see the opponent's body shape. You predict the direction of the pass. You move before the ball is kicked. When you arrive at the right spot, the ball comes straight to your feet.

Intercepting passes is safer than tackling. You win the ball without contact. You keep your balance. You are already facing forward and ready to play the next pass.

## Tackling Clean

Sometimes you cannot intercept. The attacker has the ball and is running at the goal. You need to make a tackle.

Stay on your feet as long as you can. A sweeper who stays standing can recover if the first tackle does not work. Jockey the attacker by staying low and matching the attacker's movements. Force the attacker wide, away from the center of the goal.

When you make the tackle, be firm and fair. Win the ball with the inside of your foot. Keep your eyes on the ball. A clean tackle wins the ball and keeps the game flowing.

## Clearing Danger

Sometimes you win the ball in a dangerous area near your own goal. You do not have time to pick a pass. You need to clear the ball away from danger.

Clear the ball out to the sideline. Clearing to the sideline is safe because even if the other team collects the ball, the attack is now in a wide position, far from your goal. Clearing through the center of the field is risky because a center clearance can fall to an opposing player in a dangerous scoring position.

When you clear the ball, kick the ball with purpose. A weak clearance lands in the middle of the field and creates more problems. A strong clearance to the sideline gives your team time to reorganize.

## Stay Calm

Winning the ball near your own goal is a high-pressure moment. The other team is attacking. Your teammates are scrambling. In that moment, you stay calm.

Calm players make better decisions. Calm players time their tackles better. Calm players pick the right clearance. Take a breath. Read the situation. Choose the right action. Then look up and find the next pass.

## Being the Wall

Your defenders know that you are behind them. Your defenders play with more confidence because you are there to clean up. You are the wall between the attack and the goalkeeper.

When your defenders step up to challenge an attacker, you cover the space behind your defenders. When a pass slips through, you are already there. When a deflection bounces loose, you collect the ball.

You are the safety net. You are the player who sweeps up every loose ball in the defensive third. That is why the position is called sweeper. You sweep the danger away and turn defense into attack.
