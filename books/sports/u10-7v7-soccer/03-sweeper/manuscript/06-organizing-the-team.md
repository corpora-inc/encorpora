# Organizing the Team

You see the whole field. Your teammates do not. The attackers face the opposing goal. The defenders watch the ball. You are the only player who can see the full picture. That means you are the voice of the team.

## Talking on the Field

Great sweepers talk constantly during the game. You tell your defenders where to stand. You warn your teammates about runners. You call out which side the ball is on. You let the team know when pressure is coming.

Your voice is a tool, just like your feet. A teammate who hears your call can react a half-second faster. That half-second is the difference between winning the ball and getting beat.

Talk in short, clear phrases. Say "left shoulder" when a runner is coming from the left. Say "you have time" when a teammate has space to turn. Say "back to me" when you are open for a pass. Keep the words simple and the volume loud enough for your teammates to hear.

## Positioning Your Defenders

Your two defenders are your closest partners. You work together as a unit. You tell the defenders when to push up and when to drop back. You tell the defenders when to shift left or shift right.

When the ball is on the left side of the field, call the right defender to tuck in toward the center. The right defender closes the gap and makes the defense harder to break through. When the ball switches to the right side, call the left defender to tuck in.

Your defenders trust your calls. Your defenders cannot see what you see from behind. Give the defenders clear instructions and the whole defensive unit moves together.

## Warning About Runners

Opponents like to make runs behind the defense. A runner sneaking behind your left defender is invisible to that defender. But you can see the runner clearly from your position.

Call out the runner early. Say "runner left" or "runner right" as soon as you spot the movement. Your defender hears the call and tracks the runner. A pass that would have been dangerous is now covered.

Early calls prevent goals. Late calls leave defenders scrambling. Train yourself to spot runners the moment the runners begin their movement. The earlier you call, the more time your teammate has to react.

## Encouraging Your Teammates

Leadership is not just about giving instructions. Leadership is about lifting your teammates up. When a defender makes a great tackle, tell that defender. Say "great work" or "nice tackle." When an attacker tracks back to help on defense, acknowledge the effort.

Positive words give your teammates energy. A teammate who feels supported plays with more confidence. A confident team defends better, passes better, and competes harder.

When a teammate makes a mistake, stay positive. Mistakes happen in every game. Your job is to help the team move on quickly. Say "no worries, next one" and focus on the next play. A great leader never dwells on mistakes.

## Calm Leadership

The best leaders are calm. When the other team is pressing hard and the game feels chaotic, you stay steady. Your voice stays measured. Your instructions stay clear.

Your teammates look to you in difficult moments. If you are calm, the team stays calm. Be the anchor. Be the player who keeps everyone steady when the pressure builds. Your calm energy flows through the team.

## Leading by Example

Talk matters. Actions matter more. Show your teammates what effort looks like. Sprint back on defense. Fight for every loose ball. Never give up on a play.

When your teammates see you working hard, your teammates work harder. You set the standard. A great sweeper leads with words by organizing and encouraging the team. A great sweeper leads with actions by playing with intensity, intelligence, and heart. Your team follows your lead.
