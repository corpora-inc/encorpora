# Reading the Game

Reading the game is your greatest skill. Reading the game means seeing what is about to happen before the ball arrives. You do not chase the ball. You predict where the ball will go next.

## See the Whole Field

Most players watch the ball. You watch everything. Your eyes scan the field constantly. You check the ball. You check the runners. You check the open spaces. You check where your teammates are standing.

Your head is always moving. Look left. Look right. Look at the ball. Look behind the ball. Every few seconds, scan the whole field again. Great sweepers never stop scanning.

## Watch the Spaces

The spaces between players tell you more than the ball does. A big open space in the center of the field is an invitation for the other team to attack. A gap between your two defenders is a lane for a through ball. You see these spaces and you fill them before the opponent can use them.

Position yourself between the ball and your own goal. Turn your body so that you are halfway between facing the ball and facing the field. This is called your body angle. An open body angle means you are not facing the ball straight on. Instead, your shoulders point partly toward the ball and partly toward the rest of the field. An open body angle lets you see the ball and the players around you at the same time. A sweeper who faces the ball straight on loses sight of the runners behind the play. Keep your body open and you see everything. You are always adjusting your position based on where the ball moves.

## Read the Opponent

Watch the player on the other team who has the ball. Watch where that player's eyes look. Watch where that player's body faces. A player who drops a shoulder to the left is probably going to pass to the left. A player who looks up is searching for a long ball.

Watch the runners on the other team. A runner making a sprint behind your defenders is looking for a through ball. You see that runner before your defenders see that runner. You can step across and intercept the pass.

## Anticipation

Anticipation means knowing what will happen next. You read the body language of the opponent. You read the shape of the passing lane. You start moving toward the ball before the pass is made.

When you anticipate correctly, you arrive at the ball before the attacker. The interception looks easy because you started moving early. Great sweepers make the game look slow because great sweepers read the game so well.

## Positioning

Your starting position is behind the two defenders, in the center of the field. From that central spot, you can slide left or slide right to cover either defender.

When the ball is on the left side of the field, shift your position slightly to the left. When the ball moves to the right side, shift to the right. You are always between the ball and the center of your goal. You never get pulled all the way to one side. You stay central and adjust.

When the other team attacks down the wing, your job is not to chase the winger. Your job is to cover the space in the center where a cross might land. Your defenders handle the wide areas. You protect the heart of the defense.

## The Picture in Your Head

Great sweepers carry a picture of the field in their heads. You know where every player is standing without looking. You update that picture every few seconds by scanning.

When a teammate receives the ball under pressure, you already know who is open for the next pass. When the other team wins the ball, you already know where the danger is coming from. The picture in your head keeps you one step ahead.

Reading the game is a skill you can practice. Watch soccer on television and try to predict where the next pass will go. Pay attention to the patterns. The more you read, the better you get. The game slows down for you because your mind speeds up. That is what makes you the sweeper.
