# Your Mind

The strongest muscle a goalkeeper has is not in the arms or the legs. The strongest muscle is the mind. Your body makes the save, but your mind puts you in position to make the save. Your mind keeps you sharp when the game is slow.

Every goalkeeper in history has been scored on. The greatest goalkeepers of all time have had the ball go past them. Getting scored on is part of the position. A goal against you does not mean you did something wrong. A goal against you means soccer happened.

The most important moment after a goal is the very next moment. Shake your hands out. Take a breath. Reset your focus. The goal is in the past. The next play is the only play that matters. Walk back to your goal line, set your feet, and get ready for the restart.

Save the replay for after the game. You can think about the goal later. You can learn from the goal after the final whistle. Right now, your team needs you focused on what is happening next. The best goalkeepers have short memories during a game.

Staying focused during quiet stretches is one of the hardest parts of goalkeeping. Your team might have the ball for five minutes straight. The ball might not come near you for long stretches. Those quiet minutes are dangerous. Your mind can wander. Your body can go cold. A shot can come out of nowhere.

Keep yourself engaged during quiet moments. Talk to your defenders. Adjust your position. Scan the field. Count the opposing players. Bounce on your toes. Stay connected to the game even when the game is far from your goal.

Build a routine for staying sharp. Every time the ball crosses midfield, reset your position. Every time there is a stoppage in play, check your gloves. Every time your team wins a goal kick, take a deep breath. Small habits keep your mind in the game.

Confidence comes from preparation. When you have practiced your diving, you feel confident diving in a game. When you have practiced your catches, you feel confident catching in a game. Trust your preparation.

Talk to yourself in a positive way. Tell yourself you are ready. Tell yourself you are going to make the next save. Tell yourself the ball is not getting past you. The voice inside your head matters. Make sure that voice is on your side.

Mistakes happen. You might drop a ball you should have caught. You might come off your line at the wrong time. You might kick a goal kick out of bounds. None of those moments define you. How you respond to those moments defines you. Pick the ball up, reset, and move forward.

The save that changes the game often comes at the hardest moment. Your team is tired. The score is close. The other team is pressing hard. A shot comes and you make a save that keeps your team in the game. That save gives your whole team new energy. That save can be the turning point.

You will have games where you make ten saves. You will have games where you barely touch the ball. Both kinds of games require the same level of focus. The goalkeeper's job does not change based on how busy the game is.

Be proud of your position every time you step on the field. Goalkeeping is special. The bravery, the focus, the leadership, and the skill you develop as a goalkeeper will stay with you far beyond soccer. The lessons you learn between the posts are lessons about resilience, about composure, and about stepping up when your team needs you most.

The final whistle blows. You gave everything you had. You stayed focused for the whole game. You talked to your defenders. You made saves. You distributed the ball. You led from the back. Win or lose, you did your job.

You are the goalkeeper. Be proud of that every single day.
