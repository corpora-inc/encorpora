# Set Pieces

Set pieces are the moments in a game when play restarts from a dead ball. Corner kicks, free kicks, and penalty kicks all count as set pieces. These moments are predictable. You know the ball is coming. You have time to prepare. Preparation is your greatest advantage.

## Corner Kicks

The other team has a corner kick. The ball is going to fly into your box. Your box is your territory. You own that space. Take command of the situation before the kick is taken.

Position yourself toward the back half of the goal. Stand a step or two off your line. You need room to come forward and attack the ball. Starting too far back traps you on your goal line.

Organize your defenders before the corner kick. Tell each defender to mark a specific opposing player. Make sure someone is covering the near post and someone is covering the far post. Your voice makes the defense organized.

Read the flight of the ball as soon as the corner kick is taken. If the ball is coming to your area, call loudly. Say "keeper" so your defenders know to clear out of your way. Go and get the ball.

Catch the ball at the highest point of your jump. Use two hands. Pull the ball into your chest. Land with the ball secure. A clean catch off a corner kick is one of the best plays a goalkeeper can make.

Sometimes the ball is too far away or too crowded to catch safely. In those moments, punch the ball. Use both fists together. Punch the ball as high and as far from the goal as you can. Punch toward the sides of the field, not back into the middle.

## Free Kicks

The other team has a free kick near your goal. Organize a wall. The wall is a line of your teammates standing between the ball and the goal. The wall blocks part of the goal so you only have to cover the rest.

Position the wall to cover the near side of the goal. Move to the far side where the wall is not covering. Make sure you can see the ball through the gaps.

Tell your wall to stand still and hold firm. A wall that holds its ground blocks more shots. Once the wall is set, focus your eyes on the ball. Watch the kicker's approach. Be ready to react the instant the ball leaves the kicker's foot.

## Penalty Kicks

A penalty kick is a tough moment for any goalkeeper. The shooter stands twelve yards away with a clear shot at goal. That is what makes a penalty save so special.

Stand in the center of your goal line. Stay tall. Stay big. Spread your arms wide. Look at the shooter. A confident goalkeeper puts doubt in the shooter's mind.

Pick a side to dive toward, or wait and react to the shot. Both strategies work. Try both in practice and find what works best for you.

Stay on your line until the ball is kicked. When the shooter strikes the ball, explode toward the ball. Push off hard with your legs. Reach with your hands. Even a fingertip on the ball can push a penalty kick wide.

The pressure is on the shooter, not on you. Every save is a bonus. Stand tall, pick your moment, and go for the ball with everything you have.

## Before Every Set Piece

Talk to your defenders before every set piece. Make sure everyone knows their job. Point to the players who need marking. A few seconds of organization before the kick can prevent a goal.

Be loud. Be clear. Be confident. Short sharp words work best. Say "near post" or "mark ten" or "push up." Your defenders will understand.

Set pieces are the moments where a goalkeeper takes control of the game. Own those moments. Your leadership on set pieces makes the entire team stronger.
