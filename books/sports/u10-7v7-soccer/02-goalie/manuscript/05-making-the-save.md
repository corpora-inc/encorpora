# Making the Save

The shot is struck. The ball is flying toward your goal. Everything you have practiced comes down to this moment. Your body knows what to do. Trust your training.

The cleanest save is a catch. Get your whole body behind the ball. Spread your fingers wide. Let the ball land in your hands and pull the ball into your chest. Wrap both arms around the ball. Squeeze the ball tight. A clean catch ends the danger completely. The ball is yours and your team has possession.

Catch with soft hands. Let your hands give a little as the ball arrives. Think about cushioning the ball rather than slapping at the ball. Soft hands absorb the power of the shot. Hard hands let the ball bounce out.

When the shot is low and to your side, dive. Push off with the foot closest to the ball. Extend your arms toward the ball. Get your bottom hand behind the ball and your top hand on top of the ball. Land on your side, not on your stomach. Tuck the ball into your body as soon as you hit the ground.

When the shot is high, push off the ground with both feet. Reach up with both hands. Catch the ball at the highest point you can reach. Pull the ball down to your chest as you land. High balls need strong hands and a fearless jump.

Sometimes the shot is too powerful to catch. Push the ball away from danger. Use your palm to tip the ball over the crossbar. Use your fingertips to push the ball around the post. A save does not have to be a catch. Pushing the ball to a safe place is just as valuable as holding the ball.

When you tip the ball over the bar, push the ball upward with your palm. Use the flat part of your hand. Follow through with your arm. Make sure the ball goes over the bar and not back into the danger area. A good tip sends the ball safely over the goal.

When you push the ball around the post, reach across your body and deflect the ball to the side. Push the ball wide enough that the ball rolls away from the goal. A save around the post usually leads to a corner kick. A corner kick is much better than a goal.

Smothering the ball at the attacker's feet is one of the bravest saves in soccer. The attacker is close. The ball is on the ground. You dive forward and wrap your whole body around the ball. Tuck the ball under your chest. Curl your body around the ball. Protect the ball with every part of yourself.

Get back up fast after every save. The play does not stop because you went to the ground. Spring back to your feet. Find the ball. Get into position again. The fastest goalkeepers turn one save into two saves because the fastest goalkeepers are always ready for the rebound.

Watch for rebounds. A save that bounces back into play is still dangerous. After you push the ball away, look immediately for the follow-up shot. An attacker might be waiting for the loose ball. Recover your position and prepare for the next shot.

The joy of a great save is unlike anything else in soccer. The moment you feel the ball in your gloves after a full-stretch dive is pure magic. Your teammates cheer. The crowd reacts. You kept the ball out of the net. That feeling is why you play goalkeeper.

Every save builds your confidence. The first big save in a game makes you feel unstoppable. Carry that feeling into the next shot and the next shot after that. Confidence turns good goalkeepers into great goalkeepers.

Hold the ball up after a big save. Take a breath. Look at your defenders. Give a nod. You did your job. Now get ready to do your job again.
