# The Ball Is Coming

The other team has the ball. The attack is building. You can feel the play moving toward your goal. Now your focus sharpens. Now your training takes over.

Read the attack as the ball moves forward. Watch the player on the ball. Watch where that player is looking. The attacker's eyes often tell you where the ball is going next. Read the body shape of the player with the ball. An open body shape usually means a pass. A square body shape usually means a shot.

Get into position early. Move to the spot in your goal that lines you up with the ball. Remember the angle from your warm-up. The angle is the imaginary line from the ball to the center of the goal. Stand on that line. When you stand on the angle, you are directly between the shooter and the best part of the goal. Your angle is the most important thing in goalkeeping.

Now cut down the angle by stepping forward toward the ball. Picture what the shooter sees. When you stand on your goal line, the shooter sees a wide open goal on both sides of you. But when you step forward two or three steps, your body covers more of that view. The goal behind you appears smaller to the shooter. The shooter has less open net to aim at. Two steps forward can take away half of what the shooter sees. Three steps can take away even more. Cutting the angle is how goalkeepers stop shots before the shots are even taken. You do not need to dive for a save that your positioning already prevented.

Make yourself big as the shooter prepares to strike. Spread your arms wide. Bend your knees. Stay low but not too low. A big goalkeeper covers more of the goal. Your body should fill as much space as possible between the posts.

Stay on the balls of your feet. Keep your weight forward. Lean slightly toward the ball. Your hands should be in front of your body, not at your sides. Ready hands catch the ball faster than hands that start from a resting position.

Stay on your feet as long as you can. Going to the ground too early gives the attacker an easy option. The attacker can kick the ball over a goalkeeper who is already on the ground. The attacker can go around a goalkeeper who has committed too soon. Patient goalkeepers save more shots.

Watch the ball, not the attacker's feet. The attacker might fake a shot or do a fancy move. None of that matters. The ball tells you the truth. Follow the ball with your eyes and your body will follow.

An attacker is running straight at you in a one-on-one situation. Stay calm. This moment is your moment. Close the distance by moving toward the attacker. Stay on your feet. Stay low. Spread yourself wide. Force the attacker to make a decision. Many attackers panic when a goalkeeper comes out strong.

Time your challenge. Wait for the attacker to push the ball slightly too far. When the ball is away from the attacker's feet, that is your moment. Dive at the ball. Smother the ball. Get your body behind the ball and your hands around the ball. Win the ball cleanly.

Sometimes the right choice is to stay on your line. If the attacker is far from goal, you do not need to rush out. If you have defenders between you and the ball, trust your defenders to do their job. Come out when you are the best option. Stay back when your defenders have the situation covered.

Set your feet before the shot comes. Two quick shuffles and then stop. Be balanced. Be still. Be ready. A goalkeeper who is set and balanced can dive in any direction.

Communicate with your defenders during the attack. Tell your defenders to close down the player with the ball. Tell your defenders to cover the passing lanes. Your voice helps your defenders do their job. Your voice gives you more time to prepare for the shot.

The ball is coming. You are in position. You are big. You are balanced. You are ready. This is the moment you have been waiting for.
