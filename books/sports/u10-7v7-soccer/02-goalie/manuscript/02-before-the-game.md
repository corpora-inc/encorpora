# Before the Game

Your game starts before the whistle blows. The work you do before kickoff sets the tone for everything that follows. A goalkeeper who is prepared feels confident. A goalkeeper who feels confident makes better saves.

Put your gloves on early. Get used to the feel of the gloves on your hands. Squeeze them. Open and close your fists. The gloves should feel like part of your body by the time the game begins.

Warm up your hands first. Have a teammate toss easy balls to your chest. Catch each ball cleanly with both hands. Squeeze the ball tight when you catch the ball. Feel the ball stick in your gloves. Start simple and build up from there.

Move to harder catches. Have your teammate throw balls to your left and to your right. Reach out and pull each ball in. Catch balls above your head. Catch balls down by your ankles. Touch every part of the goal with your warm-up catches.

Practice your diving before the game. Get down to the ground on your left side. Get down to the ground on your right side. The ground should feel familiar before the first shot comes. A goalkeeper who has already been on the ground is not afraid to dive during the game.

Work on your footwork. Shuffle side to side across the goal. Stay on the balls of your feet. Keep your weight forward. Quick feet help you reach shots that slow feet cannot reach. Your feet are just as important as your hands.

Take some goal kicks during warm-ups. Kick a few balls from the ground. Find your range. Know where your kicks are going to land. A confident goal kick starts with a confident warm-up.

Warm up your throws too. Roll the ball to a teammate on the ground. Throw a ball overhand to a teammate farther away. Practice both sides. Your distribution is part of your game and your distribution needs to be warm.

Visualize the saves you are going to make. Close your eyes for ten seconds. Picture yourself diving to your left and catching the ball. Picture yourself catching a high ball off a corner kick. Picture yourself standing tall in a one-on-one. See yourself making those plays before the game begins.

Take a few deep breaths. Calm your heartbeat. Nerves are normal before a game. Every goalkeeper feels nervous. The deep breaths turn nervous energy into focused energy. Breathe in through your nose. Breathe out through your mouth. Do that three times.

Check your goal before the game starts. Stand on your line. Look at both posts. Know how big your goal is. Step to the middle. Feel where the center of the goal is without looking behind you. Your body should know the goal without your eyes needing to check.

Talk to your defenders before kickoff. Tell your center back to stay close. Tell your outside backs where you want them. Make sure your defenders know you are there. A quick word before the game builds the connection you will need during the game.

Practice your positioning in the goal. An angle in goalkeeping is the imaginary line between the ball and the center of the goal. When you stand on that line, you block the most direct path to the goal. Have a teammate put the ball on the penalty spot. Stand in the center of the goal, right on that imaginary line. Now have the teammate move the ball to the left side. Shuffle across so your body stays on the new line between the ball and the middle of the goal. Move the ball to the right side. Shuffle again. Every time the ball moves, the line moves, and you move with the line. Your body should know where to stand based on where the ball is. This is called covering your angle, and goalkeepers practice this every single day.

You are ready now. Your gloves are on. Your hands are warm. Your feet are quick. Your mind is focused. Your defenders know you are behind them. The whistle blows and you are prepared for anything.
