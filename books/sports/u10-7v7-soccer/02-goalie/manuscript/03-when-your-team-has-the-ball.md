# When Your Team Has the Ball

Your team has the ball. The play is happening on the other side of the field. The ball is far away from your goal. You might think your job is done for the moment. Your job is never done.

Stay alert when your team is attacking. The ball can change direction in an instant. A misplaced pass or a strong tackle can send the ball right back toward your goal. You need to be ready for that moment.

Move off your goal line. Step forward into the field. When your team has the ball in the attacking half, you should be well off your line. Standing on your line when the ball is far away leaves too much space between you and your defenders.

Think of yourself as an extra defender. You are the sweeper behind the sweeper. If a long ball goes over the top of your defense, you are there to collect the ball. You cover the space that your defenders cannot cover. Your position off the line makes that possible.

Move with the play. When the ball shifts to the right side of the field, take a step to your right. When the ball shifts to the left side of the field, take a step to your left. Your body should always be in line with the ball. Small adjustments keep you in the right position.

Watch the field while your team attacks. Count the opposing players. Notice where the opposing forwards are standing. If an opposing forward is creeping toward the center of the field, make a note. That player is waiting for a counter-attack. You see the danger before your defenders see the danger.

Talk to your defenders constantly. Your defenders are watching the ball. Your defenders might not see the runner behind them. You can see the runner. Tell your left back to check over their shoulder. Tell your center back to drop a little deeper. Your voice keeps the defense organized.

Use simple words when you talk to your defenders. Say "left shoulder" to warn a defender about a player behind them on the left. Say "drop" to tell a defender to move closer to you. Say "hold" to tell your defenders to stay where they are. Say "push up" to tell your defenders to move forward with the team. Clear words help your defenders react quickly.

Be a passing option for your defenders. Sometimes a defender is under pressure and has no one to pass to going forward. You are always behind the defender. You are always open. Call for the ball. Receive the pass with your feet. Reset the play by passing to the other side of the field. Your feet are part of your game.

Keep your feet moving even when the ball is far away. Stay on the balls of your feet. Bounce lightly. Keep your muscles warm and your reactions sharp. A goalkeeper who is standing still and flat-footed is a goalkeeper who is not ready.

Read the game from the back. You can see patterns that other players cannot see. You can see that your team keeps attacking down the right side and the left side is wide open. Tell your teammates. You can see that the opposing team is pushing everyone forward, leaving space behind their defense. Tell your sweeper. Your brain is working the whole game.

Pay attention to the opposing goalkeeper during your team's attack. Watch where the opposing goalkeeper kicks the ball. Watch whether the opposing goalkeeper throws long or short. Know what is coming when the other team wins the ball back. That knowledge helps you prepare for the counter-attack.

Your team has the ball, but you are still playing. You are reading. You are moving. You are talking. You are covering space. You are staying sharp. A great goalkeeper never takes a play off.
