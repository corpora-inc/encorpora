# Before and After

I wrote a hundred-page playbook before I coached a single practice. The playbook had three formation shapes with numeric codes. It had a fourteen-player rotation model. It had detailed position guides for every role on the field. It had specific practice plans for specific days of the week. It even had a season arc from week one through the playoffs.

Then the season started.

Real kids showed up with real personalities, real skill gaps, and real emotions. The formation codes never left my notebook. The three-shape system was too complex for nine-year-olds to absorb in real time. The fourteen-player rotation model collapsed the first time a kid got hurt and another one really wanted a different position. Months of planning met about fifteen minutes of actual soccer.

Five games taught me more than all of that preparation. This pamphlet is what survived.

## Who This Is For

This guide is for anyone involved in U10 7v7 soccer. Coaches will find a simple system they can install in one practice. Parents will understand what the team is building and how to support it. Players will see where they fit and why their role matters. Everyone should read the whole thing. The book is short enough to finish in one sitting.

## The Goal

The goal is still to win. Winning at U10 means something specific. Put kids where they thrive. Keep the system simple enough for a nine-year-old to understand. Adapt constantly to what is actually happening on the field. The team that is organized and confident beats the team with more raw talent almost every time.

Winning also means every kid contributes. Every kid has a role that matches their strengths. Every kid gets better over the course of the season. A team where every player understands their job and feels valued is a team that wins games and has fun doing it.

## How This Book Works

Every sentence in this guide is written to be clear and complete on its own. The book doubles as language-learning material. Each sentence should make sense even if you read it in isolation. This constraint makes the writing better, not worse. Clear sentences help everyone.
