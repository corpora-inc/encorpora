# Supporting the Attack

Defense is your first job. But defending is not your only job. When your team has the ball, you become part of the attack. You push forward, you offer passing options, and you help your team keep the ball moving.

## Push Up the Field

When your team wins the ball, move forward. Do not stand in your defensive position while the rest of the team pushes into the other half. If you stay back, a huge gap opens between you and your attackers. That gap makes passing difficult.

Push up past the halfway line. Push up with your defensive partner and the sweeper. The whole team moves forward as one unit. A compact team with short passing distances is hard to defend against. You help create that compact shape by moving forward with the ball.

## Be a Passing Option

When a teammate has the ball and is under pressure, you can help by showing yourself in an open space. Move into a spot where the teammate can see you. Call for the ball if you are open. A short pass back to you relieves the pressure and keeps the ball safe.

You do not always need to make a fancy play. Sometimes the best thing you can do is receive a pass, take a calm touch, and find the next open teammate. Simple passing keeps the ball moving. Simple passing frustrates the other team.

## Overlapping Runs

As you grow as a player, you will learn to make overlapping runs. An overlapping run is when you sprint past your own teammate on the outside. Picture your winger on the ball near the sideline. You run from behind, past the winger, and into the space ahead. Now the winger has two choices: dribble inside or pass the ball to you on the overlap.

Overlapping runs surprise the other team. The other team expects defenders to stay back. When a defender suddenly appears on the wing at full speed, the other team scrambles to adjust. That confusion can create a chance to cross the ball or take a shot.

Do not overlap every time. Pick your moments. Overlap when you see open space on the wing and you are sure the sweeper can cover for you. This is an advanced move to work toward over the season.

## Know When to Get Back

The biggest risk of going forward is getting caught out of position. If your team loses the ball while you are up in the attack, there is a hole in the defense where you should be standing.

Always keep one eye on the game's balance. If the attack is not working and the ball might be lost, start moving back toward your position. Do not wait until the other team has the ball. Anticipate the turnover and get a head start on your recovery run.

Sprint back when the ball is lost. Full speed, no jogging. Every second matters on a recovery run. Your partner and your sweeper need you in the defensive line.

## Communicate with the Sweeper

Talk to the sweeper before you go forward. A quick word like "I'm going" tells the sweeper to cover your space. The sweeper can shift over and fill the gap you leave behind. When you come back, tell the sweeper "I'm back" so the sweeper can return to the central position.

The sweeper is your safety net when you join the attack. Trust the sweeper. If the sweeper says "get back," get back immediately. The sweeper sees something you cannot see.

## The Balance

Great defenders know when to go and when to stay. You feel the rhythm of the game. You sense when the moment is right to push forward. You sense when the moment calls for staying home. That sense grows with every game you play.

You are a defender who can attack. You are a complete player. When your team needs you going forward, you deliver. When your team needs you at the back, you are already there.
