# Winning the Ball

Winning the ball is the best feeling in defense. One moment the other team has the ball. The next moment you have the ball. You turned defense into attack with one play. That moment belongs to you.

## Tackling

A tackle is a direct challenge for the ball. Plant your standing foot firmly on the ground. Use the inside of your other foot to block the ball or poke the ball away from the attacker. Keep your body weight forward, not leaning back. A strong tackle is firm and committed. When you go for the ball, go for the ball with purpose.

Every tackle must be fair and clean. Win the ball first. A good referee can tell the difference between a fair tackle and a reckless one. Fair tackling is a skill, and you can become very good at fair tackling with practice.

The block tackle is your most reliable tool. You and the attacker meet the ball at the same time. You use the inside of your foot like a wall. You lock your ankle tight and hold your ground.

## Intercepting Passes

Interceptions are even better than tackles. An interception means you read the play before the attacker even received the ball. You stepped into the passing lane and took the ball away.

Watch the passer's eyes and body position. Most young players look exactly where they are about to pass. The passer's hips will point toward the target. If you see the pass coming, step in front of the attacker and take the ball.

Timing is everything on an interception. Step too early and the passer changes the target. Step too late and the ball is past you. The right moment is when the passer's foot is already swinging. At that point, the pass is committed and you can pounce.

## Heading

Sometimes the ball comes through the air. High balls, long kicks, and crosses require you to head the ball away from danger. Use your forehead, not the top of your head. Keep your eyes open and watch the ball all the way onto your forehead. A strong header sends the ball far away from the goal.

When a high ball comes into your area, call for the ball. Shout "mine" or "away" so your teammates know you are going for the header. Attack the ball. Go up and meet the ball at the highest point you can reach.

## Clearing the Ball

Sometimes you win the ball in a dangerous area near your own goal. The most important thing is to get the ball out of the danger zone. Clear the ball wide toward the sideline. Do not clear the ball through the center of the field. A clearance through the middle can land at the feet of another attacker. A clearance toward the sideline sends the ball to safety.

When you have time, a clearance can become a pass. Find the sweeper in the center. The sweeper can collect the ball and start a counter-attack. Find a teammate on the wing. A pass to the wing keeps possession for your team.

## Turning Defense into Attack

The moment you win the ball, everything changes. Your team was defending. Now your team is attacking. The other team is out of position. The other team is still thinking about their attack, not about defending.

Look up as soon as you win the ball. See which teammate is open. The sweeper is often in a great position to receive a pass. A quick pass to the sweeper can launch a counter-attack. A pass to a winger on the far side can catch the other team completely off guard.

You are not just a player who stops attacks. You are a player who starts attacks. Every ball you win is the first pass of a new attack. That power sits in your feet.
