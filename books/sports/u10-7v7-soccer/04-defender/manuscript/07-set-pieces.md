# Set Pieces

Set pieces are moments when the ball is dead and both teams organize before play restarts. Corners, free kicks, throw-ins, and goal kicks all count as set pieces. These moments matter because the other team has time to plan. You need a plan too.

## Defending Corners

When the other team takes a corner kick, you have a job to do inside the penalty area. Find the attacker you are marking. Stay close to that attacker. Stay between the attacker and the goal.

When the corner kick comes in, attack the ball. Do not wait for the ball to arrive. Go to the ball. Meet the ball at the highest point you can reach. Head the ball away from the goal. Head the ball toward the sideline or out past the top of the penalty area.

Stay on your toes before the kick. Attackers will try to sneak away from you at the last second. Watch your attacker and watch the ball at the same time. A defender who stands flat-footed will lose the race to the ball every time.

If the ball drops to the ground inside the box, clear the ball immediately. Do not try to dribble inside your own penalty area. Get the ball out of the box. Kick the ball hard and kick the ball wide.

## Defending Free Kicks

When the other team earns a free kick near your goal, hold your position in the defensive line. Your coach or sweeper may organize a wall to block the direct shot. If you are not in the wall, mark an attacker nearby.

Block the passing lanes. The free kick taker might try a quick pass to a teammate instead of a direct shot. Be ready for both options. Stay alert and react the moment the ball moves.

## Throw-Ins on Your Side

When your team has a throw-in on your side of the field, you become an option for the throw. Move into a space where the thrower can reach you. Offer a safe, short option if no one else is open. Receive the ball, control the ball, and find the next pass.

When the other team has a throw-in on your side, mark the nearest attacker. Stay close. Do not give the attacker room to receive the ball and turn. Your job is to make the restart as difficult as possible for the other team.

## Goal Kicks

On goal kicks from your own goalkeeper, move into a space where the goalkeeper can find you with a pass. A short goal kick to a defender is a safe option that keeps possession. Receive the ball with a clean first touch and move the ball forward to the sweeper or to a teammate on the wing.

If the goalkeeper kicks the ball long, push up the field to compress the space. Stay connected with your defensive partner and the sweeper. Be ready to pick up any second balls that come back toward your half.

## Attacking Set Pieces

You have a role on attacking set pieces too. When your team earns a corner kick, get into the penalty area. Use your strength in the box. Find space near the goal. Attack the ball when the corner comes in. A header from a defender on a corner kick is one of the best ways to score.

On attacking free kicks near the other team's goal, push up and be available. If the ball is crossed into the box, compete for the header. Defenders who arrive in the box with energy and determination can score important goals.

After every attacking set piece, be aware of the counter-attack. If the other team clears the ball, sprint back to your position immediately. Do not jog. The transition from attack to defense happens fast, and you need to be faster.

Set pieces are moments of opportunity and danger. You are ready for both.
