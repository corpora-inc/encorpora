# When They Attack Your Side

The ball is on your side of the field. An attacker is running straight at you. Your heart beats a little faster. That is normal. That feeling means you are ready.

Close the distance. Do not stand still and wait for the attacker to come to you. Move forward to meet the attacker. Closing the distance takes away the attacker's time and space. The less time the attacker has, the harder the decision becomes for the attacker.

But do not sprint in recklessly. Close the distance quickly at first, then slow down as you get near. The last few steps should be short and controlled. You want to arrive balanced and on your feet, not lunging forward out of control.

Stay on your feet. This is one of the most important rules for a defender. Do not dive in. Do not slide unless you are absolutely sure you will win the ball. When you leave your feet, you are out of the play. If the attacker steps around your lunge, you are on the ground and the attacker is gone. Stay upright, stay balanced, and stay in the fight.

Be patient. The attacker wants you to commit too early. The attacker wants you to lunge so the attacker can dodge around you. Do not give the attacker what the attacker wants. Wait for the right moment. Watch the ball, not the attacker's feet or shoulders. The ball tells you the truth. The attacker's body might fake one way, but the ball has to go where the attacker takes the ball.

Force the attacker toward the sideline. Your body angle decides where the attacker can go. Body angle means which direction your chest and feet are pointing. If you face the attacker straight on, the attacker can go left or right. But if you stand slightly to the inside of the field and turn your body toward the sideline, you close off the path to the center and leave only the path toward the touchline.

Picture a hallway. Your body is one wall. The sideline is the other wall. The attacker can only run down the hallway toward the corner. The sideline is your teammate. The sideline takes away half the field. Once the attacker is near the sideline, the attacker has nowhere to go. The attacker is trapped between you and the white line.

Stand with your inside foot forward and your outside foot back. Point your chest toward the sideline. Keep your knees bent and your weight low. The attacker will take the only road you leave open, and that road leads to a dead end. Your body angle made the decision for the attacker before the attacker even realized what happened.

Jockey the attacker. Jockeying means staying low, staying balanced, and moving sideways as the attacker moves. Keep your feet active. Take small, quick steps. Do not cross your feet over each other. Keep your weight on the balls of your feet so you can change direction fast.

Contain the attacker. Sometimes winning the ball means not letting the attacker go anywhere. If the attacker cannot get past you and cannot pass the ball forward, the attacker is stuck. Stuck attackers make mistakes. Stuck attackers pass the ball backward. Stuck attackers lose the ball.

When the moment comes, win the ball. You will feel the right moment. The attacker takes a heavy touch. The attacker looks down at the ball. The attacker pushes the ball too far in front. That is your chance. Step in firmly and take the ball. Plant your standing foot and use your tackling foot to poke the ball away or block the ball.

Every tackle should be firm, fair, and clean. Win the ball, not the player. A clean tackle is a beautiful thing. A clean tackle sends a message to the other team. The message says you are here, you are ready, and you will not be beaten easily.

After you win the ball, look up. Find a teammate to pass the ball to. The sweeper is often the safest option. A pass to the wing is another good choice. Do not just kick the ball away in a panic. You earned that ball. Use the ball wisely.

The attacker is coming at you. You are ready. Close the distance, stay on your feet, be patient, and force the attacker wide. You know exactly what to do.
