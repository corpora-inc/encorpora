# When They Attack the Other Side

The ball is on the opposite side of the field. Your defensive partner is dealing with the attacker. You are not directly involved right now. But your job in this moment is just as important.

Pinch toward the center. When the ball is on your partner's side, slide in toward the middle of the field. Close the space between you and your partner. The center of the defense is the most dangerous area on the field. An attacker running through the center has a straight path to the goal. By pinching in, you shut that path down.

Cover for your partner. Your partner is pressing the attacker on the other side. Your partner might get beaten. Your partner might get pulled out of position. You are the backup. If the ball comes through the middle, you are there to intercept the pass or meet the attacker. Your partner can defend with confidence because your partner knows you are covering the center.

Do not ball-watch. Ball-watching means staring at the ball and forgetting everything else. The most dangerous attacker is the one you do not see. While the ball is on the other side, check your surroundings. Look over your shoulder. See if any attacker is sneaking behind you. An attacker making a blind-side run is hoping you are only watching the ball.

Mark the attacker in your area. If an opposing player is standing near you, stay close to that player. Stay between that player and the goal. If a pass comes to that player, you want to be close enough to intercept the ball or make an immediate challenge. Do not give the attacker a free touch. Stay tight and make the attacker earn every centimeter of space.

Watch for the switch. The other team might send a long ball from one side of the field to the other. A switch of play can catch defenders off guard. If the ball suddenly flies over to your side, you need to be ready. Stay alert and expect the switch at any time.

Read the field. When you are not directly involved in the play, you have time to see the big picture. Notice where the attackers are standing. Notice where the gaps are forming. Notice where your sweeper is positioned. Use this information to adjust your position. Good defenders are always thinking, even when the ball is far away.

Communicate with your partner. Tell your partner what you see from your side. You might call out "you're covered" to let your partner know you have the center. You might call out "runner right" if you see someone making a run. Your voice helps your partner defend. A talking defense is a strong defense.

Stay connected to the sweeper. While you pinch in toward the center, keep a good distance from the sweeper. Do not drop too deep and stand on top of the sweeper. The sweeper needs room to operate. You and the sweeper should form two layers of defense, not one clump of players in the same spot.

Stay on your toes. The play can change direction in a heartbeat. One pass, one flick, and the ball is on your side. The defenders who stay alert and keep adjusting are the ones who are always ready. When the ball is on the other side, you have a chance to be the smartest player on the field. You can see the whole picture. You can read the danger before the danger arrives. You can position yourself so perfectly that when the ball comes your way, you are already there waiting.

Your partner handles one side. You handle the other. And in between, you both protect the center together. That partnership keeps the goal safe.
