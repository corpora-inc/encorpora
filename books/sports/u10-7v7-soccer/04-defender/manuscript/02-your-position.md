# Your Position

You play on one side of the defense. Your defensive partner plays on the other side. One of you covers the left. One of you covers the right. The sweeper plays behind both of you, in the center. The goalkeeper stands behind the sweeper. Together, the four of you form the defensive unit.

Picture the shape from above. The two defenders stand across the field, side by side. The sweeper sits just behind the two defenders, in the middle. The three of you form a triangle that points backward. The goalkeeper fills the space behind the triangle. The shape is compact, balanced, and hard for any attacker to break through.

Your side of the field is your territory. You own the space from the center of the field to the sideline on your side. You patrol that space. You know every blade of grass on your side of the defense. When the ball is on your side, you are the first line of defense. You are the one who meets the attacker.

Stay connected to your partner. Do not drift too far from the center of the field. If you wander all the way to the sideline, a gap opens between you and your partner. Attackers love gaps. Attackers will run straight through any hole in the defense. Keep the gap small by staying within passing distance of your partner at all times.

Do not squeeze too close to your partner either. If both defenders stand in the center of the field, the wings are wide open. Attackers will run down the sideline with nobody to stop them. Find the balance. Stay wide enough to cover your side but close enough to protect the middle.

The sweeper is your safety net. The sweeper plays behind you and reads the game. If an attacker gets past you, the sweeper is there to clean up. But do not rely on the sweeper for every play. Your job is to stop the attack before the ball reaches the sweeper. The sweeper is the last resort, not the first plan.

Talk to the sweeper. The sweeper can see things you cannot see. The sweeper can see runners sneaking behind you. The sweeper can see the ball switching from one side to the other. Listen when the sweeper talks. The sweeper might say "runner behind you" or "shift left" or "push up." Trust the sweeper's voice and adjust your position.

Move as the ball moves. When the ball is on your side, push forward toward the attacker. When the ball is on the other side, pinch in toward the center. When the ball is far up the field in the other team's half, push forward with the rest of the team. When the ball comes back toward your own goal, drop back into your defensive position.

Your position is never fixed in one spot. You slide left and right. You push up and drop back. You follow the flow of the game. The best defenders look like they are always in the right place at the right time. The secret is constant movement. Small adjustments make a big difference.

Watch the ball, watch the attacker nearest you, and watch your partner. Those three things tell you where to stand. If you can see all three, you are in a good position. If you lose track of any one of them, adjust until you can see all three again.

The shape of the defense only works when both defenders and the sweeper move together. You are one piece of a three-player puzzle. When you move, the sweeper adjusts. When your partner moves, you adjust. The defense breathes as one unit, expanding and compressing with every pass and every run.

Own your position. Know your territory. Stay connected to your partner and your sweeper. Move with the ball. The defense starts with where you stand.
