# Playing Together

You are one of three attackers. You do not attack alone. The three of you work together to break down the defense and create scoring chances. A striker who combines with teammates scores more goals than a striker who tries to do everything alone.

## The Front Three

The left wing, the center striker, and the right wing form a front line across the field. Each attacker has a zone, but the three zones overlap. You can drift into a teammate's zone to combine. The key is that all three attackers stay aware of each other's positions.

When one attacker has the ball, the other two attackers move to create options. You might make a run to receive a pass. You might pull a defender away to open space. You might hold your position to keep the defense stretched wide.

## Give and Go

The give and go is the simplest and most effective combination play in soccer. You pass the ball to a teammate. You immediately sprint into space. Your teammate passes the ball back to you. You receive the ball in a better position than where you started.

The give and go works because the defender follows the ball. When you pass, the defender relaxes for a moment. That moment is when you sprint past the defender. Your teammate sees your run and plays the return ball into your path.

Practice the give and go with the attackers on either side of you. The more comfortable you are with the timing, the more dangerous the combination becomes.

## Crossing the Ball

When you play on the wing, one of your most important jobs is crossing the ball. A cross is a pass from the wing into the middle of the field, near the goal. The center striker and the far-side wing attacker attack the cross.

Get to the end line or the edge of the box. Look up and find your teammate in the middle. Whip the ball into the area between the goalkeeper and the defenders. Your teammate runs onto the cross and finishes with a header or a volley.

When your teammate on the wing has the ball, get in the box and be ready for the cross. Time your run into the box. Arrive as the ball arrives. Attack the ball with purpose.

## Movement Off the Ball

Most of your time during a game is spent without the ball. What you do during that time matters just as much as what you do with the ball at your feet. Move constantly. Make runs. Check back. Pull defenders out of position.

A run you make without receiving the ball is still valuable. Your run might drag a defender out of the center, opening a lane for the center striker. Trust that your movement makes a difference even when the ball goes somewhere else.

## Talk to Each Other

Communication between the three attackers keeps the front line organized. Call for the ball when you are open. Point to where you want the pass. Shout a teammate's name to let the teammate know you are nearby.

Tell your fellow attackers where the space is. "Switch it." "Over here." "Turn." Short words are enough. The attacking unit that communicates moves as one connected group instead of three separate players.

## Celebrate Together

When a goal goes in, every attacker celebrates together. The striker who scored runs to the teammates. The teammates who helped create the goal share the joy. A goal is never a solo achievement. The pass, the run, the cross, the finish -- every piece matters.

A great striker makes the other attackers better. You draw defenders to create space for teammates. You make the pass that sets up another attacker's goal. You celebrate a teammate's goal as loudly as your own. The best attacking units are groups of players who lift each other up and play for each other every minute of the game.
