# Finding Space

Getting open is the most important skill for a striker. You cannot score if you cannot get free from defenders. Finding space is the art of disappearing from defenders and appearing in the right place at the right time.

## Check Your Shoulder

Before the ball comes to you, check your shoulder. Look behind you. Know where the defenders are standing. Know where the open space is waiting. A quick glance over your shoulder gives you a picture of the field behind you.

Check your shoulder every few seconds. The picture changes constantly. A defender who was on your left five seconds ago might have shifted to your right. Keep updating the picture in your head.

## Move Into the Gaps

Defenders try to stay in a line across the field. Your job is to find the gaps between those defenders. A gap between two defenders is a lane to receive a pass. A gap between a defender and the sideline is a runway to sprint into.

Watch the defenders. When two defenders drift close together, a gap opens on the far side. Slide into that gap. Your teammate with the ball can see you standing in the open lane. The pass finds you because you found the space first.

## Time Your Run

Running too early lets the defender follow you. Running too late means the ball arrives before you do. The perfect run starts at the exact moment your teammate is ready to pass.

Watch your teammate on the ball. When your teammate's head comes up and eyes look forward, that is the signal. Start your run at that moment. You and the ball arrive at the same spot at the same time.

A well-timed run is almost impossible to defend. The defender has to turn and chase. You are already moving at full speed. The ball is already on its way.

## Stay Wide to Stretch the Defense

When you play on the wing, stay wide near the sideline. A wide attacker forces the defense to spread out. A defense that spreads out has bigger gaps in the middle. You create space for the center striker by staying wide. The center striker creates space for you by pulling a defender toward the middle.

Think of the three attackers as a rubber band stretched across the field. The wider the band stretches, the harder the defense has to work to cover all three attackers.

## Check Back Toward the Ball

Sometimes the best space is behind you, closer to your own team. When the defenders block the space ahead, check back toward the ball. Drop a few steps toward your teammate who has the ball. Your teammate now has a short, easy pass to play.

Checking back pulls a defender with you. That defender leaves space behind. You receive the ball, turn, and now you face the space the defender just left.

## Create Space for Others

Not every run is for you. Sometimes you make a run to drag a defender out of position. Your run creates an open lane for another attacker. The other attacker receives the ball in the space your run created.

A great striker is generous with space. You run hard even when you know the ball is going to a teammate. Your run changes the shape of the defense. The defense cannot ignore a striker who is sprinting into space.

## Always Be Moving

A striker who stands still is easy to mark. Keep moving. Shift left. Shift right. Drop back. Sprint forward. Make the defender work to track you. A defender who is constantly chasing you will eventually lose you. That moment of freedom is when the ball finds you.

Finding space is a thinking skill. Your feet do the running, but your brain picks the direction. Read the defense. Find the gap. Time the run. Get open. That is how a striker earns the ball.
