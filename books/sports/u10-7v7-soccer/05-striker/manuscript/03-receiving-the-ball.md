# Receiving the Ball

First touch is everything for a striker. A great first touch turns a good pass into a scoring chance. A great first touch gives you an extra second before the defender arrives. That extra second is the difference between a shot on goal and a lost ball.

## Know Your Plan Before the Ball Arrives

Decide what you will do before the ball reaches your feet. Will you turn and shoot. Will you pass to a teammate. Will you dribble toward the goal. Make the decision early so your first touch sets up your next move.

Think first. Touch second. Act third.

## Open Your Body

When you receive a pass, open your body to see the field. Turn your hips so you face the direction you want to play next. A striker who receives the ball with an open body can see the goal, the defenders, and the other attackers all at once.

If you receive the ball with your back completely to the goal, you have to turn before you can do anything. That extra turn gives the defender time to close in. Receive on the half-turn whenever you can. Your body is already pointed toward the goal as the ball arrives.

## Control the Ball Into Space

Your first touch should move the ball into the space where you want to go next. Touch the ball forward if you have room to run at the goal. Touch the ball to the side if you need to escape a defender. A first touch that moves the ball into space is a weapon.

Think of your first touch as a pass to yourself. You are passing the ball from where you are standing to where you want to be in one second.

## Turn With the Ball

When you have space behind you, turn with your first touch. Let the ball roll across your body and spin toward the goal. A sharp turn leaves the defender facing the wrong direction. You are now running at the goal with the ball at your feet.

Practice turning both ways. Turn to the left. Turn to the right. Use the inside of your foot. Use the outside of your foot. A striker who can turn in both directions is twice as hard to defend.

## Shield the Ball

Sometimes a defender is right on your back when the ball arrives. You cannot turn. In that moment, shield the ball with your body.

Put your body between the defender and the ball. Use your arms for balance, not for pushing. Keep the ball on the foot that is farthest from the defender. Hold the ball until a teammate arrives to help, or until the defender backs off and gives you room to turn.

Shielding is not giving up. Shielding is patience. You hold the ball safely and wait for the right moment. Then you turn or lay the ball off to a teammate.

## Receiving in the Air

Not every pass arrives on the ground. Your sweeper might send a long ball over the defense. A teammate on the wing might cross the ball in the air. You need to control these balls with your chest, your thigh, or your foot.

Cushion the ball as the ball arrives. Pull your chest or thigh back slightly to absorb the impact. The ball drops softly to your feet instead of bouncing away.

## Under Pressure

The best strikers receive the ball in tight spaces with defenders closing in. Pressure does not rattle a great striker. You know the plan before the ball arrives. You take a clean first touch. You execute your next move quickly.

Speed of thought beats speed of feet. The striker who thinks fast plays fast. Know your plan. Open your body. Take a clean touch. Move the ball into space.
