# Attacking the Goal

Shooting is the striker's signature skill. All the movement, all the first touches, all the runs lead to one moment. The ball is at your feet. The goal is in front of you. You shoot.

## Pick Your Spot

Before you shoot, pick your spot. Look at the goal. Look at the goalkeeper. Choose the corner where you want to place the ball. A shot with a target is far more dangerous than a shot blasted without a plan.

Aim for the corners of the goal. The corners are the hardest spots for the goalkeeper to reach. A low shot into the bottom corner forces the goalkeeper to dive. A shot into the top corner sails over the goalkeeper's hands. Pick your corner and trust your aim.

## Placement or Power

Sometimes a placed shot beats the goalkeeper. You side-foot the ball smoothly into the corner. The ball is not fast, but the ball is accurate. The goalkeeper dives the wrong way or cannot reach the corner in time.

Sometimes a powerful shot beats the goalkeeper. You strike through the ball with your laces. The ball rockets toward the goal. The goalkeeper sees the shot but cannot react fast enough.

Learn both. Read the situation and choose. A close-range chance might need placement. A shot from farther out might need power.

## Near Post and Far Post

The near post is the goalpost closest to you. The far post is the goalpost farthest from you. These two posts are your targets.

A near post shot is quick and catches the goalkeeper off guard. You whip the ball across the goalkeeper's body to the near side. A far post shot uses the full width of the goal. The ball travels into the far corner. The goalkeeper has to cover a long distance.

Read the goalkeeper's position before you shoot. If the goalkeeper leans toward the near post, shoot far post. If the goalkeeper stands in the center, either post works. If the goalkeeper rushes toward you, stay calm and place the ball to one side. A calm finish beats a rushing goalkeeper.

## One on One

A one-on-one chance is you against the goalkeeper with no defenders in between. Stay calm. The goalkeeper will rush toward you to make the goal look small.

Pick your side early. Commit to your decision. Place the ball low past the goalkeeper. Going low is almost always better than going high in a one-on-one. A low shot forces the goalkeeper to get down to the ground. Many one-on-one goals roll calmly into the corner.

## Follow Every Shot

The most important habit a striker can build is following up every shot. Goalkeepers spill shots. Goalkeepers push shots back into play. The ball bounces off the post and rolls back across the goal.

Sprint toward the goal after you shoot. Be ready for the ball to come back to you. Many goals come from rebounds. The striker who follows up scores the easy goals that other players miss.

## Shoot When the Chance Comes

Do not wait for the perfect chance. Shoot when you have a reasonable look at the goal. A shot on target always causes problems. The goalkeeper has to make a save. The defenders have to clear the ball. Shots create chaos near the goal. Chaos creates goals.

Great strikers shoot often. Not every shot goes in. But every shot you do not take is a goal you will never score. When the chance comes, shoot. Trust your technique. Let the ball fly.

## Both Feet

Practice shooting with both feet. A striker who can only shoot with one foot is predictable. A defender will force you onto your weaker foot. If your weaker foot can still produce a clean shot, the defender has no safe side to push you toward.

Work on your weaker foot in practice every day. The more comfortable you get, the more dangerous you become. A two-footed striker doubles the scoring chances.
