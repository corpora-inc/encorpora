# Set Pieces

Set pieces are free chances to score. A corner kick, a free kick near the goal, a penalty -- each of these moments gives your team a planned opportunity to put the ball in the net. Great strikers love set pieces because set pieces reward preparation and focus.

## Attacking Corners

When your team wins a corner kick, get in the box. Find a spot near the goal where you can attack the ball. Stand between the defenders. Watch the ball as your teammate prepares to take the corner.

Time your run. Do not stand still and wait for the ball to come to you. Start a step or two away from where you want to meet the ball. As the corner is taken, burst toward the ball with momentum. A moving striker is much harder to mark than a standing striker.

Attack the ball with your head or your foot. Meet the ball aggressively. Be brave and go for the ball first.

The near post is a dangerous area on a corner kick. A quick striker can dart to the near post and flick the ball into the goal before the goalkeeper reacts. The far post is another good spot. A cross that sails over everyone in the middle can find you waiting at the far post with an open goal.

## Free Kicks Near the Goal

When your team earns a free kick near the goal, get in the box and be ready. The free kick might be a direct shot on goal. The free kick might be a pass to a teammate in space. Either way, you need to be in position.

Stand on the edge of the defender's line. Watch the ball. When the kick is taken, react quickly. If the shot hits the wall or the goalkeeper pushes the ball back into play, you are there for the rebound. Rebounds from free kicks produce many goals because defenders lose focus after the initial kick.

If you are the player taking the free kick, pick your target before you step up to the ball. Commit to your target and strike the ball cleanly.

## Penalties

A penalty is the most high-pressure moment in soccer. You stand twelve yards from the goal with only the goalkeeper between you and a goal. Stay calm.

Pick your spot before you place the ball on the penalty spot. Choose a side -- left or right. Choose to go high or low. Make your decision and commit. Do not change your mind during your run-up. A penalty taker who changes the decision at the last moment usually misses.

Strike the ball firmly into your chosen corner. A placed penalty into the side netting gives the goalkeeper almost no chance. Firm and accurate is the formula.

If you miss a penalty, hold your head high. Even the greatest strikers in the world miss penalties. The courage to step up and take the penalty is what matters. Your teammates respect you for taking the responsibility.

## Throw-Ins Near the Goal

A throw-in near the other team's goal is a mini set piece. Make a run toward the thrower to receive a short throw. Or make a run behind the defenders to receive a long throw near the goal. Be the striker who moves and creates a chance from a throw-in.

## Always Be in the Box

The most important habit for set pieces is simple. Be in the box. Be near the goal. Be ready for the ball. Many goals from set pieces go to the striker who simply shows up in the right place at the right time.

After every free kick, every corner, every long throw -- be in the box. Look for the rebound. Look for the loose ball. Look for the deflection. The player who wants the ball the most is the player who scores.
