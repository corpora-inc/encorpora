# Preface

This book was written before I coached a single practice.

I spent months building a complete system on paper. I designed three formation shapes with numeric codes. I mapped out fourteen-player rotation models. I wrote detailed position guides for every role on the field. I planned specific practices for specific days of the week. I even wrote a season arc from week one through playoffs.

Then the season started. Real kids showed up with real personalities, real skill gaps, and real emotions. The formation codes never left my notebook. The three-shape system was too complex for nine-year-olds to absorb in real time. The fourteen-player rotation model collapsed the first time a kid got hurt and another one really wanted a different position.

Five games taught me more than all of this planning.

This book is preserved here as a historical artifact. It is a snapshot of what a first-time coach thought the game would be like before reality arrived. Some of the ideas survived contact with real soccer — the circle-up culture, learning names by week one, the no-lines principle at practice. Those are good ideas and they still work. But the core system — the formation codes, the shape-shifting, the rigid structure — did not survive.

For what I actually learned coaching real games with real kids, read the field guide: **1-2-3: A U10 Field Guide**.
