# What It Feels Like

Let me tell you what gymnastics actually feels like.

Not what it looks like on TV.

What it feels like, in your body.

Before you do a hard skill, your hands sweat.

That is the first thing.

Then your stomach goes a little fluttery.

We call those butterflies.

You take a deep breath.

You clap chalk between your hands.

The chalk makes a little white cloud.

You look at the spot where you will land.

You picture the skill in your head.

For just one second, the gym goes quiet.

Even though there is noise everywhere, you stop hearing it.

Then you go.

Once you start moving, your body knows what to do.

Your brain almost steps out of the way.

Your muscles remember the practice.

The skill happens fast, faster than thinking.

And then you land.

When you stick the landing, it is a soft thump.

Your feet hit and stay still.

Nothing wobbles.

It feels like a little click in your bones.

When you do not stick it, you take a step.

Or two steps.

Or you fall.

That is okay too.

Everybody falls.

I have fallen off the beam more times than I can count.

I have landed on my bottom on the floor.

I have flopped into the foam pit with my legs in the air.

You laugh, you climb out, you try again.

That is how it works.

Falling is part of the lesson.

After a hard practice, my arms feel like spaghetti.

My legs ache in a good way.

My hands are white with chalk all the way up to my wrists.

I am tired and happy.

I sleep really, really well.
