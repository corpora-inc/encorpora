# What It Feels Like

I want to tell you what a game actually feels like.

Not the rules.

The feeling.

You get to the field early.

The sun is still climbing.

The grass is a little wet from the morning.

You can smell it.

It smells green and fresh and a little bit like dirt.

Your cleats sink into the soft ground.

Cleats are baseball shoes with little spikes on the bottom.

They help you grip the grass when you run.

You pull your glove out of your bag.

The leather smells like leather.

You punch the inside of the glove with your fist.

That's just something baseball players do.

I don't even know why.

It feels right, though.

You jog out to your position.

You bend down and grab some dirt.

You rub it between your fingers.

The dirt is cool and dry.

You can hear your coach shouting things to your team.

You can hear parents talking in the stands.

A dog is barking somewhere.

A baby is crying.

The umpire says, "Play ball!"

Everything gets quiet for a second.

The pitcher winds up.

The ball flies.

The batter swings.

Crack.

The ball is in the air.

It's coming right at you.

Your stomach does a little flip.

You raise your glove.

You step under the ball.

You watch it the whole way down.

Smack — it lands in your glove.

You squeeze it shut.

Your team yells.

Your coach claps.

You toss the ball back to the pitcher.

You did it.

That's what baseball feels like.

Over and over again.
