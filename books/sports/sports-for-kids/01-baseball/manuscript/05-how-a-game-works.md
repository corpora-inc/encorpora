# How a Game Works

Okay, this is the part that confuses people.

I'll go slow.

A baseball game is split into innings.

An inning is just a chunk of the game.

In each inning, both teams get a turn to bat.

One team bats first.

Then the other team bats.

When you're not batting, you're playing the field.

That means you're trying to get the other team out.

What does "out" mean?

It means a player is done with their turn.

Each team gets three outs per inning.

After three outs, the other team comes up to bat.

There are a few ways to get out.

If the batter swings and misses three times, that's a strikeout.

A strikeout is one out.

If the batter hits the ball and someone catches it in the air, that's an out.

If the batter hits the ball on the ground, the fielder can throw it to first base.

If the ball gets to first base before the runner does, the runner is out.

That part can be really close sometimes.

The crowd holds their breath.

Now, what about balls and strikes?

When the pitcher throws, the umpire decides where the ball went.

If it goes through the strike zone, it's a strike.

The strike zone is an invisible box over home plate.

If the pitch is outside the box, and the batter doesn't swing, it's a ball.

Three strikes and the batter is out.

Four balls and the batter walks to first base for free.

That's called a walk.

I love getting a walk.

You don't even have to swing.

To score a run, a player has to touch all four bases.

First base.

Second base.

Third base.

And finally home plate.

When you cross home plate, your team gets one point.

We call that point a run.

The team with the most runs at the end wins.

Most kid games are six innings long.

That feels like a long time when you're losing.

It feels like no time at all when you're winning.
