# What We Actually Do

Let me tell you what you actually do in baseball.

There are four big things.

You hit.

You throw.

You catch.

You run.

That's pretty much it.

But each one takes practice.

Hitting is the hardest part for me.

You hold the bat with both hands.

You stand sideways to the pitcher.

You watch the ball as it comes at you.

Then you swing.

You try to hit the ball really hard.

Sometimes you miss.

Okay, a lot of times you miss.

But when you connect, it feels amazing.

Throwing is the part I'm best at.

You hold the ball in your fingers, not your palm.

You step toward your target.

You snap your wrist as you let it go.

A good throw is like drawing a straight line in the air.

Catching is fun.

You wear a leather glove on one hand.

The glove is soft on the inside and stiff on the outside.

You squeeze it shut when the ball lands inside.

That's how you catch it.

If the ball is on the ground, you bend your knees.

You scoop it up like you're picking up sand.

Running is the simplest part.

You just go as fast as you can.

But you have to run smart, too.

You have to know where to run.

You have to know when to stop.

Sometimes you slide into a base.

That means you drop low and skid across the dirt.

That's how the pants get dirty.

Hitting, throwing, catching, running.

If you can do those four things, you can play baseball.
