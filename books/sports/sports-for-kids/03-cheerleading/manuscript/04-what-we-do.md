# What We Actually Do

Let me walk you through the basics.

These are the building blocks.

Chants come first.

A chant is short and it repeats.

The crowd can say it with us.

Something like: let's go Comets, let's go.

You say it twice and clap on the beats.

Anyone can learn one.

Cheers are longer than chants.

A cheer might be eight or sixteen counts.

It tells a little story.

The crowd doesn't say a cheer with us.

The crowd watches and claps at the end.

Then there are motions.

Motions are the arm shapes.

Arms straight up in a V is called a high V.

Arms down low in a V is a low V.

Arms straight out to the sides is a T.

Fists tight on your hips is called clean.

Every motion has a name.

The motions have to be sharp.

If your arm is a little floppy, you lose points.

We practice motions in a mirror.

Sometimes I practice them in my room.

My little brother thinks I look weird, but I don't care.

Then there are voices.

Yes, voices.

We train our voices like an instrument.

A cheer voice is not your normal voice.

It is higher and comes from the chest.

You have to project, which means you push the sound out.

You don't yell at the back of your throat, because that hurts.

You yell from way down.

If you do it right, you can be loud for hours.

If you do it wrong, you lose your voice.

I've lost my voice a few times, and it's the worst.

You sound like a frog for two days.
