# If You Try It

Okay, a few last things.

Most days are practice days.

Not competition days, not game days, just practice.

We practice multiple days a week, a couple hours each time.

We stretch and we condition.

That means push-ups and planks and lots of running.

Cheer is a workout.

People don't always realize that.

We work on motions in a mirror.

We drill the same eight counts ten times.

That's how you get sharp.

Some practices are hard, some are funny.

We laugh a lot.

If you have never tried cheer, here is what I would say.

You don't need to be a certain kind of kid.

You don't need to be flexible yet.

You don't need to know how to do a cartwheel.

You don't need to be loud yet.

All of that comes.

You just need to be willing to try.

Cheer has a place for tall kids and small kids.

For shy kids and loud kids.

For boys and girls.

For everybody.

You can start by learning one chant.

Just one.

Learn it with a friend.

Try it in your backyard.

See how it feels to be loud together.

If there is a team near you, ask.

Most teams welcome new kids.

If there isn't a team, that's okay.

Cheer at home, cheer at the playground.

Cheer for your friend at their soccer game.

You don't need a uniform to bring energy.

That's the secret.

Cheer is mostly about energy.

The flips are cool, the stunts are cool.

But the energy is the heart.

I have to go now.

I have practice in a little while.

Thanks for listening.

Go be loud about something today.

That's a cheer thing.

Bye.
