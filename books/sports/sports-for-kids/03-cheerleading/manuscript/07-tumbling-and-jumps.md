# Tumbling and Jumps

Tumbling is the other big piece.

Tumbling is the gymnastics part of cheer.

Yes, cheerleaders tumble, a lot.

It starts simple.

Cartwheel.

Everyone learns a cartwheel first.

Hands down, feet over, land.

The next one is a round-off.

A round-off is like a cartwheel, but you snap your feet together at the end.

You land facing the other way.

It gives you power.

Tumblers chain skills together.

Round-off into a back handspring.

A back handspring is when you spring backward onto your hands, then back to your feet.

It is so fast.

When you can do them in a row, that's a tumbling pass.

A tumbler usually starts with cartwheels and round-offs.

Then back handsprings come next.

Each new skill takes time and a coach to spot you.

Spot means the coach holds your back as you go over.

Slowly you need less help.

That's how you learn.

You have your part on the team.

I'm a strong base, that's my part.

Now, jumps.

A jump is when you push off the ground with both feet.

You make a shape in the air, then you land.

The most famous jump is the toe touch.

In a toe touch, your legs go out to the sides.

Your hands reach forward.

You look like a giant letter V.

It is harder than it looks.

Another jump is the herkie.

In a herkie, one leg goes straight to the side.

The other leg bends behind you.

It looks like you're sitting on the wind.

A good jumper makes it look effortless.

It is not effortless.

Your legs burn after.

But when a whole team hits a jump at the same time, snap, it's gorgeous.
