# Competition

Competitions are different.

Bigger, louder, scarier.

In a good way.

We compete a few times a year.

Some competitions are in school gyms.

Some are in giant arenas.

The lights at an arena are huge.

The floor is a special spring floor.

It's springy under your shoes.

Spring floors help you tumble higher.

Each team gets one shot.

Two minutes and thirty seconds.

That's a competition routine.

You walk on the floor and smile.

You hit your starting pose.

The music drops.

Then you don't think, you just do.

Tumbling, stunts, a pyramid, a dance, jumps, another stunt.

All in two and a half minutes.

The crowd screams the whole time.

You can barely hear the music sometimes.

You know it in your bones.

The hardest part is the cradle.

A cradle is how a flyer comes down safely.

The bases catch her in their arms.

If you miss a cradle, that's a fall.

Falls cost points.

We practice cradles a thousand times.

When you finish the routine, you hit your final pose.

You hold it.

The music ends.

The crowd screams louder.

Then you walk off the floor, and then you cry.

Almost everybody cries a little, happy crying.

Then you wait for scores.

You sit in your warm-up jacket.

When they call the scores, your stomach flips.

If you place, you scream and hug.

The team hug after a routine is everything.

That's why we train.
