# The Hook

There is a moment, the first warm morning of the year, when the soil smells different. You step outside in old shoes, with a cup of something warm in your hand, and the air has changed. It is sweet and dark and a little wet. Your shoulders drop without you noticing. You are home, in a way you forgot you were missing.

People ask me why I garden. They expect me to say it is the vegetables, or the flowers, or the saving of money. It is none of those. The vegetables are a happy bonus. The flowers are a gift. The money, honestly, you do not save.

What I am chasing is the slowness. A garden moves at its own pace, and that pace is not yours. You can stand over a seed all afternoon, and it will not sprout faster. You learn, very gently, to stop hurrying. Most of life pulls you forward. The garden asks you to wait, and to look, and to wait a little more.

Gardeners are not patient by nature. Most of us were anxious people who found a quiet teacher. We dig in the dirt the way other people sit in temples. Something in the chest unclenches. And every season, the lesson comes back around.

The first time I grew a tomato from seed, I cried at the kitchen sink. I did not expect to. A grown adult, holding a single warm red fruit in two hands, weeping over it like a small miracle. My partner laughed gently and kissed the top of my head. They had seen it coming. That was the moment I knew I was a gardener for life.

Gardening is not about being good with plants. It is about finding the one place where time goes soft. For some people, that place is a long bath. For some, it is a fishing pier at dawn. For some, it is a kitchen, or a bicycle, or a quiet church on a Tuesday. For me, it is a patch of dirt the size of a kitchen table. And a small green thing pushing up through it.

The pull, once you feel it, is hard to explain to a friend at dinner. You will try, and they will smile, and you will give up. That is fine. You did not start gardening to win an argument. You started because, the first time you pressed a seed into damp soil, something in you said, oh, there you are.

Have you ever stood in a yard and felt your hand reach toward a leaf, just to touch it? If you have, you already know what I am talking about. Gardening is what happens when you stop pretending you do not reach. You kneel down, on purpose, and put your hands into the place your body has been quietly asking about.

And then, very slowly, you learn that the work was never the work. The work was the calm. Once you start, the rushing leaves you. What is left is something almost like prayer. The light moves across the beds. The day, for once, is the right size.

That is what I would tell you, if you asked me on a long walk. Why does a busy and reasonable person spend their weekends pulling weeds? I would tell you it is the quietest thing I do. And I would mean it.
