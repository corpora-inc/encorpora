# Moments

Let me give you three.

The first is a sunset load. The plane takes off twenty minutes before the sun goes down. You climb through layers of orange light, the cabin warm, your friends quiet. When the door opens this time, the air is pink. The earth below you is half in shadow, half in gold. You step out, and for sixty seconds you are falling through the inside of a sunset.

It does not look real. You will see colors you did not know your eyes could find. When your parachute opens, you are floating above a world that is briefly on fire. The wind has died down to nothing. The drop zone is a tiny green square below you, with one warm light on. You pick a slow circle and let yourself land last, because nobody wants this part to end. When your feet touch the grass, the sky is purple. Your friends are walking in from their own landings. One of them just looks at you. You both shake your heads. Neither of you can find a word.

The second is a formation jump. There are eight of you on the load. You sit on the bench, helmets in your laps, and the lead jumper draws the dive on a small whiteboard. You will leave the plane in a cluster, fall together, build a circle. You all know your slot. You bump fists, you check each other's gear, you laugh nervously. Eight grown adults, scared and giddy at the same time, like kids at a sleepover.

The plane levels off. The door opens. Your lead steps out, and the rest of you follow, one heartbeat apart. In freefall, you find each other almost without thinking. Hands meet hands. The circle forms. For a few perfect seconds, eight humans hold each other in the sky. Falling at the same speed. Looking each other in the eye. Then your altimeter says it is time. You let go. You track away from each other, to give your parachutes room to open. On the ground, you cannot stop hugging.

The third is a jump after a hard year.

Maybe you have been through something. A bad season at work. A relationship that ended. A loss that made the regular world feel thin and far away. You do not jump because you want to feel something. You jump because you have not been able to feel anything, and you want to know if you are still in there.

The plane climbs. The door opens. You step out. And in those first three seconds, before the wind catches you, something inside you cracks open. You did not plan to cry. You cry anyway. The freefall does its work. Sixty long seconds of nothing but wind and breath and falling. And somewhere in there, the part of you that has been holding everything finally puts it down for a minute. The parachute opens. The world goes quiet. You hang in the harness, and you weep, and you laugh, and you weep again. Nobody can see you. That is the point.

When you land, you are not fixed. You are still carrying whatever you brought up there. But for one full hour up there, something in you remembered exactly what being alive can feel like. Skydiving did not solve your year. It just gave it back to you for sixty seconds, in pieces small enough to hold.
