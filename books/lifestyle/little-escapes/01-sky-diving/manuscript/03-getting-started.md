# Getting Started

So how do you actually do this, if you decide one day that you want to.

The honest answer is, easier than you think. Almost any drop zone in the world will take a complete beginner that same morning. They put you in the sky on a tandem jump, with an instructor on your back. You arrive, you sign the papers, you watch a short video, and you meet your instructor. You wear a jumpsuit and a harness. You climb into the plane. About twenty minutes later, you are walking back across the grass with the biggest grin of your life.

For your first jump, you do not need to be brave. You only need to be willing. The instructor does everything. He pulls the parachute. He flies the canopy. He lands you. You are along for the ride, with the best seat in the world.

If after that first jump you are interested in jumping on your own, the path is called Accelerated Free Fall. It is a training program, taught at every drop zone, that teaches you how to be a real skydiver. Not a passenger. A pilot of your own body in the sky.

The first level of the program is a long classroom day. You learn how to fall. You learn how to read your altimeter. You learn what to do if your main parachute does not open. The classroom day takes about six or seven hours. By the end of it, you know more about parachutes than most people learn in a lifetime.

Then comes your first solo jump. Two instructors jump with you, one on each side, holding your harness. They release you in freefall once you are stable. You pull your own parachute. You fly your own canopy down to the ground. The first time you do this, your face will hurt the next day from how hard you have been smiling.

The full program runs you through about seven jumps. By the end, you are jumping by yourself. From there, skydivers usually finish their first one hundred jumps over the next year. Along the way, they learn to fly relative to other jumpers. They learn to track across the sky. They learn to pack their own parachute. The community is small, friendly, and patient. Nobody at a drop zone will ever make you feel stupid for being a beginner. We were all beginners once. Most of us still feel like beginners.

What about safety. This is the question every reasonable person asks, and it deserves a real answer. Skydiving is, in fact, very safe. Not as safe as walking, but safer than driving to the drop zone. The gear is excellent. There is a main parachute and a reserve parachute, and an automatic device that will fire the reserve if you cannot. Drop zones are run by professionals who care about you. Instructors care, deeply, about every jump.

What is the cost. A tandem jump is the price of a nice dinner for two. The training program is the price of a small vacation. After that, every jump is the price of a movie ticket and a meal. People with normal jobs and normal lives do this every weekend. They are not rich. They are not unusual. They have just decided that this is the thing they do.

If you are thinking about your first jump, here is the only advice that matters. Pick a date. Tell one friend. Show up. The hard part is not the falling. The hard part is the deciding. Once you decide, the sky is already yours.
