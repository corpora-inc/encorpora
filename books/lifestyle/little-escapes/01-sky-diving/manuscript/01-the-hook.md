# The Hook

There is a moment, right after you leave the plane, that I wish I could give you for free. The world stops being a thing you live in. It becomes a thing you fall through. And for the first few seconds, your mind cannot quite catch up to what your body has just done.

People ask me why I jump. They always expect me to say it is the rush. It is not the rush. The rush is what you feel on your first five jumps. After that, something quieter takes over.

What I am chasing is the clarity. For sixty seconds in freefall, your mind has nothing else to do. No phone. No bills. No little worry about tomorrow. Just the wind, your breath, and the slow curve of the earth below you. Most of life is noisy. That minute is not.

Skydivers are not crazy. Most of us are calm people who found a way to feel calm faster. We jump out of airplanes the way other people meditate. It clears something. And then, every weekend, it clears it again.

The first time I saw a parachute open above me, I cried into the harness. I did not expect to. A grown adult, hanging in the sky under a square of soft fabric, weeping for no reason he could explain. My instructor laughed gently and patted me on the shoulder. He had seen it many times. That was the moment I knew I would be back.

Skydiving is not about being brave. It is about finding the one place where your mind goes quiet. For some people, that place is a yoga mat. For some, it is a long run on an empty road. For some, it is a fishing line, or a piano, or a kitchen at six in the morning. For me, it is thirteen thousand feet above the ground, with nothing to do but breathe.

The pull, once you feel it, is hard to describe to a friend at dinner. You will try, and they will smile politely, and you will give up. That is fine. You did not start jumping to explain it. You started jumping because, the first time you tried, something in you said, oh, there you are.

Have you ever stood at the edge of something high and felt your body lean toward the open air? Just slightly, just for a second. If you have, you already know what I am talking about. Skydiving is what happens when you stop pretending you do not lean. You step out, on purpose, into the place your body has been quietly asking about for years.

And then, very quickly, you learn that the ground was never where the fear was. The fear was in the leaning. Once you jump, the fear goes. What is left is something almost like home. Your heart slows. Your breath finds a rhythm it has been looking for. The air holds you. And you feel, maybe for the first time in a long time, that you are exactly where you are.

That is what I would tell you, if you asked me on a long walk. Why does a calm and reasonable person keep jumping out of perfectly good airplanes? I would tell you it is the quietest thing I do. And I would mean it.
