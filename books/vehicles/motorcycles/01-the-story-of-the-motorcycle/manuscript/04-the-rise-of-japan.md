## Chapter 4 — The Rise of Japan

After the Second World War, Japan was poor. Most cars were too expensive. People needed a small, cheap way to move around.

A young Japanese man saw the chance. His name was Soichiro Honda. He started a small company in 1948. He called it Honda.

In 1958, Honda built a tiny motorcycle called the Super Cub. The Super Cub was easy to ride. It was easy to fix. It was very, very cheap.

People all over the world bought the Super Cub. Today, more than 100 million Super Cubs have been sold. It is the best-selling motor vehicle of all time.

Soon, three more Japanese companies followed Honda. They were Yamaha, Suzuki, and Kawasaki. Together with Honda, they are called the Big Four.

The Big Four made motorcycles that were fast, cheap, and easy to ride. By the 1960s, Japanese motorcycles were everywhere. They were the new kings of the road.
