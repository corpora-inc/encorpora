## Chapter 5 — Faster, Faster, Faster

In the 1960s, riders wanted to go faster. Builders started a race to make the fastest motorcycle in the world.

In Italy, a small company called MV Agusta built beautiful red bikes that won race after race. In England, Triumph and Norton built powerful machines too.

Then, in 1969, Honda did something amazing. They built a motorcycle called the CB 750. It had four cylinders and a big engine. It could go more than 120 miles per hour. It was the first true superbike.

After the CB 750, every company wanted to build a superbike. The bikes got faster and faster. Soon, they had bright fairings, the smooth plastic shells that cut through the wind.

In 1990, a Japanese motorcycle called the Kawasaki ZX-11 became the fastest motorcycle in the world. It could go almost 175 miles per hour.

A few years later, the Suzuki Hayabusa went even faster. It hit 194 miles per hour. That is faster than most cars on the road today.
