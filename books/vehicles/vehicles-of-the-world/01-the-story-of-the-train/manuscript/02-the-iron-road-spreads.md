## Chapter 2 — The Iron Road Spreads

After the Rocket, railways spread quickly. Britain laid thousands of miles of track. Then the rest of the world followed.

In 1830, the first big American railway opened. It was called the Baltimore and Ohio. It connected the city of Baltimore to the Ohio River.

In France, the first railway opened in 1832. It carried coal, like the British line.

In Germany, the first long railway opened in 1839. It ran between two cities, Leipzig and Dresden.

Russia built its first railway in 1837. The line ran from Saint Petersburg to a town nearby.

Building a railway was hard work. Men dug tunnels through mountains. They built bridges over rivers. They laid every rail by hand.

Towns grew along the lines. A small village with a station could become a city. The train brought people. The train brought goods. The train brought the world a little closer.
