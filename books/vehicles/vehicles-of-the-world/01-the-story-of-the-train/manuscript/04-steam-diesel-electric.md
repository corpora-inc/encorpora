## Chapter 4 — Steam, Diesel, Electric

For about a hundred years, almost every train was a steam train. Steam locomotives were big and loud. They burned coal. They sent thick smoke into the sky.

Then came the diesel locomotive. A diesel engine burns oil instead of coal. It is smaller and cleaner. It does not need a fire and a tank of water.

Diesel trains became common in the 1940s and 1950s. Slowly, the great steam locomotives were retired. A few are still kept in museums and tourist lines.

The next step was the electric train. An electric train has no engine that burns fuel. It takes power from a wire above the track or from a third rail beside the track.

Electric trains are quiet and very clean. They are also very strong. Most trains in cities and most fast trains today are electric.

Steam, diesel, electric. Three engines, three eras.
