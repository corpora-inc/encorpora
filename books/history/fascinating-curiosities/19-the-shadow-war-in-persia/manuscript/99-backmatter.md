# What We Still Don't Know

**What was the full extent of British involvement in the 1921 coup?** General Ironside's diaries describe meetings with Reza Khan and a deliberate effort to promote his rise. The British legation in Tehran was aware of the coup in advance. But key files from this period remain classified or have been weeded from the archive. Whether British officials planned the coup, merely facilitated it, or simply chose not to prevent it cannot be determined from the available documentary record. Cyrus Ghani's reconstruction is the most detailed available, but he worked from an incomplete archive.

**How serious was the German intelligence threat in Iran before the 1941 invasion?** British intelligence assessments painted an alarming picture of German agents cultivating tribal leaders, stockpiling weapons, and preparing sabotage operations against the oil fields. Adrian O'Sullivan's research, drawing on German archives, suggests the threat was real but more limited than British reports implied. The gap between perception and reality matters because the perception drove the decision to invade a neutral country.

**What happened to the sealed British files?** Archival researchers have documented the systematic removal of documents from files relating to British operations in Iran during this period. The practice of "weeding" -- removing sensitive documents before files are declassified -- is well established in British archival culture. What the removed documents contained, and whether they would alter the historical consensus, is unknowable.

**How many Iranian civilians died as a result of the Allied occupation?** The economic disruption caused by the occupation -- inflation, food shortages, diversion of transportation -- contributed to widespread hardship, but precise mortality figures are difficult to establish. The occupation's human cost to the Iranian population has received less scholarly attention than its military and strategic dimensions.

**Did Truman actually send an ultimatum to Stalin during the Azerbaijan Crisis?** The traditional account holds that Truman delivered a blunt demand for Soviet withdrawal. The documentary evidence for such an ultimatum is thin. Some historians argue that sustained diplomatic pressure, rather than a single dramatic message, drove the Soviet decision to withdraw. The nature and timing of the American communications remain debated.


# Key Names and Terms

**Abadan** (AH-bah-dahn) -- City in southwestern Iran on the Shatt al-Arab waterway. Site of the world's largest oil refinery, built by the Anglo-Persian Oil Company beginning in 1912.

**Abrahamian, Ervand** -- Iranian-born historian at Baruch College; author of *Iran Between Two Revolutions* (1982), the standard scholarly account of modern Iranian political history.

**Anglo-Persian Oil Company (APOC)** -- Founded in 1909 following the oil strike at Masjed Soleyman. Majority-owned by the British government from 1914. Renamed Anglo-Iranian Oil Company in 1935, then British Petroleum (BP) in 1954.

**Axworthy, Michael** -- British historian; author of *A History of Iran: Empire of the Mind* (2008); accessible overview of Iranian history from antiquity to the present.

**Bakhtiari** (bakh-tee-AH-ree) -- Major tribal confederation in the Zagros Mountains of southwestern Iran. The Bakhtiari provided labor and security for early oil operations and played a role in the Constitutional Revolution of 1906-1911.

**Churchill, Winston** (1874-1965) -- As First Lord of the Admiralty in 1914, championed the government purchase of a majority stake in the Anglo-Persian Oil Company to secure oil for the Royal Navy.

**Cossack Brigade / Division** -- Persian military unit founded in 1879, trained and officered by Russians until 1920. Reza Khan rose through its ranks. After the dismissal of Russian officers, it became the instrument of the 1921 coup.

**D'Arcy, William Knox** (1849-1917) -- Australian mining magnate who secured the 1901 oil concession from the Shah of Persia. His exploration company, backed by Burmah Oil, struck oil at Masjed Soleyman in 1908.

**Ghani, Cyrus** -- Iranian-born scholar; author of *Iran and the Rise of Reza Shah* (1998); the most detailed reconstruction of the 1921 coup from available archival sources.

**Ironside, Edmund** (1880-1959) -- British general commanding forces in northern Persia in 1920-1921. His diaries document contact with Reza Khan before the coup.

**Lend-Lease** -- The American program of military and economic aid to Allied nations during the Second World War. Approximately 4.2 million long tons of Lend-Lease supplies were delivered to the Soviet Union through the Persian Corridor.

**Majles** (maj-LES) -- The Iranian parliament, established by the Constitutional Revolution of 1906.

**Masjed Soleyman** (mas-JED so-lay-MAHN) -- Town in Khuzestan province, southwestern Iran. Site of the first commercial oil strike in the Middle East, May 26, 1908.

**Mosaddegh, Mohammad** (1882-1967) -- Iranian prime minister who nationalized the oil industry in 1951. Overthrown in the Anglo-American coup of August 1953. Subject of Volume 21 of this series.

**Motter, T.H. Vail** -- US Army historian; author of *The Persian Corridor and Aid to Russia* (1952), the official military history of the supply route through Iran.

**O'Sullivan, Adrian** -- Historian; author of *Nazi Secret Warfare in Occupied Persia* (2014); detailed study of German intelligence operations in wartime Iran.

**Operation Ajax** -- CIA codename for the 1953 coup that overthrew Mosaddegh. The British counterpart was Operation Boot. Subject of Volume 21 of this series.

**Pahlavi dynasty** -- The royal house founded by Reza Khan (Reza Shah Pahlavi) in 1925. His son, Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi, ruled until the 1979 revolution.

**Persian Corridor** -- The supply route through Iran used to deliver Lend-Lease material from the Persian Gulf to the Soviet Union during the Second World War.

**Pishevari, Ja'far** (1893-1947) -- Leader of the Soviet-backed Azerbaijan People's Government in 1945-1946. Fled to the Soviet Union after the collapse of the autonomous republic.

**Qashqai** (qash-QAH-ee) -- Major tribal confederation in Fars province, southern Iran. Targeted by German agents during both world wars as potential allies against Britain.

**Reza Shah Pahlavi** (1878-1944) -- Founder of the Pahlavi dynasty. Rose to power through the 1921 coup. Ruled Iran 1925-1941. Abdicated under Allied pressure and died in exile.

**Tudeh Party** -- The Iranian communist party, founded in 1941. Supported by the Soviet Union during the wartime occupation.

**Wassmuss, Wilhelm** (1880-1931) -- German consul and intelligence operative who organized tribal resistance against Britain in southern Persia during the First World War.

**Yergin, Daniel** -- American author; *The Prize: The Epic Quest for Oil, Money, and Power* (1991) is the definitive history of petroleum's role in shaping twentieth-century geopolitics.


# Further Reading

**Daniel Yergin, *The Prize: The Epic Quest for Oil, Money, and Power* (1991)** -- The indispensable starting point. Yergin traces the history of oil from the first Pennsylvania wells to the Gulf War, with Persia/Iran as a central thread. Pulitzer Prize-winning, exhaustively researched, and written for a general audience.

**Ervand Abrahamian, *Iran Between Two Revolutions* (1982)** -- The standard scholarly account of Iranian political history from the Constitutional Revolution of 1906 to the Islamic Revolution of 1979. Dense but essential for understanding the social forces that shaped modern Iran.

**Michael Axworthy, *A History of Iran: Empire of the Mind* (2008)** -- The best single-volume overview of Iranian history. Axworthy writes with sympathy for Iranian perspectives that are often missing from Western accounts.

**Cyrus Ghani, *Iran and the Rise of Reza Shah* (1998)** -- The most detailed treatment of the 1921 coup and Reza Khan's consolidation of power. Ghani drew on Persian-language sources and British archival material to produce a reconstruction that remains the best available.

**Denis Wright, *The English Amongst the Persians* (1977)** -- Diplomatic history from the British perspective. Wright served as British ambassador to Iran and wrote with insider knowledge of the diplomatic culture he describes.

**T.H. Vail Motter, *The Persian Corridor and Aid to Russia* (1952)** -- The official US Army history of the supply route. Dry in style but rich in operational detail. Available from the US Army Center of Military History.

**Adrian O'Sullivan, *Nazi Secret Warfare in Occupied Persia* (2014)** -- The definitive study of German intelligence operations in wartime Iran. O'Sullivan drew on German, British, and Iranian archival sources to produce the most complete picture of the shadow war.

**Stephanie Cronin, *The Army and the Creation of the Pahlavi State in Iran, 1910-1926* (1997)** -- Military history of the period that produced the 1921 coup and the Pahlavi dynasty.

**Mark J. Gasiorowski and Malcolm Byrne, eds., *Mohammad Mosaddeq and the 1953 Coup in Iran* (2004)** -- Essential for the aftermath of this volume's story. Contains declassified documents and scholarly analysis of Operation Ajax.


# About This Series

**Fascinating Curiosities of Human History** is a series of short, intense books about the most interesting things people have done, built, written, and wondered about. Each volume begins with a genuine mystery or curiosity and uses it as a doorway into a world of knowledge -- the people, the places, the context, and the open questions.

The series is designed for curious readers of all ages. Every sentence should be accessible to a bright ten-year-old and fascinating to an adult. We never talk down, we never pad, and we are always honest about what we know and what we do not.

Beginning with Volume 13, the series expands from archaeological mysteries to the hidden histories of espionage -- real spies, real operations, and their real consequences. The same editorial standards apply: parametric knowledge only, honest about gaps and uncertainties, competing scholarly accounts given fair treatment. Espionage history is built on the testimony of professional liars, which makes the truth harder to find and the search for it more fascinating.
