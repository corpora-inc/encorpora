# What We Still Don't Know

**Was the Lockhart Plot a genuine conspiracy or a Cheka provocation?** The evidence supports elements of both interpretations. Colonel Berzin was reporting to the Cheka by the time the plot reached its operational phase, but whether he was a Cheka agent from the first contact with Lockhart or was turned after the conspiracy began remains unclear. Partially opened Soviet archives have not settled the question, and key documents may have been destroyed.

**What did London authorize?** Lockhart's memoirs imply he acted on his own initiative. Institutional histories suggest deeper involvement. The War Cabinet's records from 1918 are incomplete on this point. Whether Lockhart received explicit authorization to participate in a coup conspiracy, or whether London maintained deliberate ambiguity to preserve deniability, is a question the available documentary record does not resolve.

**What was Moura Budberg's actual role?** She was permitted extraordinary access to Lockhart during his detention -- a concession that implies Cheka involvement. Whether she was a recruited agent, a willing collaborator, or simply a resourceful survivor who maintained relationships on all sides is debated in the biographical literature. MI5 files on Budberg, partially declassified, show sustained suspicion but no definitive evidence of formal recruitment.

**How close did the plot come to execution?** Reilly's account suggests the operation was nearly ready. Lockhart's is more cautious. The Cheka's version implies nothing real was ever going to happen. The truth depends on whether Berzin's reports to the Cheka reflected genuine preparations or performances staged for British consumption.

**What happened to the money?** Lockhart provided funds to Berzin, but the amounts and their disposition are uncertain. If Berzin was a Cheka agent, the money went directly to Dzerzhinsky's organization -- the British funded their own entrapment.


# Key Names and Terms

**Berzin, Eduard** -- Colonel commanding a Latvian Riflemen unit guarding the Kremlin. The central figure in the Lockhart Plot. Whether he was a genuine conspirator or a Cheka agent provocateur is the core question of the affair.

**Blumkin, Yakov** (bloom-KEEN) -- Left Socialist Revolutionary and Cheka agent who assassinated German Ambassador Count Mirbach on July 6, 1918, as part of the Left SR uprising. Later rejoined the Cheka and served Soviet intelligence for years before his execution in 1929.

**Bolshevik** (BOHL-sheh-vik) -- A member of the radical socialist faction led by Vladimir Lenin that seized power in Russia in October 1917. From the Russian word for "majority."

**Brest-Litovsk, Treaty of** (brest lih-TOFSK) -- Peace treaty signed March 3, 1918, between Soviet Russia and the Central Powers. Russia ceded vast territories including Finland, the Baltic states, Poland, and Ukraine.

**Budberg, Moura** (BOO-d-berg, MOO-rah) -- Born Baroness Moura Zakrevskaya (1892-1974). Russian aristocrat, Lockhart's lover in Moscow 1918, later companion to Maxim Gorky and H.G. Wells. Suspected by MI5 of Soviet connections.

**Cheka** (cheh-KAH) -- The All-Russian Extraordinary Commission for Combating Counter-Revolution and Sabotage. Soviet secret police established December 1917 under Felix Dzerzhinsky. Predecessor to the GPU, OGPU, NKVD, and eventually the KGB.

**Cumming, Mansfield** -- First chief of the British Secret Intelligence Service (SIS/MI6), known as "C." Directed British intelligence operations from London, including Reilly's activities in Russia.

**Dzerzhinsky, Felix** (dzer-ZHIN-skee) -- Polish-born Bolshevik revolutionary who founded and led the Cheka from 1917 until his death in 1926. Known as "Iron Felix."

**Grenard, Fernand** -- French consul general in Moscow during 1918. Arrested alongside Lockhart after the plot's exposure.

**Hill, George Alexander** -- British military intelligence officer operating in Russia 1917-1918. Part of the Reilly-Lockhart-Hill triangle. Subject of Volume 15 in this series.

**Kaplan, Fanny** (kah-PLAHN) -- Socialist Revolutionary who shot and wounded Lenin on August 30, 1918. Executed September 3, 1918. Her attack, combined with the assassination of Uritsky, triggered the Red Terror.

**Latvian Riflemen** (Latviyskiye Strelki) -- Ethnic Latvian military units within the Russian and later Soviet army. Served as the Bolsheviks' most reliable military force in 1917-1918, guarding the Kremlin and enforcing Cheka operations.

**Lockhart, Robert Hamilton Bruce** (1887-1970) -- British diplomat, journalist, and author. Head of the special British mission to Soviet Russia, January-September 1918. Author of *Memoirs of a British Agent* (1932).

**Mirbach, Count Wilhelm von** (MEER-bahkh) -- German ambassador to Soviet Russia. Assassinated by Yakov Blumkin on July 6, 1918.

**Peters, Yakov** (YAH-kof PEH-ters) -- Deputy head of the Cheka. A Latvian by birth, Peters interrogated Lockhart after his arrest. Irony of ironies: Peters had previously lived in London and was acquainted with the British political world.

**Red Terror** -- Campaign of mass political repression launched by the Soviet government on September 5, 1918. Involved mass arrests, executions, and the expansion of Cheka powers. Triggered by the Kaplan and Kannegiesser assassinations and the exposure of the Lockhart Plot.

**Reilly, Sidney** (born Sigmund Georgievich Rosenblum, c. 1873-1925) -- British intelligence agent, designated ST1 by SIS. The most aggressive proponent of the 1918 conspiracy. Lured back to Russia by the Trust operation in 1925 and executed. Subject of Volume 14 in this series.

**SIS** -- Secret Intelligence Service, the British foreign intelligence agency, also known as MI6. Founded in 1909. In 1918, still a small and improvised organization.

**Trust, The** -- Soviet deception operation (1921-1926) that presented a fictitious anti-Bolshevik organization to lure Western intelligence operatives into the Soviet Union. Successfully deceived SIS and led to Reilly's capture and death.

**Uritsky, Moisei** (oo-RIT-skee) -- Head of the Petrograd Cheka. Assassinated by Leonid Kannegiesser on August 30, 1918, the same day Fanny Kaplan shot Lenin.


# Further Reading

**For the general reader:**

- Lockhart, R.H. Bruce. *Memoirs of a British Agent*. Putnam, 1932. -- Lockhart's own account. Vivid, well-written, and strategically selective. Essential reading despite its omissions.

- Schneer, Jonathan. *The Lockhart Plot: Love, Betrayal, Assassination and Counter-Revolution in Lenin's Russia*. Oxford University Press, 2020. -- The most recent comprehensive account, drawing on the widest range of sources.

**For the intelligence context:**

- Brook-Shepherd, Gordon. *Iron Maze: The Western Secret Services and the Bolsheviks*. Macmillan, 1998. -- The Allied intelligence operations in revolutionary Russia, with detailed treatment of the Lockhart affair.

- Service, Robert. *Spies and Commissars: Bolshevik Russia and the West*. Macmillan, 2011. -- The revolutionary context from both sides, excellent on the Cheka's perspective.

- Jeffery, Keith. *MI6: The History of the Secret Intelligence Service 1909-1949*. Bloomsbury, 2010. -- The authorized institutional history. Essential for understanding SIS's role and its lessons learned.

**For Moura Budberg:**

- Berberova, Nina. *Moura: The Dangerous Life of the Baroness Budberg*. New York Review Books, 2005. -- The most detailed biography, drawing on Russian literary and archival sources.

**For the related figures:**

- Cook, Andrew. *Ace of Spies: The True Story of Sidney Reilly*. Tempus, 2004. -- The revisionist biography that challenged the Reilly legend. Essential companion to Volume 14.

- Lockhart, Robin Bruce. *Reilly: Ace of Spies*. Hodder & Stoughton, 1967. -- The biography that created the Reilly legend, written by Bruce Lockhart's son.

**For the Red Terror and revolutionary context:**

- Leggett, George. *The Cheka: Lenin's Political Police*. Oxford University Press, 1981. -- The standard academic treatment of the Cheka's early years.

- Pipes, Richard. *The Russian Revolution*. Knopf, 1990. -- Comprehensive history of the revolution, with detailed treatment of the Terror.


# About This Series

**Fascinating Curiosities of Human History** is a series of short, intense books about the most interesting things people have done, built, written, and wondered about. Each volume begins with a genuine mystery or curiosity and uses it as a doorway into a world of knowledge -- the people, the places, the context, and the open questions.

The series is designed for curious readers of all ages. Every sentence should be accessible to a bright ten-year-old and fascinating to an adult. We never talk down, we never pad, and we are always honest about what we know and what we do not.

Beginning with Volume 13, the series expands from archaeological mysteries to the hidden histories of espionage -- real spies, real operations, and their real consequences. The same editorial standards apply: parametric knowledge only, honest about gaps and uncertainties, competing scholarly accounts given fair treatment. Espionage history is built on the testimony of professional liars, which makes the truth harder to find and the search for it more fascinating.
