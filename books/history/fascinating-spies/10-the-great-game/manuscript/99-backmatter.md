# Key Names and Terms

**Ajax, Operation** -- The joint MI6-CIA covert operation in August 1953 that overthrew Iranian Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh and restored Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi to full power. Code-named TPAJAX by the CIA and Operation Boot by MI6.

**Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (AIOC)** -- Formerly the Anglo-Persian Oil Company (APOC), the British-controlled oil company operating in Iran. Nationalized by Mossadegh in 1951. Later became British Petroleum (BP).

**Boyle, Joseph Whiteside** (1867-1923) -- Canadian gold mining entrepreneur, military officer, and freelance intelligence operative. Subject of Volume 13. Recovered Romanian crown jewels from Bolshevik-held territory in 1918.

**British Security Coordination (BSC)** -- The British intelligence organization based in New York during World War II, headed by William Stephenson. Conducted intelligence, propaganda, and political influence operations in the Western Hemisphere.

**Cheka** (cheh-KAH) -- The All-Russian Extraordinary Commission, the Bolshevik secret police created in December 1917 under Felix Dzerzhinsky. Predecessor of the GPU, OGPU, NKVD, and KGB.

**CIA** -- Central Intelligence Agency. Created in 1947 from the wartime OSS. The principal American foreign intelligence service.

**Cumming, Mansfield** (1859-1923) -- The first head of SIS (MI6), known by the initial "C" -- a tradition continued by all his successors.

**D'Arcy, William Knox** (1849-1917) -- British-Australian financier who obtained the first major oil concession in Iran in 1901. His concession became the basis for the Anglo-Persian Oil Company.

**Hill, George Alexander** (1892-1968) -- British intelligence officer who conducted sabotage operations in revolutionary Russia. Subject of Volume 15. Author of *Go Spy the Land* and *Dreaded Hour*.

**Lockhart, Robert Bruce** (1887-1970) -- British diplomat and intelligence officer, unofficial British representative in Bolshevik Moscow in 1918. Subject of Volume 16. Author of *British Agent* (1932).

**Mossadegh, Mohammad** (moss-ah-DEG) (1882-1967) -- Iranian Prime Minister 1951-1953. Nationalized the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company. Overthrown by Operation Ajax.

**OSS** -- Office of Strategic Services. The American wartime intelligence agency, created in 1942 under William Donovan. Dissolved in 1945; its functions reconstituted as the CIA in 1947.

**Ransome, Arthur** (1884-1967) -- British journalist, author, and intelligence source. Subject of Volume 17. Best known as author of *Swallows and Amazons*. Reported from revolutionary Russia to SIS while maintaining close relationships with Bolshevik leaders.

**Reilly, Sidney** (c. 1873-1925) -- Intelligence operative of disputed origins who worked for SIS. Subject of Volume 14. Known as the "Ace of Spies." Captured and executed by the Soviet OGPU in 1925.

**SIS** -- Secret Intelligence Service, the British foreign intelligence agency, also known as MI6. Founded in 1909.

**SOE** -- Special Operations Executive. Created in 1940 to conduct sabotage and subversion in occupied Europe. Dissolved in 1946.

**Stephenson, William** (1897-1989) -- Canadian-born businessman and intelligence officer who headed British Security Coordination in New York during World War II. Subject of Volume 18. Known by the code name "Intrepid."

**Roosevelt, Kermit, Jr.** (1916-2000) -- CIA officer who directed Operation Ajax in Iran in 1953. Grandson of President Theodore Roosevelt. Subject of Volume 21.


# Further Reading

**Broad overviews of intelligence history:**

- Andrew, Christopher. *The Secret World: A History of Intelligence*. Yale University Press, 2018. -- The most comprehensive single-volume history of intelligence from ancient times to the present.

- Walton, Calder. *Spies: The Epic Intelligence War Between East and West*. Simon & Schuster, 2023. -- The most recent scholarly synthesis, with extensive new archival research.

- Hopkirk, Peter. *The Great Game: On Secret Service in High Asia*. John Murray, 1990. -- The classic account of the Victorian-era Anglo-Russian rivalry that gave this volume its title.

**Institutional histories:**

- Jeffery, Keith. *MI6: The History of the Secret Intelligence Service 1909-1949*. Bloomsbury, 2010. -- The authorized history, essential for the institutional framework.

- Weiner, Tim. *Legacy of Ashes: The History of the CIA*. Doubleday, 2007. -- A critical history based on declassified records, indispensable for understanding CIA covert operations and their consequences.

- Haslam, Jonathan. *Near and Distant Neighbours: A New History of Soviet Intelligence*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2015. -- The Soviet and Russian perspective, drawing on sources inaccessible to most Western scholars.

**On Iran specifically:**

- Abrahamian, Ervand. *The Coup: 1953, the CIA, and the Roots of Modern U.S.-Iranian Relations*. The New Press, 2013. -- The most thorough scholarly account of Operation Ajax.

- Kinzer, Stephen. *All the Shah's Men: An American Coup and the Roots of Middle East Terror*. John Wiley & Sons, 2003. -- A readable narrative account of the oil concession through Ajax.

**On blowback and consequences:**

- Johnson, Chalmers. *Blowback: The Costs and Consequences of American Empire*. Metropolitan Books, 2000. -- The foundational argument about the unintended consequences of covert operations.

**On the Moscow network:**

- Lockhart, R.H. Bruce. *British Agent*. Putnam, 1932. -- Lockhart's own account, self-serving but vivid.

- Cook, Andrew. *Ace of Spies: The True Story of Sidney Reilly*. Tempus, 2004. -- The most thorough attempt to separate Reilly fact from legend.

- Chambers, Roland. *The Last Englishman: The Double Life of Arthur Ransome*. Faber and Faber, 2009. -- The most balanced account of Ransome's dual role.


# About This Series

**Fascinating Curiosities of Human History** is a series of short, intense books about the most interesting things people have done, built, written, and wondered about. Each volume begins with a genuine mystery or curiosity and uses it as a doorway into a world of knowledge -- the people, the places, the context, and the open questions.

The series is designed for curious readers of all ages. Every sentence should be accessible to a bright ten-year-old and fascinating to an adult. We never talk down, we never pad, and we are always honest about what we know and what we do not.
