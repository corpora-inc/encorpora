# A Note to the Reader

The nine preceding volumes told individual stories. A gold prospector who saved a queen. A master spy whose real name remains uncertain. A soldier who blew up bridges behind enemy lines. A diplomat who plotted to overthrow the Bolsheviks. A children's author who reported to MI6. A spymaster who built an intelligence empire from a Manhattan office. Three operations that shaped the modern Middle East -- the shadow war in Persia, the Tehran summit, and the coup that toppled Mossadegh.

This volume connects them.

These stories are part of the same story. The people knew each other. They operated in the same cities, sometimes in the same rooms, sometimes on the same days. The institutions they built, broke, and rebuilt became the intelligence services that shaped the Cold War. The operations they improvised in the chaos of two world wars became the templates for covert action that the CIA, MI6, and KGB would use for the rest of the century. The consequences of their decisions are still unfolding.

The connecting threads are specific. Boyle, Reilly, Hill, Lockhart, and Ransome were contemporaries in revolutionary Russia in 1917-1918. Stephenson's British Security Coordination grew directly from the networks they built. The Iran thread runs from the first oil concession in 1901 through sixty years of continuous intelligence involvement to the 1953 coup -- and then onward to 1979 and beyond. The institutional family tree -- SIS to SOE to BSC to OSS to CIA -- is a direct lineage, with specific people carrying specific lessons from one organization to the next.

This is also the volume where we confront what we still do not know. SIS files from this period remain largely classified. The CIA's own records are partially declassified but heavily redacted. The Russian archives opened briefly in the 1990s and closed again. The full story of the Great Game -- the real one, the twentieth-century one -- remains partially hidden. We will be clear about where the documentary record ends and speculation begins.
