# The Plants

The tropical rainforest holds more kinds of plants than any other biome on land.

The trees are the heart of it.

Some trees grow very tall, reaching for the sun.

Their trunks are straight and smooth.

Their roots spread wide at the bottom, like walls, to hold them up in the thin soil.

Vines climb up the trunks toward the light.

Some vines are as thick as your arm.

They wind around the trees and hang down from the branches.

Plants grow on the trees themselves, high in the air.

They have no roots in the ground at all.

They drink the rain that runs down the bark.

On the dark forest floor grow ferns and soft green plants.

They have wide, flat leaves to catch every drop of light.

Flowers bloom in every color you can think of.

Some are red, some are yellow, some are deep purple.

Many of them open at night.

Fruits hang from the branches all year.

There are sweet fruits, sour fruits, and fruits with hard shells.

Many of the foods we eat at home first grew wild in rainforests.

Cacao, the plant that gives us chocolate, came from the rainforests of South America.

The first banana plants grew in the rainforests of Southeast Asia.

Each food we love has the story of a forest somewhere on Earth.

When you look at a rainforest plant, it has often found a clever way to live.

Some leaves have a long point at the tip, so rain runs off quickly.

Some flowers smell sweet only at night, to call the right insect to visit.

The whole forest is a quiet machine, made of green.
