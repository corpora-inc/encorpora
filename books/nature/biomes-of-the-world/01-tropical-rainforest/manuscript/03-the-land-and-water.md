# The Land and the Water

The ground of the rainforest is rich with life, but the soil is thin.

Most of the goodness sits in the top few centimeters.

When a leaf falls, insects and tiny living things break it down very quickly.

The trees grab the food back almost at once.

This is why a cut-down rainforest is hard to bring back.

Without the trees, the rain washes the thin soil away.

Rivers run through every rainforest.

Some rivers are huge.

The Amazon River carries more water than any other river in the world.

In the rainy time, the rivers spread far into the forest.

The trees stand in water for months.

Fish swim between the trunks.

Other rivers are small and clear, with stones at the bottom.

The forest itself grows in layers.

High up are the tallest trees, with their crowns spread out in the sun.

Below them is a thick green roof of leaves.

Under that roof, the air is shady and still.

Down on the ground, only a little light gets through.

Each layer is a different home for the animals and plants of the biome.
