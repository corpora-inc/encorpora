# Where Rainforests Grow

Welcome to the tropical rainforest.

This is one of the great biomes of the world.

A biome is a part of the Earth with its own kind of weather, plants, and animals.

The tropical rainforest is warm all year.

It is wet all year.

It is full of life.

Most rainforests grow near the equator.

The equator is the line that runs around the middle of the Earth.

Near the equator, the sun is strong every day.

The biggest rainforest in the world is the Amazon, in South America.

There are great rainforests in Africa too, near the Congo River.

There are rainforests in Southeast Asia, on many islands.

There are smaller rainforests in Central America and on islands in the Pacific.

Each of these forests grew up on its own, far from the others.

So the plants and animals are different in each part of the world.

A monkey in the Amazon is not the same as a monkey in Africa or Asia.

But they are all part of the same biome.

When you stand inside a rainforest, the air feels heavy and warm.

The light is soft and green.

The trees are so tall that you can hardly see the sky.

You hear birds and insects all around you.

You hear water dripping from the leaves.

You feel like you are inside a living room.

That is what the rainforest is.

A whole world built out of trees and rain.
