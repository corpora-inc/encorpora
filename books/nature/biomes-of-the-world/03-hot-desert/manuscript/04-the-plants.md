# The Plants

The plants of the hot desert are masters of waiting.

They have learned how to live with almost no water.

Some of them grow long roots that reach far down into the ground.

Other plants have wide, shallow roots that spread out near the surface.

After a rainstorm, these roots drink up every drop of water before it dries.

Many desert plants store water inside their bodies for the long dry time.

Their thick stems and leaves can hold water for months, or even years.

In the deserts of the Americas, there are plants called cacti.

Cacti have thick green stems full of water, and sharp spines instead of soft leaves.

The spines stop animals from eating the plants.

The spines also make a little shade against the strong sun.

In the deserts of Africa and Asia, other thick plants do the same job.

Many desert plants are gray or pale green.

A pale color reflects the sunlight away.

Some plants drop their leaves when the dry season comes.

They stay alive as bare brown twigs until the next rain.

After a heavy rain, the desert can change overnight.

Tiny seeds that have been waiting in the soil for years burst into flower.

For a few weeks, parts of the desert are covered in red, yellow, and purple blooms.

Then the flowers fade, the seeds drop, and the desert waits again.
