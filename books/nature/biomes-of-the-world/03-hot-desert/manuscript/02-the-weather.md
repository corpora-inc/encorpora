# The Weather

The weather in the hot desert is shaped by two things.

There is too much sun, and there is not enough rain.

In some deserts, it rains only a few days each year.

In other places, it does not rain at all for many years.

The days are very hot.

In the middle of the day, the sun can heat the sand to a temperature that would burn your feet.

But the nights are very different.

There are no clouds and no trees to hold the warmth.

So as soon as the sun goes down, the heat rushes away into the sky.

By the middle of the night, it can feel cool, or even cold.

In the morning, the rocks are chilly to the touch.

Then the sun comes up, and the heat begins again.

When rain does come, it often comes all at once.

A great storm rolls in, and water pours down for an hour.

The dry rivers fill, and water rushes over the sand.

Then the sun comes back, and within a day the water is gone again.

For the plants and animals of the desert, every drop of that water counts.
