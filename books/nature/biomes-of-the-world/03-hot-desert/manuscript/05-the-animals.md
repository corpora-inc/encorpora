# The Animals

The animals of the hot desert have many clever ways to survive.

Most of them sleep through the hot middle of the day.

They come out in the cool of the morning, the evening, and the night.

Many desert animals dig burrows in the sand or under the rocks.

A burrow stays cool when the air outside is burning.

A small mouse can sleep all day in its burrow and never feel the heat.

Lizards are very common in the hot desert.

They sit on warm stones in the early sun.

When the day gets too hot, they slip into the shade or under a rock.

Snakes are common too.

They glide across the sand looking for small animals to eat.

Some snakes have a special way of moving sideways across hot sand to keep their bodies off the ground.

Scorpions hunt at night, with their tails held high.

Spiders and beetles live in every part of the desert too.

Many desert beetles can survive without ever drinking water.

They get all the water they need from the food they eat.

The largest animal of the African and Arabian deserts is the camel.

A camel can drink a huge amount of water at once, and then go for many days without drinking again.

Its wide soft feet keep it from sinking into the sand.

In the deserts of North America, foxes and small wild cats hunt at night.

In Africa, small foxes with very big ears live in the sand.

The big ears help them hear small animals moving under the ground.

The big ears also help them lose heat in the cool of the night.

High overhead, vultures and eagles drift on the warm desert air.

They watch for any small movement on the ground.

Every animal here knows how to wait for the cool, how to find shade, and how to make every sip of water last.
