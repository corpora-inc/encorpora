# The People

People have lived in the temperate grassland for many thousands of years.

The wide open land, the herds of animals, and the rich soil have shaped their way of life.

Long ago, the people of the steppes of Asia tamed the wild horse.

On the back of a horse, a person could cross the open plains much faster than on foot.

The horse changed everything for the people of the grasslands.

It changed how they hunted, how they traveled, and how they fought.

Many of the old peoples of the steppes were nomads.

They moved their herds of horses, cattle, sheep, and goats from one part of the grassland to another.

In the summer, they traveled to the cool high plains.

In the winter, they came down to lower, warmer valleys.

Their homes were round tents made of felt and wood.

A round tent like this can be taken apart in an hour, packed on the back of an animal, and put up again somewhere else.

In North America, the people of the plains lived for thousands of years alongside the great herds of bison.

They followed the herds across the open land.

Their homes were tall tents of skin stretched over wooden poles.

When the horse came to the plains, the way of life there also changed deeply.

In the pampas of South America, herders on horseback became part of the land as well.

In the last few hundred years, much of the temperate grassland has changed again.

The deep, rich soil has been plowed up to grow wheat, corn, and other grains.

Today, much of the grain that feeds the world comes from these lands.

The old way of moving with the herds is gone in many places.

But the songs, the stories, and the love of the open sky live on.

In every great grassland, the people who grew up there carry the wind in their hearts.
