# The Land and the Water

The land of the temperate grassland is mostly flat or gently rolling.

In some places, low hills rise out of the grass.

In others, the land is so flat that you can see the horizon as a perfect straight line.

The soil here is some of the richest soil on Earth.

For thousands of years, grasses have grown, died, and rotted into the ground.

This has made a thick, dark soil that is full of food for plants.

That is why so many of these lands have been turned into great wheat and corn farms.

Rivers wind through the grasslands in long, slow curves.

The rivers carry water from far-away mountains and snowmelt across the open land.

Along the banks of the rivers, you find a thin line of trees.

These trees follow the water and stand out as green ribbons in the wide sea of grass.

Away from the rivers, there are very few trees.

The wind, the cold winters, and the dry summers keep most trees from growing here.

But the grass does not need much.

Its long roots reach deep into the rich soil, and it comes back every spring.

Fire also moves through these lands from time to time.

The grass burns quickly, but the deep roots stay alive.

After a rain, the grass grows back greener and stronger than before.
