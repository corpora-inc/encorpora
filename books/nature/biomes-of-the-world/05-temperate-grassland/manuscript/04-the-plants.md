# The Plants

The grasses are the heart of this biome.

There are many different kinds of grass in a temperate grassland.

Some grasses grow only ankle-high.

Others stand as tall as a person.

Each kind of grass has a long, soft head full of seeds at the top.

In the summer, the wind carries waves of seed heads across the land.

The grass has very long roots that reach deep into the dark soil.

The roots can be longer underground than the grass is tall above the ground.

This is how the grass survives the dry times and the cold winters.

It is also how the grass quickly comes back after a fire.

Between the grasses, small flowers bloom in the warm months.

Wild sunflowers and many other bright flowers fill the grassland in summer.

Bees and butterflies move from one bloom to the next.

There are very few trees in most of the grassland.

Where they do grow, they tend to be small and tough.

They grow along the rivers, where there is more water, or in low places where snow gathers in winter.

Out on the open plain, the grass is alone.

It is the green, golden, and silver carpet that holds the whole biome together.
