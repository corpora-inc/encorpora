# The Animals

The animals of the temperate grassland are built for an open, windy world.

The biggest animals here are large grazers.

They eat the grass and move in great herds across the open land.

In North America, the most famous of these is the bison.

A bison is huge, heavy, and shaggy, with a great brown head.

Bison once moved across the plains in herds of millions.

In Eurasia, wild horses once moved in the same way across the steppes.

In the pampas of South America, smaller deer and other grazing animals took their place.

Many smaller animals live under the grass.

Prairie dogs, ground squirrels, and other small animals dig long tunnels and rooms in the soil.

They come up to feed in the day and dive back into their burrows for safety.

A grassland full of these little animals is full of little holes in the ground.

Wolves, foxes, and small wild cats hunt across the grass.

The wolf is the great hunter of the open plains.

Smaller hunters like the fox and the wild cat catch mice, rabbits, and birds.

High in the sky, hawks and eagles circle slowly.

They watch for movement in the grass below.

Many kinds of birds nest right on the ground, hidden in the long grass.

In the summer, the air is full of insects.

Grasshoppers click and jump in the warm wind.

Butterflies and bees move from flower to flower.

Snakes glide through the grass, looking for mice and birds' eggs.

Every animal here knows the same lessons.

Move with the seasons.

Hide from the hot sun.

Run from the cold wind.

Drink from the river when you can find it.
