# The Weather

The weather in the temperate grassland is shaped by the open sky.

There are very few trees and few hills to slow the wind.

There are four seasons, and each one is strong.

The summers are hot and bright.

The sun beats down on the open grass, and the days are long.

In a single afternoon, a huge storm can roll in from the horizon.

Lightning flashes far away, and heavy rain falls all at once.

The winters are cold.

In some grasslands, the temperature can drop far below freezing for weeks at a time.

Snow blows across the open land and gathers in long drifts.

The wind in winter is strong and sharp.

In the spring, the snow melts, and the rivers run high.

The grass turns green almost overnight.

Birds return from warmer lands and fill the air with sound.

In the autumn, the grass goes golden, the wind grows cooler, and the herds move toward shelter.

The grassland gets just enough rain to feed the grass.

It is not enough rain to grow great forests, but it is plenty for the grass and the animals that need it.
