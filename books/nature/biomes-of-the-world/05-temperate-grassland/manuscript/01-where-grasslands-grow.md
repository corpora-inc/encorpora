# Where Temperate Grasslands Grow

Welcome to the temperate grassland.

This is one of the great biomes of the world.

A biome is a part of the Earth with its own kind of weather, plants, and animals.

The temperate grassland is a wide land of grass with very few trees.

It lies in the middle parts of the world, between the cold north and the warm tropics.

Unlike the tropical savanna, this land has cold winters and warm summers.

The biggest temperate grasslands in the world are in North America and in Eurasia.

In North America, the great central plains are called the prairies.

In Russia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, and Mongolia, they are called the steppes.

In Argentina and Uruguay, they are called the pampas.

There are smaller temperate grasslands in southern Africa and in parts of Australia.

Each of these grasslands grew up on its own.

So the plants and animals are different in each part of the world.

But they all share the same pattern.

A sea of grass, a great open sky, strong winds, and only a few trees along the rivers.

When you stand on a temperate grassland, the wind is almost always blowing.

The grass moves like the surface of the sea.

You can see for miles in every direction.

The sky feels twice as big as it does in any forest.

In the summer, the grass is tall and green.

In the autumn, it turns golden brown.

In the winter, it is brown and stiff under the snow.

In the spring, it grows back fresh and bright.

That is the temperate grassland.

A wide, windy world of grass, sky, and changing seasons.
