# The Plants

The plants of the Mediterranean biome are tough and clever.

They have learned how to live through a long, dry summer.

Most of them are not tall.

They grow as low shrubs, with thick, leathery leaves.

The leaves are small and shiny, sometimes silver or gray-green.

A small, shiny leaf loses less water to the sun.

A pale leaf reflects more of the sunlight away.

Many of the plants give off a strong, sweet smell when you brush against them.

That smell comes from oils inside the leaves.

The oils slow water loss and help the plant survive the heat.

That is why so many of our favorite cooking herbs come from these lands.

Plants like rosemary, sage, thyme, lavender, mint, and oregano all grow wild in the Mediterranean biome.

The most famous tree of this biome is the olive tree.

An olive tree has a thick, twisted trunk and small, silver-gray leaves.

Some olive trees live for more than a thousand years.

Other common trees are short evergreen oaks, pines, and cork trees.

The wild grape vine also began in these lands and climbed up out of the bushes.

Along the coast, you may find tall stands of bamboo, fig trees, and almond trees.

Most of the plants here keep their leaves all year, instead of dropping them in the autumn.

They cannot afford to grow new leaves every spring.

So they hold on to small, tough leaves all year long.

That gives the Mediterranean biome its dusty, evergreen look.
