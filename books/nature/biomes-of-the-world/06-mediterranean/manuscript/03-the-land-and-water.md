# The Land and the Water

The land of the Mediterranean biome is often hilly.

Mountains rise close to the coast in many of these regions.

The slopes are rocky, and the soil is often thin.

The seas and oceans nearby shape the weather.

The cool water keeps the summers from getting as hot as a desert, and the winters from getting as cold as the lands further inland.

Water is precious here.

Most rivers shrink to a trickle by the end of summer.

Some smaller streams disappear completely.

When the winter rains return, the rivers fill up quickly and rush down the hills toward the sea.

In some places, deep underground water rises to make a spring.

A spring is a small pool of fresh water that flows out of the rock all year.

For thousands of years, people, animals, and trees have gathered around these springs.

Along the coast, the land meets the sea in cliffs, beaches, and small bays.

The sea is full of life.

Fish, octopus, and sea birds find food in the warm, salty water.

The biome lives between the land, the sea, and the long blue summer sky.
