# The Weather

The weather in the Mediterranean biome is unusual.

There are two clear seasons.

There is a long, hot, dry summer.

There is a shorter, mild, wet winter.

In the summer, the sky is blue for weeks at a time.

The sun is strong, and the air is dry.

Almost no rain falls for many months.

By the end of summer, the grass is brown and the streams shrink to a few quiet pools.

In the autumn, the first cool winds come from the sea.

The clouds gather, and the first heavy rains fall on the dry earth.

The land drinks up the water, and the grass turns green again.

Winters are not very cold.

It rarely snows, except high in the mountains.

The rain falls in long, soft showers and the rivers run full.

In the spring, the air is warm and the hills are green.

For a few short weeks, wildflowers cover every slope.

Then summer begins again, and the cycle starts over.

Fire is also a part of this biome.

In the long dry summer, lightning or a careless spark can set the dry brush burning.

Many of the plants here have learned to live with fire and to grow back quickly when it passes.
