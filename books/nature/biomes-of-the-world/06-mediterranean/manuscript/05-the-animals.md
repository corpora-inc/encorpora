# The Animals

The animals of the Mediterranean biome have many ways to live with the heat and the dry summer.

Most of them are small and quick.

A small animal can hide easily under a bush or in a crack between rocks.

Many of them rest through the hot middle of the day and come out in the cool of the morning and the evening.

Rabbits and hares live in many of these lands.

They feed at dusk and dawn, and they spend the day in a hidden burrow under a bush.

Foxes hunt rabbits and other small animals.

A fox knows every path through the brush.

Wild boars dig in the soft soil for roots, nuts, and small animals.

They are strong and shy, and they move in small family groups.

In some Mediterranean lands, small wild cats hunt by night.

The lynx is one of these cats, with a short tail and tufted ears.

It is now rare and lives in only a few wild corners.

Deer live in the woods at the edge of the biome too.

Snakes and lizards are everywhere.

A lizard sitting on a warm rock is a common sight in the summer.

Tortoises move slowly through the brush, eating leaves and grass.

The Mediterranean tortoise can live for many decades.

Many birds live here.

Sparrows, robins, and warblers fill the bushes with sound.

High overhead, eagles and other large birds drift on the warm afternoon air.

In the spring, butterflies dance from flower to flower.

Crickets and grasshoppers sing in the warm summer evenings.

By the sea, gulls and other sea birds nest on the cliffs and rocky islands.

Every animal here knows how to find shade in summer, how to drink at the spring, and how to wait for the cool of the winter rains.
