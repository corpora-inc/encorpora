# The People

The Mediterranean biome has been home to people for a very long time.

Some of the world's oldest cities and farms began here.

For thousands of years, people on the shores of the Mediterranean Sea have grown three plants together.

They grow olive trees for oil.

They grow grape vines for fruit and wine.

They grow wheat and other grains for bread.

Together, these three are sometimes called the heart of Mediterranean food.

To them, the people added vegetables, fruits, and the wild herbs of the hills.

People have also kept herds of sheep and goats in these lands for many thousands of years.

The animals can find food on the rocky slopes where it is hard to grow crops.

They give milk for cheese and yogurt, wool for clothing, and meat.

Because the seas and oceans are close, the people of this biome have always been fishers and sailors.

Wooden boats put out at dawn and return with fish, octopus, and other seafood.

In the other Mediterranean lands of the world, in California, Chile, southern Africa, and Australia, the people who lived there for thousands of years used the same wild plants and animals in their own ways.

When farmers from the lands around the Mediterranean Sea came to those places much later, they brought their olives, their grape vines, and their wheat with them.

Today, all five Mediterranean regions of the world grow the same kinds of food.

The land seems to invite it.

The kitchens of every Mediterranean region share the same basic gifts.

Bread, cheese, olive oil, fresh tomatoes, fish, fruit, and wine.

The people of these lands have always known how to make the most of a long, dry summer.

They eat fresh in the warm months.

They put away food in oil, in salt, and in jars for the rainy winter.

In every Mediterranean region, this kind of life has its own languages, songs, and stories.
