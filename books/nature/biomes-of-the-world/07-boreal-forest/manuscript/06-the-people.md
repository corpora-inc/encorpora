# The People

The boreal forest is one of the emptiest places on Earth.

Long winters and thin soil make it hard to grow food here.

But people have lived in this great forest for many thousands of years.

In northern Europe, the Sami people have lived in the boreal forest and the tundra above it for a very long time.

The Sami herd reindeer across the cold lands of Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Russia.

A reindeer can find food in the snow, and it gives the people meat, milk, and skins.

The reindeer also pulls sleds across the winter snow.

In Siberia, in northern Russia, many groups of people have made the boreal forest their home.

The Evenki, the Khanty, the Nenets, and others have hunted, fished, and herded reindeer here for many generations.

In Canada and Alaska, many First Nations peoples and the Dene have lived in the boreal forest for thousands of years.

They have hunted moose and caribou, fished in the cold rivers, and gathered berries in the brief summer.

Across all of these lands, the people built warm winter clothes from animal skins and fur.

They built houses of wood, bark, and skin, with small openings to keep out the cold.

They learned to travel on snowshoes and on long thin skis, and later with dog sleds.

In summer, they traveled by canoe, by long birchbark boat, or by reindeer.

These people learned the names of every tree and every animal in the forest.

They learned where the berry patches were, and where the fish were thickest.

They learned which mushrooms could be eaten and which could not.

They learned how to read the snow for the tracks of moose and bear.

In more recent times, other people have come to the forest to cut wood, to dig for ore, or to live in small towns along the rivers.

But for most of its long history, the boreal forest has been a quiet place.

A place where small groups of people share the land with the trees, the animals, and the cold.
