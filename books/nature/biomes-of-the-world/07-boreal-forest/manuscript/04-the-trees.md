# The Trees

The trees of the boreal forest are mostly evergreen.

They do not drop all their leaves at the end of summer.

Instead, they hold tiny green needles all year long.

A needle is small and tough.

It does not lose much water to the wind, and it does not break under the snow.

Many of the trees grow in the shape of a tall, narrow cone.

When heavy snow falls on a tree shaped like a cone, it slides off easily.

The most common boreal trees are spruce, pine, fir, and larch.

Spruce trees have short, sharp needles and small cones that hang down.

Pine trees have long, soft needles that grow in little bundles of two or three.

Fir trees have flat, soft needles and tall cones that point straight up.

The larch is the only common boreal tree that drops its needles in the autumn.

Before it drops them, the larch turns bright gold.

A great larch forest in October looks like a sea of gold across the cold land.

In all of these forests, the trees stand close together.

The branches are thick at the top and bare near the bottom, where the sun does not reach.

The shade between the trees is deep and quiet.

On the floor of the forest, the green is not in the leaves but in the moss.

Soft thick moss covers the ground, the fallen logs, and the rocks.

Mushrooms of many shapes and colors push up through the moss in the late summer.

Small berries grow on tiny bushes among the moss.

Sweet wild blueberries, red cranberries, and small black crowberries all grow here.

In the brief summer, the people and the animals of the forest gather them by the basket.
