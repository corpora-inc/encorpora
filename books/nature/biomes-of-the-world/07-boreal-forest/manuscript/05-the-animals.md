# The Animals

The boreal forest is home to many animals that know how to live in the cold.

Most of them have thick fur, fat under their skin, or both.

The biggest animal of the boreal forest is the moose.

A moose is a very tall deer with a long face and a heavy body.

A grown moose can be taller than the tallest person, with great wide antlers on its head.

It feeds on water plants in the summer and on twigs and bark in the winter.

A moose is shy, but if you meet one, you will not forget it.

Brown bears live in many parts of the boreal forest.

In the summer, a brown bear eats berries, fish, roots, and small animals.

In the autumn, it eats as much as it can to grow a thick layer of fat.

Then it sleeps in a den under the snow all winter long.

Wolves hunt across the forest in family groups called packs.

A wolf pack can travel many miles in a single day.

The wolves hunt moose, deer, and smaller animals.

In the deep snow, they walk in a line, each wolf stepping in the tracks of the one in front.

The lynx is a wild cat of the boreal forest.

It has thick gray fur, a short tail, and pointed tufts of hair at the tips of its ears.

Its big furry feet act like snowshoes in the deep snow.

The lynx mostly hunts the snowshoe hare.

The snowshoe hare turns brown in summer and white in winter, to match the ground.

Beavers live in the rivers and lakes of the forest.

A beaver cuts down trees with its strong teeth and builds dams and lodges of wood and mud.

A beaver pond can change a whole stretch of forest into a quiet pool full of life.

High in the trees, red squirrels chatter loudly and gather pine cones for the winter.

Wolverines, martens, and minks slip silently through the underbrush.

Many kinds of owls hunt at night.

The great gray owl is one of the tallest owls in the world, and it lives in the boreal forest.

Ravens fly between the trees, calling to each other in many different voices.

In the short summer, swarms of mosquitoes and small flies fill the air.

For a few weeks, the forest is alive with the buzzing of insects, the songs of small birds, and the splash of fish in the lakes.
