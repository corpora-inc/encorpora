# The Weather

The boreal forest is cold for most of the year.

The winter here is long and dark.

In the northern parts of the forest, winter can last for seven or eight months.

The air can drop to forty below zero, in either Celsius or Fahrenheit.

That is one of the coldest places on Earth where trees can still grow.

Heavy snow covers the ground from autumn to late spring.

The lakes and rivers freeze over, sometimes with ice as thick as a person is tall.

In the deep north, the sun barely rises above the trees on the shortest winter days.

Then comes the spring melt.

The snow turns to water, and the rivers run high and cold.

The ground stays soft and wet for many weeks.

The summer is short, but it is full of life.

In June and July, the sun stays in the sky for most of the night.

The forest grows quickly to make the most of the warm light.

Summer days can be warm, but the nights are usually cool.

By August, the cold begins to creep back in.

Autumn is short and golden.

Then the snow returns, and the long winter begins again.
