# The Animals

The tropical savanna is famous all around the world for its animals.

The grasses feed huge herds of grazing animals.

In Africa, great herds of zebras, antelopes, and buffalo move across the plains.

They follow the rains from one part of the savanna to another.

The largest land animal on Earth, the elephant, lives in the African savanna.

A family of elephants walks slowly across the grass, led by an old mother.

The giraffe walks alongside, reaching with its long neck to eat the leaves of trees.

Where there are many grazing animals, there are also hunters.

The lion is the great hunter of the African savanna.

Lions live together in family groups called prides.

The cheetah is the fastest animal on land.

It runs in short bursts to catch the antelopes that graze the plains.

The leopard is a quiet hunter that climbs trees and pulls its meals up into the branches.

Hyenas hunt in groups and clean up what the bigger hunters leave behind.

Smaller animals are everywhere too.

Baboons travel in noisy groups across the grass and through the trees.

Big birds called ostriches run across the open plain on long legs.

Vultures circle high above, watching for animals that have died.

In the wet time, frogs sing every night around the new ponds.

Insects fill the warm air, and butterflies float between the flowers.

In the rivers, hippos rest in the water all day, and come out to graze the grass at night.

In the South American savanna, big rodents and large birds take the place of the African ones.

In the Australian savanna, kangaroos hop across the grass instead of antelopes running across it.

In each of these lands, the animals look different.

But the pattern is the same.

Huge herds of grazers, quiet hunters in the grass, and great flocks of birds in the sky.

That is the savanna.
