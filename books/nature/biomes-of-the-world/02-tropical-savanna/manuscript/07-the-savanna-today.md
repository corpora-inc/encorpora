# The Savanna Today

The tropical savanna is one of the great wild lands of Earth.

It feeds enormous herds of animals.

It supports the herders and farmers who have lived here for thousands of years.

The grasslands also store a lot of water and food in their deep roots.

But the savanna is in trouble too.

In many places, people have plowed up the grass to plant crops, or fenced it off for cattle.

When the grass is broken up, the herds of wild animals lose their open path.

Roads and fences cut across the routes that elephants and antelopes have followed for ages.

Some savanna animals, like the rhinoceros and the cheetah, have become very rare.

Many groups around the world are working to protect what is left.

They set aside large parks where the wild animals can still move freely.

They work with the people who live near the parks to share the land.

They study the grass, the trees, and the herds so we understand it better.

A healthy savanna can support more life than almost any other land biome.

It is worth our care.
