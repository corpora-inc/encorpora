# The Plants

The heart of the savanna is the grass.

Tall grasses cover the land in every direction.

Some grasses grow as high as a person.

Their roots reach deep into the soil to find water.

When fire sweeps through the savanna, the tops of the grasses burn.

But the roots stay alive underground.

After the next rain, the grasses grow back, fresh and green.

Fire is a natural part of this biome.

Many of the plants here need fire to stay healthy.

Trees grow across the savanna too, but they are scattered.

They stand far apart, with grass between them.

Most savanna trees are short, with wide, flat tops.

The flat top is a wide umbrella of leaves that catches every drop of light.

Many savanna trees have thorns to keep big animals from eating them.

The famous baobab is a giant savanna tree with a thick, strong trunk.

It can store water inside its trunk for the long dry time.

Some baobabs live for more than a thousand years.

Along the rivers, taller trees grow in a thin green band.

Out on the open plain, the grass and a few scattered trees are all you see for miles.

In the wet time, small flowers bloom between the grasses.

They open quickly and last only a few weeks.

The savanna is a quiet machine, made of grass, scattered trees, sun, and patient roots.
