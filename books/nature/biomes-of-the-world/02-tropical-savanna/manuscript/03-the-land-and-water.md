# The Land and the Water

The land of the savanna is mostly flat and wide.

In some places, low rolling hills rise out of the grass.

Now and then a great rocky outcrop stands alone on the plain.

The soil under the grass is different in different parts of the savanna.

In some places it is dark and rich.

In other places it is sandy or thin.

The grasses have learned to grow in all of them.

When the rains come, water gathers in low places to make ponds and shallow lakes.

Some of these last for months, and some dry up in a few weeks.

Rivers wind across the savanna in long, slow curves.

In the wet time they are wide and full.

In the dry time they shrink to a thin trickle in the sand, or to a string of quiet pools.

Many of the animals of the savanna gather at these rivers and ponds to drink.

A pond in the dry time is a busy place.

Animals that do not usually meet stand near each other to drink.

The rivers also bring a strip of taller trees with them.

Along the banks, the trees grow thick and green even in the dry time.

These ribbons of forest cross the open savanna like green roads.

They are home to animals that need shade and water year round.
