# The People

People have lived in the tropical savanna for a very long time.

In fact, our own ancestors first stood up and walked across savannas like these.

The open grass and scattered trees shaped the way our bodies move.

Many people of the savanna keep herds of animals.

In Africa, families herd cattle, goats, and sheep across the grasslands.

The animals eat the grass and turn it into milk and meat for the people.

The herders move with their animals to follow the rains.

In the dry time they travel toward the rivers.

In the wet time they spread out across the green plains.

Their homes are often easy to take apart and carry to a new place.

Other people of the savanna grow food in small fields.

Where the soil is rich, they plant grains and beans.

They use fire carefully to clear new fields and to keep the grass fresh for their animals.

The people of the savanna know the land in great detail.

They know which plants are safe to eat and which are good for medicine.

They know the tracks of every animal in the dust.

They know the meaning of every kind of cloud in the sky.

This knowledge is passed down from parents to children, year after year.

In every savanna, this kind of life has its own languages, its own songs, and its own stories.
