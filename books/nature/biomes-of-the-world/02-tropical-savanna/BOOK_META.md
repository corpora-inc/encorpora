## Content scope

A plainspoken tour of the tropical savanna biome — grasslands with scattered trees, hot and dry most of the year, with a single wet season. Where savannas grow, what the weather is like, the iconic grasses and scattered trees, the famous animals that have shaped our imagination of "the wild," the people who have always lived alongside them, and the threats the biome faces today.

## Geographic zones (acknowledge regional differences)

- **African savanna**: by far the largest and most famous — Serengeti, Maasai Mara, miombo woodland, Sahel transition.
- **South American savanna (cerrado)**: in Brazil, with its own animals.
- **Australian savanna**: across the tropical north.
- **Indian and Southeast Asian dry forests/savanna**: smaller patches.

## Animals named (common-words discipline)

**African (most iconic and globally known)**: lion, elephant, giraffe, zebra, antelope, cheetah, leopard, hyena, rhinoceros, buffalo, hippo, ostrich, vulture, baboon.

**Other zones (only if iconic and lexically common)**: kangaroo (Australia). Skip cerrado-specific (maned wolf, capybara, anteater) — too regional.

## People

- African pastoralists (Maasai, Samburu, Fulani, etc. — not named, called "the people who herd cattle"), hunter-gatherers (San), farmers around the savanna edges.
- Australian Aboriginal peoples — traditional fire-stick farming.
- Cerrado peoples — including Indigenous groups and traditional farming communities.

Keep the language general: "the people who have lived in the savanna" rather than naming specific groups.
