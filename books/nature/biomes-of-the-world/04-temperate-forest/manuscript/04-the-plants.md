# The Plants

The trees are the heart of the temperate forest.

Many of them grow tall and strong, with thick trunks and wide branches.

Their wood is hard, and a single tree can live for hundreds of years.

Some grow even longer than that.

The most famous trees of the temperate forest are the oak, the maple, the beech, and the birch.

In different parts of the world, you find different kinds of these trees.

But they share the same shape and the same yearly story.

In the spring, new leaves open on every branch.

The leaves are pale green and soft.

Through the summer, they spread wide to catch the sunlight.

Each green leaf turns sunlight, water, and air into food for the tree.

In the autumn, the leaves change color before they fall.

Some turn bright red, some glow yellow, and some go a deep brown.

The colors paint whole hillsides for a few short weeks.

Then the leaves drop, and the tree rests through the cold.

The wood of the tree saves what it needs, deep inside.

When spring returns, the tree begins again.

Below the tall trees, smaller plants grow.

There are bushes with berries that animals love.

There are ferns and soft mosses on the rocks and old logs.

In early spring, before the big trees have leaves, wildflowers fill the forest floor.

For a few weeks, the ground is covered in bluebells, daffodils, or other bright flowers.

When the trees grow their leaves and shade the ground, the flowers fade away.

They will wait for next spring under the soil.
