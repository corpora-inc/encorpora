# The People

People have lived in the temperate forest for many thousands of years.

The mild seasons and the rich soil have always made these places good for human life.

Long ago, people in these forests hunted deer and small animals.

They gathered nuts, berries, and roots from the forest floor.

They knew which mushrooms were safe and which were not.

They built their homes from the wood of the trees around them.

A house of logs and a roof of bark can keep a family warm through a long winter.

In the spring and summer, they grew food in small fields cleared from the forest.

They planted grain, beans, and roots that would do well in the cool weather.

In autumn, they gathered the harvest and stored it away for the cold months.

In the winter, they sat by their fires, mended their tools, told stories, and waited for spring.

The forest gave them wood for fires and for building.

It gave them bark and leaves for medicine.

It gave them dyes for cloth, and food in every season.

Over time, many of these forests were cut down to make farms and cities.

The villages grew into towns, and the towns grew into the great cities of the temperate world.

But people still know the forest in their stories and songs.

Many of the old songs and stories of these lands are full of trees, wolves, deer, and the long winter nights.

In every temperate forest, this kind of life has its own languages, its own foods, and its own ways of waiting for spring.
