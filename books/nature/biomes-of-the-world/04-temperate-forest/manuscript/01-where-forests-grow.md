# Where Temperate Forests Grow

Welcome to the temperate forest.

This is one of the great biomes of the world.

A biome is a part of the Earth with its own kind of weather, plants, and animals.

The temperate forest is a forest of broad, leafy trees.

Most of the trees here lose their leaves once a year and grow new ones in the spring.

Trees that drop their leaves like this are called deciduous trees.

Temperate forests grow in the middle parts of the world, between the cold north and the warm tropics.

There are great temperate forests in the eastern part of North America.

There are temperate forests across much of Europe.

There are temperate forests in eastern Asia, in China, Korea, and Japan.

There are smaller patches in southern South America, in southeastern Australia, and in southern New Zealand.

Each of these forests grew up on its own, far from the others.

So the kinds of trees and animals are different in each part of the world.

But they all share the same pattern.

A canopy of leafy trees, a soft floor of fallen leaves, and four very different seasons.

When you walk into a temperate forest, the air feels cool and gentle.

In the summer, the sunlight falls through the leaves in soft green patches.

In the autumn, the leaves turn yellow, orange, and red.

In the winter, the trees stand bare against the sky.

In the spring, tiny green buds appear, and the whole forest wakes up again.

That is the temperate forest.

A green world that changes its face four times a year.
