# The Weather

The weather in the temperate forest changes a great deal across the year.

There are four seasons, and each one is very different.

In the summer, the days are long and warm.

It rains often, and sometimes a heavy storm rolls in over the trees.

The forest is full of green leaves and the songs of birds.

In the autumn, the days grow shorter, and the air turns cool.

The leaves of the trees change color and fall to the ground.

A walk in an autumn forest is a walk over a soft, bright carpet of leaves.

In the winter, the days are short, and the nights are long and cold.

In many places, snow covers the forest for weeks at a time.

The bare branches of the trees stand still against a gray sky.

Many animals sleep through the cold or hide in their burrows.

Then the spring comes.

The snow melts, the rains begin again, and the soil warms.

Green buds open on the branches, flowers push up from the ground, and the forest fills with sound and color once more.

The temperate forest knows how to wait through the cold, and how to celebrate when the warmth returns.
