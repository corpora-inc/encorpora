# The Animals

The temperate forest is home to many kinds of animals.

In the trees and on the ground, they all have ways to live with the changing seasons.

Deer are common in many temperate forests.

They walk softly through the trees and eat leaves, grass, and small plants.

In the summer their coats are short and brown.

In the winter their coats grow thick and gray.

Bears live in some of these forests too.

A bear eats almost anything — berries, fish, honey, and small animals.

In the autumn, a bear eats as much as it can.

Then it finds a quiet place and sleeps deeply through the coldest part of the winter.

Wolves and foxes hunt in the forest.

Wolves live in family groups and travel many miles each day.

Foxes are smaller, and they hunt alone, often at night.

Squirrels are everywhere.

In the autumn, they gather nuts and bury them across the forest.

In the winter, they dig up their hidden stores to eat.

Sometimes they forget a few, and those nuts grow into the next trees.

Rabbits and small mice live in the soft soil and under fallen logs.

Many kinds of birds fill the temperate forest with sound.

Some birds stay all year, and you can see them on the bare winter branches.

Many other birds fly south for the winter and come back when spring returns.

Woodpeckers hammer on the trunks of trees.

Owls hunt in the night.

In ponds and streams, frogs sing, and small fish swim under the ice in winter.

Butterflies and bees come out in the warm months, when the flowers open.

Every animal here knows the four seasons in its body.

Each one knows when to be busy, when to rest, and when to wake again.
