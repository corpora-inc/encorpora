# The Animals

The animals of the tundra are built to live with cold and wind.

Many of them have thick fur or thick feathers, and a layer of fat to keep warm.

The largest animals of the tundra are the caribou, also called reindeer.

Caribou live in great herds and travel long distances across the open land.

In summer they feed on the tundra plants, and in winter they dig through the snow for lichens.

The musk ox is another large animal of the tundra.

A musk ox has a heavy body and a thick coat of long hair that hangs almost to the ground.

When wolves come near, the musk oxen stand in a ring, facing out, to keep the young ones safe.

Wolves follow the herds across the tundra, hunting the old and the weak.

The arctic fox is small, with thick fur that turns white in winter and brown in summer.

It follows larger animals and eats what they leave behind, and it hunts small animals too.

The arctic hare is white in winter and has short ears to keep in its warmth.

Under the snow, small animals called lemmings dig tunnels and stay busy all winter.

Lemmings are food for the fox, the owl, and many other hunters.

The snowy owl is a large white owl that hunts across the open tundra by day and night.

It watches from a low rise and drops silently onto lemmings and other small animals.

In the short summer, huge flocks of birds arrive from the south.

Geese, ducks, and many small birds come to nest on the wet, buggy tundra.

They come because the long summer days and the many insects mean plenty of food.

For a few weeks, clouds of mosquitoes and small flies fill the air.

Then the birds raise their young quickly and fly south again before the cold returns.

Along the northern edge, where the tundra meets the frozen sea, the polar bear hunts on the ice.
