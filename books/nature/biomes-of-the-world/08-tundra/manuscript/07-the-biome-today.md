# The Biome Today

The tundra is one of the wildest and emptiest biomes, but it is changing fast.

Of all the biomes, the tundra is warming the most quickly.

The far north is growing warmer year by year.

As the air warms, the frozen ground begins to thaw.

When the permafrost thaws, the land can sink, crack, and turn to mud.

Roads, homes, and pipelines built on the frozen ground can bend and break.

The thawing ground also releases gases that had been locked in the frozen soil for a very long time.

These gases rise into the air and can make the whole Earth a little warmer still.

As the tundra warms, shrubs and small trees are slowly spreading north into it.

Some animals of the warmer south are moving north as well.

People also come to the tundra to search for oil, gas, and metal in the ground.

Their roads and machines can leave marks on the land that heal very slowly in the cold.

Many people and groups today are working to take care of the tundra.

They set aside great parks where the caribou and the birds can still roam.

They work with the people who have lived here for many generations to protect the land.

They study the tundra to understand how it is changing and how to keep it healthy.

The tundra is fragile and slow to heal, and it holds an enormous store of frozen carbon.

It is worth our care.
