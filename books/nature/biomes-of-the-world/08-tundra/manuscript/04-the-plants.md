# The Plants

The plants of the tundra are small, tough, and low to the ground.

There are no trees here, because trees cannot live in the frozen ground and cold wind.

Instead, the tundra is covered with low mats of small plants.

Staying low is the secret to living here.

Near the ground, the wind is weaker and the air is a little warmer.

Many tundra plants grow in tight cushions or thick mats to hold in their warmth.

Mosses grow thick and soft over much of the ground.

Lichens grow on the bare rocks and on the soil in gray, green, and yellow patches.

A lichen is not one plant but two kinds of life living together as one.

Lichens can live where almost nothing else can, and they grow very slowly.

Some lichens on the tundra are hundreds of years old.

Small grasses and low flowering plants grow between the mosses.

In the short summer, the tundra bursts into bloom.

For a few bright weeks, the ground is covered with tiny flowers.

There are small yellow, white, pink, and purple flowers everywhere.

The plants must flower, make seeds, and store food very quickly before the cold returns.

A few small shrubs grow here too, like dwarf willow and dwarf birch.

These are the same kinds of trees that grow tall in warmer lands.

But here they grow as tiny bushes, only as high as your ankle, creeping along the ground.

Small berries ripen on some of these low plants at the end of summer.

The animals and the people of the tundra gather them while they can.
