# The Weather

The tundra is cold, dry, and windy for most of the year.

The winter is long, dark, and bitterly cold.

In the far north, the winter can last for eight or nine months.

The air can drop far below freezing, colder than a home freezer.

For many weeks in the deep of winter, the sun does not rise at all.

The land lies in a long blue and dark twilight, day after day.

Strong winds blow across the open land, with no trees to slow them down.

The wind picks up the dry snow and drives it along the ground.

Very little snow or rain falls here.

The tundra is so dry that it is sometimes called a cold desert.

But the water that does fall cannot sink into the frozen ground.

So it sits on top, and the land is dotted with shallow pools and wet ground.

The summer is short and cool.

For a few weeks, the sun stays in the sky almost all day and all night.

The top of the frozen ground softens, and the land turns green and bright.

Even in summer, the air stays cool, and frost can return on any night.

Then the short summer ends, the sun sinks low, and the long winter begins again.
