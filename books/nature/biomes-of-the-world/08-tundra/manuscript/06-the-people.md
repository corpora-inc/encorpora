# The People

People have lived on the tundra for many thousands of years.

To live here takes deep knowledge of the cold land and its animals.

In North America, the Inuit and other northern peoples have lived on the tundra and the frozen coast for a very long time.

They hunted caribou, fished in the rivers and the sea, and hunted seals along the ice.

They made warm clothing from animal skins and fur, sewn to keep out the wind.

They built homes from what the land gave them, and traveled by sled across the snow.

In northern Europe, the Sami people have followed the reindeer across the tundra for many generations.

The reindeer gave them meat, milk, skins for clothing, and a way to pull sleds.

In northern Russia, many peoples have made the tundra their home.

The Nenets, the Chukchi, and others have herded reindeer across these cold lands for a very long time.

Some of them still travel with their herds today, moving with the seasons.

In summer they follow the reindeer north toward the coast.

In winter they move south toward the shelter of the forest edge.

Across all these lands, the people learned to read the weather, the snow, and the ice.

They learned the paths of the caribou and the reindeer.

They learned where the fish gathered and when the birds would come.

They learned to store food from the short summer to last through the long winter.

The tundra gives little, and it asks much, but people have made good lives here for a very long time.

Their songs, their stories, and their languages grew up with the cold wind and the wide open land.
