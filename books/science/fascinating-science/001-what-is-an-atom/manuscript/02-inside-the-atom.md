# Inside the Atom

An atom has three types of particles inside it. Protons. Neutrons. Electrons. These three particles make up every atom in the universe.

The center of the atom is called the nucleus. The nucleus contains the protons and the neutrons. The nucleus is tiny compared to the whole atom. If the atom were the size of a football stadium, the nucleus would be the size of a marble on the fifty-yard line.

Protons carry a positive electrical charge. Every proton has the same amount of positive charge. The number of protons in an atom determines what element the atom is. One proton means hydrogen. Six protons means carbon. Twenty-six protons means iron. The number of protons is the most important fact about an atom.

Neutrons carry no electrical charge. Neutrons are neutral. Neutrons sit in the nucleus next to the protons. Neutrons help hold the nucleus together. Without neutrons, the protons would push each other apart because they all carry the same positive charge.

Electrons carry a negative electrical charge. Electrons move around the nucleus in a cloud. They do not orbit like planets around a sun. They exist in a fuzzy region of probability around the nucleus. The electron cloud is where you are likely to find an electron at any given moment.

An electron has almost no mass compared to a proton or neutron. A proton is about 1,836 times heavier than an electron. Almost all of the mass of an atom is in the nucleus.

In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the number of protons. The positive charges and the negative charges balance out. The atom has no overall charge. This balance is the default state of an atom.

Most of an atom is empty space. The nucleus is dense and tiny. The electron cloud is large and mostly nothing. If you could remove all the empty space from every atom in your body, you would shrink to a size smaller than a grain of salt. Your mass would stay the same. You would just be incredibly dense.
