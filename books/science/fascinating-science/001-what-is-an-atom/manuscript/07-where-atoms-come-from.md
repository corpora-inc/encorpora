# Where Atoms Come From

The hydrogen and helium in the universe were created in the first few minutes after the Big Bang. The universe began as an incredibly hot, dense point about 13.8 billion years ago. As it expanded and cooled, protons and neutrons formed. Those protons and neutrons combined into the simplest nuclei. Hydrogen and helium were the first elements.

For millions of years, the universe was a cloud of hydrogen and helium gas. Then gravity pulled the gas together into clumps. The clumps grew denser and hotter. When the center of a clump reached about ten million degrees, nuclear fusion ignited. A star was born.

Stars are atom factories. Inside a star, hydrogen atoms fuse together to form helium. This fusion releases enormous energy. The energy is what makes a star shine. Our sun fuses about six hundred million tons of hydrogen into helium every second.

Larger stars fuse heavier elements. After a star burns through its hydrogen, it begins fusing helium into carbon. Then carbon into oxygen. Then oxygen into silicon. Then silicon into iron. Each stage produces a heavier element. Iron is the heaviest element a star can produce through normal fusion.

Elements heavier than iron are created when massive stars explode. These explosions are called supernovas. A supernova releases more energy in a few seconds than the sun will produce in its entire lifetime. The extreme conditions of a supernova forge elements like gold, silver, platinum, and uranium.

The explosion scatters these heavy elements across space. The elements mix with clouds of hydrogen and helium gas. Eventually, gravity pulls the enriched gas together to form new stars and planets. Our solar system formed from one of these enriched clouds about 4.6 billion years ago.

Every atom in your body heavier than hydrogen was made inside a star. The calcium in your bones. The iron in your blood. The carbon in your skin. You are made of stardust. That is not a metaphor. It is a fact of nuclear physics.
