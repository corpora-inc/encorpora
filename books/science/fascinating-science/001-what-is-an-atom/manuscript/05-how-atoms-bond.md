# How Atoms Bond

Atoms rarely exist alone. Most atoms bond with other atoms to form molecules. A molecule is a group of atoms held together by chemical bonds. Water is a molecule. Oxygen gas is a molecule. Sugar is a molecule.

Atoms bond because of their electrons. The electrons in the outermost shell of an atom determine how that atom interacts with other atoms. These outer electrons are called valence electrons. An atom with a full outer shell is stable. An atom without a full outer shell will seek to fill it.

There are two main types of chemical bonds. Covalent bonds and ionic bonds.

In a covalent bond, two atoms share electrons. Both atoms contribute electrons to a shared pair. The shared electrons orbit both nuclei. This sharing holds the atoms together. Water is held together by covalent bonds. Each hydrogen atom shares one electron with the oxygen atom.

In an ionic bond, one atom gives electrons to another atom. The atom that loses electrons becomes positively charged. The atom that gains electrons becomes negatively charged. Opposite charges attract. This attraction holds the two atoms together. Table salt is held together by ionic bonds. Sodium gives an electron to chlorine.

A water molecule contains two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. Scientists write this as H2O. The H stands for hydrogen. The O stands for oxygen. The 2 means there are two hydrogen atoms.

A molecule of carbon dioxide contains one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms. Scientists write this as CO2. Plants absorb carbon dioxide from the air and use it to build sugar molecules.

The bonds between atoms store energy. When bonds break, energy is released. When bonds form, energy is absorbed. Every chemical reaction in the universe involves breaking old bonds and forming new ones.
