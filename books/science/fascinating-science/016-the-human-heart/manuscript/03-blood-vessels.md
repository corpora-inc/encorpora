# Blood Vessels

Blood does not flow freely through your body. It travels through tubes called blood vessels. Blood vessels form a network that reaches every tissue in your body. If you laid all your blood vessels end to end, they would stretch about 100,000 kilometers. That is enough to circle the Earth two and a half times.

There are three main types of blood vessels: arteries, veins, and capillaries.

Arteries carry blood away from the heart. Remember it this way: "artery" and "away" both start with "a." Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood to your tissues. The largest artery is the aorta. The aorta exits the left ventricle. It arches upward and then curves down through your chest and abdomen. Branches split off from the aorta and lead to every part of your body.

Artery walls are thick and elastic. Elastic means they can stretch and snap back. When the heart contracts, it pushes a wave of blood into the arteries. The artery walls expand to absorb this pressure. Then they snap back, pushing the blood forward. This creates a pulse. A pulse is a rhythmic throbbing you can feel in certain places. You can feel your pulse on your wrist or the side of your neck.

Veins carry blood back toward the heart. Most veins carry blood that is low in oxygen. Vein walls are thinner than artery walls. The blood in veins flows under lower pressure. Veins have small valves inside them. These valves prevent blood from flowing backward. When you stand up, blood in your legs must flow upward against gravity. The valves keep it moving in the right direction.

Capillaries are the smallest blood vessels. They are microscopic. Microscopic means too small to see without a microscope. Capillary walls are only one cell thick. This thinness is important. Oxygen passes through the capillary wall and enters the surrounding tissue. Carbon dioxide passes from the tissue into the capillary. Nutrients pass out. Waste passes in. All exchange happens in the capillaries.

Arteries branch into smaller and smaller vessels called arterioles. Arterioles connect to capillaries. Capillaries merge into slightly larger vessels called venules. Venules merge into veins. Veins carry the blood back to the heart.

This is a closed loop. Blood never leaves the vessels under normal conditions. The heart pushes it out through arteries. Capillaries make the exchange. Veins bring it home.
