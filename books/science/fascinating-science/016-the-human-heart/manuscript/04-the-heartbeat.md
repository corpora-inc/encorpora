# The Heartbeat

Your heart beats about 100,000 times per day. Each beat is a carefully timed sequence of events. The timing is controlled by electrical signals that originate inside the heart itself.

The natural pacemaker of the heart is called the sinoatrial node. We usually call it the SA node. It is a small cluster of cells in the wall of the right atrium. A cluster is a group of things close together. The SA node generates electrical impulses. An impulse is a sudden burst of energy. These impulses set the pace of the heartbeat.

The SA node fires about 60 to 100 times per minute in a resting adult. Each impulse spreads across both atria. The atria contract together. They squeeze blood into the ventricles.

The impulse then reaches another node called the atrioventricular node. We call it the AV node. The AV node sits between the atria and the ventricles. It pauses the signal for a fraction of a second. This pause is essential. It gives the atria time to finish emptying before the ventricles contract.

After the pause, the signal travels down a pathway called the bundle of His. His is pronounced "hiss." It was named after Wilhelm His Jr., the scientist who discovered it. The bundle of His splits into two branches. One branch goes to the right ventricle. The other goes to the left ventricle. The branches spread into tiny fibers called Purkinje fibers. These fibers deliver the signal to the muscle cells of both ventricles. The ventricles contract powerfully and simultaneously.

One complete cycle of contraction and relaxation is called a cardiac cycle. The contraction phase is called systole. Systole comes from a Greek word meaning "to draw together." The relaxation phase is called diastole. Diastole comes from a Greek word meaning "to expand."

During systole, the ventricles squeeze and push blood out. During diastole, the ventricles relax and fill with blood. The cycle repeats endlessly.

A doctor can measure the heart's electrical activity with a machine called an electrocardiogram, or ECG. The ECG shows the electrical impulses as waves on a screen. Each wave has a specific shape. Doctors read these shapes to check if the heart is working properly.

An irregular heartbeat is called an arrhythmia. Arrhythmia means "without rhythm." Some arrhythmias are harmless. Others are dangerous. If the SA node fails, doctors can implant an artificial pacemaker. This small device sends electrical signals to keep the heart beating steadily.

The heart is its own drummer. It sets its own beat and keeps perfect time.
