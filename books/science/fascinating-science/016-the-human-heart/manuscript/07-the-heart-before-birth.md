# The Heart Before Birth

The heart is one of the first organs to develop. It forms early because the growing embryo needs blood flow to survive. An embryo is an organism in its earliest stages of development.

About 18 to 20 days after conception, heart cells begin to appear. They form a simple tube. This tube starts to beat around day 22. At this point, the embryo is smaller than a grain of rice. The beating tube pushes fluid through the tiny body.

Over the next several weeks, the tube folds and twists. It bends into an S-shape. It divides into chambers. Walls called septa grow between the chambers. A septum is a wall that divides two spaces. The plural is septa. By about eight weeks, the heart has four distinct chambers.

A fetal heart works differently from a heart after birth. A fetus is an unborn baby after about eight weeks of development. The fetus does not breathe air. Its lungs are filled with fluid. It gets oxygen from the mother through the placenta. The placenta is an organ that connects the fetus to the mother's blood supply. Oxygen and nutrients pass from the mother's blood to the fetal blood through the placenta.

Because the fetal lungs are not in use, the heart has a shortcut. There is an opening between the right atrium and the left atrium. This opening is called the foramen ovale. Foramen means "opening" in Latin. Ovale means "oval-shaped." Blood passes through this opening, bypassing the lungs. Most blood goes directly to the left side of the heart and out to the body.

There is another shortcut called the ductus arteriosus. This is a small blood vessel that connects the pulmonary artery to the aorta. It diverts blood away from the lungs. These two shortcuts ensure that blood reaches the body quickly without a wasted trip to lungs that are not yet working.

At birth, everything changes. The baby takes its first breath. The lungs expand. Blood begins flowing to the lungs to pick up oxygen. The foramen ovale closes. It usually seals shut within the first year of life. The ductus arteriosus also closes. It typically closes within the first few days after birth.

Sometimes these structures do not close properly. A foramen ovale that stays open is called a patent foramen ovale. Patent means "open." This condition is common. About one in four people have it. Most never know. It rarely causes problems.

The transformation at birth is remarkable. In a matter of seconds, the heart switches from fetal circulation to the circulation pattern it will use for the rest of life.
