# Four Chambers

The human heart has four chambers. A chamber is an enclosed space. Each chamber plays a specific role in moving blood.

The two upper chambers are called atria. One is called an atrium. The atria are smaller and thinner. They receive blood that flows into the heart. Think of them as receiving rooms.

The two lower chambers are called ventricles. A ventricle is a thicker, more muscular chamber. The ventricles pump blood out of the heart. They do the heavy lifting.

The right atrium receives blood from the body. This blood has already delivered its oxygen to the cells. It is low in oxygen. It is dark red. The blood enters the right atrium through two large veins. A vein is a blood vessel that carries blood toward the heart. The two veins are the superior vena cava and the inferior vena cava. Superior means upper. Inferior means lower.

From the right atrium, blood flows down into the right ventricle. The right ventricle pumps this blood to the lungs. In the lungs, the blood picks up fresh oxygen. It also releases carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide is a waste gas that cells produce. You exhale carbon dioxide with every breath.

The blood returns from the lungs to the left atrium. This blood is now rich in oxygen. It is bright red. It enters the left atrium through the pulmonary veins. Pulmonary means "related to the lungs."

From the left atrium, blood flows into the left ventricle. The left ventricle is the strongest chamber. Its walls are the thickest. The left ventricle pumps oxygen-rich blood to the entire body. It must push blood all the way to your toes and the top of your head. This requires great force.

Between the chambers are valves. A valve is a flap that opens in one direction. Valves prevent blood from flowing backward. There are four valves in the heart. The tricuspid valve sits between the right atrium and right ventricle. The mitral valve sits between the left atrium and left ventricle. The pulmonary valve guards the exit from the right ventricle. The aortic valve guards the exit from the left ventricle.

When valves close, they make a sound. A doctor can hear this sound with a stethoscope. A stethoscope is a tool for listening to sounds inside the body. The heartbeat sound "lub-dub" comes from valves closing. The "lub" is the mitral and tricuspid valves closing. The "dub" is the aortic and pulmonary valves closing.

Four chambers. Four valves. One purpose: keep blood moving.
