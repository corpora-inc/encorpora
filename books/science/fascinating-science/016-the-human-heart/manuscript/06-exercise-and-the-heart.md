# Exercise and the Heart

Exercise makes your heart stronger. The heart is a muscle. Like any muscle, it gets stronger when you use it. Regular exercise is one of the best things you can do for your heart.

When you exercise, your muscles need more oxygen. They are working harder. They are burning more energy. To deliver more oxygen, the heart beats faster. It also beats harder. It pumps more blood with each contraction. The amount of blood the heart pumps per minute is called cardiac output.

A resting heart rate for a healthy adult is 60 to 100 beats per minute. During intense exercise, the heart rate can rise to 150, 170, or even 200 beats per minute. The heart rate is the number of heartbeats in one minute. You can measure it by counting your pulse for 15 seconds and multiplying by four.

Athletes often have lower resting heart rates. A well-trained endurance athlete might have a resting heart rate of 40 to 50 beats per minute. This is because their hearts are stronger. A stronger heart pumps more blood per beat. It does not need to beat as often. This is called stroke volume. Stroke volume is the amount of blood pumped with each beat. Training increases stroke volume.

Aerobic exercise is especially good for the heart. Aerobic means "with oxygen." Aerobic exercise includes running, swimming, cycling, and brisk walking. During aerobic exercise, your breathing rate increases. Your heart rate increases. Your body uses oxygen to burn fuel steadily over a long period.

Regular aerobic exercise lowers resting blood pressure. It raises levels of HDL cholesterol. HDL stands for high-density lipoprotein. HDL is often called "good" cholesterol. It helps remove harmful cholesterol from the arteries. Cholesterol is a waxy substance in the blood. Too much of the wrong kind can clog arteries.

Exercise also strengthens the walls of blood vessels. It makes them more flexible. Flexible vessels handle pressure changes better. Exercise reduces inflammation. Inflammation is swelling and irritation in tissues. Chronic inflammation damages arteries over time. Chronic means long-lasting.

People who are inactive have a higher risk of heart disease. Heart disease is the leading cause of death worldwide. Even moderate exercise makes a difference. Walking 30 minutes a day significantly reduces risk.

The heart responds to how you live. Challenge it, and it grows stronger. Neglect it, and it weakens. Every step counts.
