# Blood Pressure

Blood pushes against the walls of your arteries as it flows. This push is called blood pressure. Blood pressure is the force of blood pressing on the inside of your blood vessels.

Blood pressure is measured with two numbers. The top number is called systolic pressure. Systolic pressure is the force when the heart contracts. It is the higher number. The bottom number is called diastolic pressure. Diastolic pressure is the force when the heart relaxes. It is the lower number.

A healthy blood pressure for an adult is about 120 over 80. We write it as 120/80. The unit of measurement is millimeters of mercury, written as mmHg. This unit comes from early pressure-measuring devices that used a column of mercury.

Blood pressure is measured with a device called a sphygmomanometer. That is a long word. It comes from Greek roots meaning "pulse" and "measure." A cuff wraps around your upper arm. The cuff inflates and squeezes the artery closed. Then the cuff slowly deflates. A doctor listens with a stethoscope for the sound of blood flowing. The point where the sound begins is the systolic pressure. The point where the sound disappears is the diastolic pressure.

High blood pressure is called hypertension. Hyper means "above" or "excessive." Tension refers to pressure. Hypertension means the blood pushes too hard against artery walls. Over time, this damages the arteries. It makes the heart work harder. It increases the risk of heart attack and stroke. A heart attack happens when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked. A stroke happens when blood flow to part of the brain is blocked.

Hypertension is called the silent killer. Most people with high blood pressure feel no symptoms. They do not know they have it. That is why regular checkups matter.

Low blood pressure is called hypotension. Hypo means "below." Hypotension can cause dizziness and fainting. It means the body is not getting enough blood flow.

Many factors affect blood pressure. Salt increases blood pressure. Exercise temporarily raises it but improves it over time. Stress raises it. Sleep lowers it. Obesity raises it. Obesity means having too much body fat.

Keeping blood pressure in a healthy range protects the heart, the brain, the kidneys, and every organ in the body. The force behind every heartbeat matters.
