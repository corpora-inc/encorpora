# Consumers

Consumers are organisms that cannot make their own food. They must eat other organisms to get energy. Every animal on Earth is a consumer. Consumers are also called heterotrophs. Heterotroph means "other-feeder."

There are several types of consumers. The type depends on what the consumer eats.

Primary consumers eat producers. They are also called herbivores. A herbivore is an animal that eats only plants or algae. Rabbits, deer, cows, caterpillars, and many fish are herbivores. Primary consumers are the first link in the food chain after producers. They take the chemical energy stored in plants and convert it into their own body tissues.

Secondary consumers eat primary consumers. They are also called carnivores or predators. A carnivore is an animal that eats other animals. A predator is an animal that hunts and kills other animals for food. Frogs eat caterpillars. Snakes eat frogs. Hawks eat snakes. Each of these is a secondary consumer when it eats a herbivore.

Tertiary consumers eat secondary consumers. They sit higher on the food chain. A tertiary consumer is often a top predator. A top predator is an animal that no other animal regularly hunts. Eagles, sharks, lions, and wolves are examples of top predators. Top predators control the populations of animals below them in the food chain.

Some consumers eat both plants and animals. These are called omnivores. An omnivore eats a mixed diet. Bears are omnivores. They eat berries, fish, and insects. Humans are omnivores. We eat plants, meat, fungi, and many other foods. Pigs, raccoons, and many birds are also omnivores.

Scavengers are consumers that eat dead organisms. A scavenger does not hunt live prey. It feeds on animals that have already died. Vultures are scavengers. Hyenas often scavenge. Many insects, such as beetles and flies, are scavengers. Scavengers play an important role by recycling nutrients from dead organisms back into the ecosystem. A nutrient is a substance that an organism needs to live and grow.

Consumers get less energy than the organisms they eat. When a rabbit eats grass, it does not absorb all the energy in the grass. Some energy is lost as heat. Some energy goes into parts of the grass that the rabbit cannot digest. Digest means to break down food so the body can use it. As a general rule, only about ten percent of the energy at one level of a food chain passes to the next level. This is called the ten percent rule.

The ten percent rule explains why food chains are usually short. There is not enough energy to support many levels. Most food chains have three to five levels. There is plenty of grass but fewer rabbits. There are fewer rabbits than grass because each rabbit needs a lot of grass. There are fewer hawks than rabbits because each hawk needs many rabbits. Energy limits the length of every food chain.
