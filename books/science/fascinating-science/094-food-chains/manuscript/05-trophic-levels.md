# Trophic Levels

A trophic level is a position in a food chain. The word trophic comes from a Greek word meaning "nourishment." Each trophic level represents one step in the transfer of energy from one organism to another.

The first trophic level is occupied by producers. Plants, algae, and photosynthetic bacteria sit at this level. They capture energy from the Sun and store it as chemical energy. Producers support all the trophic levels above them.

The second trophic level belongs to primary consumers. These are the herbivores that eat producers. A grasshopper eating a leaf is a primary consumer. A zooplankton eating phytoplankton is a primary consumer. Zooplankton are tiny animals that drift in water. Primary consumers convert plant energy into animal energy.

The third trophic level contains secondary consumers. These are carnivores that eat herbivores. A frog eating a grasshopper occupies the third trophic level. A small fish eating zooplankton is also at this level.

The fourth trophic level and above contains tertiary consumers and top predators. A snake eating a frog is a tertiary consumer. An eagle eating a snake sits at the fifth trophic level. Most food chains do not extend beyond four or five trophic levels.

Energy decreases at each trophic level. This is the most important concept in food chain science. When a grasshopper eats a leaf, it absorbs only about ten percent of the leaf's energy into its body. The rest is lost. Some energy is used by the grasshopper for movement and body heat. Some passes through as waste. Some was in parts of the leaf the grasshopper did not eat.

This energy loss at each level can be shown in an energy pyramid. An energy pyramid is a diagram shaped like a triangle. The base is wide and represents producers. They contain the most energy. Each level above is narrower. It contains less energy. The top level is the narrowest. It contains the least energy.

Biomass also decreases at each trophic level. Biomass is the total mass of living organisms at a given level. A field may contain 1,000 pounds of grass. The rabbits eating that grass may total 100 pounds. The fox eating the rabbits may weigh 10 pounds. Biomass decreases because energy decreases. Less energy means less growth. Less growth means less living material.

The number of organisms also tends to decrease at higher trophic levels. A meadow may have millions of grass plants, thousands of insects, hundreds of mice, and one hawk. This pattern is called a numbers pyramid. There are exceptions. One large tree can support thousands of insects. In that case, the numbers pyramid is inverted at the base.

Trophic levels help scientists understand how ecosystems function. They reveal why top predators are rare. They explain why plants dominate most landscapes. They show why removing one level can disrupt an entire system. Energy flows upward through trophic levels, diminishing at every step. This simple rule shapes every ecosystem on Earth.
