# Energy from the Sun

All food chains begin with energy. Almost all of that energy comes from the Sun. The Sun sends light and heat to Earth every day. Living things capture this energy and pass it from one organism to another. An organism is any living thing.

Energy is the ability to do work. Work in science means moving something or changing something. Every living thing needs energy to survive. Animals need energy to move, breathe, and grow. Plants need energy to build leaves, stems, and roots. Even bacteria need energy to divide and multiply. A bacterium is a tiny single-celled organism.

The Sun produces energy through nuclear fusion. Fusion is the process of combining small atoms to form larger ones. Deep inside the Sun, hydrogen atoms fuse into helium atoms. This releases enormous amounts of energy. Some of that energy reaches Earth as sunlight.

Sunlight travels 93 million miles from the Sun to Earth. It takes about eight minutes to make the journey. When sunlight reaches Earth, some of it is absorbed by the land and oceans. Some is reflected back into space. And some is captured by living things.

Plants capture sunlight through a process called photosynthesis. Photosynthesis means "making with light." During photosynthesis, a plant takes in carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil. It uses sunlight to combine these ingredients into glucose. Glucose is a type of sugar. Sugar stores chemical energy. The plant uses this energy to grow and stay alive. Photosynthesis also produces oxygen as a byproduct. A byproduct is something produced during a process that is not the main goal. Animals breathe this oxygen.

The green color of plants comes from a molecule called chlorophyll. A molecule is a group of atoms bonded together. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light from the Sun. It reflects green light. That is why most plants look green. Chlorophyll is found in structures called chloroplasts inside plant cells. A cell is the smallest unit of life.

Not all food chains start with sunlight. A few ecosystems get energy from chemicals instead. Deep in the ocean, hydrothermal vents release hot, mineral-rich water. Certain bacteria use these chemicals to produce energy through a process called chemosynthesis. Chemosynthesis means "making with chemicals." These bacteria are the base of food chains in the deep sea, where no sunlight reaches.

But the vast majority of life on Earth depends on the Sun. Sunlight powers photosynthesis. Photosynthesis creates food. Food fuels every animal, fungus, and bacterium that cannot make its own energy. The Sun is the ultimate source of energy for nearly every food chain on the planet.
