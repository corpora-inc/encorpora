# Food Webs

A food chain is a simple, straight line showing who eats whom. But nature is rarely that simple. Most organisms eat more than one type of food. Most organisms are eaten by more than one predator. When you connect all the food chains in an ecosystem, you get a food web. A food web is a network of interconnected food chains.

Consider a meadow. Grass is eaten by rabbits, mice, grasshoppers, and deer. Rabbits are eaten by foxes, hawks, and snakes. Mice are eaten by owls, snakes, and foxes. Grasshoppers are eaten by frogs, birds, and spiders. Each of these connections is a food chain. Together, they form a complex web.

Food webs are more realistic than food chains. A food chain suggests that each organism eats only one thing and is eaten by only one predator. This is almost never true. A bear eats berries, fish, insects, and honey. A robin eats worms, seeds, and fruit. Most animals have varied diets. A food web captures this complexity.

Food webs show that organisms can occupy more than one trophic level. A human who eats salad is a primary consumer. A human who eats chicken is a secondary consumer. A human who eats tuna is a tertiary or quaternary consumer, depending on what the tuna ate. Quaternary means fourth. Many omnivores shift between trophic levels depending on what they eat at any given time.

The connections in a food web provide stability. If one species disappears, other species can switch to alternative food sources. Stability means the ability to stay in balance. A fox that loses its rabbit prey can eat more mice instead. A bird that loses one type of seed can eat another type. The more connections in a food web, the more stable the ecosystem tends to be.

Keystone species play a crucial role in food webs. A keystone species is an organism whose presence has a large effect on the ecosystem relative to its abundance. Abundance means how many there are. Sea otters are a keystone species in kelp forest ecosystems. Sea otters eat sea urchins. Sea urchins eat kelp. Without sea otters, sea urchin populations explode. They consume all the kelp. The entire kelp forest ecosystem collapses. Many other species that depend on kelp disappear.

Scientists study food webs to understand ecosystem health. A healthy ecosystem has a diverse, well-connected food web. Diverse means having many different types. When a food web loses species or connections, the ecosystem becomes fragile. Fragile means easily damaged.

Food webs exist in every environment on Earth. Ocean food webs connect phytoplankton to whales. Forest food webs connect trees to bears. Desert food webs connect cacti to roadrunners. Each web is unique, but they all follow the same principles: energy flows from producers to consumers, and connections between species determine the strength of the system.
