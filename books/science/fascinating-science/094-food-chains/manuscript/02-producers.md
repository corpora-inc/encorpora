# Producers

Producers are organisms that make their own food. They do not need to eat other organisms to get energy. They capture energy from the Sun or from chemicals and convert it into food. Producers form the base of every food chain.

Plants are the most familiar producers. Grasses, trees, flowers, and shrubs are all producers. They use photosynthesis to turn sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose. This glucose stores energy that the plant uses for growth, repair, and reproduction. Reproduction is the process of creating new organisms.

Algae are another important group of producers. Algae are simple organisms that live in water. Some are tiny, single-celled creatures that float in the ocean. Others are large, like kelp. Kelp is a type of seaweed that can grow over 100 feet tall. Algae perform photosynthesis just like plants. They produce a huge amount of the world's oxygen. Scientists estimate that algae produce about half of all the oxygen on Earth.

Phytoplankton are microscopic algae that drift near the surface of oceans, lakes, and rivers. Microscopic means too small to see without a magnifying tool. Phytoplankton are incredibly important. They are the base of most aquatic food chains. Aquatic means relating to water. Tiny animals eat phytoplankton. Larger animals eat those tiny animals. The chain continues all the way up to large fish, whales, and seabirds.

Some bacteria are also producers. Cyanobacteria are bacteria that perform photosynthesis. They are among the oldest living things on Earth. Fossils of cyanobacteria date back 3.5 billion years. A fossil is the preserved remains of an ancient organism. Billions of years ago, cyanobacteria produced the oxygen that filled Earth's atmosphere for the first time. Without them, animals could never have evolved. Evolved means changed gradually over many generations.

Producers are sometimes called autotrophs. Autotroph means "self-feeder." This name fits because producers feed themselves. They do not depend on eating other organisms.

The total amount of food that producers create is called primary productivity. Primary productivity varies by ecosystem. Tropical rainforests have high primary productivity because they receive abundant sunlight and rain. Deserts have low primary productivity because water is scarce. Oceans have moderate primary productivity overall, but some ocean regions are extremely productive.

Producers matter because they convert nonliving energy into living matter. Without producers, there would be no food for any other organism. Every animal on Earth depends on producers, directly or indirectly. When you eat a salad, you eat producers directly. When you eat a steak, you eat an animal that ate producers. Producers are the foundation of life.
