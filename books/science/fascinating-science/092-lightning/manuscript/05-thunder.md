# Thunder

Thunder is the sound that lightning makes. Every lightning bolt produces thunder. If you see lightning but do not hear thunder, the storm is too far away for the sound to reach you. Thunder can be heard from about ten miles away under good conditions.

Thunder is caused by the rapid heating of air. When a lightning bolt passes through the air, it heats the air to about 54,000 degrees Fahrenheit in a fraction of a second. This sudden heating causes the air to expand violently. The expanding air creates a pressure wave. A pressure wave is a disturbance that moves through a medium. The medium in this case is air.

The initial pressure wave is a shock wave. It moves faster than the speed of sound. As it spreads outward, it slows down and becomes an ordinary sound wave. A sound wave is a vibration that travels through air, water, or solid material. Sound waves reach your ears, and you hear thunder.

Thunder has different sounds depending on your distance from the lightning. Close lightning produces a sharp, loud crack or bang. This is because the shock wave reaches you before it has time to spread out and weaken. Distant lightning produces a low, long rumble. The rumble happens because different parts of the lightning bolt are at different distances from you. Sound from the nearest part arrives first. Sound from the farthest part arrives last. The sounds overlap and create a rolling rumble.

You can estimate how far away lightning is by counting seconds between the flash and the thunder. Light travels almost instantly to your eyes. Sound travels about one mile in five seconds. If you count five seconds between the flash and the thunder, the lightning is about one mile away. If you count ten seconds, it is about two miles away.

Thunder can be surprisingly loud. Close thunder can exceed 120 decibels. A decibel is a unit used to measure the loudness of sound. A normal conversation is about 60 decibels. A rock concert is about 110 decibels. Sounds above 120 decibels can cause pain in human ears.

Thunder can also create infrasound. Infrasound is sound with a frequency too low for human ears to detect. Frequency is the number of vibrations per second. Humans hear sounds between about 20 and 20,000 vibrations per second. Thunder produces vibrations below 20 per second. You cannot hear infrasound, but you can feel it. It is the deep rumble you sense in your chest during a close storm.

Sometimes lightning occurs without audible thunder. This is called heat lightning. Heat lightning is actually lightning from a distant storm. The light is visible because it reflects off clouds, but the thunder dissipates before it reaches you. Dissipate means to spread out and weaken. All lightning makes thunder. If you cannot hear it, the storm is simply too far away.
