# Types of Lightning

Not all lightning is the same. Scientists classify lightning by where it occurs and how it behaves. The main categories are cloud-to-ground, intra-cloud, cloud-to-cloud, and cloud-to-air lightning.

Cloud-to-ground lightning is the most studied type. It is also the most dangerous to people and structures. This type travels from a thundercloud to the surface of Earth. It follows the stepped leader and return stroke process described earlier. Most cloud-to-ground lightning carries negative charge from the cloud to the ground. About five to ten percent carries positive charge. Positive lightning is rarer but more dangerous. It carries more current and lasts longer.

Intra-cloud lightning is the most common type. It occurs entirely within a single cloud. The discharge moves between the positively charged upper region and the negatively charged lower region of the same cloud. Intra-cloud lightning lights up the cloud from inside. From the ground, it looks like the whole cloud is flickering. Some people call this sheet lightning because the cloud appears to glow like a bright sheet.

Cloud-to-cloud lightning travels between two separate clouds. The discharge bridges the gap between a charged region in one cloud and an oppositely charged region in another. This type is less common than intra-cloud lightning.

Cloud-to-air lightning extends from a cloud into the clear air around it. It does not reach the ground or another cloud. These bolts often branch outward in dramatic patterns. They are relatively rare.

Ball lightning is a mysterious phenomenon. It appears as a glowing sphere of light, usually about the size of a grapefruit. It floats through the air for several seconds before disappearing. Ball lightning has been reported for centuries, but scientists still do not fully understand it. Some theories suggest it is a plasma phenomenon. Others suggest it involves burning silicon particles from the ground.

Sprites, jets, and elves are types of lightning that occur above thunderstorms. Sprites are large, reddish flashes that appear high above the cloud. They can extend 50 miles into the atmosphere. Blue jets shoot upward from the top of a cloud. Elves are expanding rings of light that last less than one millisecond. A millisecond is one thousandth of a second. These phenomena were not confirmed by science until the 1990s because they are difficult to observe from the ground.

Volcanic lightning occurs during volcanic eruptions. Ash particles collide and separate charges, just like ice particles in a thundercloud. The result is spectacular lightning within the eruption plume. A plume is a column of material rising into the air.

Each type of lightning reveals something different about electricity in nature. From familiar bolts hitting the ground to mysterious glowing spheres and red sprites in the upper atmosphere, lightning takes many forms.
