# A Flash in the Sky

Lightning is a giant spark of electricity in the atmosphere. The atmosphere is the layer of gases that surrounds Earth. Lightning happens during thunderstorms. A thunderstorm is a storm with heavy rain, strong winds, and electrical discharges. A discharge is a sudden release of stored energy.

Lightning is one of the most powerful forces in nature. A single bolt can heat the air around it to 54,000 degrees Fahrenheit. That is five times hotter than the surface of the Sun. This extreme heat happens in a fraction of a second. The bolt itself lasts less than one second, but its effects can be devastating.

About 100 lightning bolts strike Earth's surface every second. That is about 8 million strikes per day. Lightning occurs on every continent, including Antarctica. It is most common in tropical regions near the equator. The equator is the imaginary line around the middle of Earth. The country with the most lightning is the Democratic Republic of the Congo in central Africa.

Lightning has fascinated humans for thousands of years. Ancient Greeks believed lightning was a weapon of Zeus, the king of their gods. Norse people believed it came from Thor's hammer. For most of human history, people did not understand what caused lightning. They saw the flash and heard the thunder, but the science behind it was a mystery.

In 1752, Benjamin Franklin performed a famous experiment. He flew a kite during a thunderstorm. A metal key was attached to the kite string. When lightning struck near the kite, electrical charge traveled down the string to the key. Franklin felt a spark. This experiment helped prove that lightning is electrical. It was also extremely dangerous. Others who tried the same experiment were killed.

Lightning is not unique to Earth. Scientists have observed lightning on Jupiter, Saturn, and Venus. Jupiter's lightning bolts can be thousands of times more powerful than those on Earth. Lightning may also occur in the dust clouds of volcanic eruptions. A volcanic eruption is when a volcano releases lava, ash, and gas.

Every bolt of lightning tells a story about electricity, heat, and the physics of the atmosphere. Lightning is beautiful and terrifying. It is a reminder that nature contains forces far more powerful than anything humans have built. Understanding lightning helps us stay safe and appreciate the electrical nature of our world.
