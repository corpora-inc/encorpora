# Electric Charge

To understand lightning, you first need to understand electric charge. Electric charge is a basic property of matter. It comes in two types: positive and negative. These names are just labels. They describe how charged objects interact with each other.

All matter is made of atoms. An atom is the smallest unit of an element. Every atom has a nucleus at its center. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons. Protons have a positive charge. Neutrons have no charge. Electrons orbit the nucleus. Electrons have a negative charge.

In a normal atom, the number of protons equals the number of electrons. The positive and negative charges balance out. The atom is electrically neutral. Neutral means having no overall charge. But electrons can move from one atom to another. When an atom loses an electron, it becomes positively charged. When an atom gains an electron, it becomes negatively charged. A charged atom is called an ion.

Opposite charges attract each other. Positive attracts negative. Like charges repel each other. Positive repels positive. Negative repels negative. This is the fundamental rule of electric charge. Attract means to pull toward. Repel means to push away.

Static electricity is the buildup of electric charge on the surface of an object. Static means not moving. The charges build up and stay in one place until they are released. You experience static electricity when you rub your feet on a carpet and then touch a metal doorknob. Electrons transfer from the carpet to your body. When you touch the doorknob, the extra electrons jump to the metal. You feel a small shock. This is a tiny version of lightning.

The force between charges is called the electric force. The electric force follows Coulomb's law. Coulomb's law states that the force between two charges depends on two things: the size of the charges and the distance between them. Larger charges create a stronger force. Closer charges create a stronger force.

Voltage is a measure of how strongly charges are pushed. It is the difference in electrical potential between two points. Potential is stored energy. A battery has voltage. The higher the voltage, the more energy is available to move charges. Lightning involves extremely high voltages. A lightning bolt can have a voltage of 300 million volts.

Electric charge is the foundation of lightning. When charges separate and build up in a cloud, voltage increases. When the voltage becomes large enough, the air breaks down and conducts electricity. The result is lightning. Every flash begins with the simple physics of positive and negative charges.
