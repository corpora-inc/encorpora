# Types of Volcanoes

Not all volcanoes look the same. Their shape depends on what erupts from them and how. Scientists classify volcanoes into several types based on their structure and eruption style.

Shield volcanoes are broad and gently sloping. They look like a warrior's shield lying on the ground. Shield volcanoes form from eruptions of low-viscosity lava. The lava flows easily and spreads over large areas before cooling. Layer after layer of thin lava flows builds a wide, dome-shaped mountain. Mauna Loa in Hawaii is a shield volcano. It is the largest active volcano on Earth by volume. From its base on the ocean floor to its summit, Mauna Loa rises more than 9,000 meters. That is taller than Mount Everest, measured base to peak.

Stratovolcanoes are tall, steep, and cone-shaped. They are also called composite volcanoes. Composite means made of multiple materials. Stratovolcanoes are built from alternating layers of lava, ash, and rock fragments. They erupt both effusively and explosively. The alternating layers create a strong, steep structure. Mount Fuji in Japan is a stratovolcano. Mount Rainier in Washington state is a stratovolcano. These volcanoes are among the most beautiful and most dangerous on Earth.

Cinder cones are the smallest and simplest type of volcano. They form when gas-rich magma erupts and blasts small fragments into the air. These fragments, called cinders or scoria, fall back around the vent and pile up in a cone shape. A vent is the opening through which volcanic material escapes. Cinder cones rarely exceed 300 meters in height. They often form on the flanks of larger volcanoes. Paricutin in Mexico is a famous cinder cone. It appeared suddenly in a farmer's cornfield in 1943. Within a year, it had grown to over 300 meters tall.

Lava domes form when thick, viscous lava erupts slowly and piles up around the vent. The lava is too sticky to flow far. It builds a rounded, bulging mound. Lava domes can be dangerous. They are unstable. If the dome collapses, it can trigger pyroclastic flows. A lava dome grew inside the crater of Mount St. Helens after its 1980 eruption.

Calderas are not a type of volcano but a type of volcanic feature. A caldera is a large, basin-shaped depression that forms when a volcano's magma chamber empties during an eruption. The ground above the emptied chamber collapses inward. Calderas can be enormous. Yellowstone Caldera in Wyoming is about 72 kilometers long. It formed during a massive eruption roughly 640,000 years ago. The entire Yellowstone region sits on a supervolcano. A supervolcano is a volcano capable of producing an eruption of magnitude 8 on the Volcanic Explosivity Index, ejecting more than 1,000 cubic kilometers of material.

Each type of volcano tells a story about the magma that feeds it. Runny magma builds wide shields. Sticky magma builds steep cones. The form follows the physics.
