# Inside the Earth

Earth has layers. Understanding these layers is essential to understanding volcanoes. The material that erupts from volcanoes comes from deep inside the planet.

The outermost layer is the crust. The crust is the thin, solid shell of rock on Earth's surface. It is the ground you stand on. There are two types of crust. Continental crust forms the land. It is thicker, averaging about 35 kilometers. Oceanic crust forms the ocean floor. It is thinner, averaging about 7 kilometers. Both types of crust are solid rock, but they differ in composition and density. Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance.

Beneath the crust lies the mantle. The mantle is a thick layer of rock that extends about 2,900 kilometers deep. The mantle is not liquid. Most of it is solid. But it is so hot and under such pressure that it flows very slowly over long periods. This slow flow is called convection. Convection is the movement of material caused by differences in temperature. Hotter material rises. Cooler material sinks. This cycle creates currents in the mantle that move the tectonic plates above.

The upper part of the mantle, just below the crust, contains a zone called the asthenosphere. The asthenosphere is a partially molten, soft layer of rock. Partially molten means some of the rock has melted while the rest remains solid. The tectonic plates sit on top of the asthenosphere and slide over it. The asthenosphere is where most magma originates. Magma is molten rock below Earth's surface. When magma reaches the surface, it is called lava.

Below the mantle is the outer core. The outer core is a layer of liquid metal, mostly iron and nickel. It extends from about 2,900 kilometers to 5,100 kilometers deep. The temperature here ranges from about 4,400 to 6,100 degrees Celsius. The movement of this liquid metal generates Earth's magnetic field.

At the very center is the inner core. The inner core is a solid ball of iron and nickel. It is solid despite temperatures around 5,500 degrees Celsius because the pressure is so extreme. The pressure at the center of Earth is about 3.6 million times the pressure at the surface.

Heat flows from the core through the mantle toward the surface. This heat drives everything. It powers the convection currents in the mantle. It moves the tectonic plates. It melts rock into magma. It pushes magma upward through cracks and weak points in the crust. When that magma reaches the surface, a volcano erupts.

Earth is not a cold, dead rock. It is an active, hot planet. Its interior churns and flows. Volcanoes are the visible proof of the heat and motion hidden beneath our feet.
