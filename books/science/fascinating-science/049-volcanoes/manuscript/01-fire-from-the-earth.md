# Fire from the Earth

A volcano is an opening in Earth's surface where molten rock, gas, and ash escape from below. The word volcano comes from Vulcan, the Roman god of fire. Volcanoes are not rare. There are about 1,500 potentially active volcanoes on land. Many more exist beneath the ocean.

A volcanic eruption is one of the most powerful events on Earth. An eruption is the release of material from inside a volcano. Some eruptions are gentle. Lava flows slowly down the slope. Lava is molten rock that has reached the surface. Other eruptions are violent. They blast rock, ash, and gas high into the sky. The largest eruptions reshape entire landscapes.

Volcanoes exist because Earth is hot inside. The deeper you go, the hotter it gets. At Earth's center, temperatures reach about 5,500 degrees Celsius. That is roughly the temperature of the surface of the sun. This internal heat drives the movement of rock and the eruption of volcanoes.

Humans have lived alongside volcanoes for all of history. Ancient civilizations built cities on volcanic slopes. Volcanic soil is rich in minerals. Minerals are natural, non-living substances with specific chemical compositions. Plants grow well in volcanic soil. Farmers have always known this. The danger of eruption is the price for fertile land.

Volcanoes are not confined to Earth. Mars has the largest known volcano in the solar system. It is called Olympus Mons. It stands about 22 kilometers tall. That is nearly three times the height of Mount Everest. Jupiter's moon Io is the most volcanically active body in the solar system. Its surface is constantly reshaped by eruptions. Volcanoes exist wherever a rocky body has enough internal heat.

On Earth, volcanoes are not randomly distributed. They cluster along specific belts and zones. The most famous is the Ring of Fire. The Ring of Fire is a horseshoe-shaped band of volcanoes and earthquake zones that borders the Pacific Ocean. About 75 percent of Earth's active volcanoes sit along this ring. The Ring of Fire exists because of the movement of tectonic plates. Tectonic plates are the massive slabs of rock that make up Earth's outer layer.

Volcanoes are destructive. They bury cities. They darken skies. They change climates. But volcanoes also create. They build islands. They enrich soil. They release gases that form the atmosphere. The story of volcanoes is the story of Earth itself.
