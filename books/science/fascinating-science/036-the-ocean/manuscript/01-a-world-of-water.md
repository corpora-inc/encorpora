# A World of Water

The ocean is a single, connected body of salt water that covers most of Earth. It stretches from pole to pole. It touches every continent. The ocean holds about 97 percent of all the water on the planet. Only 3 percent of Earth's water is fresh water. Fresh water means water without significant salt.

We give different parts of the ocean different names. The Pacific Ocean is the largest. It covers more area than all the land on Earth combined. The Atlantic Ocean is the second largest. The Indian Ocean is the third. The Southern Ocean surrounds Antarctica. The Arctic Ocean sits at the top of the world, mostly covered in ice.

These names are human inventions. The water does not stop at the borders we draw on maps. A drop of water in the Atlantic can travel to the Indian Ocean. It can reach the Pacific. It can circle the globe. The ocean is one system.

The ocean is old. It formed more than four billion years ago. Earth was young and hot. Volcanoes released enormous amounts of water vapor into the atmosphere. Water vapor is water in its gas form. As Earth cooled, the vapor condensed. To condense means to change from gas to liquid. Rain fell for millions of years. The rain filled the lowest places on Earth's surface. Those low places became the ocean basins. A basin is a large, bowl-shaped depression.

The ocean is not flat. Its floor has mountains taller than anything on land. It has valleys deeper than any canyon. The deepest point is the Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench. A trench is a long, narrow depression in the ocean floor. The Challenger Deep reaches nearly 11,000 meters below the surface. Mount Everest could fit inside it with room to spare.

The average depth of the ocean is about 3,688 meters. Depth means the distance from the surface to the bottom. Most of the ocean floor has never been seen by human eyes. We have better maps of Mars than we have of the ocean floor.

The ocean is not just water. It is a habitat. A habitat is a place where organisms live. The ocean contains the largest habitat on Earth. Life began in the ocean. Every major group of animals has members that live in the ocean. From bacteria too small to see to whales longer than buses, the ocean holds life at every scale.

The ocean shapes our weather. It stores heat from the sun. It moves that heat around the planet. It produces much of the oxygen we breathe. It absorbs carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Carbon dioxide is a gas that traps heat. The ocean is not separate from us. We depend on it every day.
