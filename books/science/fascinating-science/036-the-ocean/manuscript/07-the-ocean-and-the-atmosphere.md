# The Ocean and the Atmosphere

The ocean and the atmosphere are locked in a constant exchange. The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding Earth. The ocean and the atmosphere trade heat, water, and gases every second of every day. This exchange drives weather and shapes the climate.

The ocean absorbs heat from the sun. Water has a high heat capacity. Heat capacity is the ability of a substance to absorb and store thermal energy. Water absorbs heat slowly and releases it slowly. Land heats up fast and cools down fast. This difference is why coastal areas have milder climates than inland areas. The ocean acts as a thermal buffer. A buffer is something that reduces the impact of change.

Evaporation connects the ocean to the water cycle. The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between the ocean, the atmosphere, and the land. The sun heats the ocean surface. Water molecules escape into the air as water vapor. This is evaporation. The vapor rises, cools, and condenses into clouds. Clouds produce rain. Rain falls on land and flows back to the ocean through rivers. The cycle repeats endlessly. The ocean is the engine of this cycle. About 86 percent of all evaporation on Earth comes from the ocean.

The ocean produces storms. Hurricanes form over warm ocean water. A hurricane is a large, rotating storm with strong winds and heavy rain. Warm water heats the air above it. The warm, moist air rises quickly. More air rushes in to replace it. The Coriolis effect causes the system to spin. If the water is warm enough and conditions are right, the system intensifies into a hurricane. The warmer the water, the stronger the potential storm.

The ocean absorbs carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas. A greenhouse gas traps heat in the atmosphere. The ocean absorbs about 25 percent of the carbon dioxide that humans release by burning fossil fuels. This absorption slows the rate of atmospheric warming.

But absorbing carbon dioxide has a cost. When carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater, it forms carbonic acid. This process is called ocean acidification. Acidification means the water becomes more acidic. More acidic water makes it harder for organisms to build shells and skeletons from calcium carbonate. Coral, shellfish, and some plankton are especially vulnerable.

The ocean also absorbs excess heat caused by the greenhouse effect. More than 90 percent of the extra heat trapped by greenhouse gases goes into the ocean. This causes the ocean to expand. Warm water takes up more space than cold water. This thermal expansion contributes to sea level rise. Melting glaciers and ice sheets add more water, raising sea levels further.

The ocean is not a passive bystander in Earth's climate system. It is an active participant. It stores heat, moves it around the planet, absorbs gases, and generates storms. What happens in the atmosphere affects the ocean. What happens in the ocean affects the atmosphere. They are one system.
