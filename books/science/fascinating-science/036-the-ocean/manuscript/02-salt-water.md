# Salt Water

Ocean water is salty. The salt is mostly sodium chloride. Sodium chloride is the same chemical found in table salt. If you could remove all the salt from the ocean and spread it over the land, it would form a layer more than 150 meters thick.

On average, every kilogram of ocean water contains about 35 grams of dissolved salt. Dissolved means mixed into the water so completely that you cannot see the individual crystals. Scientists call this measurement salinity. Salinity is the amount of salt in a given quantity of water.

Where does the salt come from? Rain falls on land. The rain is slightly acidic. Acidic means it contains substances that can dissolve minerals. The rain breaks down rocks. It carries the dissolved minerals into rivers. Rivers carry the minerals to the ocean. This process has been happening for billions of years.

Salt also enters the ocean from below. Hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor release minerals directly into the water. A hydrothermal vent is an opening in the seafloor where hot, mineral-rich water escapes from deep inside Earth. These vents add iron, manganese, and other elements to the ocean.

The ocean does not keep getting saltier. Salt leaves the ocean too. Some salt gets trapped in sediment on the ocean floor. Sediment is material that settles to the bottom. Some salt crystallizes when shallow seas evaporate. To evaporate means to change from liquid to gas. The salt that enters and the salt that leaves roughly balance over long periods.

Salinity is not the same everywhere. Near the equator, heavy rain dilutes the surface water. To dilute means to make less concentrated. Near the poles, freezing sea ice leaves salt behind, making the remaining water saltier. In enclosed seas like the Red Sea, strong evaporation makes the water very salty. In areas where large rivers meet the ocean, the water is less salty. These areas are called estuaries.

Salinity affects ocean life. Most marine organisms are adapted to a specific range of salinity. A sudden change can be harmful or fatal. Some organisms, like salmon, can survive in both salt water and fresh water. This ability is rare.

Salinity also affects how water moves. Saltier water is denser. Dense means heavier per unit of volume. Dense water sinks below less dense water. This difference in density drives deep ocean currents. A current is a continuous flow of water in a particular direction. The salt in the ocean is not just a chemical fact. It is a force that shapes how the entire ocean circulates.
