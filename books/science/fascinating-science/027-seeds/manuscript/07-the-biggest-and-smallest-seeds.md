# The Biggest and Smallest Seeds

Seeds come in an astonishing range of sizes. The biggest seed in the world belongs to the coco de mer palm. The coco de mer grows on the Seychelles islands in the Indian Ocean. Its seed can weigh up to 25 kilograms. That is about 55 pounds. It is roughly the size of a human torso. The seed takes six to seven years to mature on the tree. It takes another two years to germinate.

The coco de mer seed is a single giant nut enclosed in a hard shell. It is the largest single-seeded fruit in the plant kingdom. The tree produces these enormous seeds because they contain a massive food reserve. The seedling can grow in the deep shade of the forest floor for years before it reaches sunlight. That stored food keeps it alive.

At the other extreme are orchid seeds. Orchid seeds are the smallest seeds in the world. A single orchid seed weighs about one millionth of a gram. You could fit thousands of orchid seeds on the head of a pin. An orchid pod can contain up to three million seeds.

Orchid seeds are so small because they contain almost no food reserve. There is no endosperm. The embryo is just a tiny ball of cells. How does it survive? Orchid seeds depend on fungi. When an orchid seed lands in the right spot, it forms a partnership with a specific soil fungus. The fungus provides nutrients to the seedling. Without this fungal partner, the orchid seed cannot germinate. This partnership is called mycorrhiza. Mycorrhiza means "fungus root."

Between these extremes, the variety is vast. A coconut is a large seed, weighing about 1.5 kilograms. An avocado pit is a seed about the size of a golf ball. A sesame seed weighs about 3 milligrams. A poppy seed weighs about 0.3 milligrams.

Size reflects strategy. Large seeds invest heavily in each offspring. They provide a big food supply. The seedling has a strong start. But the parent can only produce a few. Small seeds invest little in each offspring. The seedling is fragile. But the parent produces millions. Quantity compensates for vulnerability.

Acorns are medium-sized seeds. An oak tree may produce 70,000 acorns in a good year. Squirrels eat many. Deer eat some. Insects bore into others. But a few survive. A few are enough.

There is no single best seed size. Each size is an answer to a different set of challenges. The coco de mer thrives with enormous seeds. The orchid thrives with seeds like dust. Both strategies work. Both have persisted for millions of years.
