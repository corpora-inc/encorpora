# Seeds Feed the World

Human civilization depends on seeds. Agriculture began when people learned to plant seeds deliberately. This happened about 10,000 years ago in the Fertile Crescent. The Fertile Crescent is a region in the Middle East. Early farmers planted wheat and barley. They saved seeds from the best plants and planted them the next season. This practice is called selective breeding.

Selective breeding changed seeds over thousands of years. Wild wheat has small seeds that shatter and fall to the ground easily. Shattering helps wild plants disperse their seeds. But for farmers, shattering is a problem. Seeds that fall to the ground are hard to collect. Early farmers chose plants whose seeds stayed on the stalk. Over generations, domesticated wheat developed seeds that hold tight until harvest.

Corn tells a similar story. Modern corn descends from a wild grass called teosinte. Teosinte has tiny ears with fewer than a dozen small, hard kernels. Through thousands of years of selective breeding, farmers transformed teosinte into the large, full ears of corn we know today. A modern ear of corn has about 800 kernels.

Three crops provide most of humanity's calories: wheat, rice, and corn. All three are seeds. Wheat feeds Europe, the Middle East, and North America. Rice feeds Asia. Corn feeds the Americas and is used as animal feed worldwide. Together, these three crops grow on about 500 million hectares of farmland. A hectare is about the size of two football fields.

Seeds are nutritious because they are designed to fuel a growing plant. They are packed with carbohydrates for energy. They contain proteins for building new cells. They contain oils for concentrated energy storage. They contain vitamins and minerals. When you eat bread, pasta, rice, oatmeal, or tortillas, you are eating seeds.

Legumes are another crucial group of seed crops. Legumes include beans, lentils, chickpeas, soybeans, and peanuts. Legumes are especially rich in protein. In many parts of the world, legumes are the primary source of protein in the diet.

Seed banks protect the future. A seed bank is a facility that stores seeds from thousands of plant varieties. The Svalbard Global Seed Vault is built into a mountain on a remote Arctic island in Norway. It holds over one million seed samples from around the world. If a crop variety is lost to disease, drought, or war, seeds from the vault can restore it.

Climate change threatens many crop varieties. Temperatures are shifting. Rainfall patterns are changing. Scientists use seed banks to preserve genetic diversity. Genetic diversity is the variety of genes within a species. More diversity means more options for breeding crops that can handle new conditions.

Seeds built civilization. They continue to sustain it. Every meal traces back to a seed planted in the ground.
