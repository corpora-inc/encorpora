# Germination

Germination is the moment a seed comes to life. It is the process by which a seed begins to grow into a new plant. The dormant embryo wakes up and starts developing.

Germination begins when the seed absorbs water. This process is called imbibition. Imbibition means "to drink in." The seed swells as water enters. The seed coat softens. Enzymes inside the seed activate. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a chemical reaction. The enzymes break down the stored food into sugars. These sugars provide energy for growth.

The radicle is the first structure to emerge. The radicle is the embryonic root. It pushes through the softened seed coat and grows downward into the soil. It anchors the seedling. It begins absorbing water and minerals from the soil. A seedling is a young plant that has just emerged from a seed.

Next, the shoot emerges. It grows upward toward light. In dicots, the hypocotyl often curves into a hook shape. This hook protects the delicate plumule as it pushes through the soil. Once the hook reaches the surface, it straightens. The first leaves unfold.

In monocots, a protective sheath called a coleoptile covers the emerging shoot. The coleoptile pushes through the soil first. The leaves emerge from inside it once it reaches the surface.

The first true leaves are different from the cotyledons. They are the first leaves that look like the adult plant's leaves. Once these leaves open and begin photosynthesis, the plant can make its own food. Photosynthesis is the process of using light energy to make sugar from carbon dioxide and water. The seedling is now independent.

Not every seed germinates successfully. Many seeds land in places where conditions are wrong. Some land on rock. Some land in deep shade. Some are eaten by animals before they can sprout. The odds are against any individual seed. That is why plants produce so many.

An orchid can produce millions of seeds from a single pod. Most will fail. But a few will find the right spot. A few will germinate. A few will grow into adult plants that produce their own seeds.

Germination is a race. The seedling must establish roots and leaves before its food supply runs out. Speed matters. The stored food in the endosperm or cotyledons is limited. Once the seedling can photosynthesize, it has won the race.

Germination is one of the most dramatic events in nature. A hard, dry, seemingly lifeless object transforms into a living, growing plant.
