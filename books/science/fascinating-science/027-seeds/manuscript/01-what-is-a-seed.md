# What Is a Seed

A seed is a tiny package that contains a new plant. It is the starting point for most plants on Earth. Inside every seed is everything needed to grow a new plant. A seed is a remarkable invention of nature.

Seeds come from flowering plants and cone-bearing plants. Flowering plants are called angiosperms. Angiosperm comes from Greek words meaning "enclosed seed." Their seeds develop inside a fruit. Cone-bearing plants are called gymnosperms. Gymnosperm means "naked seed." Their seeds sit exposed on the scales of cones. Pine trees are gymnosperms. Apple trees are angiosperms.

Not all plants make seeds. Ferns reproduce with spores. A spore is a tiny cell that can grow into a new plant. Mosses also use spores. But seed plants dominate the Earth today. They are the most successful group of land plants.

Seeds give plants a major advantage. A spore is a single cell. It is fragile. It needs moist conditions to survive. A seed is much more complex. It contains a baby plant, a food supply, and a protective coat. This combination makes seeds tough. They can survive drought. They can survive cold. They can travel long distances.

The oldest known seeds in the fossil record are about 365 million years old. They come from the late Devonian period. The Devonian period is a span of geologic time. These ancient seeds were simple. Modern seeds are far more advanced.

Humans depend on seeds. Wheat, rice, and corn are seeds. They feed billions of people. Beans and lentils are seeds. Sunflower seeds and peanuts are seeds. Coffee comes from seeds. Chocolate comes from cacao seeds. Cotton fibers grow from seeds.

Farmers plant seeds to grow crops. Gardeners plant seeds to grow flowers and vegetables. Foresters plant seeds to grow trees. Every great forest begins with seeds scattered on the ground.

A single seed weighs almost nothing. Yet it carries the blueprint for an entire organism. An oak tree that stands 30 meters tall and lives for 500 years begins as an acorn. An acorn is the seed of an oak. It fits in the palm of your hand.

Seeds are small, but their impact is enormous. They shaped the history of life on land. They shape human civilization today.
