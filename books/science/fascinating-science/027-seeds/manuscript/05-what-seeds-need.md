# What Seeds Need

Seeds need specific conditions to germinate. The three most important factors are water, temperature, and oxygen. Without all three, the seed remains dormant.

Water is the trigger. A dry seed is inactive. Its metabolism is nearly zero. Metabolism is the sum of all chemical reactions in a living thing. When water enters the seed, metabolism restarts. Enzymes begin working. Cells begin dividing. Growth begins. Without water, none of this happens.

Temperature matters because enzymes work best within a specific range. Most seeds germinate in temperatures between 15 and 30 degrees Celsius. Some seeds need cool temperatures. Lettuce seeds germinate best below 25 degrees. Some seeds need warmth. Pepper seeds need at least 20 degrees. Some seeds need a period of cold before they will germinate. This cold requirement is called stratification. Stratification ensures the seed does not sprout in autumn only to be killed by winter.

Oxygen is essential for cellular respiration. Cellular respiration is the process by which cells convert sugar into energy. A germinating seed burns through its food reserves rapidly. It needs oxygen to release the energy stored in those reserves. Seeds buried too deep in waterlogged soil may not get enough oxygen. They fail to germinate.

Some seeds have an additional requirement: light. Certain species need light to germinate. Their seeds are usually tiny. They land on the soil surface. Light tells them they are in a good position. If they were buried deep, no light would reach them. They would stay dormant. Lettuce and some grasses require light. Other seeds germinate better in darkness. Large seeds with big food reserves can afford to sprout underground.

Soil quality matters too. Seeds need soil that holds moisture but also drains well. Soil that stays too wet rots the seed. Soil that is too compacted blocks the root from penetrating. Good soil has a mix of mineral particles and organic matter. Organic matter is decomposed plant and animal material.

Farmers and gardeners pay close attention to these conditions. They plant seeds at the right depth. They water them carefully. They time their planting to match the right temperature. They prepare the soil. Every decision is based on what seeds need.

In nature, there is no farmer. Seeds must find the right conditions on their own. This is why plants produce seeds in vast quantities. Some will land in perfect spots. Most will not. Natural selection favors seeds that are well-suited to their environment. Natural selection is the process by which organisms with favorable traits survive and reproduce more successfully.
