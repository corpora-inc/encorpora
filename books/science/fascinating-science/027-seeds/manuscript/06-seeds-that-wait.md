# Seeds That Wait

Some seeds germinate within days. Others wait for years. Some wait for centuries. The ability of a seed to remain alive but inactive is called dormancy. Dormancy is a survival strategy.

A dormant seed is not dead. It is alive but in a state of suspended animation. Its metabolism is extremely slow. It uses almost no energy. It breathes so little that scientists can barely detect it. But the embryo inside is alive, waiting.

There are different types of dormancy. Physical dormancy means the seed coat is too hard or waterproof to let water in. The seed cannot germinate until the coat is broken or worn down. Fire can crack seed coats. Freezing and thawing can crack them. Passing through an animal's gut can erode them. Acid in the stomach weakens the coat enough for water to enter.

Chemical dormancy means the seed contains substances that prevent germination. These chemicals must be washed away by rain or broken down over time. Some desert seeds have chemical inhibitors in their coats. A light rain is not enough. Only a heavy, soaking rain washes the inhibitors away. A heavy rain means the soil will stay moist long enough for the seedling to establish itself.

Some seeds require specific environmental cues. Smoke triggers germination in certain species. These plants evolved in fire-prone environments. After a wildfire, the soil is cleared of competing plants. Nutrients are abundant. Sunlight reaches the ground. Smoke chemicals tell the seed that conditions are right.

The seed bank is the collection of dormant seeds in the soil. Every patch of soil contains seeds at various depths, from various species, in various stages of dormancy. The seed bank can persist for decades. When a forest is cleared or a field is abandoned, seeds from the seed bank sprout. Plants appear that nobody planted. They were waiting in the soil all along.

Scientists have germinated ancient seeds. In 2005, researchers germinated a date palm seed that was about 2,000 years old. It was found at the ancient fortress of Masada in Israel. The seed sprouted and grew into a healthy tree. They named it Methuselah.

In the Arctic, scientists found lupine seeds frozen in permafrost. Permafrost is ground that stays frozen year-round. These seeds were estimated to be about 10,000 years old. Some of them germinated.

Dormancy is patience encoded in biology. A seed waits until the world is ready. Then it grows.
