# The Life of the Sun

Stars are born, they live, and they die. The Sun is about halfway through its life. It has been shining for about 4.6 billion years. It will continue to shine for about another five billion years.

The Sun was born in a nebula. A nebula is a giant cloud of gas and dust in space. About 4.6 billion years ago, part of a nebula began to collapse. Collapse means to fall inward. Gravity pulled the gas and dust closer together. As the material compressed, it grew hotter. The center became so hot and dense that nuclear fusion began. Hydrogen atoms fused into helium atoms. The Sun ignited. It began to shine.

The leftover gas and dust around the young Sun formed a spinning disk. Over millions of years, material in this disk clumped together to form the planets, moons, and asteroids of our solar system. Earth formed from this same disk of material about 4.5 billion years ago.

Right now, the Sun is in a stage called the main sequence. The main sequence is the longest stage in a star's life. During this stage, a star steadily fuses hydrogen into helium in its core. The outward push of energy from fusion balances the inward pull of gravity. This balance keeps the Sun stable.

The Sun slowly gets brighter over time. It is about 30 percent brighter today than it was when it first formed. This happens because as helium builds up in the core, the core slowly contracts and heats up. Fusion speeds up. The Sun produces more energy. In another billion years, the Sun will be bright enough to make Earth too hot for liquid water.

In about five billion years, the Sun will run out of hydrogen in its core. The core will shrink and heat up even more. Hydrogen fusion will continue in a shell around the core. The extra energy will cause the Sun's outer layers to expand enormously. The Sun will become a red giant. A red giant is a star that has swelled to many times its original size. The Sun's outer edge may reach the orbit of Earth.

After the red giant phase, the Sun will shed its outer layers. These layers will drift into space as a glowing shell of gas called a planetary nebula. The name is misleading. A planetary nebula has nothing to do with planets. It is just a shell of gas around a dying star.

The Sun's core will remain as a white dwarf. A white dwarf is a small, dense, hot remnant of a star. It is about the size of Earth but contains most of the Sun's original mass. A white dwarf no longer produces energy through fusion. It slowly cools over billions of years. Eventually, it will become a cold, dark object called a black dwarf. No black dwarfs exist yet. The universe is not old enough for any white dwarf to have fully cooled.

The Sun's life story is the story of all medium-sized stars. Born in a cloud, shining for billions of years, expanding into a giant, and fading into a quiet remnant. Our Sun is currently in its prime, and we are fortunate to live beside it during this stable, generous era.
