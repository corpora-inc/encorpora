# How Big Is the Sun

The Sun is enormous. Its diameter is about 864,000 miles. A diameter is the distance across the center of a circle or sphere. Earth's diameter is about 7,918 miles. You could line up 109 Earths across the face of the Sun.

Volume tells us how much space something takes up. The Sun's volume is so large that about 1.3 million Earths could fit inside it. Imagine filling a giant hollow Sun with tiny Earths. You would need more than a million of them.

The Sun's mass is about 333,000 times the mass of Earth. Mass is the amount of matter in an object. Matter is anything that takes up space and has weight. The Sun contains 99.86 percent of all the mass in the solar system. Everything else combined — all the planets, moons, asteroids, and comets — makes up less than one percent.

The Sun's gravity is powerful because it has so much mass. Gravity is a force that pulls objects toward each other. The more mass an object has, the stronger its gravity. The Sun's gravity holds the entire solar system together. It keeps Earth in orbit. An orbit is the curved path one object takes around another.

If you could stand on the surface of the Sun, you would weigh about 28 times more than you weigh on Earth. A person who weighs 100 pounds on Earth would weigh 2,800 pounds on the Sun. Of course, no one could actually stand on the Sun. It is far too hot, and it has no solid surface.

Despite its size, the Sun is a medium-sized star. Astronomers call it a yellow dwarf star. An astronomer is a scientist who studies space. A yellow dwarf is a star of average size and temperature. Some stars are much larger. The star Betelgeuse is about 700 times wider than the Sun. Other stars are smaller. A red dwarf star can be one-tenth the size of the Sun.

The Sun's size will change over time. In about five billion years, it will grow into a red giant. A red giant is a star that has expanded to many times its original size. The Sun will become so large that it may swallow Mercury, Venus, and possibly Earth. But that is billions of years in the future.

The Sun is big enough to dominate our solar system. Its size creates the gravity that keeps planets in their orbits. Its size produces the energy that lights and warms our world. Understanding the Sun's size helps us understand our place in the universe.
