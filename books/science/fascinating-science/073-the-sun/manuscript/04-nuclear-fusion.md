# Nuclear Fusion

The Sun makes energy through nuclear fusion. Nuclear fusion is the process of combining small atomic nuclei to form larger ones. This process releases enormous amounts of energy. It is the engine that powers the Sun.

Fusion happens in the Sun's core. The core is the center of the Sun. The temperature there is about 27 million degrees Fahrenheit. The pressure is about 250 billion times the air pressure on Earth's surface. Pressure is the force applied to an area. At these extreme conditions, hydrogen nuclei collide and fuse together.

A hydrogen nucleus is a single proton. In the Sun's core, protons move very fast because of the intense heat. They crash into each other with great force. Normally, protons repel each other. Repel means to push away. Protons are all positively charged, and positive charges push apart. But at extreme temperatures and pressures, protons get close enough for the strong nuclear force to take over. The strong nuclear force is the most powerful force in nature. It holds atomic nuclei together.

The Sun uses a process called the proton-proton chain. In this chain, four hydrogen nuclei combine to form one helium nucleus. The helium nucleus has two protons and two neutrons. Some of the hydrogen's mass converts into energy during this process. This follows Einstein's famous equation, E equals mc squared. E stands for energy. M stands for mass. C stands for the speed of light. A small amount of mass creates a huge amount of energy because the speed of light is such a large number.

Every second, the Sun converts about 600 million tons of hydrogen into about 596 million tons of helium. The missing four million tons become energy. This energy takes the form of light and heat. Four million tons of matter converting to energy every second sounds like a lot. But the Sun is so massive that it can keep this up for billions of years.

The energy produced in the core does not reach the surface quickly. It takes thousands of years for energy to travel from the core to the surface. Photons bounce around inside the Sun, absorbed and re-emitted over and over. A photon is a particle of light. This zone where photons bounce is called the radiative zone.

Above the radiative zone is the convective zone. In this zone, hot plasma rises toward the surface and cooler plasma sinks back down. Convection is the movement of heat through a fluid by the motion of the fluid itself. This is similar to water boiling in a pot. Hot water rises and cool water sinks.

Nuclear fusion in the Sun creates the light and heat that sustain life on Earth. Scientists are working to create fusion reactors on Earth. A reactor is a machine that controls a reaction. If humans can master fusion, we could have a nearly unlimited source of clean energy.
