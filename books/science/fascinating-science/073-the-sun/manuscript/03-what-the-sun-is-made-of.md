# What the Sun Is Made Of

The Sun is made mostly of two elements. An element is a basic substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances. The two main elements in the Sun are hydrogen and helium.

Hydrogen makes up about 73 percent of the Sun's mass. Hydrogen is the simplest element. Its atom has just one proton and one electron. A proton is a tiny particle with a positive electric charge. An electron is a tiny particle with a negative electric charge. Hydrogen is also the most common element in the universe.

Helium makes up about 25 percent of the Sun's mass. Helium is the second simplest element. Its atom has two protons, two neutrons, and two electrons. A neutron is a tiny particle with no electric charge. Helium was first discovered in the Sun before it was found on Earth. The word helium comes from Helios, the Greek word for the Sun.

The remaining two percent of the Sun is made of heavier elements. These include oxygen, carbon, neon, and iron. These elements exist in tiny amounts compared to hydrogen and helium. But even tiny amounts matter. Scientists study these trace elements to learn about the Sun's history.

Scientists know what the Sun is made of because of spectroscopy. Spectroscopy is the study of light. When light passes through a prism, it spreads into a rainbow of colors. A prism is a glass shape that splits light. Each element produces a unique pattern of lines in this rainbow. These lines are like fingerprints. Scientists match the lines to known elements.

The Sun's material exists as plasma. Plasma is gas that is so hot that atoms lose their electrons. The electrons separate from the nuclei. A nucleus is the center of an atom. It contains protons and neutrons. In plasma, electrons and nuclei move freely. Plasma conducts electricity. It responds to magnetic fields. A magnetic field is an invisible area of force around a magnet.

The Sun's composition changes over time. Deep inside the Sun, hydrogen turns into helium through nuclear fusion. Nuclear fusion is a process where small atoms combine to form larger atoms. The Sun converts about 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second. Over billions of years, the Sun slowly uses up its hydrogen fuel.

The Sun is a giant ball of mostly hydrogen and helium in the state of plasma. Its composition is simple, but the physics that governs it is extraordinary. Two basic elements, extreme heat, and powerful gravity create the engine that powers our solar system.
