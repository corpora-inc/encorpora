# The Surface of the Sun

The Sun does not have a solid surface like Earth. It is made entirely of plasma. But it does have a visible surface called the photosphere. The photosphere is the layer of the Sun that we can see. The word photosphere means "sphere of light."

The temperature of the photosphere is about 10,000 degrees Fahrenheit. This sounds extremely hot. But compared to the Sun's core, the photosphere is cool. The core is about 27 million degrees Fahrenheit. Temperature decreases as you move from the core to the surface.

The photosphere has a grainy texture called granulation. Each grain is called a granule. A granule is the top of a column of rising hot plasma. Each granule is about 600 miles across. That is roughly the size of Texas. Hot plasma rises in the center of each granule. Cooler plasma sinks at the edges. The entire pattern of granules changes every few minutes.

Sunspots are dark patches on the photosphere. A sunspot is a region where the temperature is lower than the surrounding area. Sunspots appear dark because they are cooler, but they are still extremely hot — about 6,500 degrees Fahrenheit. Strong magnetic fields cause sunspots. A magnetic field is an invisible area of force. The Sun's magnetic fields can be thousands of times stronger than Earth's magnetic field.

Sunspots come and go in a cycle of about eleven years. This is called the solar cycle. At the peak of the cycle, the Sun has many sunspots. At the low point, it has few or none. The solar cycle affects space weather. Space weather is the condition of the space environment between the Sun and Earth.

Above the photosphere is the chromosphere. The chromosphere is a thin layer of the Sun's atmosphere. It appears as a reddish glow during a total solar eclipse. A total solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes directly between the Sun and Earth, blocking the photosphere from view.

Above the chromosphere is the corona. The corona is the outermost layer of the Sun's atmosphere. It extends millions of miles into space. The corona is extremely hot — over one million degrees Fahrenheit. Scientists are puzzled by this. The corona is hotter than the photosphere even though it is farther from the core. This mystery is called the coronal heating problem.

Solar flares erupt from the Sun's surface. A solar flare is a sudden burst of energy and light. Flares happen when magnetic fields on the Sun snap and reconnect. They release energy equivalent to millions of nuclear bombs. Solar flares can affect radio communications on Earth.

The Sun's surface is a dynamic, ever-changing place. It boils with granules, darkens with sunspots, and erupts with flares. It is violent and beautiful at the same time.
