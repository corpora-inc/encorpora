# Sunlight

Sunlight is the light and energy that travels from the Sun to Earth. It takes about eight minutes and twenty seconds to make the journey. Sunlight travels at the speed of light, which is about 186,000 miles per second. Nothing in the universe travels faster.

Sunlight is a form of electromagnetic radiation. Electromagnetic radiation is energy that travels in waves. These waves come in many types. The full range of types is called the electromagnetic spectrum. The spectrum includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared light, visible light, ultraviolet light, X-rays, and gamma rays. The Sun produces all of these types.

Visible light is the part of the spectrum that human eyes can see. It includes all the colors of the rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and violet. White sunlight is actually a mix of all these colors. You can see this when sunlight passes through a prism or through raindrops to make a rainbow.

Each color of light has a different wavelength. A wavelength is the distance between two peaks of a wave. Red light has the longest wavelength that we can see. Violet light has the shortest. Beyond violet is ultraviolet light. Beyond red is infrared light. Our eyes cannot see ultraviolet or infrared, but our instruments can detect them.

Ultraviolet light from the Sun can damage skin. It causes sunburn. Over time, too much ultraviolet light can cause skin cancer. Earth's atmosphere blocks most ultraviolet light. The ozone layer is a part of the atmosphere that absorbs much of this harmful radiation. Ozone is a molecule made of three oxygen atoms.

Infrared light from the Sun carries heat. You feel this heat when sunlight warms your face. Infrared radiation makes up about half of the energy in sunlight. Objects that absorb sunlight get warm because they absorb infrared radiation.

The atmosphere affects sunlight before it reaches the ground. Some light reflects back into space. Some gets scattered by molecules in the air. Scattering means the light bounces off in many directions. Blue light scatters more than red light. This is why the sky looks blue during the day. At sunrise and sunset, sunlight passes through more atmosphere. The blue light scatters away, and the sky turns red and orange.

Plants use sunlight to make food through photosynthesis. Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy. Plants absorb red and blue light. They reflect green light, which is why most plants look green. Without sunlight, photosynthesis cannot happen. Without photosynthesis, most life on Earth would not exist.

Sunlight is more than just brightness. It is a river of energy flowing from the Sun to Earth. It warms our planet, drives our weather, feeds our plants, and makes our world visible.
