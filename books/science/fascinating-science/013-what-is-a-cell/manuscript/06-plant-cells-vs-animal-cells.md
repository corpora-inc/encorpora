# Plant Cells vs Animal Cells

Plant cells and animal cells share many features. Both have a nucleus. Both have mitochondria. Both have a cell membrane. Both have cytoplasm filled with organelles. But plant cells have several structures that animal cells lack.

The most obvious difference is the cell wall. A cell wall is a rigid layer outside the cell membrane. It gives the plant cell its shape. It provides support and protection. The cell wall is made of cellulose. Cellulose is a tough carbohydrate. A carbohydrate is a molecule made of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. When you eat celery, you feel the crunch of cell walls.

Animal cells do not have cell walls. They have only the flexible cell membrane. This is why animal cells can change shape easily. A white blood cell can squeeze through tiny gaps. A muscle cell can contract. Plant cells cannot do these things.

Plant cells also have chloroplasts. A chloroplast is an organelle that captures light energy. Chloroplasts are green. They contain a pigment called chlorophyll. A pigment is a substance that absorbs certain colors of light. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light. It reflects green light. That is why plants look green.

Chloroplasts perform photosynthesis. Photosynthesis means "putting together with light." During photosynthesis, the plant takes in carbon dioxide and water. It uses light energy to combine them. It produces glucose and oxygen. The glucose is food for the plant. The oxygen is released into the air. Animals breathe this oxygen.

Plant cells usually have one large central vacuole. A vacuole is a storage sac. The central vacuole holds water, nutrients, and waste. When the vacuole is full of water, it pushes against the cell wall. This pressure makes the plant firm and upright. When the vacuole loses water, the plant wilts.

Animal cells may have small vacuoles. But they never have one large central vacuole like plant cells do.

Some organisms do not fit neatly into plant or animal categories. Fungi have cell walls, but their walls are made of chitin, not cellulose. Chitin is the same material in insect shells. Fungi do not have chloroplasts. They cannot photosynthesize.

Bacteria are different from both plant and animal cells. Bacteria have cell walls, but they lack a nucleus. Their DNA floats freely in the cytoplasm. Cells without a nucleus are called prokaryotic. Cells with a nucleus are called eukaryotic. Plant and animal cells are eukaryotic. Bacteria are prokaryotic.

These differences matter. They shape how each organism lives, grows, and survives.
