# The Cell Membrane

Every cell has an outer boundary. This boundary is the cell membrane. The cell membrane is a thin, flexible layer that surrounds the cell. It separates the inside of the cell from the outside world.

The cell membrane is made of lipids. Lipids are fats. The main lipids in the membrane are called phospholipids. A phospholipid has two parts. One part loves water. The other part hates water. The water-loving part is called hydrophilic. The water-hating part is called hydrophobic.

Phospholipids arrange themselves in two layers. This double layer is called a lipid bilayer. The water-loving heads face outward. They touch the watery environment. The water-hating tails face inward. They hide from water. This arrangement is very stable.

The cell membrane is not a solid wall. It is more like a sea of moving molecules. Scientists call it the fluid mosaic model. Fluid means it flows. Mosaic means it has many different pieces. Proteins float in the lipid bilayer like icebergs in an ocean.

Some proteins span the entire membrane. They form channels. A channel is a tiny tunnel. Water and small molecules pass through these channels. Other proteins sit on the surface. They act as receivers. They detect signals from other cells.

The cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell. This property is called selective permeability. Selective means it chooses. Permeability means the ability to let things through. Small molecules like oxygen and carbon dioxide pass through easily. Large molecules need help.

Some molecules move from areas of high concentration to areas of low concentration. Concentration means how much of a substance is in a space. This natural movement is called diffusion. Diffusion requires no energy.

Sometimes the cell needs to move molecules against the flow. This is called active transport. Active transport requires energy. The cell uses energy to pump molecules through special proteins.

Water also moves across the membrane. The movement of water across a membrane is called osmosis. Osmosis is a special type of diffusion. It keeps the cell balanced. Too much water, and the cell could burst. Too little, and it could shrivel.

The cell membrane is the gatekeeper. It decides what gets in and what stays out.
