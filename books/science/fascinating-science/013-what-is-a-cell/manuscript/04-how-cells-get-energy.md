# How Cells Get Energy

Every cell needs energy. Energy powers everything the cell does. It powers movement. It powers growth. It powers repair. Without energy, a cell dies.

The main energy source for most cells is a molecule called glucose. Glucose is a simple sugar. You get glucose from the food you eat. Your blood carries glucose to every cell in your body.

Cells break down glucose to release energy. This process is called cellular respiration. Respiration means breathing. Cellular respiration is like breathing at the cell level. The cell takes in glucose and oxygen. It releases carbon dioxide, water, and energy.

Most cellular respiration happens inside the mitochondria. Mitochondria are organelles shaped like beans. A single one is called a mitochondrion. The mitochondria have a smooth outer membrane and a folded inner membrane. The folds increase the surface area. More surface area means more space for chemical reactions.

Cellular respiration has three main stages. The first stage is called glycolysis. Glycolysis means "sugar splitting." It happens in the cytoplasm, not in the mitochondria. Glycolysis breaks one glucose molecule into two smaller molecules. It produces a small amount of energy.

The second stage is called the citric acid cycle. Some people call it the Krebs cycle. It was named after Hans Krebs, the scientist who described it. This stage happens inside the mitochondria. It breaks down the molecules from glycolysis even further. It releases carbon dioxide.

The third stage is called the electron transport chain. This stage also happens in the mitochondria. It produces the most energy. Oxygen is essential here. Without oxygen, this stage cannot run.

The energy from cellular respiration is stored in a molecule called ATP. ATP stands for adenosine triphosphate. Think of ATP as a tiny rechargeable battery. When the cell needs energy, it breaks apart an ATP molecule. The energy is released instantly. The cell makes and uses billions of ATP molecules every day.

Some organisms get energy without oxygen. This process is called fermentation. Yeast uses fermentation to make bread rise. Your muscle cells use fermentation during intense exercise when oxygen runs low. Fermentation produces much less energy than cellular respiration.

Mitochondria are sometimes called the powerhouses of the cell. Every movement you make depends on them.
