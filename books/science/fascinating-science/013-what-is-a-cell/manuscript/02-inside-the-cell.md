# Inside the Cell

A cell is like a tiny factory. It has many parts. Each part has a job. These parts are called organelles. An organelle is a small structure inside a cell that performs a specific function.

The nucleus is the control center of the cell. It is usually round. It sits near the middle of the cell. The nucleus contains DNA. DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. DNA is a long molecule that carries instructions. These instructions tell the cell what to do. They tell it how to grow. They tell it what proteins to make.

A protein is a molecule that does work inside the cell. Proteins build structures. Proteins speed up chemical reactions. Proteins carry messages. The cell makes thousands of different proteins.

The nucleus has its own covering. This covering is called the nuclear envelope. The nuclear envelope has tiny holes called pores. Molecules move in and out of the nucleus through these pores.

Around the nucleus is a thick, jelly-like fluid. This fluid is called cytoplasm. Cytoplasm fills most of the cell. All the organelles float in the cytoplasm. Cytoplasm is mostly water. It also contains salts, sugars, and other molecules.

The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of folded membranes. A membrane is a thin, flexible sheet. The endoplasmic reticulum helps make proteins and lipids. Lipids are fats. Some endoplasmic reticulum has tiny dots on its surface. These dots are called ribosomes. Ribosomes are the machines that build proteins.

The Golgi apparatus looks like a stack of flat pancakes. It receives proteins from the endoplasmic reticulum. It packages them. It labels them. Then it sends them where they need to go. Think of it as the cell's post office.

Lysosomes are the cleanup crew. A lysosome is a small sac filled with digestive chemicals. These chemicals break down waste. They break down worn-out parts. They break down invaders like bacteria. Without lysosomes, the cell would fill with garbage.

Every organelle matters. Remove one, and the cell cannot survive.
