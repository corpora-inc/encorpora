# The Building Block of Life

Every living thing is made of cells. A cell is the smallest unit of life. It is the building block of every organism. An organism is any living thing. You are an organism. A tree is an organism. A bacterium is an organism. A bacterium is a tiny living thing made of just one cell.

Some organisms have only one cell. We call them single-celled organisms. Bacteria are single-celled. Amoebas are single-celled. An amoeba is a tiny creature that lives in water. It finds food and moves around using just one cell.

Other organisms have many cells. A human body has about 37 trillion cells. A trillion is a thousand billion. That number is hard to imagine. If you counted one cell per second, it would take over a million years to count them all.

Cells are very small. Most cells are too small to see with your eyes alone. You need a microscope. A microscope is a tool that makes tiny things look bigger. The first useful microscope was built in the 1600s.

Robert Hooke was an English scientist. In 1665, he looked at a thin slice of cork through a microscope. Cork comes from the bark of a tree. He saw tiny box-shaped spaces. They reminded him of small rooms in a monastery. Monks call their small rooms cells. So Hooke called these tiny spaces cells. The name stuck.

A few years later, Antonie van Leeuwenhoek built better microscopes. He was Dutch. He was the first person to see living cells. He saw bacteria in water. He saw red blood cells. He called the tiny living things "animalcules." That means "little animals."

Scientists later developed cell theory. Cell theory has three main ideas. First, all living things are made of one or more cells. Second, the cell is the basic unit of life. Third, all cells come from other cells. No cell appears from nothing. Every cell has a parent cell.

Cell theory is one of the most important ideas in biology. Biology is the study of living things. Understanding cells helps us understand life itself.
