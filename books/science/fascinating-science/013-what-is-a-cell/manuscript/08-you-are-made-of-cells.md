# You Are Made of Cells

You began as a single cell. When a sperm cell met an egg cell, they fused into one cell. That single cell contained all the DNA needed to build your entire body. This first cell is called a zygote. A zygote is a fertilized egg.

The zygote divided. One cell became two. Two became four. Four became eight. The divisions continued. After about five days, there were roughly 100 cells. This tiny ball of cells is called a blastocyst. The blastocyst implanted in the uterus wall.

As divisions continued, cells began to specialize. To specialize means to take on a specific role. Some cells became skin cells. Some became nerve cells. Some became blood cells. This process is called differentiation. Differentiation means becoming different.

Every cell in your body contains the same DNA. A skin cell has the same genes as a brain cell. But each cell type uses only certain genes. A skin cell activates genes for making skin proteins. A brain cell activates genes for making brain proteins. The rest of the genes stay silent.

Your body has over 200 different types of cells. Red blood cells carry oxygen. They have no nucleus. They are shaped like tiny doughnuts. White blood cells fight infections. They can change shape to surround and destroy bacteria. Nerve cells are very long. Some nerve cells stretch from your spine to your toes.

Skin cells form a protective barrier. They live for about two to three weeks. Then they die and are replaced. You shed millions of skin cells every day. The dust in your house contains many dead skin cells.

Bone cells build and maintain your skeleton. They are embedded in a hard matrix of calcium and minerals. A matrix is a surrounding substance. Bone cells constantly rebuild bone tissue throughout your life.

Your cells are always working. Right now, your cells are digesting food. They are fighting germs. They are building new tissue. They are sending electrical signals through your brain. They are carrying oxygen through your blood.

Some of these processes can go wrong. When cells divide out of control, the result is cancer. Cancer is a disease of uncontrolled cell growth. The abnormal cells form masses called tumors. Understanding cells helps scientists develop treatments for cancer and many other diseases.

You are a community of trillions of cells. They cooperate. They communicate. They depend on each other. Every breath you take, every thought you have, every move you make starts with your cells.
