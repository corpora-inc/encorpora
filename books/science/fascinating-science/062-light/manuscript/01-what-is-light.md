# What Is Light

Light is energy you can see. It travels from a source to your eyes, and your brain turns it into an image of the world. Without light, there is no vision. Without light, there is no color. Light is one of the most fundamental things in the universe.

Light is a form of electromagnetic radiation. Electromagnetic radiation is energy that travels through space as waves. These waves do not need a medium. A medium is a substance that waves travel through, like air or water. Sound needs air. Light does not. It moves through the vacuum of space. A vacuum is a space with no matter in it. Sunlight crosses 150 million kilometers of empty space in about eight minutes.

Light behaves as both a wave and a particle. This is called wave-particle duality. As a wave, light has a wavelength and a frequency. Wavelength is the distance between two consecutive peaks of a wave. Frequency is the number of wave peaks that pass a point in one second. As a particle, light comes in discrete packets of energy called photons. A photon is the smallest unit of light. Each photon carries a specific amount of energy. The energy of a photon depends on its frequency. Higher frequency means higher energy.

The idea that light is made of photons comes from quantum physics. Quantum physics is the branch of science that studies matter and energy at the smallest scales. Albert Einstein proposed the photon concept in 1905. He showed that light striking a metal surface knocks electrons loose. Electrons are tiny, negatively charged particles. This photoelectric effect could only be explained if light came in individual energy packets.

Most of the light we experience comes from the sun. The sun is a star. Stars produce light through nuclear fusion. Nuclear fusion is the process of combining light atomic nuclei to form heavier ones, releasing enormous energy. In the sun's core, hydrogen atoms fuse into helium. This reaction releases photons. Those photons bounce around inside the sun for thousands of years before reaching the surface and streaming into space.

Other sources of light include fire, electric bulbs, and bioluminescent organisms. Bioluminescence is the production of light by living things. Fireflies, some deep-sea fish, and certain fungi all produce their own light through chemical reactions.

Light is fast, fundamental, and strange. It is a wave. It is a particle. It needs nothing to travel through. It has shaped every scientific revolution from Newton to Einstein. Understanding light means understanding the universe at its most basic level.
