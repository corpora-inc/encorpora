# Light Is Everything

Light is not just something we see with. It is woven into the structure of the universe. It connects energy and matter. It defines the limits of speed and causality. It carries information across billions of light-years.

Almost everything we know about the universe beyond Earth comes from light. Astronomers study starlight to determine temperature, composition, age, and distance. When starlight is spread into a spectrum, dark lines appear at specific wavelengths. These are absorption lines. Each chemical element absorbs light at characteristic wavelengths. By reading these lines, scientists identify what stars are made of. This technique is called spectroscopy. Spectroscopy is the study of how matter interacts with light.

In 1929, Edwin Hubble noticed that absorption lines in the light from distant galaxies were shifted toward red. This redshift means the galaxies are moving away from us. The farther a galaxy is, the faster it recedes. This discovery proved the universe is expanding.

The cosmic microwave background is the oldest light in the universe. It was released about 380,000 years after the Big Bang. The Big Bang is the event that began the expansion of the universe. The universe cooled enough for atoms to form and light streamed freely into space. That ancient light still fills the universe, stretched by expansion into microwaves.

Light carries energy. Plants capture light through photosynthesis. Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in sugars. Almost all life on Earth depends on this process. Fossil fuels are the remains of ancient organisms that captured sunlight millions of years ago. When you burn gasoline, you release stored sunlight.

Solar panels convert light directly into electricity. They contain semiconductor materials. A semiconductor conducts electricity under certain conditions. When photons strike a semiconductor, they knock electrons loose, creating current. More sunlight hits Earth's surface in one hour than humanity uses in a year.

Light is essential to modern communication. Fiber optic cables transmit data as pulses of light. A single fiber can carry trillions of bits per second. The global internet depends on undersea fiber optic cables that span oceans.

Medical science uses light in powerful ways. Lasers perform surgery. A laser is a device that produces a concentrated beam of light at a single wavelength. Lasers cut tissue precisely, seal blood vessels, and correct vision. X-rays image bones. Infrared sensors measure body temperature.

Light defines the boundary between what we can know and what we cannot. Nothing travels faster than light. There are parts of the universe so far away that their light has not had time to reach us. The observable universe has an edge, not because space ends, but because light has a finite speed and the universe has a finite age.

Light is energy. Light is information. Light is the tool with which we see, communicate, and understand. From the photons leaving a distant star to the glow of a screen in a dark room, light is everything.
