# The Speed of Light

Light travels at 299,792,458 meters per second in a vacuum. This is the fastest speed possible in the universe. Scientists call it c. Nothing with mass can reach this speed. Nothing can exceed it. The speed of light is a law of nature.

At the speed of light, you could circle the Earth about 7.5 times in one second. You could travel from Earth to the moon in 1.3 seconds. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth. Light from the nearest star beyond the sun, Proxima Centauri, takes about 4.2 years to arrive. Distances in space are so large that scientists measure them in light-years. A light-year is the distance light travels in one year. One light-year is about 9.46 trillion kilometers.

For most of history, people believed light traveled instantly. The first person to show that light has a finite speed was the Danish astronomer Ole Romer in 1676. He observed the moons of Jupiter. He noticed that the timing of their eclipses shifted depending on how far Earth was from Jupiter. He reasoned that the shift happened because light needed time to cross the extra distance. His estimate was rough but revolutionary.

In 1849, the French physicist Hippolyte Fizeau measured the speed of light on Earth. He sent a beam through a gap in a spinning toothed wheel. The light traveled to a mirror several kilometers away and bounced back. By adjusting the wheel's speed, he could block or pass the returning light. His result was remarkably close to the modern value.

Albert Einstein's theory of special relativity, published in 1905, placed the speed of light at the center of physics. Einstein showed that the speed of light is the same for all observers, regardless of how fast they move. If you throw a ball from a moving train, the ball's speed adds to the train's speed. Light does not work this way. A beam from a moving flashlight travels at c, no matter how fast the flashlight moves.

Special relativity has strange consequences. As an object approaches the speed of light, time slows down for it relative to a stationary observer. This is called time dilation. At the speed of light, an object with mass would need infinite energy to keep accelerating. This is why the speed of light is an absolute limit.

The speed of light connects space and time. Einstein showed that space and time form a single fabric called spacetime. The most famous equation in physics, E equals mc squared, uses the speed of light. Energy equals mass times the speed of light squared. A small amount of mass contains enormous energy because c is such a large number.

The speed of light is not just fast. It is the speed at which the universe communicates with itself. It is the speed of cause and effect. It is the ultimate speed limit.
