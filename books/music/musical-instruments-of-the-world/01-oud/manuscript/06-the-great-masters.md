## Chapter 6 — The Great Masters

The oud has been played for over a thousand years. In all that time, a few people changed it forever.

The first was a thinker more than a player, named al-Farabi. He lived from about the year 872 to the year 950, working in the great city of Baghdad.

Al-Farabi wrote a giant book about music. He explained how strings vibrate. He explained why some notes sound sweet together. He explained why music can change how a person feels. His book was read in Arabic and in Latin for centuries.

The second great oud man was a singer named Ziryab, born in Iraq around the year 789. As a young man, he had to leave Baghdad in a hurry.

Ziryab traveled west across all of North Africa. He arrived in the city of Córdoba, in Spain, in the year 822. The king of Córdoba loved his music and gave him a house, a salary, and time to teach.

In Spain, Ziryab opened a music school. Some say it was the first conservatory in the world. He added a fifth course of strings to the oud, and he changed how plectrums were made.

Long after Ziryab, in our own time, a player from Iraq lifted the oud to a new place. His name was Munir Bashir, and people called him the king of the oud.

Before Bashir, the oud usually played with other instruments. Bashir put it on stage alone, and he played long, quiet, deep solos. People around the world heard him and fell in love with the oud all over again.

There were many others. Farid al-Atrash made the oud the voice of Egyptian films. Anouar Brahem from Tunisia plays oud with jazz musicians from Europe and America.

The oud is still alive. Every year, a new master is born somewhere in the world.
