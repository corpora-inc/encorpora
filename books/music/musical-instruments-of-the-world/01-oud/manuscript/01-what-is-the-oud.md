## Chapter 1 — What Is an Oud?

An oud is a string instrument. It is a kind of lute, with a body shaped like a pear cut in half.

The back of the oud is round and deep, like a wooden bowl. The front is flat. We call the flat front the soundboard.

The neck of the oud is short, and it has no frets. Frets are the little metal lines you see on a guitar. Without them, the player can slide between notes very smoothly.

Most ouds have eleven strings, grouped into six pairs. We call each pair a course. Five of the courses have two strings tuned the same. The sixth course has only one string. That is how you get eleven.

The player holds the oud across the lap. One hand presses the strings on the neck. The other hand plucks them with a thin pick called a risha.

The sound is warm and deep. It is a little bit sad and a little bit sweet, all at once.
