## Chapter 2 — Where the Name Comes From

The name oud is an Arabic word that means wood. The instrument is named after the thing it is made of.

Long before the oud, there was an older cousin called the barbat. The barbat was played in ancient Persia more than two thousand years ago.

The barbat had a wooden body and silk strings. Its sound was long and soft. People in the courts of kings played it.

Slowly, the barbat changed. The body grew rounder, the strings doubled into pairs, and the neck got shorter. By about the year 700, people in the Arab world were playing what we now call the oud.

From there, the oud traveled. It went to Egypt and North Africa. It crossed the sea to Spain. It went north into Turkey and Greece. Each place gave the oud a little of its own voice.

But the name stayed the same. Wherever it went, the wooden instrument was still the oud.
