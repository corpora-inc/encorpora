# U10 7v7: The Playbook — Style Guide

## Voice

Dry, practical, dense with information. Plain language a 9-year-old can read. No motivational cliches. No preaching. The goal is to win. Every paragraph is actionable.

## Structure

- Meaty paragraphs over short choppy ones — but break wall-of-text paragraphs exceeding ~8-10 lines.
- Lists when they genuinely clarify (drill steps, position attributes, multiple concepts in a sequence).
- Blockquotes for key rules, position summaries, and principles that should pop visually.
- **Bold** for key concepts where a skimmer would miss something important. Not every paragraph — where it matters.
- Every drill, every position, every set piece described with enough detail to execute without further research.

## Formatting

- Markdown with pandoc extensions.
- Chapters start with `#` (H1). Sections use `##`. Subsections use `###`.
- Image placeholders: `![description](images/placeholder.png)` — only where diagrams add real value.
- No emoji. No exclamation marks unless quoting speech.

## Audience

- First-time U10 7v7 coaches who have never coached before.
- Parents reading along to understand what the coach is doing.
- 9-year-old players who can read the position guide and set piece chapters themselves.

## Technical Notes

- Build-out line: cover both scenarios (with and without) since league rules vary.
- 7v7 means 6 field players plus a goalkeeper.
- Formation notation: 2-1-1-2 (backs-sweeper-point-wing strikers). No left mid/right mid.
- Six unique positions: LB, RB, Sweeper (defensive mid), Point (attacking mid), LW (wing striker), RW (wing striker).
- The formation system uses three shapes, presented defense-first: defensive (6-0, everyone defends), neutral (2-1-1-2, two interlocking triangles), attacking (2-1-3).
- Position guide organized by role: Goalkeeper, Backs (merged LB/RB), Sweeper, Wings (merged LW/RW), Point. The six field positions (LB, RB, Sweeper, Point, LW, RW) still exist — the guide is organized by shared role.
- Center-control philosophy: Defense denies the center, forces opponent wide (sidelines are the best defenders). Offense attacks through the center first. GK distributes to the sidelines, never through the center. Backs pinch into the center, not hold the flanks. Clearances go OUT toward the sidelines.
