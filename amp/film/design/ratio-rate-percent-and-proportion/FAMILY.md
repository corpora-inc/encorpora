# Ratio, rate, percent and proportion, grades 4 to 7: family design

Nine skills, 53 problem types. Planning snapshot: `src/curriculum/catalog.ts`, the engine templates and `film/facts.ts`, read for this design. This design covers the idea, the pictures, how lessons go, the tools, the seam, what is misfiled, the pilot. Per-type concepts use these foundations and pictures.

---

## 1. The idea underneath the whole family

**Two amounts are tied so that whatever you multiply one by, you must multiply the other by too.** The tie is a single number: the amount of one that goes with exactly one of the other. Every problem in this family is finding that tie, using it, or checking that it holds.

- Grade 4, "7 times as many": the tie is 7; one amount is seven copies of the other.
- Grade 6, "3 cups of blue for every 5 of white": a batch; both amounts scale together; the tie is 3/5 cup of blue per cup of white.
- Grade 6, "39 dollars for 3 hours": the tie with a 1 in it, 13 dollars per hour.
- Grade 6, "35%": the tie to a hundred; 35 for every 100.
- Grade 7, "proportional": the tie written as a rule, y = kx, and seen as a line through zero.
- Grade 7, "increases by 23%": the tie from the original (100%) to the new amount (123%).
- Grade 7, "1 inch represents 4 feet": the tie between a drawing and the thing.

There are always **two multipliers** in play, and telling them apart is most of the understanding. *Across*: the tie between the two kinds of thing (dollars per hour, blue per white, the constant k). *Along*: the factor that scales a whole batch to a bigger or smaller one (double the recipe, 3 hours to 9 hours). A ratio table has both as its two directions. Either gets the answer; a child who sees both never guesses.

The family's one enemy, at every grade, is **adding where it should multiply**: "7 more" for "7 times as many"; 3:5 becomes 5:7 when "two more cups" are added; x goes from 8 to 6 so y "goes down by 2"; a 20% rise then a 25% drop "is 5% off". The pictures below are chosen so that adding looks wrong.

### The foundations, in the order they are built

1. **Times as many means copies. More than means extra.** (Grade 4) 6 is 2 times 3 because it is two copies of 3. "Student R has 3 more" is a different sentence about the same numbers. In 42 = 7 × n, 42 is seven copies of n, so n is the smaller amount and comes from dividing. Define *times as many*. Taken from family 7 (equal groups, division as the missing factor) and family 8 (the bar).

2. **A ratio says how two amounts go together: "for every".** (Grade 6) 3 cups of blue for every 5 cups of white describes one batch; both numbers belong to it. The order of the words is the order of the numbers. Part to part (blue to white, 3:5) and part to whole (blue to all paint, 3:8) are different ratios about one batch. Define *ratio*, *a to b*, *a:b*, *for every*.

3. **Equivalent ratios are the same tape with a different box.** (Grade 6) 3:5, 6:10 and 21:35 are one shape, three boxes big, five boxes big, holding 1, 2 and 7 per box. Scaling both amounts by the same factor keeps the ratio; scaling one of them, or adding to both, breaks it. The *biggest* box that fits both amounts gives simplest form (20:28 with boxes of 4 is 5:7). Define *equivalent ratios*, *simplest form*. Taken from family 7 (common factors) and family 9 (equal pieces of a stated whole).

4. **Find one.** (Grade 6) The unit rate is the amount that goes with exactly 1 of the other: 39 dollars for 3 hours is 13 dollars per hour. The word after *per* is the one you divide by. Once you know the amount for one, you know it for any number: multiply. Every ratio has two unit rates (dollars per hour, hours per dollar); the question says which. Define *rate*, *unit rate*, *per*. In grade 7 the amounts become fractions (3/4 cup for every 3/8 cup) and nothing else changes; family 9 gives the division.

5. **Percent is for every hundred.** (Grade 6) 35% means 35 of every 100, the same as 0.35 and 35/100: a third name for the hundredths the child already owns. The whole is always 100%. Ten percent is one tenth, one percent is one hundredth, and every other percent is built from them: 15% is 10% and half of 10%. To find a whole from a part, find what 1% (or 10%) is, then count up to 100. Define *percent*, *%*, and *of* as "times". Taken from family 2 (the hundredths flat, moving a digit one place).

6. **Proportional means one multiplier for every pair.** (Grade 7) y = kx: every y is the same k times its x. In a table, y ÷ x is the same in every row. On a graph, the pairs lie on one straight line through the origin, because 0 of one goes with 0 of the other; the height at x = 1 is k. A straight line that misses the origin (y = x + 4) is not proportional. Define *proportional*, *constant of proportionality*, *origin*. Taken from family 13 (the plane) and family 11 (the equation).

7. **The original is one hundred percent.** (Grade 7) A percent change is a percent *of the original amount*. Up 23% is 123% of the original: one multiplication by 1.23. Down 42% leaves 58%: one multiplication by 0.58. To undo, divide by that multiplier; never take the percent off the new price. After a change, the new amount becomes the new 100%, which is why two changes multiply and never add. Simple interest is the same percent of the original principal every year; compound interest moves the 100% every year. Define *original*, *increase/decrease by*, *markup*, *markdown*, *discount*, *tax*, *tip*, *commission*, *interest*, *principal*, *simple interest*.

8. **A scale is a rate between a drawing and the thing.** (Grade 7) 1 cm represents 2 m: every length on the plan is multiplied by 2. Area has two lengths, so area is multiplied by 2 × 2. Define *scale*, *scale drawing*, *scale factor*. Taken from family 24 (area); given to family 26 (dilations).

What this family gives on: the tape and the double number line to family 11 (an unknown part), proportional to family 14 (slope is the unit rate), percent to families 21 (money) and 16 (percent growth), the scale factor to 26 and 28, the fixed ratio in similar triangles to 30, percent chance to 34.

## 2. The visual language: the tape, the double number line, and what they become

One picture that grows: a bar cut into equal boxes (grade 4), two such bars for a ratio (grade 6), the same two amounts on two number lines that share their ticks (grade 6), the double line with 100 on top (percent, grade 6), the double line folded into the coordinate plane (grade 7). A seventh grader looking at y = kx should recognize the tape from grade 6, with the same two colors.

### The shapes

| Picture | What it is | Grades |
|---|---|---|
| **tape** | One strip per amount, made of boxes; every box on screen is the same size and holds the same value; strip lengths are to scale with the ratio | 4 (one box against k copies), 6, 7 (percent chains) |
| **double number line** | Two parallel lines sharing tick positions; top line one amount, bottom line the other; 0 over 0 | 6, 7 (fractional ticks) |
| **percent bar** | The double number line with a bar between: 0 to 100% on top, 0 to the whole below; shading to scale (exists: `bar`) | 6, 7 |
| **percent grid** | The hundredths flat from place value, 10 by 10; a cell is 1%, a column 10% (exists: `blocks`) | 6 |
| **ratio table** | Two columns (or rows), one per amount; one row per batch; arrows along and across (exists: `table`, needs arrows) | 6, 7 |
| **proportional graph** | The double number line folded: the bottom line swung up as the y axis; each matched pair a point; the line through the origin; (1, k) marked (exists: `axes`, `dot`, `label`) | 7 |
| **scale pair** | The plan and the real figure side by side with their lengths, the real figure's unit grid showing k × k squares per plan square | 7 |

Every one is a scratch-paper sketch: a bar with lines through it, two lines with matching ticks, a two-column table, two axes and a line from the corner.

### The tape, precisely

- A **strip** is a row of boxes. All boxes on the screen share one width and height. The first-named amount is teal, the second rust, the strip's name at its left end. A brace at the right end carries the strip's total ("42"), or "?" when it is asked.
- **Stacked** (left-aligned, one above the other) when the question compares the parts. **Joined** (end to end as one bar, bracket "all" above) when the question is about the whole. The lesson slides between the two on the word "altogether".
- The **box value** is written inside one gold box the moment it is found ("each box is 6"), then copies itself into every box while the braces count up. This is the family's central move: *find one box and you have them all*.
- **Times as many** (grade 4): the small amount is one box; the large amount is k copies of it, arriving one per counted word. **More than**: the same strip with a short rust piece added at the end, drawn to the size of the difference, labeled "+7". The two pictures sit side by side once, and the additive misread dies there.
- **Regroup** (simplest form): strips of unit cells (20 and 28) fade their inner lines into bigger boxes (4 cells each): 5 boxes and 7. "The biggest box that fits both."
- **Same box test** (equivalent ratios): a candidate pair (4 students, 10 adults) is laid over the tape; each strip's box value is computed (4 ÷ 2 = 2, 10 ÷ 5 = 2); one shared box value means equivalent; two different values do not.

Never: a strip whose length is not its count of boxes; a pie for a ratio; a tape with different box sizes in the two strips.

### The double number line, precisely

- Two lines, top teal, bottom rust, 0 above 0 at the left, the same tick positions on both, labels above the top and below the bottom. Ticks are to scale: 3 hours is three times as far as 1.
- The **unit tick** is the tick at 1 on the "per" line; it lights gold, and the value across from it on the other line is the unit rate, boxed. Dividing the known amount into equal jumps down to the 1 tick is the division made visible.
- **Hops**: arcs along both lines at once, the same number of jumps, each labeled with its step (+13 above, +1 below). Counting arcs, never ticks, as in place value.
- **Stretch** (grade 7): the view zooms to show fractional ticks (1/4, 1/2, 3/8) so 3/4 cup for every 3/8 cup is read the same way as whole numbers.
- **Fold** (grade 6 to 7 handoff): the bottom line swings up ninety degrees to become the y axis; every matched label pair rises as a point; a straight line through them passes through the corner where 0 met 0. Said once, aloud: "the double number line is the graph, folded."

### Colors mean one thing each

- **teal**: the first-named amount (blue paint, hours, the original price, the plan).
- **rust**: the second amount (white paint, dollars, the real room); also a piece taken away (a discount, the cut part of a bar) or added (the raise).
- **gold**: the one thing being looked at right now: the box being filled, the unit tick, the 1% mark, the cell y ÷ x is computed in, the deciding word in the problem ("per", "of", "original"). One gold thing at a time.
- **blue**: a scale-factor arrow (×3, ÷4), always drawn twice, once on each amount, so the eye sees the same thing done to both.
- **muted**: ruled-out choices, empty cells, the old percent scale after a rebase.

### The motions (each one proves a foundation)

- **Copy** (foundation 1): one box, then k copies arrive on the count. "Times as many" is copies.
- **Join** (2): two stacked strips slide into one bar with the "all" bracket. Part to whole is the same tape seen whole.
- **Fill** (3, 4): the gold box takes its value; the value propagates; the braces count up to the totals. One box found, all found.
- **Rescale** (3): the box value changes from 1 to 2 while the strips keep their shape. Equivalent ratios are one tape.
- **Divide to one** (4): on the double number line, the known amount is cut into equal jumps down to the unit tick. Per means divide.
- **Shade** (5): the percent bar fills to p%; the amount is read on the lower scale. The reverse (a known amount, its percent marked) and the hop to 1% then to 100% is "find the whole".
- **Grow / cut** (7): a rust piece extends the bar past 100% (the scale continues to 110, 120, 123) or hatches and leaves the last p% (58% remains). The original stays labeled 100% throughout.
- **Rebase** (7): the current bar relabels as 100% and the old labels fade. The second change is of the new price; compound interest rebases every year and simple interest never does.
- **Fold** (6): the double number line becomes the graph. Through the origin because 0 meets 0.
- **Plot from the table** (6): a row lights, its point drops onto the plane; after three rows the line draws; a row whose point misses the line, or a line that misses the origin, is seen.
- **Dilate** (8): the plan rectangle grows from a corner by k; each unit square becomes a k × k block of smaller squares, counted. Area takes the factor twice.
- **Dim**: choices that cannot be right fade together in one beat with one spoken reason.

What is deliberately not a picture: "is over of" triangles, cross-multiplication X's, pies for ratios, any bar not drawn to the size it claims.

## 3. How lessons in this family should go

Every film opens by saying what we are looking at and building it (the tape from the story, the double line from the two amounts, the bar from the whole), defines the words the problem uses, says what the picture is for, then works the example. The five moves from place value run on every type: **Read**, **At a glance**, **The sure fast way**, **Sense**, **Check**.

### Read: the words that say what is asked

- *for every*, *to*, *a:b*: a ratio; the order of the words is the order of the numbers; "to all" or "of the" means the whole is one of the two.
- *times as many*: copies; multiply to get the larger, divide to get the smaller. *more than*: extra; add or subtract.
- *per*, *each*, *for one*: a unit rate; divide by the count of the thing after "per".
- *p% of*: multiply by p/100. *what percent of*: part divided by whole, written per hundred. *p% of what*: the whole is asked; find 1%.
- *increases by*, *marked up*, *tax*, *tip*, *interest*: a piece of the original is added. *decreases by*, *discount*, *off*: a piece of the original is removed. *is now*, *new price*: the amount after the change. *was*, *original*, *before*: undo.
- *proportional*, *varies directly*: y = kx, one multiplier, through the origin.
- *1 cm represents 2 m*: a rate from plan to real; *area* means the factor twice.

### At a glance: what is known before any work

- Which amount is bigger. The one with "times as many" is bigger; a part is smaller than its whole; a share is smaller than the total; p% of an amount is less than the amount when p < 100 and more when p > 100; after a decrease there is less, so the original was more than the new price.
- About how big. 10% of the amount first, then build: 91% of 132 is a bit under 132 (about 119); 23% of 160 is about a quarter, 40; 188 dollars for 8 hours is a bit over 20 an hour.
- For a ratio table or tape: is the along factor a whole number? 5 white to 60 white is ×12, so the answer is 3 × 12 at once.
- For a graph question: does the line pass through zero? If not, it is not proportional, whatever else it does.

### The sure fast ways (each always works)

- **Find one box, multiply.** Any missing amount in a ratio or table: divide the known amount by its box count, multiply by the asked box count. The along factor, when whole, is the same thing done faster.
- **Per X: divide by the number of X.** 188 dollars per 8 hours: 188 ÷ 8. 3/4 cup per 3/8 cup: 3/4 ÷ 3/8. Never the other way; the picture puts the 1 on the "per" line.
- **Percent of: multiply by the hundredths.** 23% of 160 is 0.23 × 160. For a quick exact answer, 10% and 1% build any percent; for an estimate, round the amount first, then take 10%.
- **Whole from a part: find 1%, count to 100.** 277.24 is 58%: 1% is 4.78, 100% is 478. Equivalently, divide by 0.58.
- **One multiplier for a change.** Up p%: × (1 + p/100). Down p%: × (1 − p/100). Change rate: change ÷ original. Undo: ÷ the multiplier. Two changes: multiply the multipliers (1.2 × 0.75 = 0.9).
- **Proportional test.** y ÷ x the same in every row, and (0, 0) fits. For a rule: y = (a number) × x and nothing added.
- **Scale.** Length × k. Area × k × k. A new drawing's scale is real length ÷ drawn length.
- **Simplest form: the biggest box that fits both.** The greatest common factor from family 7, named as a box.

Not taught: cross-multiplication as a rule (a proportion is two equal ratios on one double number line; the scale factor or the unit rate solves it, and `conversion-proportion-blanks` is written as "same units, same order, same factor"); subtracting percents of different wholes; "the bigger pack is cheaper".

### Sense, then check

- Sense before boxing: the direction (bigger or smaller) and the estimate from the glance.
- Check after: multiply the unit rate back by the count; simplify the answer pair and see the given ratio; apply the percent change to the original found and land on the new price; add the parts of a split and get the total; for a graph, put x = 1 in and read k, put x = 0 in and read 0; for a scale, divide the real length by the plan length and get the scale.
- Multiple choice: dim together everything that goes the wrong direction (a smaller amount for the larger, a price that grew on a discount), everything with a swapped order (a point read as (y, x), a ratio in the reverse order), and any line that does not pass through zero. Then prove the one that remains.

### Realistic misconceptions, and the picture that makes each impossible

1. **Adding instead of multiplying.** 3:5 with two more cups becomes 5:7; x from 8 to 6 means y down by 2; "7 times as many" solved as +7. The tape has boxes; adding a cup to one strip visibly breaks the box.
2. **Part to part read as part to whole.** "3 blue for every 5 white" heard as 3/5 blue. The join shows 8 boxes.
3. **Dividing the wrong way.** 3 ÷ 39 hours per dollar when dollars per hour was asked. The unit tick sits on the "per" line.
4. **Multiplying for the smaller amount.** "80 is 8 times as many as Student B's" answered 640. The copies are drawn: Student B's box is one of eight.
5. **Taking the percent off the new price.** 277.24 after a 42% drop, "original" answered 277.24 × 1.42. The bar is labeled 100% at the original and the new price sits under 58%.
6. **Adding successive percents.** +20% then −25% as −5%. The rebase: the second change is of a different bar.
7. **Percent as whole hundredths slipping a place.** 0.125 read as 1.25% or 12%; 8/5 as 0.16. The grid: 12.5 cells of a hundred; the flat and a half for 1.6.
8. **A percent cannot be over 100.** 21/8 "must be" 26.25%. Two flats and more are shaded.
9. **Area scaled by k.** 22.5 cm² at 1:2 answered 45. The dilation shows four small squares in every plan square.
10. **Any straight line (or any increasing table) is proportional.** y = x + 4 chosen. The line misses the origin on screen.
11. **Swapped axes.** (4, 8) read as 8 pounds cost 4 dollars. The axis names are on the picture and read on the word.
12. **Totals compared instead of unit prices.** Store B "cheaper" because 2.88 < 3.74. Two double number lines, both read at the 1 tick.
13. **Simplest form by dividing one number, or subtracting.** The regroup fades cell lines in both strips at once.

A cast fits this family unusually well: the additive misread is universal and a student voice saying it ("so two more white means two more blue?") gives the teacher the beat to untangle it on the tape.

### Lines worth remembering

- "Times as many means copies. More than means extra."
- "A ratio is for every. Do the same to both."
- "Find one box and you have them all."
- "Per means for one. Divide by the number of them."
- "Percent means for every hundred. The whole is one hundred percent."
- "Ten percent is one tenth. Build the rest from it."
- "To find the whole, find one percent, then count to a hundred."
- "Proportional: one multiplier for every row, and the line goes through zero."
- "The original is one hundred percent."
- "After a change, the new price is the new one hundred percent."
- "Scale a length by k. Scale an area by k twice."

Pacing: grade 4 in the early-grades voice (short sentences, one idea per beat, the problem read aloud). Grades 6 and 7 can hold a little more but use the same patient sequence: plain words, then the picture, then a pause; a motion finishes before the voice moves on.

## 4. Tools to build

Exists and serves as is: `bar` (percent bar with share and amount scales, partition, shade, marks), `table` (cells, rows, columns, `given`, highlight), `blocks` (the hundredths flat as the percent grid), `axes`, `plot`, `dot`, `label`, `grid`, `dim` (specified in the place-value design; specified), `stack` (every cents computation), the slope triangle as drawn ad hoc in `line-slope`.

### 4.1 `tape` (new)

```json
{ "tape": { "id": "t", "layout": "stacked", "box": null,
  "strips": [
    { "id": "y", "name": "yellow", "boxes": 7, "ink": "accent", "total": 42 },
    { "id": "b", "name": "blue",   "boxes": 3, "ink": "warm",   "total": "?" } ] } }
```

- One box size for the whole figure, sized by the kit so the longest strip fits its card. `boxes` is the count; `total` a number, `"?"` or absent (a brace at the right end). `box` is the value each box holds, `null` until found. `layout`: `stacked` or `joined` (one bar, bracket "all" above).
- Parts: `t.y`, `t.y.box.3`, `t.y.total`, `t.y.extra`, `t.all`, `t.box`.
- Actions: `fill` (`{"do":"fill","target":"t","box":6,"at":"six"}`: the gold box takes the value, every box copies it, braces count up); `join` / `stack` (slide between layouts); `copy` (`"strip":"y","to":7`: one box grows to seven, one per counted word); `extra` (`"strip":"y","n":7`: a rust piece of n box-units appended, labeled +7; for "more than"); `regroup` (`"into":4`: cells merge into boxes of 4 in both strips at once); `rescale` (`"box":2`: value changes, shape does not); `test` (`"pair":[4,10]`: writes each strip's box value, same or different); `highlight`, `show`, `hide`.

### 4.2 `dnl` (new; shares the scale code of `bar`)

```json
{ "dnl": { "id": "d",
  "top":    { "name": "hours",   "ink": "accent", "to": 8 },
  "bottom": { "name": "dollars", "ink": "warm",   "to": 188 },
  "ticks": 8, "labels": { "top": [0, 8], "bottom": [0, 188] } } }
```

- Two lines with shared tick positions, 0 over 0; labels shown as the lesson names them (`show d.top.label.3`). `ticks` may be fractional (`"step": 0.125`) for grade 7.
- Parts: `d.top`, `d.bottom`, `d.tick.k`, `d.top.label.k`, `d.bottom.label.k`, `d.unit` (the tick at 1 on the named line, gold, with "?" across), `d.hop.k`.
- Actions: `unit` (`"line":"top"`); `divide` (`"from":8,"to":1`: equal jumps down to the unit tick, the step written); `hops` (arcs on both lines on counted words, each with its step); `stretch` (zoom to a range with finer ticks); `fold` (the bottom line swings up as the y axis, matched labels become points, a line draws through them and the corner; the figure becomes a proportional graph with the same two inks).

### 4.3 `bar` extensions

- `"max": 125` on the share scale: the scale and a muted extension zone run past 100%.
- `grow` (`"to": 1.23`): a rust piece extends the bar; the amount scale continues. `cut` (`"to": 0.58`): the last part hatches rust and the remaining teal length is the new amount.
- `rebase`: the current bar relabels 0 to 100% (and its amount), old labels fade to muted; used for the second change and for each compound year.
- `pair`: a second bar aligned beneath (original and new; two stores; two banks) with marks on a shared share scale.
- `mark` with `"top": "?"` or `"bottom": "?"` for the asked side of a known point.

### 4.4 `table` extensions

- `arrow` (`{"do":"arrow","from":"t.cell.1.2","to":"t.cell.3.2","tex":"\\times 3","ink":"blue"}`): along or across, drawn on the word; a pair of along arrows (one per column) is the "same to both" picture.
- `"?"` cells rendered muted; `fill` (`"cell":"t.cell.3.1","text":"36"`) on the word.
- `derive` (`"col": "y/x"`): a muted third column of quotients written row by row; the proportional test.
- `diffs`: the differences between consecutive rows written between them (simple interest: equal; compound: growing).
- `register`: a running-balance table whose last column updates as lines are added (credit card months).

### 4.5 Graph additions (P7)

- `link` (`"row":"t.row.2","point":"P2"`): the row lights and its point drops, for plotting a table.
- `tri`: the slope triangle as a named part (rise rust, run teal) from `line-slope`, so the unit rate reads as the height at x = 1.
- Marks for the origin and (1, k) are `dot` and `label` as today.

### 4.6 `dilate` (P8)

`{"do":"dilate","target":"plan","about":"A","by":2}` scales a `poly` or `grid` group from a corner; a `grid` gains `"blocks": k` so heavy lines show the plan squares inside the real grid. Serves both scale-drawing types and family 26 later.

### 4.7 Smaller

- `dim` for choices, as specified in the place-value design.
- A sector shaded by fraction for `circle-graph-read` (one type): today an `angle` with fill at full radius suffices; the proper `sector` arrives with family 28.

### Build order

1. `tape` and `dim`: grade 4 comparison and grade 6 ratios, 11 types, and the family's first lessons.
2. `dnl` with `fold`: unit rates, double number lines, conversions, fractional rates, and the grade-7 graph bridge, 9 types.
3. `bar` extensions: percent and percent change, 25 types.
4. `table` arrows, `derive`, `diffs`, `register`; graph `link` and `tri`: ratio tables, proportional relationships, interest and credit, 9 types.
5. `dilate`: scale drawings, 2 types.

## 5. Which lessons need the strongest writer, and where the seam is

**Strongest writer, because the lesson is the design:**

- The first lesson of each picture: `times-as-many-comparison` (copies against extra), `ratio-pair-blanks` (the tape born, the fill), `unit-rate` (the double line and the unit tick), `percent-change-final` (the bar past 100%, the original as 100%), `proportional-graph` (the fold, the origin, (1, k)). If these are not beautiful, nothing after them will be.
- Mixed types that teach reading, where there is no recipe and the example must be chosen to expose the story: `staar-ratio-statements` (simplest form, split a total, unit rate), `mcas-ratio-statements-select`, `mcas-comparison-equations-select` (the unknown in both positions; equations that say the same thing three ways), `percent-applications` (seven stories under one name), `percent-chain` and `percent-chain`'s reverse cases, `staar-salary-percent`, `variation-constant` (four kinds), `proportional-match-table`, `credit-card-balance`, `credit-cost-concepts`, `return-claim-check`, `interest-table-type`, `show-work-percent-count`, `scale-drawing-work`, `mixed-quantity-discount`.
- Anything whose insight is a motion that proves: `percent-change-original` (the bar read backward), `successive-percent-change` (the rebase), `interest-table-type` (rebase every year against never).

**A capable cheaper model can write from a concept once the kit above exists:** one-method types whose picture is one component with named actions and whose concept is fully written: `comparison-amount`, `comparison-factor`, `times-as-many-counts`, `equivalent-ratios-select`, `ratio-missing`, `conversion-proportion-blanks`, `unit-price-sentence-slots`, `unit-rate-pair-blanks`, `better-buy`, `ratio-table-missing`, `double-number-line-missing`, `dollar-rate-scale`, `unit-price`, `fraction-unit-rate`, `mcas-fraction-rate-select`, `percent-of`, `percent-grid-model`, `fraction-forms-blanks`, `percent-forms-and-parts`, `staar-rename-percent-fraction-decimal`, `benchmark-percent-estimate`, `share-remainder-percent`, `percent-amounts-blanks` (done), `percent-change-rate`, `mcas-markup-price-select`, `simple-interest-blanks`, `loan-offer-compare-blanks`, `proportional-rule-choice`, `graph-of-proportional-rate`, `scale-drawing`, `circle-graph-read`.

This division of work requires a complete concept, a fitting kit, and independent instructional and visual review. The review for this family must recompute every percent and every cent, since `--dry` cannot see a bar whose shaded length does not match its percent; that check belongs in `bar`'s own layout report.

## 6. What looks badly conceived, misfiled or off grade, and what the problems never practice

> Base cases and trick questions are wanted in the app; a flag here means a weak choice for a lesson's main example, or a fix to a template, never a reason to drop the type.

**Answers in the wrong form.** `percent-of` returns 3003/25 for 91% of 132 and `percent-change-final` returns 2183/5 units and 984/5 dollars. A percent of an amount, and a price, are decimals (120.12, 436.6, 196.80); accept and display them so. `mixed-quantity-discount` returns 12.6 dollars; a price is 12.60. `percent-change-original` states a rate as "100/3 percent"; the natural form is 33⅓%.

**Degenerate seeds.** `ratio-missing` with 5 blue for every 5 white (anything equals itself); `percent-change-rate` with a share of 10% before and after (relative change 0, and "relative percent change of a percent" is confusing language for grade 7); `proportional-rule-choice` with only three choices.

**Above grade.** `variation-constant` includes inverse variation, which is in no grade-7 standard (it is high-school algebra); the direct-variation cases are fine. `interest-table-type` asks the learner to tell simple from compound interest and project a compound account, which is HSF.LE.A.1, not 7.RP.A.3 (simple interest only); keep it as the bridge to family 16 but mark it a stretch and let the lesson say "the same amount each year" against "the same percent of a growing amount" without the exponential formula. `mixed-quantity-discount` sits under grade-6 percentages but needs mixed-number products and a markdown (grade 7).

**Misfiled.** `credit-card-balance`, `credit-cost-concepts` and `return-claim-check` are financial literacy (APR, late fees, rates of return) and belong with `financial-literacy-middle` in family 21, reusing the percent bar and the register table from here. `staar-salary-percent`'s "how many more does a pilot earn than a chef in 6 years" has no percent in it (4.OA.A.3 arithmetic); split the raise cases from the comparison cases and send the latter to family 21. `staar-rename-percent-fraction-decimal`'s "decimal as a mixed number" case is `decimal-mixed-number-blanks` from family 2 again; its standard links (4.NF.C.6, 5.NF.B.3, 7.NS.A.2) miss the one it serves here, 6.RP.A.3c. `circle-graph-read` is a percent of a turn and leans on family 28's picture; keep it here, note the dependency.

**Badly conceived presentation.** `graph-of-proportional-rate` offers graphs as sentences ("A line starting at (0, 0) and passing through (2, 40)"); a graph question should show four small graphs. The lesson will draw them on one plane; the app should too. `show-work-percent-count`'s step list (8/25, 16, 24, 48, 32, 8) cannot be read from the problem text; a show-work type whose steps a reader cannot name needs its prompts stated in the template before a lesson can teach it.

**Standard clauses no problem type practices** (exact text):

- 6.RP.A.3a: "plot the pairs of values on the coordinate plane. Use tables to compare ratios." No grade-6 type plots a ratio table, and none compares two ratios with tables (`better-buy` compares unit rates without a table). The `fold` motion teaches the first clause anyway; a type for the second is missing.
- 6.RP.A.2: "use rate language in the context of a ratio relationship... 3/4 cup of flour for each cup of sugar." No type asks for *both* unit rates of one ratio (flour per sugar and sugar per flour); `unit-rate-pair-blanks` gives two rates of two different things.
- 6.RP.A.3b: "constant speed." Grade-6 unit rates are all babysitting dollars, bottles and pages; speed first appears in grade 7 with fractions.
- 6.RP.A.3c: "solve problems involving finding the whole, given a part and the percent." Only `percent-forms-and-parts` does, as one of its cases. Thin for the hardest grade-6 percent idea.
- 6.RP.A.3d: "manipulate and transform units appropriately when multiplying or dividing quantities." `conversion-proportion-blanks` sets up one conversion; nothing multiplies or divides with units.
- 4.OA.A.1: "Interpret a multiplication equation as a comparison, e.g., interpret 35 = 5 × 7 as a statement that 35 is 5 times as many as 7." Types go from words to equations; none goes from a given equation to the comparison sentence.
- 7.RP.A.1: "ratios of lengths, areas." Only speed and recipes; no area rates.
- 7.RP.A.2c: "Represent proportional relationships by equations... t = pn." Only `proportional-graph` asks for an equation, from a graph; none from a table or a verbal description.
- 8.EE.B.5 and HSF.LE.A.1 are linked from `graph-of-proportional-rate` and `interest-table-type` but are not grade-7 standards; family 14 and family 16 own them.

Well covered: 4.OA.A.2 (including "distinguishing multiplicative comparison from additive comparison"), 6.RP.A.1, 7.RP.A.2a, b and d, every example named in 7.RP.A.3 (simple interest, tax, markups and markdowns, gratuities and commissions, fees, percent increase and decrease, percent error), and both halves of 7.G.A.1 (lengths and areas; reproducing at a different scale, by `scale-drawing-work`).

## 7. The pilot: five problem types

The pilot covers the arc from grade 4 to grade 7, every new tool, both multipliers, and the family's two worst misconceptions (adding, and taking the percent off the new price) made impossible on screen.

| # | Problem type | Grade | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `times-as-many-comparison` | 4 | The family's seed and its enemy in one frame: copies against extra, both drawn to scale; multiple choice with the reversed sentence and the additive sentence dimmed together; the early-grades voice. Exercises `tape` copy and extra, and `dim`. |
| 2 | `ratio-pair-blanks` | 6 | The tape is born from a story: 7 yellow to 3 blue, 42 yellow; one gold box becomes 6; blue is 18; the join makes 60. Part to part and part to whole in one lesson; the "find one box" line. Exercises `fill` and `join`. |
| 3 | `unit-rate` | 6 | The double number line is born: 188 dollars over 8 hours cut down to the unit tick, 23.50 per hour, a decimal answer in cents; "per means for one"; the multiply-back check. Exercises `dnl`, `unit`, `divide`. |
| 4 | `percent-change-final` | 7 | The percent bar from `percent-amounts-blanks` grows past 100% (160 up 23% is 196.80) and is cut (down 42% leaves 58%); one multiplier; "the original is one hundred percent", the line the next wave (`percent-change-original`, `successive-percent-change`) stands on. Exercises `max`, `grow`, `cut`. |
| 5 | `proportional-graph` | 7 | The fold: a table's pairs rise as points, the line passes through the origin, (1, k) is the unit rate, (4, 8) is read in words with the swapped reading dimmed. Exercises `fold`, `link`, `tri`. |

If the five land, the next wave completes the tool set and the standards gaps the pilot leaves: `percent-forms-and-parts` (the whole from a part), `ratio-table-missing` (along and across arrows), `better-buy` (two double lines, three-way choice), `percent-change-original` (the bar read backward), `successive-percent-change` (the rebase), `scale-drawing` (the dilation and k twice).
