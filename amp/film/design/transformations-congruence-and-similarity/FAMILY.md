# Transformations, congruence and similarity, grade 8 to Geometry: family design

Eight skills, 41 problem types, grade 8 and Geometry. Planning sources: the catalog entries and generated examples; the standards' exact text. This design covers the idea, the picture system, how lessons go, the kit, the seam, what is misfiled, the pilot. Per-type concepts use these foundations and pictures.

---

## 1. The idea underneath the whole family

This family is one act repeated: **pick a figure up and set it down somewhere else.**

If you set it down without stretching it (a slide, a turn, a flip), nothing about it has changed but where it is. Every length, every angle, every pair of parallel sides, every area is exactly what it was: tracing paper never stretches. If you first stretch it evenly away from one point (a dilation), every length grew by the same factor and nothing else changed. **Congruent** means the first act lands one figure exactly on another. **Similar** means the second act does. Everything else in the family (the coordinate rules, corresponding parts, SSS, SAS, ASA, AA, the parallel cut, the altitude facts, areas growing by the square) is what you can read off a landing once you know which corner landed on which.

A child who owns the landing owns the family. The foundations, in the order they are built; each later lesson names the ones it stands on in one line and shows their picture.

1. **A transformation is a rule for every point.** (Grade 8) Feed in a point, get out its image: (x, y) → (x + 3, y − 5). A figure moves when its corners move; connect the images and the image figure is there. The learner already owns "a rule turns each input into one output" (family 13) and the four quadrants; this is that rule applied to points.

2. **Tracing paper never stretches.** (Grade 8, 8.G.A.1) A slide, a turn and a flip are the three ways to move a traced copy. They keep every length, so they keep every angle, every parallel pair, every perimeter and area. These three are the **rigid motions**. The only thing a rigid motion can change besides position is the way round the corners go (clockwise or counterclockwise), and only a flip changes that.

3. **Every rule about a point is read off the L.** (Grade 8) From the origin to (3, 1) is "over 3, up 1": an L, the same slope triangle the child drew in family 14. A flip over the x-axis turns "up" into "down": (3, −1). A flip over the y-axis turns "over right" into "over left": (−3, 1). A half turn does both: (−3, −1). A quarter turn counterclockwise turns the whole L: what pointed right now points up, what pointed up now points left: 1 to the left, 3 up: (−1, 3). A flip over y = x swaps the arms: (1, 3). No sign rule has to be memorized; the child draws the L, moves it, and reads where its arms point. The rules (x, y) → (−y, x) and the rest are the picture written down.

4. **Congruent means a rigid motion lands one on the other.** (Grade 8 → Geometry, 8.G.A.2, HSG.CO.B.6) To name the motion from two figures, go round the letters first: if A→B→C goes clockwise on one and counterclockwise on the other, a flip happened; otherwise a slide or a turn. Then watch one corner go: only y changed sign, flip over the x-axis; both changed, half turn; the same amount added to both, slide. Confirm on a second corner. Because the landing is exact, every side and every angle of one is equal to its match on the other (HSG.CO.B.7); that is a consequence, not a separate fact.

5. **A dilation stretches from one point.** (Grade 8 → Geometry, 8.G.A.3, HSG.SRT.A.1) Choose a center and a factor k. Every image point is on the ray from the center through the original, k times as far: the L from the center ("over 2, up 4") becomes k times as large ("over 6, up 12"). So every length is multiplied by k, every angle stays, every line becomes a parallel line, and a line through the center stays where it is. From the origin, "k times as far" is simply multiply both coordinates. From another center, go to the center first, then k times the L, then back. k greater than one pushes away from the center; k less than one pulls toward it.

6. **Similar means a dilation, then a rigid motion, lands one on the other.** (Grade 8 → Geometry, 8.G.A.4, HSG.SRT.A.2) So every angle matches and every side is k times its match, the same k. Two sides that scaled by different factors mean no single dilation did it: not similar. Congruent is similar with k = 1.

7. **The letters tell who lands on whom.** (Grade 8 → Geometry) △ABC ~ △FDE says A landed on F, B on D, C on E, whatever the pictures look like. Write the names one over the other and read down the columns: a corner is a column, a side is two neighboring columns (the third side wraps round). Matching angles are copied, never scaled. Matching sides are scaled by k, written as new over old and kept the same way up in every fraction.

8. **Three parts with a side lock a triangle; two angles lock a shape.** (Geometry, HSG.CO.B.8, HSG.SRT.A.3) The criteria are the givens that force the landing. Three sides: the two arcs from the base's ends cross at one point. Two sides and the angle between: the third corner is fixed. Two angles and a side: the two rays cross once. Two sides and an angle not between them: the free side swings and can hit the ray in two places, so two different triangles fit: not sufficient. Three angles alone: any size fits; that is similarity (AA already fixes the third angle, since the three make 180°).

9. **A parallel cut makes a smaller copy from the same corner.** (Geometry, HSG.SRT.B.4, B.5) DE parallel to BC in triangle ABC makes triangle ADE a dilation of triangle ABC from A, with k = AD/AB. So part over whole equals part over whole on the two cut sides, and part over part equals part over part, and the same k gives DE/BC. Two segments that cross with a parallel pair (or an equal pair of angles) make a bow tie: a half turn about the crossing and a stretch, so the two triangles are similar with the crossing point matching itself.

10. **An altitude to the hypotenuse makes three right triangles of one shape.** (Geometry, HSG.SRT.B.4, B.5) Each small triangle shares an acute angle with the big one, so all three are similar. Stand them the same way and the proportions read themselves: a leg squared is the piece of the hypotenuse it touches times the whole hypotenuse; the altitude squared is one piece times the other. Add the two leg facts and the Pythagorean theorem falls out: that is what the standard asks to be proved, and the lesson shows it once as the coda.

11. **Lengths times k, areas times k squared, volumes times k cubed.** (Grade 8 → Geometry) A unit square dilated by 3 is a 3 by 3 of unit squares; a unit cube becomes 3 layers of 3 by 3. From a ratio of areas, k is its square root; from a ratio of volumes, its cube root. Perimeter and surface area follow lengths and areas.

**Words defined before they are used**, in a few plain words the moment they appear: image and original ("the figure after, the figure before"); the prime ("A prime is where A landed"); the arrow rule; clockwise ("the way clock hands go"); center of a turn or a dilation; scale factor ("what every length is multiplied by"); rigid motion; congruent; similar; corresponding ("matching: the parts that land on each other"); orientation ("the way round the letters go"); "must be true" ("true for every case, not just this picture").

**From other families.** The plane and the rule (13); the slope triangle L (14); angles: the triangle's 180°, vertical angles, the angles a line makes crossing two parallels (23); ratio, scale factor and solving a proportion (10, 11); area as unit squares and volume as layers (24, 25); distance on the plane as a hypotenuse, and the Pythagorean theorem as a fact this family re-proves (27); square roots and √80 = 4√5 (4); a statement with its reason (29). **To other families:** similar right triangles make the fixed ratios of trigonometry (30); the dilation with the circle (28); coordinate proofs (27); matrices as transformations (31).

## 2. The visual language

Two of the curriculum's pictures carry the family: the coordinate plane (P7) for every type with coordinates, and figures that move (P8) for every type without. One rule for both: **the figure and its image are drawn to scale, and the motion is drawn, not described.** A 90° turn is 90° on screen; a dilation by 3 is three times as long; a 55° angle is 55°.

### The plane and the figures

The plane has light grid lines, axes numbered every unit (haloed), and a view chosen so the figure, its image and the apparatus fit with margin. The **original** is teal, its corners dots with labels placed outside the figure and clear of axis numbers. The **image** is rust with primed labels. When a chain has a third figure (ABC, DEF, GHI), it is violet. When a figure is picked up, a **ghost** of it stays behind in muted outline, so before and after are both on screen. Figures without coordinates (two similar triangles, a cut triangle, a bow tie, a right triangle with its altitude) are drawn true to their given lengths and angles, with lengths written on the sides they measure and the right angle boxed.

### Color means one thing each

- **teal**: the original figure and its measurements.
- **rust**: the image (the figure after).
- **violet**: a third figure in a chain.
- **blue**: the apparatus of the motion (below).
- **gold**: the one thing being read right now: the asked vertex and its image, the pair of matching sides in a proportion, the shared angle, the word in the question that says what is asked. One gold thing at a time.
- **muted**: ghosts, choices that have been ruled out, the dimmed rest during a recap.

### The apparatus of a motion (blue)

Each motion has one piece of apparatus, which is also what the child sketches.

- **The L.** From a point (the origin, or any center) to a point: the dashed horizontal arm and the vertical arm of the slope triangle, each labeled with its length ("over 3, up 1"). The L turns with a turn, flips with a flip, and grows k times for a dilation. Every coordinate rule is read off where its arms point afterward.
- **Mirror.** The mirror line, and a dashed perpendicular from the vertex to its image crossing it with a right-angle box and equal ticks on both halves: same distance, other side.
- **Pivot.** The center as a dot, two dashed radii of equal length (ticked), and the arc from the vertex to its image with an arrow for the direction and its degrees; a right-angle box between the radii for a quarter turn.
- **Arrow.** One arrow from the asked vertex to its image labeled as the child would say it ("right 3, down 5") and in the problem's notation (⟨3, −5⟩); faint copies at the other corners: the same arrow everywhere.
- **Rays.** From the center through every corner, drawn past the image; the L from the center to one corner and the k-times L to its image; parallel marks on one original side and its image.

### Marks: the figure's own notation

Equal sides get the same number of tick marks (one, two, three); equal angles the same number of arcs; a right angle a box. A side the two triangles share, or a pair of vertical angles, is marked as it is named. Marking the figure is the scratch-paper habit for every criteria, proof and theorem type: mark what is given, then read what you have.

### The board cards

- **Names card.** The two triangle names written one above the other with the relation between them (≅ or ~), the three columns ruled. A pair of neighboring columns lights gold while the matching side lights gold on both figures; one column for an angle. This is the whole method for corresponding parts.
- **Ratio card.** k written as a fraction with its two sides named, image over original ("new over old"), and every later fraction the same way up.
- **Rule card.** (x, y) → (−y, x) with the specific point dropping through it on the next line: (3, 1) → (−1, 3).
- **Proof card.** Two columns, statement and reason, filled one row at a time; the figure's parts named in a row light as it is written.

### The motions (each proves a foundation)

- **Trace.** A translucent copy lifts off the original (the ghost stays) and moves as one piece; its labels ride and take primes as it lands. Proves foundation 2: the figure that moved is the figure that was there.
- **Slide**, **flip**, **turn.** The copy moves along the arrow; turns over across the mirror; rotates about the pivot with the arc sweeping. During each, the L goes along, and the image coordinates appear beside the landed vertex. Proves foundation 3; the flip also shows orientation reverse (the letters now read the other way round).
- **Project.** Rays shoot from the center; each image corner slides out (or in) along its ray to k times the distance; the image draws; parallel marks appear. Proves foundation 5, including a line through the center staying put.
- **Land.** After a sequence, the moved copy settles onto the target figure; the two outlines coincide and pulse once. A near miss shows the gap at one corner. This is the definition of congruent and similar made visible (foundations 4 and 6), and the check for every "which sequence" type.
- **Stack.** The names card builds letter by letter under the figures; columns light with their sides. Foundation 7.
- **Pull apart and stand.** The small triangle of a cut figure, the two of a bow tie, the three of an altitude figure are copied out, turned and slid to sit side by side the same way up, matching angle in the same corner, so matching sides sit in the same place. Then the proportion is read across. Foundations 9 and 10.
- **Swing and cross.** A base is fixed; for SSS two arcs from its ends cross at one point above the base; for SSA a side swings on a compass arc and hits the ray at two points, and two triangles are drawn, both fitting the givens; for ASA two rays cross once. Foundation 8.
- **Grow.** A unit square on the grid becomes k by k and its cells are counted; a unit cube becomes k layers (the app's cube solids). Foundation 11.
- **Dim.** Choices that cannot be right fade together in one beat with the one reason said aloud.

### The scratch-paper sketch

For a coordinate type: a small cross for the axes, the one asked point, its L, and the motion done to the L; the quadrant it landed in. Never all three corners unless all three are asked. For a correspondence type: the two names stacked. For similar triangles: the two drawn the same way up with corners marked 1, 2, 3, and k written beside them as new over old. For a parallel cut: part and whole written on each cut side. For the altitude: the three right triangles pulled apart. For criteria and proofs: the given figure marked.

## 3. How lessons in this family go

### The five moves here

1. **Read.** The problem exactly as the app shows it, choices included. Gold on the word that decides: which figure is the original, which corner is asked (C′, not A′), clockwise or counterclockwise, about the origin or about a named center, across which line, the scale factor as a number (½ shrinks), the order of a chain, the letters of the correspondence, "must be true" or "not always true". One beat says what the answer must look like ("a point in the second quadrant", "longer than 12", "a degree measure less than 180").
2. **At a glance.** The quadrant the image must land in; the orientation of the two figures; whether the image is bigger or smaller than the original; which triangle has the bigger sides; whether a choice scales an angle. Most multiple-choice types lose half their choices here.
3. **The sure fast way.** Below, one per kind, each taught with the picture that says why; never a trick that fails on some case.
4. **Sense.** The sketch confirms: the image is where the turn lands; the same distance from the mirror; the bigger triangle has every side bigger; each hypotenuse piece is less than the hypotenuse; the area ratio is the square of the length ratio.
5. **Check.** Undo the motion and get the original back (flip again, turn the other way, divide by k); do a second corner; cross-multiply the proportion; compute all three side ratios; for a chain, land the copy on the target.

### The sure fast ways

| Kind | Fast and sure | Why it always works |
|---|---|---|
| Slide by ⟨a, b⟩ | add a to x and b to y | every point moves the same |
| Flip over the x-axis; the y-axis | y → −y; x → −x | cross the mirror to the same distance: only the arm that crosses changes |
| Flip over y = x; y = −x | swap; swap and change both signs | the L's arms trade places |
| Half turn about the origin | change both signs | through the center, the same distance beyond |
| Quarter turn about the origin | swap the two numbers, then let the quadrant it lands in set the signs | the L turns; its arms trade lengths and point where the quadrant says |
| Dilation from the origin | multiply both by k | k times as far along the ray |
| Dilation from center C | from C, write the L to P; multiply both arms by k; add back to C | the same, measured from C |
| A chain | one point only, in the written order, one line per step, a sketch per landing | |
| Name the motion from two figures | orientation first (flip or not), then what changed from A to A′, then confirm on B | one corner can agree with two motions (a point on an axis); two corners cannot |
| Scale factor from coordinates | image ÷ original on one nonzero coordinate (or one side); confirm with a second | every coordinate from the center is multiplied by k |
| Rigid, dilation or neither | three side lengths (horizontal and vertical sides by subtraction, slanted ones by the hypotenuse): all equal, rigid; one factor, dilation; otherwise neither | the definitions |
| Rigid from a rule | a rule that only changes signs, swaps x and y, or adds constants is rigid; a factor other than ±1, or x and y mixed into one coordinate, is not | lengths kept or not |
| Corresponding parts | stack the names; a corner is a column, a side is two neighboring columns, the third wraps | the statement is the landing written down |
| Missing side of similar triangles | k from the one pair with both known; the missing side is k times its match | all sides scale by one k |
| An angle in a similar or congruent triangle | copy it; the third angle is 180° minus the other two | angles never scale |
| Parallel cut | part/whole = part/whole on the two cut sides, or part/part = part/part; the parallel side's ratio is part/whole, never part/part | the small triangle is the big one dilated from the top corner by part/whole |
| Bow tie | pair the corners by angle (vertical with vertical, the given equal pair, the rest), then stack the names | a half turn about the crossing and a stretch |
| Altitude to the hypotenuse | a leg squared = the piece it touches × the whole hypotenuse; the altitude squared = one piece × the other | the three triangles stood the same way |
| k, k², k³ | lengths × k, areas and surface areas × k², volumes × k³; from an area ratio k = √, from a volume ratio k = ∛ | the grown square and cube |
| A line under a dilation | same slope; from the origin the intercept × k; from C: unchanged if C is on the line (plug it in), else dilate one point and draw the parallel through it | lines go to parallel lines; a line through the center stays |
| Which criterion | mark the figure; count sides and angles and where they sit: three sides; two sides and the angle between; two angles and any side; two sides and an angle not between: not sufficient; three angles: similar only | foundation 8 |
| Must be true after rigid motions | congruent, every length, every angle, perimeter, area: always; orientation reversed once per reflection, so odd flips reverse, even keep | tracing paper |

The sure way is also the fast way here; there is no faster trick worth teaching. Where a child can be faster, it is by doing less: one corner, not three; one pair, not the whole proportion table.

### Ruling out

The decoys are the misconceptions, so the lesson names them. Quadrant: a choice in the wrong quadrant is gone before any arithmetic. Direction of k: an image bigger than the original cannot come from k = ½. Orientation: a reflection cannot be the answer when both figures read the same way round. Angles: any choice that doubles or halves an angle is gone (the 46° for a 23° angle under k = 2). Correspondence: a proportion that pairs non-neighboring columns is gone. Rigid statements: "larger", "smaller", "a different area" are never true after rigid motions. Dilation about a center: the choice you get by treating the center as the origin is the planted decoy; one glance at the center kills it. Criteria: a choice naming SSS when an angle is given is gone. Dim them together with the one reason, then prove the survivor.

### What children actually get wrong, and the picture that makes it impossible

1. **Reflecting over the x-axis changes x.** The name misleads. The mirror and the dashed perpendicular show the x arm untouched; the point crosses the x-axis, so its height changes.
2. **Clockwise and counterclockwise swapped; the wrong sign on a quarter turn.** The arc with its arrow at the pivot, and the L turning, land the point in the right quadrant; signs are read, not recalled.
3. **Treating a dilation center as the origin.** The composition type's decoy (9, −18) is exactly 3 × (−3, −6) reflected. The L drawn from the center, not the origin, is the lesson.
4. **Adding k instead of multiplying, or scaling one coordinate; thinking ½ enlarges.** The rays: every point k times as far, pulled in for k < 1.
5. **Scaling an angle in a similar figure.** The pull-apart-and-stand motion lays the small triangle's angle on the big triangle's angle: they coincide.
6. **Reading correspondence from the picture or alphabetically instead of from the statement.** △ABC ~ △FDE pairs AB with FD. The names card, every time.
7. **Flipping the ratio or mixing which triangle is on top.** The ratio card keeps new over old on both sides; the check is that k agrees on a second pair.
8. **The parallel cut: DE/BC = AD/DB.** The dilation from A shows DE/BC is part over whole; the part/part fraction is a different number (3/3 is 1 while 3/6 is ½ in the given example).
9. **Believing a turn or flip can make a triangle larger, or that orientation survives a flip.** The traced copy never changes size; the letters visibly reverse on the flip.
10. **SSA accepted as SAS ("side, side, angle, so it works").** The swing: two triangles drawn from the same givens.
11. **AA or AAA taken as proof of congruence.** Any size fits; the dilation shows it.
12. **Area or volume scaled by k.** The grown square and cube: 9 cells, 27 cubes.
13. **The altitude facts paired wrong (a leg with the far piece).** Standing the three triangles the same way puts the leg and the piece it touches in the same place.
14. **A chain done in the wrong order, or all three corners transformed when one was asked.** One point, one line per step, in the written order.

### Lines to remember

- "The letters tell who lands on whom."
- "Tracing paper never stretches. Slide, turn, flip: same size, same shape, new place."
- "Draw the L, move the L, read where its arms point."
- "Flip over the x-axis: you cross the x-axis, so your height changes sign."
- "Quarter turn: swap the numbers; the quadrant sets the signs. Half turn: both signs."
- "Dilate: from the center, k times as far. From the origin, that is multiply both."
- "New over old, the same way up every time."
- "Angles copy; sides scale."
- "Two angles fix the shape. Three parts with a side fix the triangle, unless the angle is not between the sides."
- "A parallel cut is a smaller copy from the same corner."
- "A leg squared is the piece it touches times the whole hypotenuse."
- "Lengths times k, areas times k squared, volumes times k cubed."
- "One flip turns the letters round; two flips turn them back."

### One method or mixed

Most types here are one method. These are mixed and must be taught as reading, one example per kind, never as one recipe: `dilation-measure` (factor from lengths, image point from a center, what is kept), `transformation-invariant-claims` (which transformation preserves; is this image rigid from coordinates), `congruence-by-rigid-motion` (which sequence; which parts must be equal), `aa-similarity` (third angle; which proportion), `geo-rigid-statement`, `geo-parallel-statement` and `similarity-by-transformation` (statements judged for every case), `similar-triangles-work` (a chain: angle, k, two sides).

At Geometry the foundation is most of the lesson: once the swing has been seen, criteria are reading; once the three triangles have stood the same way, the altitude facts are reading. At grade 8, a short "why" with the L before each kind of motion, then the example.

## 4. Tools to build

**Exists today:** the plane (`axes`, `grid`, haloed numbers), `poly` with `solid` tiles that lift and cast a shadow, `seg` with `ticks`, `dash`, `arrows`, `extend`, `dot`, `angle` with `right`, `label`, `dim`, `circle` with points gliding on it, `vector`, `turn` about a point, `flip` over a line, `slide`, `move`, `view`, `highlight`, `group`, the board with wrapped sentence choices, `table` (for `congruence-claims-table`), and the app's cube solids through `scene`. Specified by the place-value design and needed by nearly every multiple-choice type here: `dim`. Open kit wishes from KAIZEN that this family makes requirements: labels that ride turned and flipped parts; labels that avoid axis numbers.

### 4.1 `copy` and the transform actions: the kit computes the image

```json
{ "do": "reflect", "target": "t", "over": "x-axis", "as": "t1", "primes": true, "ghost": true, "readout": "C" }
```

- `copy` lifts a translucent copy of a polygon (its dots, labels and marks ride) and leaves a muted ghost. `translate` (`by: [3, -5]`), `reflect` (`over`: `x-axis`, `y-axis`, `y=x`, `y=-x`, or two point names), `rotate` (`about`, `deg`, negative for clockwise), `dilate` (`center`, `k`) each copy and move in one action, or act on an existing copy. The kit computes every image coordinate from the figure's points, as `stack` computes digits; the author paces. `primes: true` renames labels A → A′ as they land; `readout: "C"` writes the landed coordinates of one corner beside it.
- `--dry` checks that the landed coordinates equal the answer in `facts.ts` for the example's seed, and refuses a `dilate` whose `k` or center disagrees with the problem.
- Chains: actions on the same copy in sequence; each landing can `ghost` so the path of one point stays visible.

### 4.2 Apparatus parts

- `ell`: `{ "ell": ["O", "P"], "labels": true }` draws the dashed horizontal and vertical arms from one point to another with their lengths; it is a part, so `rotate`, `reflect` and `dilate` carry it along with the copy when it is in the same `group`.
- `mirror`: the line (an axis or two points) with the perpendicular from a named vertex to its image, the right-angle box and equal ticks.
- `pivot`: the center dot, radii to a named vertex and its image, the arc with an arrow and degrees; `right: true` boxes a quarter turn.
- `arrow`: the slide's vector from a vertex to its image, with a TeX label and faint copies at the other corners.
- `rays`: from the center through each corner of a polygon, past its image; the two L's (original and k times) on one named corner; parallel marks on one side pair.
- Each is a figure part shown on the word; `--dry` reports a label that lands on an axis number or another label.

### 4.3 `land`

`{"do":"land","target":"t1","on":"u"}` slides the moved copy the last bit onto the target and pulses the coinciding outlines. If they do not coincide within the grid's tolerance, the kit shows the gap at the worst corner and `--dry` reports it, so a false "congruent" cannot be staged.

### 4.4 Marks by count, labels that ride

`angle` gains `marks: 1 | 2 | 3` (arcs); `seg.ticks` takes a count (confirm it does). Labels, tick marks, arcs and right-angle boxes travel with `turn`, `flip`, `slide` and the transform actions and stay upright. A `stand` helper is not a new primitive: `copy` plus `rotate`, `reflect`, `slide` with a widened `view` pulls a triangle out of a composite figure and sets it beside the others; the author gives the destination, the kit does the motion.

### 4.5 `names` card

```json
{ "do": "names", "id": "nm", "tri": ["ABC", "FDE"], "rel": "\\sim" }
{ "do": "pair", "of": "nm", "side": "AB", "at": "AB" }
```

A board card: two names stacked, three ruled columns, the relation between. `pair` lights the columns of a side (or one column for an angle) gold and writes the matched part beside it (AB ↔ FD); the matching sides on the figure light in the same beat through `highlight`.

### 4.6 `meet` points: swing and cross

A point defined by an intersection: `{ "meet": ["c1", "c2"], "pick": "upper" }` for two circles, `{ "meet": ["c1", "r"], "pick": 1 }` for a circle and a ray or segment (`pick` chooses when there are two). With `glide` on a circle already in the kit, the SSA swing is a segment to a gliding point and two `meet` dots where it hits the ray; SSS is two circles and their one upper meet; ASA is two rays (`seg` with `extend`) and their meet.

### 4.7 `proof` card

```json
{ "do": "proof", "id": "p", "given": "...", "prove": "..." }
{ "do": "prove", "of": "p", "row": 1, "statement": "$\\angle SEM\\cong\\angle LEI$", "reason": "Vertical angles are congruent.", "marks": ["aSEM", "aLEI"], "at": "Vertical" }
```

Two columns, statement and reason, one row per beat; `marks` light the named figure parts while the row is written. The curriculum plan lists this card under family 29; it is built here first, to the depth two types need (five or six rows, reasons as short sentences). Wrapped text fits the phone layout; `--dry` reports a row that overflows.

### 4.8 Small additions

`dim` as specified for place value; a coordinate readout `(@x, @y)` for any named point, not only points on a plot; `view` that widens by itself when an image or rays leave the card (reported by `--dry`).

### Build order, by what each piece unlocks soonest

1. `copy` and the four transform actions with primes, ghosts, riding labels and readouts: the eleven grade-8 types and the eight Geometry coordinate types.
2. `ell`, `mirror`, `pivot`, `arrow`: the "why" of every coordinate rule.
3. `rays`, `dilate` from any center, `land`: compositions, center-finding, the "is it rigid or a dilation" and "which sequence" types.
4. Marks by count and the `names` card: the seven correspondence and similar-side types.
5. `meet` points: criteria.
6. `proof` card: the two proof types.
7. `dim` and readouts, if not already landed with place value.

## 5. The seam: who writes what

**The strongest writer.** Lessons where the screenplay is the design, because a motion proves or the reasoning is the content:
- `identify-rigid-motion` and `transformation-rule-slots`: the picture of the family is born here (the trace, the L, the three apparatus pieces, orientation). A weak author describes the motions instead of staging them.
- `transformation-composition-image` and `geo-dilation-center`: the L from a center that is not the origin, order of a chain, reading rays backward to find a center.
- `triangle-congruence-criteria`: the swing and the crossing arcs are the explanation HSG.CO.B.8 asks for; the decoys are sentences that reason.
- `geo-altitude-hypotenuse` (with `similarity-theorem-length` and `rg-altitude-segment` as its siblings): standing the three triangles the same way, deriving the three facts once, the Pythagorean coda, roots simplified.
- `crossing-segment-similarity`: the bow tie's correspondence from two different givens.
- `two-column-congruence-proof`, `similarity-proof-aa`: every reason must be right and in order.
- The "must be true" and "not always true" types: `geo-rigid-statement`, `geo-parallel-statement`, `transformation-invariant-claims`, `geo-area-changing-transformation`, `similarity-by-transformation`: judging a statement for every case, not one picture.
- The mixed types named in Part 3.

**A capable cheaper model, from a concept and the finished kit.** One method, one kit action, the answer checkable by `--dry`:
- `transformation-image`, `place-transformed-image`, `dilation-image`, `dilation-scale-factor`, `dilation-rule-slots`: one transform action, one readout, after the foundation lessons exist to point at.
- `mcas-congruence-statement-select`, `corresponding-angle-transfer`, `aa-similarity`'s proportion case, `similar-triangles-side`, `similar-triangle-side`: the names card and one ratio.
- `geo-parallel-segment-length`, `parallel-segment-steps`, `geo-parallel-perimeter`: the side-splitter after its concept lesson shows the dilation from the corner.
- `scale-factor-effects-blanks`, `similar-solid-volume`, `similar-solid-scale-chain`: k, k², k³ on the grown square and cube.
- `geo-dilation-line-image`: same slope, intercept times k; `geo-reflection-side`: two corners through two flips; `congruence-claims-table`: reading rules.

The review of a proof, a "must be true" or a criteria lesson stays with the top model, as PROCESS.md requires: the fault in those is in the reasoning, which no check catches.

## 6. Misfiled, badly conceived, and what the problems never practice

> Flagging a type here means its lesson should not inherit the fault, never that the app should drop it.

**Misfiled or mislinked.**
- `transformation-composition-image` sits in `rigid-motions` but half of it is a dilation about a center that is not the origin: the hardest idea in the grade-8 half, filed under the easiest skill. Move it to `dilations-similarity`, and say in the template that a non-origin center is a stretch for 8.G.A.3.
- `similar-triangles-side` and `similar-triangles-work` are linked to 6.RP.A.3 (ratio reasoning). They are 8.G.A.4 (similar figures) with HSG.SRT.B.5 ahead; the proportion is the tool, not the standard.
- `scale-factor-effects-blanks` is linked to 7.G.B.6 (area and volume of composed figures). Its content is how a scale factor changes perimeter and area; the nearest standard is 7.G.A.1 (scale drawings), and the k² fact itself is not stated in any Common Core standard.
- `similar-solid-volume` and `similar-solid-scale-chain` have no standard, and the k³ relation is not in Common Core (it is a common state standard). Keep them, mark them as state-standard material, and note that they stand on this family's k rather than on `dilation-properties` (HSG.SRT.A.1), where they are filed.
- The skill `similarity-theorems` is linked to HSG.SRT.B.4 (prove theorems), but every one of its seven types applies a theorem (B.5); none proves. Either relink the skill or add a proof type.
- `similar-triangle-side` is described as "find a missing length" but its variants ask for area and perimeter of similar triangles; rename it so the lesson is designed for what it asks.
- `transformation-sequence-mapping` is described as a sequence, but its example answer is one transformation; fine, but the template text should not promise a chain it does not give.

**Badly conceived or needing a check.**
- `congruence-by-rigid-motion`'s first example reads "Triangle ABC is congruent to triangle DEF" with no coordinates in the text; the figure must carry them or the item cannot be answered. Confirm the figure is always present, and that `facts.ts` exposes it to the concept stage.
- `two-column-congruence-proof` and `similarity-proof-aa` answers are option ids ("sh", "ra"); the concept stage needs the option texts from `facts.ts` to write a lesson that matches the app's reasons word for word.
- `triangle-congruence-criteria`: if any variant gives two sides and a right angle not between them, the correct assessment is the hypotenuse-leg case, not "not sufficient"; check the generator excludes it or offers it.
- `geo-dilation-center`'s second example gives k and asks only the center; fine, but it is a different kind from the first (both unknown); treat as two cases.
- Three types share one figure and one method: `geo-altitude-hypotenuse`, `similarity-theorem-length`, `rg-altitude-segment` (exact roots, integers, nearest tenth). `geo-parallel-segment-length` and `parallel-segment-steps` are the same problem answered whole and in steps. `dilation-image`, `dilation-scale-factor` and `dilation-rule-slots` are one idea three ways. Their lessons should share one concept and one picture, with the difference stated.

**Standards clauses the problems never practice** (exact text).
- 8.G.A.1 a, b, c ("Lines are taken to lines, and line segments to line segments of the same length... Angles are taken to angles of the same measure... Parallel lines are taken to parallel lines"): no grade-8 type asks what a rigid motion keeps; the first Geometry type to do so is `transformation-invariant-claims`. The grade-8 foundation lesson teaches it anyway, as the reason the rules work.
- 8.G.A.2 and 8.G.A.4, "describe a sequence that exhibits the congruence" and "...the similarity": at grade 8 only single motions are asked (`identify-rigid-motion`); sequences appear only in Geometry types.
- HSG.CO.A.4, "Develop definitions of rotations, reflections, and translations in terms of angles, circles, perpendicular lines, parallel lines, and line segments": no type asks for a definition. The mirror's perpendicular and the pivot's equal radii are those definitions drawn; the Geometry foundation lesson says so.
- HSG.CO.A.5, "draw the transformed figure": only `place-transformed-image` (grade 8) draws; nothing in Geometry does.
- HSG.CO.B.7, the "if and only if": the forward direction is practiced (`congruence-by-rigid-motion`'s second kind); the converse, that six equal parts imply a rigid motion exists, is never asked.
- HSG.CO.B.8, "Explain how the criteria for triangle congruence (ASA, SAS, and SSS) follow from the definition of congruence in terms of rigid motions": `triangle-congruence-criteria` applies the criteria; no type asks for the explanation. The swing and crossing arcs are that explanation, so the lesson gives it.
- HSG.SRT.A.3, "Use the properties of similarity transformations to establish the AA criterion": `aa-similarity` applies AA; nothing establishes it.
- HSG.SRT.B.4, "the Pythagorean Theorem proved using triangle similarity": never asked; the altitude lesson's coda shows it. The converse of the parallel-cut theorem is practiced (`parallel-segment-steps`, "what must EC be so that DE is parallel to BC").
- 8.G.A.3's dilations "using coordinates" and HSG.SRT.A.1a's "leaves a line passing through the center unchanged" are both practiced; no gap there.

## 7. The pilot: five problem types to build first

The pilot covers the whole arc in one sitting: both grade bands, every apparatus piece, the kit computing images, the names card, the motion that proves a criterion, and the five habits on open answers, letter choices and sentence choices.

| # | Problem type | Grade | Why it is in the pilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `identify-rigid-motion` | 8 | The picture is born: the traced copy and its ghost, the mirror, the pivot, the arrow, orientation read from the letters, "what changed from A to A′" confirmed on B, decoys dimmed by orientation and by the second corner. Exercises `copy`, `reflect`, `rotate`, `translate`, `mirror`, `pivot`, `arrow`, `dim`. If this is not beautiful, nothing after it will be. |
| 2 | `transformation-image` | 8 | The highest-volume test type. The L turned a quarter turn: swap, then the quadrant sets the signs; the asked corner only; the readout at the landing; quadrant rule-out. Exercises `ell`, readouts, `--dry` checking the landed point against `facts.ts`. |
| 3 | `transformation-composition-image` | 8 | The hardest grade-8 idea: the L from a center that is not the origin, k times, then a flip or half turn, in the written order; the planted decoy that treats the center as the origin. Exercises `rays`, `dilate` with a center, chained actions with ghosts. If the picture carries this, `dilation-image`, `dilation-scale-factor` and `geo-dilation-center` follow. |
| 4 | `triangle-congruence-criteria` | Geo | The motion that proves: arcs that cross once for SSS, rays that cross once for ASA, the swing that hits twice for SSA; marking the figure as the scratch-paper habit; sentence choices that are the misconceptions, named and dimmed. Exercises `meet`, marks by count, `names`. The explanation HSG.CO.B.8 asks for and the app never does. |
| 5 | `parallel-segment-steps` | Geo | The parallel cut as the grade-8 dilation from the top corner, so the family's two halves meet; part/whole against part/part, the one trap that costs the most points; a steps-format answer. Exercises `rays` on a plain figure, pull-apart-and-stand, the ratio card. Serves four sibling types at once. |

If the five land, the next wave is `geo-altitude-hypotenuse` (standing three triangles, the Pythagorean coda), `mcas-congruence-statement-select` (the names card on a select-many), `geo-rigid-statement` (orientation and parity on sentence choices) and `two-column-congruence-proof` (the proof card), which complete the kit.
