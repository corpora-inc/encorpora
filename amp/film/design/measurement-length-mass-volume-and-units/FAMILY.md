# Measurement: length, mass, volume and units, K to A1: family design

Eight skills, 42 problem types, from "which strip is shorter" to cubic centimeters and percent error. Planning snapshot: `src/curriculum/catalog.ts`, the engine templates and `film/facts.ts` for every variant, snapshot 2026-11-09. Place value's design is the model, and this family leans on it harder than any other: metric conversion is the shift, and mixed units are the trade with a new group size.

This design covers the idea, the ladder, the picture, how lessons read, the kit, the seam, the alignment findings, the pilot. Per-type concepts use these foundations and pictures.

---

## 1. The idea underneath the whole family

Measuring is choosing a unit and counting how many copies of it fill the thing, end to end, with no gaps and no overlaps. Everything from kindergarten to Algebra 1 is that one act and its consequences: what about a thing can be counted this way (its attributes), how to compare two things before you have a unit, how the count changes when the unit changes, how tools do the counting for you, how to rename an amount in a different unit, and how units ride through arithmetic.

The line the child must own by grade 2 and still be using in Algebra 1: **the amount never changes; the count changes with the unit. Smaller unit, more of them.** A child who holds that line never multiplies in the wrong direction, because before any arithmetic they know whether the number should get bigger or smaller.

### The foundations, in the order they are built

1. **A thing has several attributes you can measure, and each is a different question.** (K) How long, how heavy, how much it holds. Color and shape are not amounts. Define *attribute* in a child's words: something a thing can have more or less of. Each attribute has its own fair way to compare: lay side by side, hang on a balance, pour one into the other. [K.MD.A.1]

2. **Compare fairly.** (K) Lengths are compared only with the starts lined up; on a balance the heavier side goes down and level means the same; a container holds more if the other's water fits inside with room to spare. The words: longer, shorter, taller, heavier, lighter, holds more, holds less. [K.MD.A.2]

3. **Comparisons chain, and a third thing can carry a length.** (1) If red is shorter than blue and blue is shorter than purple, red is shortest without looking. A string as long as the door can be carried to the table. [1.MD.A.1]

4. **A unit, and measuring is counting it.** (1) Same-size copies, starting at the object's start, touching, not overlapping; the count is the length. From family 1: the last number said is how many. A gap, an overlap, a start in the middle, or two sizes of unit mixed in all break the count, and the lesson shows each break, not a rule against it. [1.MD.A.2]

5. **Smaller unit, more of them.** (1 to 2) The same ribbon takes more cubes than paper clips, because a cube is shorter. The ribbon did not change; the count did. This is the key to the whole of grades 4 and 5. [2.MD.A.2]

6. **A ruler is the units already laid down, with the count written at every join.** (2) The number at a mark says how many units from the zero end. An object's length is the number of spaces between its two ends: end reading minus start reading. Starting at 0 is the easy case where that is just the end reading. Fraction marks are one space cut into halves or quarters. The ruler is a number line (P3) on a stick, and from here on every measuring tool is one. [2.MD.A.1, A.4; 3.MD.B.4]

7. **Standard units, each with a benchmark you carry around.** (2 to 4) Everyone's cube is a different size; a centimeter is the same everywhere. For every unit the child owns one or two real things that are about that size (section 3 lists them). Estimating is repeating a benchmark; a "reasonable unit" is the right attribute at the right size. [2.MD.A.3; 3.MD.A.2 estimate; 4.MD.A.1 relative sizes]

8. **Every tool is a number line: find the step, then read where the top points.** (3) The beaker's scale, the thermometer, the balance with its standard masses. The step between two labeled marks is the gap divided by the jumps (place value's line, reused). The liquid's top is the reading. On a balance the standard masses are the units: when the beam is level, add them. [3.MD.A.2]

9. **One-step stories in one unit are family 8's bars with grams and liters in them.** (3) Totals, differences, equal groups, shares. "How many grams heavier" is a difference; the bar shows it. Nothing new is taught; the lesson names the picture and reads the story. [3.MD.A.2]

10. **Converting is renaming; the fact tells the group size.** (4) "1 foot = 12 inches" says a foot is a group of twelve inches. Renaming in the smaller unit makes the count grow: multiply by the group size. The two-column table (1, 12), (2, 24), (3, 36) is the double ruler written as numbers. Before computing, say the direction aloud. [4.MD.A.1]

11. **Metric is place value with new names.** (4 to 5) Kilo is the thousands column, centi the hundredths, milli the thousandths; the base unit (meter, gram, liter) is the ones. Converting is the shift from family 2; the point stays and the digits move. [4.MD.A.1, 5.MD.A.1]

12. **To the bigger unit, divide, and the leftover stays in the smaller unit.** (5) 23 feet: groups of three make 7 yards with 2 feet left over. A mixed measure is two counts in two units; convert every part before combining anything; when the small count reaches the group size, trade (12 inches become 1 foot, exactly as ten ones became a ten, but the group is twelve). From family 7: division with a remainder; from family 2: the trade. [5.MD.A.1]

13. **A conversion fact is a fraction equal to one, and units cancel like factors.** (A1) 12 inches over 1 foot is the same length over itself: one. Multiplying by one changes nothing but the name. A chain is several ones; the unit left standing is the answer's unit. A compound unit is converted piece by piece: a square unit twice, a cubic unit three times, a rate's top and bottom each. [HSN.Q.A.1; 6.RP.A.3d]

14. **A reading is not the truth; it is the middle of where the truth could be.** (A1) An instrument's reading with its largest possible error is an interval; the greatest and smallest possible values are its ends. And before a model is written, each quantity is named with its unit, and the rate's unit decides the variable's unit. [HSN.Q.A.2, A.3; 7.RP.A.3]

### What this family takes from others, by name

Counting (1): the count is how many. Place value (2): the shift for metric, the trade for mixed units, "find the step first" for scales. Addition and subtraction (6): differences ("how much warmer", "how much longer"), hops on the line. Multiplication and division (7): equal groups for the group size, division with a remainder for leftovers. Fractions (9): a space cut into fourths; 5½ meters. Ratio (10): the conversion factor as a rate, the double number line. Area and volume (24, 25): the square meter and the cubic meter for compound units. Time (20): hours over minutes is the clock unrolled. The bar (P4) from family 8 carries every story.

## 2. The visual language

One picture grows across the grades: **the unit laid along the thing** becomes **the ruler**, the ruler becomes **two rulers on one thing**, and two rulers become **the fraction equal to one**. Beside it stand the tools that are rulers in disguise (beaker, thermometer, balance) and the three K pictures of comparing before you have a unit. Everything is drawn to the size it claims, and everything is labeled, because the test of a picture here is whether a child could sketch it on scratch paper and get the answer from the sketch.

### The object and the unit

- Objects (ribbon, strip, crayon, pencil) are horizontal strips in teal, drawn to scale against their units: a ribbon spanned by 9 paper clips is exactly 9 clips long; a paper clip "as long as 3 cubes" is exactly 3 cubes long. Real things (a pumpkin, a jug, a bowling ball) are the app's own object figures.
- Units are small identical shapes laid in a row touching the object's lower edge, each one touching the next: a cube is a square, a paper clip a rounded loop, a centimeter an empty tick-space. A count label beside the row ticks as each unit lands.
- In K comparisons, two strips (teal and rust) start on one vertical **start line**. Nothing is compared until both touch it.

### The ruler

- A horizontal bar, zero at the left end, a tick at every unit, the number under each tick, the unit's name at the right end. The **spaces** between ticks are the units; a space lights when it is counted. Fraction marks are shorter ticks, labeled when the lesson names them.
- The object sits just above the ruler. Thin gold lines drop from its two ends to the ticks they meet. The spaces between the lines light one at a time with a count.
- **The double ruler:** a second scale under the first, the same length and the same zero, with a tick at every larger unit. The larger unit's scale is on top in **blue**, the smaller unit's below in **violet**, each named at its left end. Every blue tick sits exactly over a violet tick: 1 ft over 12 in, 2 ft over 24 in. A gold vertical line through both scales reads one length in both names at once.
- **Vertical rulers:** the beaker's scale and the thermometer are the same ruler stood on end, lowest label at the bottom, the step read from two labels. The liquid is a teal fill; a gold line runs from its top to the scale.

### Groups of units

- **Brackets:** a bracket over three consecutive foot-spaces is labeled "1 yd"; brackets are laid left to right; the spaces left unbracketed keep their own count. This is the remainder, visible.
- **The re-cut bar for capacity and weight:** one bar is a gallon; cut it into 4 quarts, each quart into 2 pints, each pint into 2 cups, the whole never changing length. The same move is 16 ounces in a pound. It is family 9's re-cut with unit names on the pieces, so the child who saw a quarter sees why 4 gallons is 16 quarts.

### The chart with unit headers

The place-value chart with the place names replaced by unit names: km | hm | dam | m | dm | cm | mm (kg to mg, kL to mL the same). The base unit's column is the ones; the point sits beside it; digits sit under their headers. Converting is the shift, exactly as in family 2, and the lesson says once: "kilo is the thousands column."

### The table

Two columns, larger unit on the left in blue, smaller on the right in violet, one row per read-across of the double ruler, "× 12" on an arrow above the columns and "÷ 12" below. The table is the double ruler written down, and the lesson builds it from the ruler before it is ever read on its own.

### The balance and the containers

- The balance: two pans on a beam that tilts toward the heavier side and sits level when the sides match. Mass is not size, so the picture never pretends it is: standard masses are labeled blocks ("100 g", "500 g"), and the truth is the running total label that counts as they land.
- Containers: a bottle, jug, jar or bowl in outline; water as a teal fill. Pouring drains one and raises the other; a spill over the rim, a level exactly at the rim, or room left under the rim is the whole answer.

### Colors, one meaning each

- **teal:** the thing being measured and its measure: the strip, the liquid, the digits of the given amount.
- **rust:** the second thing in a comparison; what is taken away in a difference.
- **gold:** the one thing being read right now: the question's unit word, the tick under an object's end, the read-across line, the pair of units cancelling, the digit that decides.
- **blue:** the larger unit, everywhere: its scale, its table column, the bottom of the conversion fraction. **violet:** the smaller unit: its scale, its column, the top of the fraction. The child learns that violet numbers are the big ones. "Smaller unit, more of them" is colored.
- **muted:** unlit spaces, ruled-out choices, empty table cells, placeholder zeros.

### The motions, and what each one proves

- **Align** (K): two strips slide until both touch the start line; the one sticking out is longer. Comparing ends without aligning starts is the error the motion makes impossible.
- **Tilt** (K): the pan with the heavier object drops; level means equal. Done once with a big pillow against a small rock, so size and weight come apart.
- **Pour** (K): the water leaves A and rises in B; it spills, fits exactly, or leaves room. Done once with a tall thin glass into a short wide one.
- **Lay** (1): units arrive one at a time from the object's start, touching, the count ticking. A second pass with a gap, an overlap, and a start in the middle shows the count go wrong.
- **Re-lay** (1 to 2): the same object measured again with a longer unit; fewer copies. The object never moves.
- **Join** (2): the laid units fuse into one ruler; numbers appear at the joins, 0 at the start. The number at a join counts the units before it.
- **Slide** (2): the object slides from 0 to start at 3; the lit spaces slide with it, still five; the end now reads 8. Length is 8 minus 3, and it was 5 all along.
- **Cut** (2 to 3): one space splits into two, then four, labels ½, ¼, ¾ appearing. The same cut as place value's zoom, into two instead of ten.
- **Step** (3 and up): the arc between two labeled marks with the step written on it, from `numberline`. Always before any reading.
- **Fill and read** (3): the water rises to a mark; the gold line runs to the scale.
- **Level** (3): standard masses land one by one, the beam settling, the total counting 100, 200, 500, 700, 900.
- **Across** (4): the gold line reads one tick in both scales; a table row writes itself from it.
- **Shift** (4 to 5): family 2's motion in the unit chart.
- **Bracket** (5): groups of n spaces bracketed left to right; the leftovers stay lit and counted.
- **Re-cut** (4 to 5): the gallon bar into quarts, pints, cups.
- **Cancel** (A1): the same unit on the top of one fraction and the bottom of another light gold together and fade together; what remains is the answer's unit. This is standard notation, kept visually apart from the board's mistake strike.
- **Square and cube** (A1): a one-meter square with 100 centimeters along each side; cut into 10 by 10 cells, each 10 cm by 10 cm; zoom into one cell: 10 by 10 again. A hundred hundreds. Then the cube: a hundred of those layers.
- **Interval** (A1): the reading drops as a dot; the error is bracketed on both sides; the stretch shades with both ends filled.

### What a student should sketch

K to 1: two strips on a start line; a row of touching boxes under a strip. Grade 2: a short ruler with the strip's two ends marked, "8 − 3". Grade 3: a scale with two labels and the step on an arc. Grade 4: two unit names with the fact between them and an arrow with the direction: "ft → in, smaller, ×12". Grade 5: "23 ÷ 3 = 7 R 2, so 7 yd 2 ft". A1: the fractions with their units, the matching units slashed. Every picture above is one of these sketches, drawn well.

## 3. How lessons in this family go

Every film opens by naming the attribute and the unit, says the foundation it stands on in one line ("you already know a ruler is a number line"), builds its picture in front of the learner, and only then shows the problem as the app shows it, with its choices. Then the five habits, in this family's form.

**Read.** Four questions, answered before any work, each in a beat:
- Which attribute? The unit names it, and the attribute names the tool: ruler for length, balance or scale for how heavy, measuring cup or beaker for how much it holds, thermometer for how hot, clock for how long it takes.
- Which unit is asked? That word goes gold. The answer is in that unit and no other, and the lesson says what the answer must look like ("a count of inches, bigger than 7").
- Which direction? Is the asked unit smaller (the number grows) or larger (the number shrinks)? Said aloud before any arithmetic, every time, in grade 4 and in Algebra 1.
- Which act? One measure read off a tool; a rename; a difference; a total; a count of whole groups with a leftover. The verb in the story decides, and the bar from family 8 shows it when there is a story.

Grade K to 2 vocabulary is settled once: how long, how heavy, how much it holds. Grade 3 says "mass" for grams and kilograms and "weight" for pounds, with one line that both answer "how heavy."

**At a glance.**
- The direction tells you the answer is bigger or smaller than the given number before you touch it.
- Metric: the prefix's zeros tell you how many places the digits move; 11 km has three more zeros as meters.
- "Which measurement has the greater number" needs no arithmetic ever: the smaller unit has the bigger number. This shortcut always works and is taught as the method, on the double ruler.
- A mixed measure's small part must be less than the group size, so "2 yd 7 ft" is nonsense on sight.
- Benchmarks kill choices: a 7-foot pencil, a 2-gallon car, a 12-inch drive between towns.
- A decimal factor rounds for an estimate first: 29 meters at about 3 feet each is about 90 feet, so 95.1.

**The sure fast way.** Each always works; the picture says why.
- On a ruler: end minus start; or count the spaces between the ends, never the ticks.
- On any scale: find the step (gap divided by jumps), then count from the nearest label.
- On a balance: when it is level, add the standard masses.
- Metric: name the prefix, shift the digits, fill with zeros.
- Customary: the fact is the group size. Smaller unit: multiply by it. Larger unit: divide by it; the remainder stays in the smaller unit.
- Mixed measures: every part to the smallest unit first, then combine; for the reverse, divide and keep the remainder.
- A chain: write each fact as a fraction with the unit you want to lose on the opposite side from where it is now; cancel; multiply the numbers; the unit left is the answer's.
- A compound unit: convert the unit, then raise the factor with it: 100 cm per meter becomes 10,000 for square centimeters and 1,000,000 for cubic.
- An error bound: the reading plus or minus the percent of the reading; greatest is plus, smallest is minus.

Never taught: "when you convert, multiply" (it fails half the time), moving a point for customary units, or any rule about "bigger to smaller" without the picture that says why the count grows.

**Sense.** More small units than big ones; a difference is less than both amounts; a leftover is less than the group size; the whole bows are at most the length divided by one bow; a chain's leftover unit matches the question; for a decimal factor, the estimate.

**Check.** Convert back (11,000 m ÷ 1,000 = 11 km). Multiply back with the remainder (7 × 3 + 2 = 23). On a scale, count from the other label. Add a difference back. Reread the question's unit against the answer's unit; the lesson asks "inches, or feet?" aloud. In Algebra 1, the units that cancelled are the check: if the unit left is not the one asked, a factor is upside down.

**Rule out.** Choices that go the wrong direction dim together with the one reason ("these are smaller than 7, and inches must be more than feet"). A unit of the wrong attribute dims (gallons for a car's weight). A mixed form with a leftover too big dims. A concatenation (225 minutes for 2 hours 25 minutes, 38 inches for 3 feet 8 inches) is named as the trap it is and dims. In a chain, choices whose units do not come out of the cancelling dim. Then the one left is proved, never just chosen.

### Benchmarks

The estimation and "reasonable unit" types (six of them) rest entirely on a child owning a few real sizes. One benchmark card per attribute, drawn once with the app's object figures and reused by every lesson that needs it:

| Unit | About the size of |
|---|---|
| centimeter | the width of a fingernail |
| inch | the top joint of a thumb |
| foot | a sheet of paper's long side, a big shoe |
| yard | a baseball bat, a big step |
| meter | a little more than a yard, a doorway's width |
| kilometer | ten minutes of walking |
| mile | twenty minutes of walking |
| gram | a paper clip |
| kilogram | a liter bottle of water, a big textbook |
| ounce | a slice of bread |
| pound | a loaf of bread, a football |
| ton | a small car |
| milliliter | a few drops; a teaspoon is five |
| cup | a cup |
| liter | a big water bottle |
| gallon | a milk jug |
| degrees Celsius | 0 water freezes, 20 a comfortable room, 37 a body, 100 water boils |

The lesson picks the benchmark, repeats it along the object, and reads the estimate; the choices then rule themselves out.

### The misconceptions children actually have, and the motion that ends each

- **K:** judging length by where the ends are when the starts differ (align). Judging weight by size, a big pillow against a small rock (tilt). Judging capacity by height, a tall thin glass against a short wide one (pour).
- **Grade 1:** counting units laid with gaps or overlaps, or starting in the middle of the object (lay, done wrong on screen); mixing two sizes of unit in one count; believing the longer unit gives more units because "bigger is more" (re-lay).
- **Grade 2:** reading the end number on a shifted ruler (8 for a strip from 3 to 8), the most-missed ruler item there is (slide); counting ticks instead of spaces, so a 5-cm strip reads 6 (lit spaces with a count); on a fractional ruler, counting the little ticks as inches (cut).
- **Grade 3:** assuming the step is 1 when the labels go by 2 or 5 or 10 (step, before every reading); taking one standard mass as the answer instead of their total (level with the count); counting ticks between two thermometer readings instead of degrees (step).
- **Grade 4:** the wrong direction, dividing feet into inches; the wrong fact, 3 and 12 and 16 and 8 confused (the fact is read aloud and written as the two names first); concatenation, 3 ft 8 in = 38 in, 2 h 25 min = 225 min (every part converted on the double ruler, then combined); metric zeros, 1 km = 100 m (the unit chart with every column named).
- **Grade 5:** 23 ft = 2 yd 7 ft, the trade not finished; 7.67 yd when yards and feet were asked (bracket); 5 m + 35 cm = 40 of something (nothing is added until the names match); 2938 m = 29.38 km, two shifts instead of three (the chart's columns).
- **Algebra 1:** the factor upside down, so the unit appears twice on top and nothing cancels (cancel shows the pair, or fails to); square meters to square centimeters by 100 (square); converting only the top of a rate (both scales); "greatest possible mass" by subtracting (interval).

### Lines worth remembering

"Line up the starts." "No gaps, no overlaps, same unit every time." "Smaller unit, more of them." "A ruler is the units already laid down. Count the spaces, not the lines." "Length is end minus start." "Find the step first." "Read where the top of the liquid points." "Level means equal; then add the masses." "Converting changes the count, never the amount." "Smaller unit, bigger number: multiply. Bigger unit, smaller number: divide." "Kilo is the thousands column." "Every part gets converted; the leftover stays in the small unit." "A conversion fraction is one; put the unit you want to lose on the other side." "Square the unit too." "The answer is the unit left standing." "The reading is the middle of where the truth could be."

### Pace and voice

K to 2: short sentences, the real objects, the problem read exactly as the child sees it, no strike-outs, the count said aloud as it ticks. Grades 3 to 5: one idea per beat, the direction said before the arithmetic, the picture holding still while it is read. Algebra 1: the double ruler once, as the why, then the board carries the work with units written in every line; show why conversion works even when learners already know a procedure.

## 4. Tools to build

**Exists today:** the app's scene figures for ruler, thermometer, balance scale, number line, coins and cube solids (used through `scene`); `bar` with two aligned scales and `partition`/`shade` (the percent bar is already a double number line); `table` with cells, rows, columns, `given` and highlights; `stack`/`digits` with headers, the point line and the shift (as specified by place value); `grid`, `counters`, `poly`, `seg`, `label`, `dim`, `view`, `slide`, `move`; `numberline` as specified (drop, arcs, half, catch, zoom); `dim` for choices; the board with `watch`, `recap` and the math check.

**New or extended, as specs.**

### 4.1 `ruler`: the unit laid, the ruler, the double ruler, the brackets

Built on the `numberline` planner (ticks, labels, the step arc, zoom), with the object, the lit spaces, a second scale, fraction cuts and brackets added, and a vertical mode. It draws the app's own ruler and unit figures, so the lesson's picture is the problem's picture.

```json
{ "id": "r", "ruler": "P", "w": 16, "unit": "in", "len": 36, "numbers": true,
  "object": { "from": 3, "to": 8, "tex": "\\text{strip}", "ink": "accent" },
  "scales": [ { "unit": "ft", "per": 12, "ink": "blue" } ],
  "sub": 4, "vertical": false, "start": false }
```

- Parts: `r.space.k`, `r.tick.k`, `r.num.k`, `r.object`, `r.object.start` and `r.object.end` (the gold drop lines), `r.count` (live count of lit spaces), `r.scale.ft`, `r.scale.ft.tick.k`, `r.scale.ft.num.k`, `r.sub.k.j` (fraction ticks and their labels), `r.bracket.k`, `r.leftover`, `r.step`, `r.across`.
- `{"do":"lay","target":"r","n":9,"shape":"clip","at":"one"}`: unit shapes arrive one per counted word (the `at`/`nth` pattern), touching, from the object's start; `"wrong":"gap" | "overlap" | "offset" | "mixed"` draws a broken layout and the count label shows the wrong count or "?"; `"unit":"cube"` after a clip `lay` is the re-lay.
- `{"do":"join","target":"r"}`: the laid units fuse into the ruler; numbers appear at the joins.
- `{"do":"slide","target":"r.object","to":3}`: the object moves along the ruler; its lit spaces move with it; the drop lines and their readings update.
- `{"do":"light","target":"r","from":3,"to":8}`: spaces light one at a time with the count.
- `{"do":"cut","target":"r.space.3","into":4}`: fraction ticks grow in one space, labels on the word.
- `{"do":"across","target":"r","at":24,"scale":"ft","row":"t"}`: the gold line through both scales at that length, both readings glowing; `row` writes a table row.
- `{"do":"bracket","target":"r","per":3,"tex":"1\\ \\text{yd}","from":0,"to":23}`: brackets lay left to right; unbracketed spaces stay lit and counted as `r.leftover`.
- `{"do":"step","target":"r","between":[0,10]}`: the step arc; shared with `numberline`.
- `"vertical": true` stands the ruler up for beaker and thermometer scales; `"numbers": false` with `"labels": [10, 20]` gives a scale with only some marks labeled.

### 4.2 Unit headers on the chart

`digits` and `stack` take free-text `headers` (`["km","hm","dam","m","dm","cm","mm"]`) with `"base": "m"` naming the ones column, so the point sits beside it. `shift` and the placeholder zeros work unchanged. This is a small extension of what place value specified; it unlocks every metric type.

### 4.3 `table` arrows and fill from the ruler

`{"do":"arrow","of":"t","from":"col.0","to":"col.1","tex":"\\times 12","side":"above"}` draws the operation between two columns; `"side":"below"` for the inverse. Rows written by `across` land in their cells. The roadmap already lists arrows between cells; this is that piece, built first for conversions.

### 4.4 `balance` actions

On the app's balance figure: `{"do":"tilt","target":"bal","toward":"left"}`, `{"do":"level","target":"bal"}`, `{"do":"place","target":"bal.right","mass":200,"at":"two"}` lands a labeled block on a pan while the beam settles and a total label counts. Parts: `bal.left`, `bal.right`, `bal.beam`, `bal.total`.

### 4.5 `container`: fill, pour, read

```json
{ "id": "c", "container": "P", "shape": "beaker", "w": 4, "h": 8, "capacity": 40,
  "scale": { "step": 2, "every": 10 }, "level": 0, "ink": "accent" }
```

`{"do":"fill","target":"c","to":28}` raises the water; `{"do":"read","target":"c"}` runs the gold line to the scale; `{"do":"pour","from":"c1","to":"c2"}` drains one and raises the other, spilling over the rim when the capacity is passed. Shapes: beaker, bottle, jug, jar, bowl, glass (the app's outlines). Parts: `c.water`, `c.top`, `c.rim`, `c.scale`.

### 4.6 Thermometer read line and difference bracket

On the app's thermometer figure: `read` (gold line from the liquid's top to the scale), and `{"do":"difference","between":["t1","t2"]}` for two thermometers on aligned scales: the stretch between the two tops lights on one scale with a bracket, counted by the step.

### 4.7 Board: `cancel`, and a check that understands units

`{"do":"cancel","line":"l3","pairs":[["g","g"],["kg","kg"]]}` lights each matching pair gold, strikes both with one thin slash, and fades them, leaving the surviving unit bright. AUTHORING gains one line: a cancelled unit is notation, distinct from the mistake strike, which stays reserved.

The board math check must learn units, or authors will strip them from the board to pass `--dry`, which is exactly wrong for this family. A line like `7\ \text{ft} = 84\ \text{in}` is true when the numbers agree under the conversion facts the lesson states (or the standard table); a chain line is true when the numbers multiply out and the units cancel to the stated unit. Facts given in the problem are read from `facts.ts`.

### 4.8 Small pieces

- `numberline` `catch` gains `"closed": "both"` for the error interval, and `"center"` with `"radius"` to draw it from a reading and an error.
- `bar` pieces take names (`"names": "\\text{qt}"`) so the re-cut gallon reads "1 qt" on each piece; `partition` already keeps the old cuts when refining.
- The square-meter zoom is `grid` [10, 10] with `view` into one cell holding another `grid` [10, 10]; exists.
- An `icon` part drawn from a small set (pumpkin, jug, bottle, bowl, paper clip, cube, crayon, loaf, car, bat, bottle, teaspoon) with a label: the benchmark card and the K comparisons need pictures of things, and object counters use the same set.

### Build order

1. `ruler` with `lay`, `join`, `slide`, `light`, `cut`, `across`, `bracket`, `step`: the spine; unlocks grades 1, 2, 4 and 5 (about 25 types).
2. Unit headers on the chart, `table` arrows, `bar` piece names: every metric type, the table type, the capacity and weight renames.
3. Board `cancel` and the unit-aware check: Algebra 1 and every grade-5 board that writes units.
4. `balance`, `container`, thermometer actions, `icon`: grade 3 and kindergarten.
5. The error interval and the square-meter view.

## 5. Where the strongest writer is needed, and where the seam is

**Strongest writer.**
- The four K types (`measurable-attribute-choice`, `compare-lengths`, `compare-weights`, `compare-capacity`): the lesson is a motion that proves (align, tilt, pour) plus the first vocabulary of measuring; the misconceptions (size is not weight, height is not capacity) must be staged with real objects, not described; the K voice is the hardest to hold.
- `unit-size-and-count`: the family's one idea, in a multiple-choice "why" with reasons as decoys.
- `correct-unit-measure` and `order-lengths`: counterexamples staged so the error is seen, and a picture built from two sentences of transitivity.
- `shifted-ruler-length`: the slide that proves end minus start is the whole lesson.
- The mixed, read-carefully types: `mass-volume-story`, `conversion-chain-story`, `customary-unit-rename` (a rename and a two-step comparison under one name), `customary-unit-reverse`, `customary-mixed-units`, `benchmark-length-estimate`. No recipe; one example per kind, worked from the story.
- `compound-unit-conversion` and `rate-unit-chain`: the "why" (one as a fraction; square the unit too) is the lesson, and a weak author writes the rule instead of staging the square.
- `measurement-error-bound` and `model-quantity-roles`: the quality is in the reasoning about what a reading means and which quantity plays which role; nothing to check but the thinking.

**A capable cheaper model, from a written concept, once the kit above exists.** One method, one picture with named actions, and `--dry` with the unit-aware check catching most slips:
- Reading a tool: `read-liquid-volume`, `read-thermometer`, `balance-mass`, `measure-with-units`.
- The shift: `metric-length-rename`, `metric-length-reverse`, `meters-to-kilometers-blanks`.
- The double ruler or table: `unit-rename-larger`, `rename-time-units`, `conversion-table-slots`, `mixed-length-total`, `mixed-unit-border`, `customary-equivalent-select` (heavy arithmetic, but one method on the gallon bar).
- Benchmarks, once the card exists: `measuring-tool-choice`, `customary-unit-choice`, `reasonable-measure-choice`, `object-for-measure`, `unit-relative-size`.
- The factor as a fraction: `fl-metric-customary-conversion`, `unit-chain-conversion`, `approximate-unit-factor`.
- The cut: `fractional-ruler-length`, `measurement-line-fraction-story`.

The first lesson of each group goes through the whole loop with a strong writer; the cheaper model takes the rest only after the blind test on that group. `measure-with-units` sits on the K-to-1 voice and should be tested before it is handed over.

## 6. What looks misfiled, badly conceived or off grade; what the standards ask that nothing practices

> Base cases and trick questions belong in the app. A flag here means a weak choice for a lesson's main example or a mismatch with the standard, never "drop it."

**Misfiled.**
- `elapsed-time-intervals` is clock arithmetic (3.MD.A.1) and belongs to family 20, as the curriculum plan says. `rename-time-units` the plan also sends to family 20; this design asks for it back: 4.MD.A.1 names hours, minutes and seconds among the units to know, the picture is the same double ruler (hours over minutes), and its trap (2 h 25 min = 225) is this family's concatenation trap. Months to years is not in the standard; keep it as a stretch.
- `measurement-line-fraction-story` and `fractional-ruler-length` are one skill (read a fractional length between two marks) in two grades; either both stay here as the ruler with the cut, or both go to family 9. The plan moves only one; move neither or both.
- `unit-conversion` (grade 4, 14 types) is three skills in one: conversion (5 types), benchmarks and tools (6 types: `customary-unit-choice`, `reasonable-measure-choice`, `object-for-measure`, `unit-relative-size`, `measuring-tool-choice`, with `benchmark-length-estimate` from grade 2), and the two time types. A "benchmarks and tools" skill would let its six lessons share one picture and one concept.

**Off grade.**
- `unit-size-and-count` is filed grade 1 but is 2.MD.A.2, and its "how many long units" variant is division (3.OA.A.3); fine as a stretch, wrong as grade 1's main example.
- `fractional-ruler-length` in grade 2 answers 11/4 inches; halves and fourths of an inch are 3.MD.B.4, and fractions on a line are grade 3.
- `unit-rename-larger` (28 quarts to gallons) in grade 4 is 5.MD.A.1; 4.MD.A.1 is larger to smaller only.
- `conversion-chain-story` (5½ m with 30-cm bows; 5.5-yard rods to inches) uses fractions, decimals and remainder interpretation: grade 5 content under a grade-4 skill.
- `fl-metric-customary-conversion` (672 g ÷ 28) and `measurement-error-bound` (percent of a quantity, 7.RP.A.3) are grades 6 and 7 inside an Algebra 1 skill; fine as applications, but neither is HSN.Q.A.3.
- `unit-relative-size` compares across systems ("a kilogram is heavier than a pound", quart against liter); 4.MD.A.1 says "within one system of units." Keep as benchmark knowledge, say so in the template.

**Badly conceived.**
- `metric-length-reverse` reports its answer as a fraction in lowest terms (2938 m = 1469/500 km; 1976 mL = 247/125 L). 5.MD.A.1's own example is "5 cm to 0.05 m": the answer is 2.938, a decimal, and the checker must accept it as the canonical form.
- `benchmark-length-estimate`'s repeated-benchmark variant generates physically false stories ("a window is about 4 paper clips long": 12 centimeters). The idea is right (my hand span is 15 cm, the desk is 4 spans); the generator must pick objects whose true size fits the benchmark count.
- `model-quantity-roles` shows a blank fourth choice (`| ]`) in a generated variant.
- `read-thermometer` has no standard; Celsius temperature is not in CCSS measurement at all. It is a fine scale-reading type and serves foundation 8; keep it, label it as such, and keep readings above zero (negatives are grade 6).
- `measuring-tool-choice` has no standard and is vocabulary; keep its film short.

**Standards clauses nothing practices (exact words).**
- 1.MD.A.1: "compare the lengths of two objects indirectly by using a third object." `order-lengths` chains two stated comparisons; no type carries a length with a string or a stick.
- 2.MD.A.1: "selecting and using appropriate tools such as rulers, yardsticks, meter sticks, and measuring tapes." No type chooses among length tools for an object's size (`measuring-tool-choice` chooses across attributes).
- 2.MD.A.4: "Measure to determine how much longer one object is than another, expressing the length difference in terms of a standard length unit." The skill links it; no grade-2 type puts two objects on one ruler and asks the difference.
- 3.MD.A.2: "estimate liquid volumes and masses of objects" has no grade-3 type (estimation appears only in grade 4's reasonable-measure types); and "Add, subtract, multiply, or divide to solve one-step word problems" should be confirmed to generate all four operations; the variants shown are a product and a difference.
- 4.MD.A.1: "hr, min, sec": seconds appear nowhere. "l, ml": larger to smaller for liters should be confirmed in `metric-length-rename`.
- 4.MD.A.2: word problems with "liquid volumes, masses of objects" that "require expressing measurements given in a larger unit in terms of a smaller unit" (a 2-kg bag scooped in 250-g scoops): none; every grade-4 conversion story is length. And "Represent measurement quantities using diagrams such as number line diagrams that feature a measurement scale" is served only by `measurement-line-fraction-story`.
- HSN.Q.A.1: "choose and interpret the scale and the origin in graphs and data displays": nothing here; if families 13 and 14 practice it, the catalog should link it there.
- HSN.Q.A.3: "Choose a level of accuracy appropriate to limitations on measurement when reporting quantities": the skill is named for precision and no type asks it. A type is missing: given sides measured to the nearest centimeter, which reported perimeter is justified; or a scale that reads to the nearest 10 g and the report that overstates it.
- K.MD.A.1: "Describe several measurable attributes of a single object": the type asks for one attribute per question; a select-all form (which of these can be measured about the pumpkin) would meet the clause.

## 7. The pilot: five problem types to build first

The pilot covers the picture born, grow into the ruler, split into two rulers, bracket into groups, and become a fraction equal to one, with every new kit action exercised and the family's traps on screen.

| # | Problem type | Grade | Why it is in the pilot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `unit-size-and-count` | 1 (really 2) | The idea is born: lay the small unit and count; re-lay the longer unit and count fewer; "smaller unit, more of them" said for the first time, in a multiple-choice "why" whose decoys are the misconception. Exercises `lay`, re-lay, the count, the K-to-2 voice, and `dim` with a reason. |
| 2 | `shifted-ruler-length` | 2 | The units join into a ruler; the strip slides from 0 to 3 and its five spaces slide with it; length is end minus start. The most-missed ruler item in the grade. Exercises `join`, `slide`, `light`. |
| 3 | `customary-unit-rename` | 4 | The double ruler with the fact as group size, the direction said before the arithmetic, a table row written from a read-across, and the two-step variant (7 feet against 81 inches) that makes it a reading lesson. Exercises `across`, table arrows, the board with units. |
| 4 | `customary-mixed-units` | 5 | Brackets with leftovers (23 ft to 7 yd 2 ft), every part converted before combining (2 cups 4 fl oz to 20), the concatenation trap named and dimmed, the trade with a group of twelve. A mixed type with several sub-kinds: the strongest writer. Exercises `bracket`, the re-cut bar. |
| 5 | `compound-unit-conversion` | A1 | The fraction equal to one, units cancelling, and the square-meter zoom that proves 10,000, then the cube (17 m³ to 17,000,000 cm³) and a rate with two units to convert. The conversion adults still get wrong. Exercises `cancel`, the unit-aware board check, the square view. |

If the five land, the next wave is `compare-lengths` and `compare-capacity` (the K motions and voice), `balance-mass` and `read-liquid-volume` (the grade-3 tools), `metric-length-reverse` (the chart with unit headers, and the decimal answer fixed), `measurement-error-bound` (the interval), and `model-quantity-roles`, which completes the kit and shows every grade band before batch production.
