# Angles and triangles, grades 4 to 8: family design

Four skills, 20 problem types. Planning sources: `src/curriculum/catalog.ts`, the generated examples, the standards' exact text, snapshot 2026-11-09. This design covers the idea, the picture, how the lessons go, the kit, the seam, the flags, the pilot. The per-type concepts use these foundations and pictures.

---

## 1. The idea underneath the whole family

**An angle is an amount of turn, and turns add.** Not a shape, not two lines, not how long the arms are: how far one arm has turned away from the other, measured against the full circle. Once a child owns that, every unknown angle in this family is the same problem: *what must these turns add up to?* The whole angle a ray was cut from (grade 4); a straight line, half a turn, 180 (grade 7); all the way around a point, 360 (grade 7); the three corners of a triangle, which make a straight line (grade 8). Add the parts you know, take them from the total, and the unknown is what is left. Where an angle is written as an expression, the same sentence becomes an equation and the balance from family 11 solves it.

One companion idea lives in the grade-7 construction skill: **a triangle is a hinge**. Two sides joined at a corner must reach across the third, and three sides lock the hinge into exactly one triangle, while three angles fix only its shape. It is small, it has its own picture, and it joins the main idea in grade 8 when sides and angles are reasoned about together.

### The foundations, in the order they are built

1. **An angle is a turn.** (Grade 4) Two rays from one endpoint, the *vertex*. Its size is how far you turn one ray to land on the other. The rays can be any length; lengthening them changes nothing. Picture: a ray that sweeps and an arc that grows behind it.
2. **Degrees: a full turn is 360.** (Grade 4) The circle around the vertex is cut into 360 equal one-degree turns; an angle that turns through n of them measures n degrees (4.MD.C.5). The landmarks are fractions of the turn: a quarter turn is the square corner, 90; a half turn is a straight line, 180; 45 is half a square corner; 60 and 30 are a third and a sixth of a half turn. From family 20: the clock hand's quarter hour is a quarter turn.
3. **The protractor is a ruler for turns.** (Grade 4) Half a circle of one-degree marks. Center on the vertex, zero along one ray, read where the other ray crosses. Two scales run opposite ways and always add to 180; the one that started at zero on your ray is yours, and the landmark look (more or less than a square corner?) confirms it. From family 19: measuring is counting copies of a unit with no gaps; the degree is the unit.
4. **Angles add.** (Grade 4) A ray drawn inside an angle cuts it into two angles whose measures add to the whole; the missing part is the whole minus the known part (4.MD.C.7). This is family 6's part-whole placed on turns, and it is the engine of everything after.
5. **Two lines crossing: side by side makes a line, across makes a match.** (Grade 7) Angles that sit next to each other on a straight line add to 180; angles all the way around a point add to 360; the two angles across a crossing are equal, because a half turn about the crossing lands each line on itself and each angle on the one opposite. When an angle is "x plus seventy-five", the sentence "they add to 180" or "they are equal" is an equation (family 11, with like terms from family 12).
6. **Sides must reach.** (Grade 7) Two sides hinged at a corner span the third only if together they are longer than it; equal is flat, not a triangle. So the third side lies strictly between the difference and the sum of the other two (7.G.A.2).
7. **What fixes a triangle.** (Grade 7) Three sides: the hinge has one position, so exactly one triangle. Three angles that add to 180: the shape is fixed but the size is free, so more than one (every size, all the same shape; this is the angle-angle idea of 8.G.A.5 seen from the other side). Two sides and the angle between them, or two angles and a side: one. Two sides and an angle not between them: the second side swings on an arc and may miss the far ray, touch it, or cross it twice: none, one, or two.
8. **Parallel lines make the same angles with any line that crosses them.** (Grade 8) Slide the crossing along the transversal from one parallel to the other and the angle it carries lands exactly on the next one, because parallel lines point the same way. With foundation 5, the eight angles at two crossings are only two sizes, and the two sizes add to 180.
9. **A triangle's corners make a straight line.** (Grade 8) Through one corner draw the line parallel to the opposite side. The two base angles slide up along the sides (foundation 8) and sit beside the top angle, filling the straight line: 180. This is the "argument in terms of transversals" 8.G.A.5 asks for, and it is one motion.
10. **The outside angle is the two far corners together.** (Grade 8) Extend a side past a corner. The same parallel line through that corner cuts the outside angle into a copy of each far corner, so exterior equals the sum of the two remote interior angles; equivalently it is 180 minus the near corner. Equal sides give equal base angles (fold the triangle along the line from the apex to the middle of the base; the sides land on each other, so the base angles do), a borrowed Geometry fact that family 26's flip proves in one beat.

### What this family takes, and gives

Takes: part-whole and "the whole is the sum of the parts" (family 6); fractions of a whole, 3/4 of 360 and a circle in 8 equal parts (families 9 and 10); the clock as the first circle of turn (family 20); the unit and the ruler (family 19); naming shapes by what they have, right/acute/obtuse (family 22); the balance, "do the same to both sides", like terms (families 11 and 12); slide, turn, flip keep every angle (family 26, informally in grade 7, as rigid motions in grade 8).

Gives: the circle's arcs and sectors as fractions of a turn (family 28); the protractor and the degree to geometric construction and proof (families 29, 26); "same angles, same shape" to similarity (family 26); the right triangle's angle that picks a ratio (family 30); the triangle inequality and the exterior angle to the Geometry proofs.

Two rules the ladder implies, as in every family: a lesson never re-teaches a foundation it does not own (it says "the whole is the sum of the parts" once and draws it), and when an old picture gets a new meaning (the straight line *is* an angle of 180; the triangle's corners *are* a straight line taken apart), the lesson says so once, as the point of the moment.

## 2. The visual language: the turn, the figure drawn true, and motions that prove

This family uses two of the curriculum's pictures: **P10, the circle**, for what an angle is, and **P8, figures that move**, for everything done with angles and sides. Every figure is drawn true: a 70° angle is 70°, a 14 cm side is twice a 7 cm side. The child's scratch-paper sketch is the target: the figure with the given numbers written in it, a question mark on the unknown, the square at a right angle, ticks on equal sides, a line continued where an outside angle is asked.

### The angle

- Two rays from a vertex dot, with an **arc** drawn near the vertex between them. All arcs in one figure share one radius, so arc length *is* the measure: a bigger angle has a visibly longer arc and equal angles have identical arcs. A right angle gets the small square instead of an arc.
- The **measure** rides the arc's middle, just outside it: `110°`, `x°`, or `?` for the unknown. Letters sit at the vertex and on the rays. Reflex angles (past 180) are drawn the long way round, so "the full turn" looks like what it is.
- The **name**: the vertex letter is always the middle letter of ∠WXZ. The board shows the name with the middle letter gold when it is read aloud ("angle W, X, Z: X is the corner").
- An angle may be a **wedge**: the sector between the rays, filled in its color, that can be lifted off the figure as a tile and moved. Wedges are how angles are compared, copied and added without words.

### The circle of turn (grade 4 only, then recalled in one beat)

A faint ring around the vertex with 360 one-degree ticks, heavier at every 10, labeled at 0, 90, 180, 270. Landmark sectors shade in: quarter (90), half (180), three quarters (270), eighth (45). A circle cut into n equal angles is the same ring with n radii and k sectors shaded, each labeled 360 ÷ n.

### The protractor

A translucent half-disc drawn to true degrees: center mark, baseline, one-degree ticks (heavier at 5, numbers at every 10), the **outer scale** 0 to 180 left to right and the **inner scale** 0 to 180 right to left, as on the app's protractor. It is placed by a motion the child should copy (slide the center onto the vertex, rotate until the zero line lies along a ray), and read by a gold arc that runs from 0 along the chosen scale to where the other ray crosses, the crossed number glowing. At that crossing both scales' numbers can be shown side by side (100 and 80) and the wrong one dimmed with the reason. Where a problem type has the app's own protractor figure, the film uses that figure; the kit's protractor must match it exactly so the lesson's protractor is the problem's protractor.

### Two lines, parallels, triangles

- A **straight angle** is a line with a vertex dot on it and a half-disc arc: the picture that says "this is an angle, and it is 180".
- A **crossing** is two lines with letters at their four ends, so ∠APB and ∠CPD can be found by their letters. The four angles get two colors: the two across from each other share a color and an arc count.
- **Parallel lines** carry the arrow marks on both lines; the transversal is a plain line. A wedge copied at one crossing slides along the transversal to the other.
- A **triangle** is drawn with its three angles' arcs and numbers, equal sides ticked, a right angle squared, and any extended side continued as a muted line so the exterior angle has a place to be drawn. A line parallel to a side through the opposite corner is drawn muted with the arrow marks: the construction, not part of the problem.
- Most grade-7 and grade-8 problems arrive as sentences with no figure. The film then **draws the sentence**, one clause at a time, as a child would on scratch paper: "Lines AC and BD cross at P" draws two lines, labels the ends, dots P; "angle CPD measures x plus seventy-five" writes `x+75` in that arc. The sketch is the lesson's first teaching move, not a preliminary.

### Sides and the hinge

- Given lengths are drawn to scale with their numbers beside them. The longest side lies flat as a base.
- **Compass arcs**: from each end of the base, a muted arc swings with the other side's length as radius. Where the arcs cross is the top corner. Two arcs that cross: a triangle (the crossing above and its mirror below are one triangle). Arcs that touch on the base line: flat, not a triangle. Arcs that miss: no triangle, and the gap is drawn.
- **The hinge**: two sides joined at one end, the free ends joined by the third side as a dashed segment with a live length label. The hinge opens from folded (length: the difference) to flat (length: the sum); the two ends of that range are drawn as hollow dots on a number line beneath, with whole-number ticks between them when the problem counts them.
- **The swing**: for two sides and a non-included angle, the given angle is drawn, the known side laid along one ray, and from its far end the second side swings as an arc toward the other ray: it misses, touches, or crosses twice.

### Color means one thing each

- **teal**: the angle or side asked for: its arc, its `x°` or `?`, its wedge, and its term in every equation.
- **rust**: the first known angle or side; **blue**: the second; **violet**: a third. Each quantity keeps its color on the figure, in the equation and in the answer. Two angles that are equal share a color and an arc count: the figure says "equal" before the voice does.
- **gold**: the one thing being looked at: the number where a ray crosses the protractor, the word in the question that says what is asked ("measure", "value of x", "could not"), the tick where a swinging arc lands, the middle letter of an angle's name. One gold thing on screen at a time.
- **muted**: construction (the parallel line through a corner, an extended side, compass arcs once they have met, the ring of turn), ruled-out choices, the dimmed board in a recap. Totals (180, 360, the whole) are written plain: they are facts, not quantities drawn.

### The motions (each one proves a foundation)

- **Sweep.** A ray starts on the zero ray and turns to its place; the arc grows behind it and a degree readout counts up. Then the rays lengthen and shorten while the readout holds. Proves foundation 1 and 2: an angle is a turn, measured as a fraction of the circle, and the arms' length is nothing.
- **Place and read.** The protractor slides so its center lands on the vertex, rotates so zero lies along a ray; the gold arc runs along the scale to the other ray. Foundation 3, as a habit the child copies.
- **Fit.** Two wedges slide together to share a ray; their arcs join into one arc whose readout is their sum. Reverse: a ray cuts a wedge, and the part not named is what is left. Foundation 4.
- **Half-turn.** A crossing turns 180° about its point: each line lands on itself, each angle on the one across. Foundation 5: vertical angles are equal, in one motion and no words.
- **Line it up.** Two neighboring wedges at a crossing fit against a straight-angle half-disc and fill it exactly: 180. Three or four wedges around a point fill the ring: 360.
- **Slide along.** A wedge copied at one parallel line slides along the transversal and lands exactly on the corresponding wedge at the other. Foundation 8. (A half-turn then gives the alternate angle; together, two sizes.)
- **Corners to a line.** A triangle's three wedges, in three colors, lift off and slide together onto a straight angle, filling it (the seeing). Then the reason: the muted parallel through the top corner is drawn; the two base wedges slide up their own sides and sit beside the top wedge (the argument). Foundation 9.
- **Fill the outside.** A side extends; the outside wedge is drawn in teal; the two far corners' wedges slide into it and fill it with nothing left over. Foundation 10.
- **Fold.** An isosceles triangle folds along the line from its apex to the base midpoint; the equal sides land on each other; the base angles coincide. Foundation 10's borrowed fact.
- **Swing the compass.** Arcs swing from the base's ends and meet, touch, or miss; the hinge opens from folded to flat with the third side's length counting. Foundations 6 and 7.
- **Scale up.** A triangle with fixed angles grows from one corner, every angle's readout holding while every side's grows. Foundation 7: three angles do not fix the size.
- **Dim.** Choices that cannot be right fade together in one beat with one reason said aloud. Nothing is struck out one by one.

What the kit's figure primitives already do (true-to-degree `angle`, `seg` with ticks and parallel arrows and extension, `turn`, `flip`, `slide`, points on circles, `glide`) carries about half of this; section 4 says what is new.

## 3. How lessons in this family should go

### The habits, on every film

1. **Draw the sentence.** If the problem has a figure, it is on screen from the first word; if not, the film sketches it clause by clause and says that this is what to do on scratch paper. Every given number goes onto the figure in its arc or beside its side; the unknown gets a teal `?` or `x`. The word that says what is asked goes gold, and the film says what the answer must look like: a number of degrees, a value of x (not an angle), a length, "exactly one or more than one or none".
2. **Name the total.** Before any arithmetic: what must these add up to? The whole angle the ray cut. 180 on a line. 360 around a point. 180 in a triangle. Equal across a crossing. This one question is the family's method; the lesson asks it out loud every time.
3. **At a glance.** What is known with no work: the angle is obtuse, so its measure is over 90 (and a protractor reading of 80 is out); the part is less than the whole, so the equation must subtract; two sides are 6 and 14, so the third is between 8 and 20; the three given angles add to 180, so there is a triangle and it has every size.
4. **The sure fast way.** Add the known parts, take them from the total; or set the two equal expressions equal; solve on the board with the operation in the margin, as family 11 does.
5. **Sense.** Does the number match the look? An angle drawn acute cannot be 110. A part is smaller than its whole. x must make every expression a positive angle. The third side is inside the window.
6. **Check.** Add the parts back to the total. Read the protractor on the other scale and see the two readings make 180. Put x back into every expression and add them up. Close the triangle with the arcs. For multiple choice, the ruled-out choices dim together with the reason; the chosen one is proved.
7. **Answer the question.** "Find x, then answer what was asked." The board writes what is asked at the top of the work and boxes only that: when the exterior angle is asked, 13 (the x) and 49 (the base angle) are steps, and 98 is the answer.

### The openings: one short "why" shot per skill, shared by every film in it

As the place-value decimals films all open with the same twenty-second cut of the flat, each skill here opens with its foundation drawn live, then the example. Later films in the skill recall it in one beat.

- **Angle measure (grade 4), about twenty seconds:** the sweep with the readout; the ring of turn; "a full turn is three hundred sixty; a quarter turn is a square corner, ninety; a half turn is a straight line, one hundred eighty." The protractor films add the place-and-read; the adjacent films add the fit.
- **Angle relationships (grade 7), about fifteen seconds:** two lines cross; the half-turn lands each angle on its opposite ("across: equal"); two neighbors fill the straight angle ("side by side: one hundred eighty"); the ring for "around a point: three hundred sixty". The letters rule: two angles at a crossing that share a ray letter are side by side; two that share only the corner letter are across.
- **Triangle construction (grade 7), about fifteen seconds:** the hinge and the compass arcs: "two sides must reach past the third."
- **Triangle angle theorems (grade 8), about twenty seconds:** corners to a line, then the parallel through the top corner and the slide. The exterior-angle and parallel-line films reuse the same muted parallel; the isosceles film adds the fold.

### Reading these problems

- **The middle letter is the corner.** ∠WXZ, ∠YXZ and ∠WXY all have corner X; the rays are named by the other letters, so the ray shared by two of the names is the cutting ray. The lesson reads names this way every time, because the grade-4 adjacent problems and the grade-7 crossing problems are unreadable without it.
- **"Extended past Y to W"** means W is beyond Y on line XY, so the exterior angle at Y is ∠ZYW and the far corners are X and Z.
- **"Side BC is opposite ∠A"** tells which side swings: the one not touching the given angle.
- **"Which equation finds x"**: the equation is the sentence "the whole is the sum of the parts" written in the problem's own numbers; nothing is computed.
- **"Could" or "could not"**, **"not 30°"**, **"cannot be the sum"**: the asked polarity goes gold and the recap says it again, because the common wrong answer on these is the right answer to the opposite question.
- **"Value of x"** against **"measure of ∠F"**: gold the one asked, and keep it on the board.

### The sure fast ways (each one always works)

- **Protractor:** center on the corner, zero along a ray, read where the other ray crosses, on the scale that started at zero. The two scales add to 180; the look (more or less than a square corner) picks the right one when the eye slips.
- **A ray inside an angle:** part = whole − other part. **Two parts given:** whole = part + part.
- **A circle in n equal angles:** 360 ÷ n each; k of them is k × that. A fraction of the turn: that fraction of 360 (a quarter is 90, so three quarters is 270, with no division at all).
- **At a crossing:** across, equal; side by side, 180; all around, 360 minus the others.
- **Angle expressions:** write the total or the equality, collect like terms, solve with the operation in the margin, then substitute to get the angle asked.
- **Parallel lines cut by a transversal:** every angle in the figure is one of two sizes, and the two sizes add to 180. Equal when both are acute or both obtuse; add to 180 when one is each. Name the pair (corresponding, alternate, same-side) for the reason; use the two sizes for the speed.
- **A triangle:** the third angle is 180 minus the other two. With expressions: add all three, set equal to 180.
- **An exterior angle:** the two far corners added; or 180 minus the near corner, whichever is given. Both come from the same parallel-line picture, and the film shows they agree.
- **Isosceles:** set the base angles equal, solve, find the base angle, then the exterior angle asked (at the apex it is twice a base angle; at a base corner it is the apex plus the other base angle).
- **Can three lengths make a triangle:** add the two shorter and compare with the longest. One check, not three; the other two inequalities hold by themselves because the longest side is the longest.
- **The third side:** strictly between the difference and the sum. Least whole number: difference + 1. Greatest: sum − 1. How many whole numbers: sum − difference − 1, read off the hinge's number line as the ticks strictly inside the window.
- **How many triangles:** three sides that reach: one. Three angles adding to 180: more than one (infinitely many sizes); not adding to 180: none. Two sides and the angle between, or two angles and a side: one. Two sides and the angle not between: swing the arc and count the crossings. At 30°, the straight-across distance from the far end to the other ray is half the slant distance (fold the 30° picture into an equilateral triangle to see it), which is the only case the app generates; a shorter swinging side misses, an equal one touches once, a longer one crosses twice unless it is longer than the slant, when it crosses once.

### Ruling out nonsense choices

- **Which ray is 155° from the zero ray:** 155 is obtuse, so every ray on the acute side of the square corner dims at once; two remain; read.
- **Which equation finds x:** the known part is less than the whole, so x is less than the whole; any equation that adds, that takes the whole from the part (negative), or that sets x equal to the whole dims together.
- **Which benchmark:** more or less than a square corner first; then nearer the half of it (45) or the thirds of a straight angle (60, 30); 180 dims unless the arms are a straight line.
- **Which set makes a triangle:** for each set, two shorter against the longest; all the failing sets dim in one beat with the one reason ("they do not reach").
- **Could not be the third side:** anything at or outside the window dims; the boundary value is the flat case and dims with it.
- **How many triangles:** three angles can never be "exactly one"; three sides can never be "more than one"; both dim before any arithmetic.
- **Which sum cannot be:** two of the four angles at a crossing add to either twice an angle (across), 180 (side by side), or 360 minus twice an angle (the other pair); an expression of a different form dims.
- **Protractor select-many:** read every ray's number once on one scale, then every angle between two rays is a difference of two readings; the statements that match stay, the rest dim together.

### Realistic misconceptions, and how the picture makes each one impossible

These are what children actually do, not slots to fill.

- **Reading the wrong protractor scale** (80 for an angle of 100; 10 for 170). The gold arc starts at the zero that lies on the ray, and the look check says "bigger than a square corner".
- **A longer arm means a bigger angle.** The sweep holds its readout while the arms stretch.
- **Placing the protractor's edge, or its 0, on the vertex instead of its center.** The place motion is shown as the habit, and a misplacement in the `watch` card gives a reading that fails the look check.
- **Adding when the part is asked** (110 and 50 gives 160 for ∠WXY). The fit motion: the named part is what is left when the known part leaves the whole.
- **Setting side-by-side angles equal, or across angles to 180** (x + 75 = 2x + 72 when they are a linear pair, or the reverse). The letters rule and the half-turn decide which relation it is before any equation is written.
- **Stopping at x, or giving the base angle when the exterior angle is asked.** "Answer the question" is on the board from the start.
- **Taking 180 minus the far angle for an exterior angle, or adding the near corner.** The fill motion: only the two far wedges fit in the outside angle.
- **Treating the boundary as a triangle** (6, 8, 14). The arcs touch on the base line and the hinge lies flat: nothing to be inside.
- **Checking only one pair, or forgetting the lower bound** (3 as a third side with 9 and 12). The hinge folds to the difference and no further.
- **"Three angles make exactly one triangle."** The scale-up: every readout holds while the triangle grows.
- **Dividing 180 instead of 360 for a circle cut in equal angles, or multiplying the wrong way for a fraction of the turn.** The ring labeled 360 and the shaded sectors counted.
- **Assuming every angle at two parallel crossings is equal.** Two sizes, two colors, and the straight angle they fill.

### Lines worth remembering (said once, at the moment they apply, then in the recap)

- "An angle is a turn. The arms can be any length."
- "Full turn, three sixty. Half turn, a straight line, one eighty. Quarter turn, a square corner, ninety."
- "Center on the corner, zero on a ray, read where the other ray crosses."
- "The two scales add to one eighty. Take the one that started at zero."
- "The middle letter is the corner."
- "Angles add. The part is the whole minus the other part."
- "Across: equal. Side by side on a line: one eighty. All the way around: three sixty."
- "Parallel lines: two sizes, and they add to one eighty."
- "A triangle's corners make a straight line."
- "The outside angle is the two far corners put together."
- "Equal sides, equal angles."
- "Two sides must reach past the third."
- "The third side lives between the difference and the sum, and never at the ends."
- "Three sides lock a triangle. Three angles only fix its shape."
- "Find x, then answer the question."

## 4. Tools to build

**Already in the kit and used as is:** `angle` (three points, fill, ink, r, the right-angle square), `seg` (ticks for equal sides, `arrows` for parallel marks, `extend` for a line, `dash`), `poly` with `solid` tiles, `dot`, `label`, `dim` (the measuring bracket), `circle`, points `{on: circle, deg}` and `{foot}`, `glide`, `turn`, `flip`, `slide`, `move`, `draw`, `highlight`, `view`, the board (`problem` with choices, `write` with margin notes, `watch`, `term`, `recap`), and `sweep` from the wave kit (a sector fill of a swept angle with TeX riding its bisector). The board's `dim` for choices is specified by the place-value design.

**New, in build order (each item unlocks the lessons named):**

### 4.1 `angle` grows (every film in the family)

- `tex` on an angle part: the measure riding the arc's bisector just outside the arc, in the angle's ink; `"@deg"` makes it a live readout.
- `arcs: 1 | 2 | 3`: equal-angle marks.
- Orientation: the angle is drawn counterclockwise from the first named ray to the second, so a reflex angle is drawn the long way round by naming the rays in that order; `"full": true` draws the whole ring.
- `{"do":"sweep","target":"a","from":"ray1","at":"turns","dur":2}`: the second ray starts on the first and turns to its place; the arc grows; the readout counts. `{"do":"stretch","target":"a","by":1.6}` lengthens or shortens both rays with the readout held.
- `ring`: the circle of turn as a figure part: `{ "id": "r", "ring": "V", "r": 3, "ticks": 360, "heavy": 10, "labels": [0, 90, 180, 270], "cuts": 8, "shaded": [0, 1, 2], "tex": "45^\\circ" }`. Serves the grade-4 definition beat and `turn-degrees`; `cuts` and `shaded` are the P10 "sector shade by fraction" the roadmap lists.

### 4.2 `protractor` (`protractor-reading`, `protractor-ray-choice`, `protractor-angles-select`)

```json
{ "id": "pr", "protractor": "B", "zero": "A", "r": 6, "scales": "both" }
```

- Drawn to true degrees, translucent, matching the app's protractor drawing exactly (ticks every degree, heavier at 5, numbers every 10, inner and outer scales), so a lesson over the app's real figure and a lesson on a built figure look the same. If the app's renderer exposes the protractor through `scene`, the kit wraps it rather than drawing a second one; either way the parts below must exist.
- Parts: `pr.center`, `pr.base`, `pr.outer`, `pr.inner`, `pr.tick.155`, `pr.num.outer.150`, `pr.num.inner.30`, `pr.read` (the gold reading arc).
- `{"do":"place","target":"pr","from":[dx, dy, deg]}`: starts displaced and misaligned, slides its center onto the vertex, then rotates until zero lies along the zero ray. The habit, shown.
- `{"do":"read","target":"pr","ray":"C","scale":"outer","at":"crosses"}`: the gold arc runs from 0 along that scale to the ray; the crossed number lights; a readout counts. `"scale":"both"` shows both numbers at the crossing for the two-scale beat; `{"do":"dim","target":"pr.num.inner.80"}` fades the wrong one.

### 4.3 `wedge`: the angle as a tile (`adjacent-angles`, `adjacent-angle-equation`, all of grades 7 and 8)

```json
{ "id": "wA", "wedge": ["B", "A", "C"], "r": 1.8, "fill": "warm-fill", "ink": "warm", "tex": "52^\\circ", "solid": true }
```

- A sector at the middle point between the two rays, filled, with its measure riding its bisector; a `solid` tile that `slide`, `turn` and `flip` move with a lift and shadow, the label traveling with it (the "labels that ride turned parts" wish from KAIZEN, built here).
- `{"do":"copy","target":"wA","as":"wA2"}`: a duplicate lifts off while the original stays, so the figure keeps its given angles while copies move. `{"do":"fit","target":"wA2","onto":"wB","side":"ccw"}`: slides and turns the copy so its vertex and one ray coincide with another wedge's, the two arcs joining; a shared readout can be shown for the joined angle. This is **fit**, **slide along**, **corners to a line** and **fill the outside**; **half-turn** and **fold** are the existing `turn` and `flip` on wedges.
- A straight angle and a full turn are wedges too: `"wedge": ["A","P","C"]` with A, P, C collinear draws the half-disc.

### 4.4 Compass arcs and meeting points (`triangle-side-test`, `rg-triangle-third-side-test`, `triangle-third-side`, `triangle-conditions`, `triangle-uniqueness`)

- `arc`: `{ "id": "a1", "arc": "L", "r": 8, "from": 0, "to": 180, "ink": "muted", "dash": true }`, drawn by `draw` as a compass swing; `r` may be bound to a segment's length (`"r": "seg:LP"`) so the swing is visibly that side.
- New point kinds: `{ "meet": ["a1", "a2"], "which": 1 }` (the first or second crossing of two arcs, or of an arc and a ray `{ "meet": ["a1", "ray:XY"] }`; undefined when they miss, and any part built on it hides); `{ "along": ["R", "P"], "dist": 5 }` (a point a given distance from R toward P, for the arm that falls short and the dashed gap from its end to P).
- A length readout on a segment's label: `"tex": "@len"` with `"of": "seg-id"`, counting as a point glides. With a point on a circle gliding from 0° to 180°, this is the hinge opening with the third side's length counting from the difference to the sum.
- `gap`: a highlight on the dashed segment between an arm's end and the point it cannot reach.

### 4.5 Board

- `dim` for choices (specified).
- A `facts` beat is not a component: the totals (180, 360, the whole) are written with `write` and `note`, and the equation's terms carry the figure's colors with `\cw{}`, `\cb{}`, `\ca{}`.

Everything else this family needs (parallel marks, the extended side, the right-angle square, the balance's margin notes) exists. `view` on the triangle's growth and `move` on its corners give **scale up** without new kit.

## 5. Which lessons need the strongest writer, and which can go to a cheaper one

**Strongest writer, because the screenplay is the argument:**

- **The first lesson of each skill**, which builds the skill's foundation on screen for every later film to recall: `protractor-reading` (the sweep, the ring, the place-and-read), `angle-equation` (the half-turn, the straight angle, the letters rule, two kinds of equation), `rg-triangle-third-side-test` (the hinge and the arcs, the strict window), `triangle-angle-sum` (corners to a line and the parallel-line argument).
- **The reasoning types**, where the quality is in the thinking and a weak author will describe instead of show: `triangle-conditions` (five situations under one name, each a different motion), `triangle-uniqueness` (the swinging arc, the 30° half fact proved by a fold, four outcomes), `angle-claim-reasoning` (three kinds of question, one of them about the form of an expression), `angle-chase-chain` (choosing the route: the parallel inside the triangle is the family's own construction, and the film should say so), `protractor-angles-select` (several rays, differences of readings, the "not 30°" polarity).

**A capable cheaper model, once the concept is written and the kit above is complete:** `adjacent-angles`, `turn-degrees`, `protractor-ray-choice`, `adjacent-angle-equation`, `angle-benchmark-estimate`, `angle-pair-measure`, `triangle-side-test`, `triangle-third-side`, `triangle-exterior-angle`, `parallel-lines-angle`, `geo-isosceles-exterior-angle`. Each has one method, a figure the concept can specify to the part ("the wedge at A copies and fits onto the exterior angle at B"), board arithmetic the dry check verifies, a named misconception and a shared opening already written by the skill's first lesson. Require a complete concept, a fitting kit, and independent review of the first wave.

Review stays with a model at least as strong as the writer, as PROCESS.md says.

## 6. What looks badly conceived, misfiled or off grade, and what the standards ask that the problems never practice

> Base and trick cases are wanted in the app; a flag here means only that a case is a weak choice for a lesson's main example.

**Problem types.**

- `triangle-uniqueness` is above grade 7 as written. Both examples put the given angle at 30° so the swinging side can be judged against half the slant side; that half fact is Geometry's special right triangle. If every variant uses 30°, the lesson can carry it with the fold (foundation 7) and should say it is borrowed; if any variant uses another angle, the type needs trigonometry and belongs in Geometry or precalculus. Also check that the "exactly two" case is ever generated; if not, the choice is a distractor the lesson still has to teach.
- `angle-benchmark-estimate` has no standard. It is a real and useful skill (it is the sense check for every protractor reading), best filed under 4.MD.C.6 as estimation; its kite and gate figures must be drawn to the true degree or the type is unanswerable.
- `triangle-angle-sum`, `triangle-exterior-angle` and `parallel-lines-angle` are judged to serve only 8.EE.C.7, yet half their examples have no x at all; their primary standard is 8.G.A.5, with 8.EE.C.7 for the expression variants. `angle-chase-chain` likewise. Align the catalog.
- `geo-isosceles-exterior-angle` uses the base-angle theorem, a Geometry fact (HSG.CO.C.10), in grade 8. Acceptable with the fold shown as the reason, but the catalog should say it borrows. Its two cases (exterior at the apex: twice a base angle; at a base corner: apex plus the other base angle) are different and both need a beat.
- `rg-triangle-third-side-test`, `triangle-side-test` and `triangle-third-side` are three types of one idea with three question shapes; one concept serves all three, and the lessons should share their opening. `triangle-third-side` asks three things (least, greatest, how many); the count is the off-by-one trap and needs the hinge's number line.
- `triangle-conditions` and `triangle-uniqueness` overlap (both "how many triangles"); the second is the swinging-arc case of the first.
- `angle-pair-measure`'s description names a bigger angle and a vertical pair, but both examples are three angles around a point. Read every variant before writing; if three kinds live under the name, the lesson has three cases.
- `angle-chase-chain` always resolves to the exterior angle of the small triangle (∠DEC = ∠A + ∠B), so the "chain" collapses to one fact for a child who sees the construction; the lesson should show both the long route and the short one, and the generator might vary which angle is asked.
- Degenerate film seeds are not visible in the examples; before writing, check each type's `film` seed. Weak main examples would be a protractor reading of 90 or 180, a crossing where x comes out 0, a set like 1, 1, 1, or three angles of 60.

**Standards clauses never practiced (exact text).**

- 4.MD.C.6: "Sketch angles of specified measure." The app never asks for a sketch. The lessons teach the protractor habit in both directions anyway (`protractor-ray-choice` is sketching's multiple-choice shadow).
- 7.G.A.2: "Draw (freehand, with ruler and protractor, and with technology) geometric shapes with given conditions." Never asked; the compass-arc picture is the lesson's stand-in for drawing.
- 7.G.B.5: "complementary" angles appear in no example (no 90° whole). If no variant has one, the clause is unpracticed; a 90° whole in `angle-equation` or `adjacent-angles` would cover it.
- 8.G.A.5: "Use informal arguments to establish facts ... arrange three copies of the same triangle so that the sum of the three angles appears to form a line, and give an argument in terms of transversals why this is so." Every problem is "find the angle"; the argument is never asked. The lessons give the argument as the opening, so the standard is taught even where it is not tested. "The angle-angle criterion for similarity of triangles" is never practiced directly; `triangle-conditions`' three-angle case is its other face, and the lesson should name it.
- 7.EE.A.1: "factor" and "rational coefficients" are not exercised here; only collecting like terms is, in `angle-claim-reasoning`. A secondary link, no change needed.
- 8.EE.C.7a (one solution, infinitely many, none) is not practiced in this family and should not be.

## 7. The pilot: five problem types

Chosen so one sitting shows every grade, every new tool, the habits on every kind of question, and the misconceptions standardized tests aim at.

| # | Problem type | Grade | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `protractor-reading` | 4 | The picture is born here: the sweep with the readout, the ring of turn, the protractor placed and read, the two scales that add to 180 with the look check deciding. Main example a reading of 100 (where 80 looks plausible), with 170 as the quick second case. Exercises 4.1 and 4.2. |
| 2 | `adjacent-angles` | 4 | Angles add: the fit, the middle-letter rule, part = whole − part, and the realistic trap of adding (110 and 50 answered 160). Exercises `wedge`, `copy`, `fit`. The multiple-choice sibling `adjacent-angle-equation` follows in the next wave on the same kit. |
| 3 | `angle-equation` | 7 | The crossing: the half-turn proves "across: equal", the straight angle proves "side by side: 180", the letters rule tells which is which from a sentence, and the two cases (set equal; add to the whole) become equations on the balance. The equal-versus-180 confusion is the family's highest-stakes error. |
| 4 | `rg-triangle-third-side-test` | 7 | Sides must reach: the arcs that meet, touch or miss, the hinge that opens from the difference to the sum, the boundary that is flat, "could" and "could not" in one type. Exercises 4.4; its method covers `triangle-side-test` and `triangle-third-side`. |
| 5 | `triangle-angle-sum` | 8 | The family's central argument: corners to a line, then the parallel through the top corner and the slide; numbers in one case, expressions in the other, "find x, then answer the question". Exercises `copy`, `fit` along a transversal and parallel marks; the same construction then serves the exterior-angle and chain lessons. |

If the five land, the next wave is `turn-degrees` (the ring with cuts), `parallel-lines-angle` (slide along, two sizes), `triangle-exterior-angle` (fill the outside), `triangle-conditions` (what fixes a triangle: arcs and scale-up) and `protractor-angles-select` (select-many over one protractor), which complete the tool set and the question kinds.
