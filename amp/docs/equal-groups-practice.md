# Equal Groups practice

Twenty deterministic families give learners several ways into whole-number multiplication and division: unit arrays, equal groups, sharing, measurement, partial products, nested groups, comparisons, and two-step stories. Every family links to a specific page of _The Equal Groups Workshop_. A learner can move from a result to the reasoning and representations behind it.

Remainder decisions use distinct questions and answer units. With 17 items and a group size of 5, complete kits asks for 3 kits, capacity asks for 4 vehicles, and leftovers asks for 2 items. Exact-divisibility cases also occur, so capacity does not always mean adding one. These are components of interpretation; the isolated questions do not cover an entire multistep-story standard.

Small arrays use at most five rows and five columns. Pairing uses quantities through twenty. Grouped-counter diagrams contain at most eight boxes with two through eight places each; filled circles are items and outlined circles are empty places. Split arrays keep equal square cells and mark two disjoint row regions A and B. Labels identify givens and units rather than printing a computed answer. Larger pairing quantities are presented as concise text tasks.

Sharing asks for items per tray; measuring asks for the number of fixed-length pieces. Missing-factor and missing-dividend problems reverse the direction of a product. The zero/one family supplies cases outside the older multiplication-facts range. Comparison problems distinguish a quantity from a times-as-many factor. Partial-product tasks ask for one region or the tens contribution, not the complete product. Two-step problems require combining quantities before finding remaining stock or change.

The definitions in `src/content/equal-groups-definitions.ts` specify skill, instructional grade, book page, and bounded objective. `src/content/equal-groups-coverage.ts` records 26 selected components as partial numeric practice or planned. This checklist is not an exhaustive standards inventory. In particular, constructing models and equations, explaining properties, fluent delayed recall, estimation, exhaustive factor pairs, prime/composite classification, and full multidigit domains require separate evidence. A correct numeric answer does not certify those requirements.

Run `npm test`, `npm run audit`, and `npm run review:math` from `amp`. The exact Python checker is independently authored and solves public prompts and figure data without importing the generators. The browser sweep exercises every registered family at phone and landscape sizes and saves short-phone screenshots for these families. Structural tests check cell geometry, item counts, domain edges, invalid figures, deterministic generation, and book bindings. Independent mathematical and visual review complements these checks.

The official [grade-three operations standards](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/OA/) distinguish group meanings, operation properties, fluency, stories, and explanations. The [grade-four operations standards](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/OA/) include comparison, remainder interpretation, factor pairs, and patterns. Editorial links identify selected components; they do not imply complete coverage or publisher endorsement.
