# AMP for Schools

Schools, districts and single teachers use AMP through a roster layer on top of the learner the practice loop already knows. This document is the design every school change follows: what each person gets, the data model, the seams into existing code, and sign-in.

Families use the same model at their own size: see [families, classes and adult learners](families.md).

Build order:

1. Foundation: roster, access, license and settings tables; `can`, `defineSchoolRoute`, `resolveSettings`, event log; the seams; `scripts/school-admin.ts`.
2. Evidence: rollup trigger, level days, heartbeat, read queries.
3. First run: UI kit and `SchoolFrame`; guided setup, invites, calendar, import wizard, seats; card, code and pictures; Start class; printable cards.
4. Teacher: Today, class map, student record, gradebook, evidence sheet, time report, report-card PDF, exports.
5. Classroom: levels and standings with settings at every scope; workbook stack and roster marking.
6. School and district: overview, rollups, year rollover, integrations with Google and Microsoft sign-in, privacy center, activity log.
7. Launch: `/schools`, scope and sequence, trust page, parent link; synthetic seed for 30, 1,000 and 10,000 students; capture rig; full sweep and polish; load test; links from the site.

It scales by collapsing. A lone teacher never sees school admin screens. A district adds one level above. Phones get cards instead of grids at 320 and 390 px.

## How a school starts

No school exists until we issue it a license. There is no self-serve start: `/schools` offers "Talk to us", and `/school/new` without a setup invitation creates nothing and goes to `/schools`.

1. **We issue it.** After the contract and the invoice or PO, an operator runs `npm run school:admin -- create-school <private-input.json>` with `{name, adminEmail, seats, startsOn, endsOn, reference, timezone?}`. One transaction makes the org (setup pending), the license and an admin setup invitation; the command mails the invitation through the account mail path and prints JSON with the setup link. Dates are whole days in the school's time zone (America/New_York unless given), and the license runs through `endsOn`. The same name and admin again, while setup is pending, sends the same invitation and creates nothing. It runs with the server's `AMP_ACCOUNTS_URL` and `AMP_ACCOUNTS_SECRET`, so the link is the one the server reads, and its mail settings to send.
2. **The admin opens the link,** signs in or signs up (the mailed link confirms their address), and accepts as the school's admin. They land in guided setup at the calendar. The school step is already answered, and its name, "Just my class" or "A school", and time zone stay editable.
3. **Setup:** calendar, what students see, the data agreement, then the roster and teachers. The roster step says how many seats the license holds and through when.

A free pilot is the same command with reference `Pilot`, given on purpose. Schools that set themselves up before this keep their 30-day evaluation license until it ends; nothing migrates them.

### When a school pays

Before the license is issued: by contract and invoice or PO, recorded as the license's `reference`. `set-seats`, `extend-license` and `issue-license` change a license afterwards, and "Request more seats" on the license screen writes to the company mailbox. A later Stripe seat-pack checkout can become a second way to get a license, issuing it the same way. Prices never appear in this repository.

### One account, several places

One account can be a parent, have a free assessment under way and work at schools, all at once (`hasHome` and `homePlace` in `src/web/features/onboarding/guard.ts`).

- **`/`** opens where the person last worked, the family side or a specific school, remembered per account on the device (`place-memory.ts`, recorded as each screen settles). With nothing remembered it's the family side when they have learners, a trial, consent or a purchase, and the school when they only work at schools. A school whose setup this admin hasn't finished never takes `/` from someone with a family side. Back and Forward to `/` reopen what it showed then.
- **The place switcher** (`src/web/features/navigation/PlaceSwitcher.tsx`) is one menu in the family drawer, the school's rail and drawer, and setup's rail and bar. It lists the family side, the adult's own practice ([families](families.md)), and each school with the person's role there, or "Finish setting up", and only places this account can open. Choosing one remembers it.
- `npm run test:school-places-ui` checks both in real browsers: a trial and a half-finished setup, a parent who runs a school, the switcher everywhere it appears, and Back, Forward and reload between the two sides.

## Student experience is the school's choice

Schools have real philosophies here, and this control is a selling point. Settings cascade **district → school → class → student**. A higher level can lock one, and the screen shows who set it.

| Setting                 | Values                            | Starts at |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------- | --------- |
| Show levels to students | Hidden, student's choice, shown   | Hidden    |
| Standings               | Off, class, school, district      | Off       |
| Standings measure       | Growth this term, practice, level | Growth    |
| Device mode             | Shared, personal                  | Shared    |

- Setup offers three starting points, "Quiet focus", "Levels visible" and "Friendly competition". A teacher running an AP class as a competition changes it for that class unless the school locked it.
- **Standings:** independent work only, from `amp_skill_days` and `amp_level_days`. A board shows the top 10 and your own line, never a ranked list down to last place: a small board lists at most its top three quarters, and nobody's place shows unless they're listed. Off the list, your line reads as your own progress. Class boards use names; wider boards use generated handles, group by grade level, take only students whose own class chose at least that wide, and are cached for a minute. A teacher can leave any student off a class's board, and a student left off anywhere is off every wider board. A level board needs levels shown to students; otherwise it counts growth, so a hidden level stays hidden. A teacher of the class who practices is a line on its board and map, marked and never ranked, unless they switch "Show me in my class" off ([families](families.md#the-teacher-in-their-class)).
- **Never affects scheduling:** time, grades, levels and standings don't feed what's scheduled next.
- **Device mode** sits on the same screen, with the same "set by" and lock. A starting point doesn't change it, because it's about the devices and not what students see.
- **Real data later:** every setting change goes in the event log, so outcomes under each setting can be compared once we have scale.
- The family product stays as it is.

## The grade model

Percent correct is the wrong grade, because AMP serves harder work as students succeed. AMP suggests a **grade range** for a class and term from four components, with fixed defaults. **The teacher decides the grade.**

| Component       | Question                              | Evidence                                                                                                                                                         | Weight |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Course progress | How far through this course's ground? | Strand working levels against the course band and where the calendar says the class should be                                                                    | 40%    |
| Growth          | How much ground this term?            | Objectives newly demonstrated, plus levels gained, against a pace of one grade per year                                                                          | 30%    |
| Practice        | Did the work get done?                | Active minutes against the teacher's weekly target, with problems counted only where no time could be measured (paper); if none is set, against the class median | 20%    |
| Retention       | Does it stick?                        | Independent success on spaced review problems, the one place accuracy isn't skewed by adaptivity                                                                 | 10%    |

- **The gradebook:** suggested range as letter and percent (for example "B to A−, 84–91"), and a final grade the teacher types in whatever form the school uses: B+, 87, 3, Pass. No scale or weight settings; the one number a teacher can set is the weekly target. Too little work shows "Not enough work yet", never a low grade.
- **Every grade opens to evidence:** the objectives, the dates and the actual problems, so "why a B?" takes one click. Overrides are logged. Export as a column to blend with the rest of the course grade.
- **Mixed-grade classes:** a class with no course grades each student against their own grade level.
- **Implementation:** versioned policy in `src/schools/grade-policy.ts`, tuned by agents, following `practice-policy.ts`. Closed terms are frozen with their inputs. Open terms compute on read from rollups and level days, with no replay and no learner lock.
- **Growth this term:** the pace of the level on the days its reported uncertainty was at most 0.6 grades, across the days the term has run (`src/schools/growth.ts`, chosen in `docs/engine/growth.md`). A student whose estimate has not settled has no growth figure, shown as "—", never as zero. The same figure serves the overview, the grade model and the standings board, so a student's board never disagrees with their teacher.
- **What never moves a suggestion:** how many problems a student answered on screen, and how many they got right outside spaced review. `tests/schools-grade-policy.test.ts` proves it.
- **Objectives:** a skill counts on the day a student's own independent successes reach two, with no ancestor credit, and only if it isn't well below where they started the term.
- **Tests with personas:** fast accelerator, steady at-level, two grades behind but growing fast, high accuracy with little work, inactive, joined mid-term.

## Data model

### Principles

1. **Learning stays one identity.** `amp_learners` and everything hanging off it stay as they are. Schools add a roster layer that points at a learner; evidence never moves.
2. **Shaped like OneRoster.** Clever, ClassLink and most SIS exports speak it, so CSV, OneRoster and future sources all become the same rows.
3. **One concept, one table.** One table for people, one for class membership, one for the calendar, one settings mechanism, one event log.
4. **One rule in one place.** One function decides who can do what, one resolves settings, one defines a route.
5. **Evidence is immutable, projections are rebuildable, closed grades are frozen.**
6. **History is kept.** Enrollments and roles have dates, and changes are events.

**Keys and integrity:**

- A school student's learner has `account_id='school:<person id>'`, one synthetic account per student. Never a shared class account: `/me` takes an exclusive lock and lists every learner.
- **A learner has exactly one owner**, enforced by `UNIQUE(learner_id)`, because `transaction()` requires a matching `account_id`. A move is one transaction: revoke the seat grant, move the account, clear the old link, set the new one. The old school keeps frozen grades.
- **Deleting doesn't erase a registrar's record by accident.** Evidence rollups cascade from `amp_learners`, so existing deletion and retention reach them. `amp_people` tombstones through `deleted_at`; `learner_id` and `grant_id` are `ON DELETE SET NULL`. Triggers on those links set `deleted_at` and the seat's `released_at`, so no deletion path needs to know about schools. The tombstone keeps no personal fields: `given_name` becomes `Removed`, and `family_name`, `display_name` and `email` become null. `identifier` stays, so final grades still map to the school's SIS record, and so does the generated `handle`. A move clears the old link, so the old school's row is scrubbed the same way. Agreements copy the signer's name, so deleting a teacher can't block or rewrite history.
- **Dates.** A role or enrollment is active from `begin_on` up to, but not including, `end_on`, in the org's timezone. Ending one today stops access on the next request.
- Evidence rollups key on `learner_id`, so they survive transfers and class changes.
- `UNIQUE(tenant_id, account_id)` where present. Email is indexed but not unique, because siblings often share a parent's address. One active enrollment per class, person and role.
- No hot rows: nothing per-org is written during practice, and `hasAccess` still reads only `amp_grants`.
- Grant rows use channel `pilot` with id `school-seat:<seat>`, satisfying every license gate and avoiding three CHECK widenings.

### Keeping branching small

**This is the complete list of places existing code learns about schools:**

1. `learningAccount` recognises `Bearer st_…`, returning the owner of the student's one learner (`school:<person id>`, or a family's parent: [families](families.md#how-a-family-is-a-class)) and binding the request to that learner.
2. A wrapper on `authenticateIdentity` (`main.ts:91`) gives `st_` a 401 on parent-only routes, even with a cookie on a shared device.
3. `learningConsentActive` gets one `school:` branch: student active, with an accepted agreement at the nearest org up the path.
4. `parent()` gains a sibling, `familyManaged()`, on the routes students can't use: create learner, birth year, starting point, reset, self-marking paper. One word changes per route.
5. `/me` delegates to a separate `studentMe()`, parent-shaped with `account.kind:'student'`. `LegacyApp`, practice, lessons and progress then run unchanged. Its `consent.status` stays `active` so the app never routes a student to parent setup; `canPractice` carries the truth, and false means "ask your teacher".
6. `web/auth.ts` (`accessSession`, `signOut`) and `trialSessionOptions` handle student tokens, and a bad student token goes to `/join`. Parent-only routes also answer a student 401, so only `/me` decides that a sign-in has ended.
7. One synchronous `isStudentDevice()` turns off analytics and engagement before they start. It is true with a stored student token and on the sign-in screens themselves (`/join`, `/c`), which run before any token exists.
8. One hook in `/batches` stores the day's level.
9. `insights.ts` excludes `school:%` so school use doesn't skew family reports.
10. Parent chrome is hidden on `account.kind` in the drawer and `LegacyApp`, and `CurriculumDetails` reads the resolved level setting. A student's direct link to a family, purchase, trial, setup or workbook screen goes back to practice.

**Inside `src/schools/`:**

- **`defineSchoolRoute({ action, ... })`** is the only way to add a route, so a route can't skip authorization, and a test walks the whole table against a cross-tenant matrix.
- **`can(actor, action, target)`** is the only authorization rule: admin covers descendants, teacher covers their classes, viewer reads. Unauthorized returns 404. A student token never passes it.
- **`resolveSettings(chain)`** is one pure function, one zod schema, one screen component. It resolves per enrollment, org chain then class then person.
- **Sources** are plain functions from a file or API to normalized rows, and one reconciler writes them. A row with an ID whose name no longer matches, or a new row that matches someone typed in by hand, is held for an admin. Importing adds and updates; it never removes anyone.

Staff stay ordinary accounts, so there is no `kind:'staff'`: a person can be a teacher and a parent at once.

## Sign-in

### Students

- **Card:** a printable card with a QR holding a revocable per-student link, `/c#<token>`. The token is in the fragment, so it never reaches a server or proxy log, and it leaves the address bar and history before anything else reads the URL. Phones open it from the OS camera; `/join` scans it in the browser (`BarcodeDetector`, with lazy `jsqr` as fallback). Requires `Permissions-Policy: camera=(self)`. Only a hash is stored, so printing again makes new cards and the old ones stop working.
- **Class code:** code → tap your name → 3 of 16 pictures, which a student chooses and confirms the first time. Failure counters are per student and class, so a school behind one address isn't locked out: five wrong tries pause that student for 60 seconds, and many across a class pause the class for five minutes. A new code reopens a paused class; resetting a student's pictures clears that student.
- **Google and Microsoft:** an authorization code flow with PKCE, state and nonce (`src/schools/oidc.ts`), reusing existing client IDs, so students never enter the parent user table. It verifies the ID token's signature, issuer, audience and nonce, and issues the same `st_` session a card gets. An admin turns a provider on per school (`amp_orgs.sign_in_providers`), which creates an `sso` credential for each rostered student with an email; the lookup hash covers the provider, tenant and address, so one tenant's credential can't answer in another. An email two students in the tenant share gets no credential, and those students keep using cards. The button appears on `/join/$code` only when that class's school turned it on. No match, or an email the provider doesn't vouch for (Microsoft's follows `xms_edov`, as `providerSignupRefused` does), ends on the class page with a clear message and the code and card. Each console must allow `<origin>/api/v1/school/join/oidc/<google|microsoft>/callback`. Add them to `infra/auth-providers.ts` once they're registered: `providers check` stops a deploy on a declared URI a provider rejects.
- **Why cards are the default:** Google blocks under-18 Education users from third-party apps until an admin allows the app. Cards and codes work with no IT involvement. Turning on Google leads to a one-page IT handoff with our client ID.
- **Start class board:** big code and QR on the projector, with names lighting up as students arrive. This is the demo moment.
- **Device modes:** the `deviceMode` setting. Shared (session storage, signed out after 30 idle minutes, clears `amp.learner` and `amp.trial`) or personal (local storage, 90 days, revocable by the teacher). A student in two classes signs in as shared if either class says so.

### Families

A teacher or admin makes a read-only link from a student's record ("Share with family"), and can copy it again or revoke it. It's a `share` credential whose token is derived from the credential's id with the server's secret, as an invite is, so nothing secret is stored and the same link can be copied for as long as it lasts (366 days). A class's "Parent links" action makes a fresh link for every student as a CSV for the school's own mail system; every link it replaces stops, and each change is logged. Aides and viewers neither see nor make links.

The link is `/p#<token>`. The first thing the app does is take the token out of the address bar and keep it in that tab's session storage, so a reload works and the token ends with the tab. It reads one student through a rate-limited public route and returns nothing about anyone else: the student's name as the school shows it, class names, levels by strand, active time and objectives gained this term, recent objectives, and final grades only where a teacher entered one. Levels show whatever the student's own setting, because the reader isn't the student. The page has no navigation, no analytics and no engagement tracking, and carries `noindex` and `no-referrer`. A revoked or expired link, an ended enrollment or a deleted student all read as "Ask the school for a new link".

**Not everything.** It covers most small schools. These are the additions:

| Case                                        | With today's sign-in                                            | What we add                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any email address                           | Works (password, passkey)                                       | The mailed invite link proves the address, so no second confirmation email; the email text stops saying "parent account". A copied link binds an account but proves nothing about a mailbox, because an admin can copy it: that person confirms their address the usual way |
| Google Workspace or Microsoft 365           | Works unless IT restricts third-party apps                      | IT handoff page with the admin-consent link; email fallback                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Microsoft doesn't vouch for the email       | `providerSignupRefused` correctly refuses to create the account | Clear message and email fallback. Needs a test on a real education tenant                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Invite address differs from sign-in address | Would create a mismatch                                         | Invite binds to whichever account accepts it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| School mail filter eats the invite          | Stuck                                                           | "Copy invite link" is always visible                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Teacher leaves                              | Account stays, but access must end                              | Role end date, checked on every request                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Clever, ClassLink, Okta, SAML               | **Not covered**                                                 | Later: the same person resolver students use                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

A new staff account isn't treated as a new parent: the guard sends them to `/school` rather than `/try`, and no follow-up enrollment runs.

## Other architecture

**Evidence (no frontier replay in request paths):**

- An `AFTER INSERT` trigger on `amp_attempts` (same pattern as `0008-skill-evidence.sql`) upserts `amp_skill_days`, using `weight>=1` from `0079` for independence.
- `/batches` stores `amp_level_days`; paper results also write one. Term start and end levels are read, not replayed.
- Active time is a heartbeat on student sessions only, reusing `EngagementClock`'s 60-second idle rule, written in one statement outside the learner lock with deltas clamped to elapsed time.
- Today's rules (`src/schools/today.ts`) run over the rollup and `amp_skill_evidence` for the roster: stuck, shared gap, no practice against target, ready for more, a good day. Thresholds sit in the grade policy.
- Pending migrations share one transaction, so the trigger ships in a later PR than the base tables.

**Workbook stack:** client-driven, concurrency 2. The teacher's browser requests each student's PDF through a staff route reusing extracted helpers from `src/workbook/routes.ts`, with a fixed workbook number so retries don't create new ones. It retries 503s and merges with lazy `pdf-lib` in a Web Worker, in files of up to 40 students. No job table, no stored PDFs. The QR on the page opens a teacher marking route, a roster grid using the existing paper weights. Marks are drafts until saved, and saved marks don't change, because attempts are immutable.

Another screen links a group sheet with `.../classes/$class/workbooks?students=<comma-separated person ids>&skill=<skill id>`. The students are preselected and each workbook leans on the skill: today's idea teaches it when a lesson can, and it takes up to half of practice. Unknown students or skills are dropped, so a stale link still opens the tab.

**School and district overview:** `/school/$org` is a head's or superintendent's morning read: the week in a sentence, seats, what needs them, the term's weekly minutes, where students stand against their own grade, then a table of classes or schools. A district's is the same screen rolled up. It reads per-student facts bound by the org's `path`, never the frontier. Weeks come from `amp_learner_weeks`, one row per learner so practice writes no shared row, and first and latest levels come straight off a covering index on the generated `overall_level`. At 10,000 students in 12 schools the district overview's p95 stays under 300 ms with no cache, so data changed anywhere shows on the next read; `scripts/verify-school-org-ui.ts` builds that district and fails above it. A teacher, and a lone teacher's one-class school, go straight to their classes.

**Year rollover:** `.../year`, a wizard with a URL per step, ending in a dry run that does the whole thing in a transaction and rolls it back, so what it reports is what will happen. Confirming runs the same transaction: a new year and terms, a copy of each chosen class with `rolled_from`, a new join code, its teachers and its settings; students moved up a grade; the chosen grade finishing at each school, with roles and enrollments ended and seats freed. Confirming the same dates again changes nothing, and overlapping dates are refused. The new classes start empty for next year's roster, or with the same students from the first day if the admin says so. Last year's classes aren't archived: they keep their URLs and grades, teachers keep their enrollment so they can read what they taught, and the year switcher on the class list reaches them. The class list shows the year running today, else the next one coming, else the last one that ran.

**Campus transfer:** the role and enrollments at the old school end today and a role begins at the new one, same person and learner, so evidence and frozen grades don't move. A seat on a license that covers the new school stays; otherwise it's released and one taken from the new school's license, or the student shows "needs a seat". It needs `students.transfer` at both schools, so in practice a district admin.

**Privacy center:** `.../privacy`. The whole-school export is a ZIP of CSVs streamed a keyset page at a time, admin only, and logged. Deleting one student or a selection uses `removeStudent`. Deleting all of a school's student data needs the school's name typed and runs from the browser, a batch per request, each student their own transaction: the log says a deletion is underway, so a failure or a reload resumes with no job table. This is the full erasure the data model mentions: final grades and the SIS ID go too, leaving a tombstone with no one's name on it. A student who moved to another school in the district is that school's now, and is left alone.

**Activity log:** `.../activity`, admins only. Sentences are built on read from `amp_school_events` and today's names, so no name is ever written to the log and a tombstone reads "Removed". Pages are keyset on `(at, id)`, one index scan per org. Filters live in the URL; the person filter carries only an ID, and the name typed to find them travels in a request body.

**Public pages:** `src/web/features/schools/`. `/schools` takes its counts from `/api/v1/schools/curriculum`, and its product screens from one list, `screens.ts`. The scope and sequence is built once from the live catalog in `src/schools/scope-and-sequence.ts`; the web page, the CSV and the PDF all read that build, with a long cache and an ETag. The PDF is an HTML document through the workbook renderer (`renderWorkbookPdf`), Letter landscape, rendered once per release. Objective counts, problem types and lessons are never typed into copy.

**Trust, built in:** students get no analytics or attribution, and staff get no ad collection or marketing mail. Setup includes accepting a finished data agreement (published at `/schools/trust`), covering FERPA school-official use and school-authorized COPPA consent. The privacy center shows the agreement, whole-school export, deletion and the activity log. Prices never appear in this public repo.

**Frontend:** `src/web/features/school/`, shaped like `features/workbooks/`. A new `SchoolFrame` (sidebar on wide screens, top bar and drawer on phones) resets the legacy global element rules. New kit in `src/web/ui/`: `DataGrid` (sticky header and first column, card fallback, virtualized for big rosters), link `Tabs`, `StatTile`, `Sheet`, `EmptyState`, `Avatar`, `Badge`, `SearchField`, `Stepper`, plus dense-table and heading tokens. Charts build on `LevelChart` and the frontier map, using the `dataviz` and `frontend-design` skills.

**Integrations:** `.../integrations` lists CSV and spreadsheet paste with column auto-mapping and a dry-run diff; OneRoster 1.2 CSV import; configurable gradebook CSV plus OneRoster results; Google and Microsoft sign-in. Clever, ClassLink and Google Classroom show a real "set this up with us" request that records an event and notifies the mailbox, never a button that fails.

**Licensing:** operated by privileged agents, with no internal admin UI. `scripts/school-admin.ts` issues schools (`create-school`, above), creates orgs, issues licenses and adjusts seats with JSON output, reading names from a private input file, and direct database access covers the rest. Adding seats or issuing a license seats the students waiting under that org, longest-waiting first; renewals replace seats on a license that has ended. A school's own license screen shows seats and dates, with "Request more seats" notifying the company mailbox.

**Deleting an org:** `npm run school:admin -- delete-org <private-input.json>` with `{orgId, confirm, commit?, allowLicensed?}` removes a school, district or family org entirely: its whole tree, people, roles, enrollments, classes, calendars, seats, licenses, credentials, student sessions, term grades, external refs, agreements, settings and activity log, in one transaction. `confirm` must be the org's exact current name. Without `"commit": true` it is a dry run that prints the per-table counts and changes nothing. It refuses an org with a parent (name the root; children go with it), a license in force whose reference is not "Pilot" or "Evaluation" unless `allowLicensed` is true, a person with rows in another tree, and a school learner with commerce orders. Only learners owned by `school:` accounts are deleted, with their evidence; a family child or an adult learner linked as a member is detached and keeps its record, and a seat grant on it is revoked. The activity log is the one append-only table it deletes: the command sets `amp.retention_purge='school-org'` inside its transaction, the way retention does for commerce, and nothing else may. Production runs it in the API pod through `kubectl -n corpora-amp exec`, with the input file copied in and removed afterward.

## Demo places

Before any agreement or payment, a teacher or school leader explores AMP fully alive in **the demo school**: Brightwater Elementary, with made-up students and twelve weeks of believable practice. Schools buy through white-glove sales, so the demo is built to present well, and it is the same every day, so someone presenting it can know its students and classes by heart. The real class never comes from a demo: it comes from the real onboarding (the data agreement, then the roster, with our help). Families and tutors don't get demos; their children are real and start unnamed with the free allowance.

- **One shared school per demo zone** (`src/schools/demo/shared.ts`). **`POST /api/v1/school/demo`** `{kind: "class" | "school", timezone?}` puts the caller in the zone's current school (New York unless the device says otherwise) in under a second: a few rows, nothing built. It answers `{demo: {orgId, classId, path}}`, where `path` is the hero class's Today; a second request (a double-click) waits for the first under the visitor's lock and returns the same membership. **`DELETE /api/v1/school/demo`** takes the caller out of it.
  - **Class:** the caller teaches the hero class, Grade 5 Math: 24 students from `GRADE_5_CLASS`, with one of everything Today talks about (a shared gap, a student stuck on one idea, two ready for more, fast growth from behind, a late joiner, a quiet student), a short last week, standings on, and a stack of workbooks from two school days ago with five still unmarked. To them the place is their class ("solo"), and the bar's action is **Set up your real class**.
  - **School:** the same school, with Grades 1–4 and 6 taught by three demo teachers who are only names, listed as the school's active staff. The caller is its admin, and teaches the hero class too; the bar's action is **Talk to us**. Asking for the school after the class adds the admin role to the same membership.
- **Static content, today's dates** (`roster.ts`, `shift.ts`, `snapshot.ts`): every class's term is honest: each sitting the production planner picks the problem, a misspecified learner answers from that student's true level, and level days are the production estimator over those answers (`simulate.ts`, `truth.ts`). It is computed once per `DEMO_TERM_VERSION` at a fixed anchor week in New York standard time, one term per weekday (a Tuesday's term has Tuesday's sitting, ending at 9:00), on a developer machine (`npm run demo:terms`: 700 to 900 CPU seconds a weekday, up to 6.6 GB with eight threads), and checked in under `src/schools/demo/terms/` (brotli, about 2 MB a weekday, each file under the 5 MiB limit), so it ships in the API image. **No API pod ever simulates a term**, warm or cold: a pod reads the weekday's file when it writes a school, parses each student's term only when it writes that student (twelve at a time), and lets it go. Writing a school adds about 130 MiB at peak to an idle API process. Each file's key is the version and a digest of its jobs (the roster and the day); `tests/demo-terms.test.ts` fails when a key is stale and proves the server bundle has no simulation in it. A pod whose image lacks a current term logs `demoTermsMissing` (and an error at boot), and a demo request answers `503 demo_preparing` ("The demo is being prepared"); it never computes. `amp_demo_snapshots` (migration 0113) is no longer read; rows an older release wrote go after a week. Each day it is written with every date moved by whole weeks so it ends on the zone's today, each time at the same wall-clock hour in the zone. The hero class's printed workbooks roll as on the anchor day too (`seedDate` in `src/workbook/compose.ts`), so their paper marks match. So two days show the same students, answers, marks and levels, and only the dates differ. Bump `DEMO_TERM_VERSION` when the model or the roster changes what a term is, then run `npm run demo:terms` (it computes only the weekdays whose key changed) and commit the files; `npm run demo:terms -- --check <weekday>` recomputes one weekday and compares it with the shipped one. An engine release needs neither: a shipped term is the engine's output when it was made, as a real student's history is.
- **Rewritten every day:** a zone's school for a day is a row of `amp_demo_schools` (migration 0114). The warmer (every ten minutes on each pod; on in production, `AMP_DEMO_WARM=off|on`) reads the weekday's shipped term and, an hour before the switch, writes the day's school as a new org with the bulk writer; one pod claims the day and the others wait or read. `AMP_DEMO_WARM` only controls this nightly write; there is no simulation for it to control. The current school is the newest fully written one whose sitting has ended: the pointer moves at 9:00 in the zone (`switchAt`), on every pod at once, and only to a whole school (`built_at` is set last). So nothing a visitor sees is later than now: before 9:00 they see yesterday's school, where nobody has practiced today yet, and its "here today" stays empty. The day's write wipes whatever visitors changed. Two hours after the switch the old school goes through the tree removal (`src/schools/org-delete.ts`). Zones: New York and the zones of visitors this week; a zone nobody visits for a week stops being written. A visitor's place moves to the new school on their next request (`followDemo`); a link into the old school answers `409 demo_moved` with the new path, and the app opens it instead of showing an error. With no school yet (first boot, a new zone), the first request writes it under the 180 s wait and everyone else waits for that write; while a new day's school is still being written, visitors join yesterday's.
  - **Bulk writes:** each distinct problem and outcome is sent once and every answer is assembled in the database with the learner's own ids. For learners with no answers yet, `amp.bulk_answers` holds the per-answer triggers (migration 0113) and the writer numbers the answers and writes skill days and skill evidence once, the same rows the triggers write (`tests/school-demo-terms.integration.ts`).
- **Visitors play:** assigning, printing, "Practice as", records, Start class and the rest work as anywhere. What one visitor changes, others may see until the day's rewrite; everything in it is synthetic. A new class code is the one thing not offered (`signin.code` is refused in a demo): one visitor's code would be everyone's.
- **Visitors never see each other.** A visitor's person is marked `demo_visitor`, and every list of people leaves out a visitor who isn't the viewer (`visibleToSql` in `demo/sql.ts`): the staff list, class teacher names, report cards, the teacher's line on the map and boards, the activity log and its people search (an event by or about another visitor is not shown). The made-up teachers and students have no account and are never visitors. `tests/school-demo.integration.ts` reads every view as two visitors and finds neither in the other's responses.
- **"Practice as":** each demo student's roster row opens practice as that student on this device (`POST /api/v1/school/demo/students/:student/session`, the session a picture sign-in issues); signing out hands the device back. Only a demo student is found, by an admin or a teacher of their class; a real student never is. On another device, the class code and a student's name work too: every demo student's pictures are fox, boat and kite. The hero class's "here today" moves with the clock (`refreshPresence`).
- **Marked, never counted:** `amp_orgs.demo` (migration 0102). A quiet "Demo" label sits in the bar beside the AMP logo and on the place's row in the drawer's switcher. A demo is never billed: `demo` grants are not revenue, and the problem insights, its problem reports included, leave demo students out (`demoLearnerSql`). A demo's license never makes a visitor's own practice free.
- **Synthetic, and it adds no one:** `can` refuses every action that would add, import, move or write to a person (`NOT_IN_A_DEMO` in `authz.ts`): adding students, roster import, transfers, staff invitations, seat and integration requests, rollover and setup. The app doesn't offer them. So a real child's name never enters a demo, and a demo sends no mail. Its data agreement is AMP's own ("Accepted by AMP, for this demo"), which the made-up students practice under; a real class's agreement comes from its school.
- **Retention:** a visitor's place goes after `RETENTION.idleDemoDays` (30) without a visit. Demos made for one visitor before the shared school are removed by the retention job through the same tree removal.
- `tests/school-demo.integration.ts` proves joining fast, the school alive, visitors invisible to each other, the daily switch at 9:00 with nothing later than now, the removal, and nothing counted; `scripts/verify-school-demo-ui.ts` walks it in real browsers (screenshots in `docs/reviews/school-demo/`).

## Demo, journey, load and gallery

Each command needs `AMP_TEST_DATABASE_URL`, a fixture database, never the shared `/amp` preview. Each makes a throwaway schema and drops it when it stops. Every name is synthetic.

**Seed and preview.** `scripts/school-demo-world.ts` builds a school through the real code paths: issued by `create-school` with its license, its admin's invitation accepted, guided setup and the data agreement through the API, a CSV roster through the real source and the reconciler in chunks, teachers' invitations accepted, and seats. The hero class, Grade 5 with Ms. Okafor and 30 students, answers real problems through the practice API (`scripts/school-class-fixture.ts`, shared with the teacher verification), so Today, the gradebook, standings and the class map read real evidence. Every other class's term is backfilled into the rollups by SQL (`seedTermEvidence` in `scripts/school-org-fixture.ts`). Last week was short, this week is back up, one class is quiet, one has no teacher, a few students wait for seats, and levels spread across grades.

```sh
npm run school:demo -- --size 1000 --port 4420   # 30 | 1000 | 10000; ports 4420-4429
```

`--size 30` is one teacher with 30 students, `1000` a K–8 school, and `10000` a district of twelve schools. It runs the real API and Vite on loopback. Open `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/demo` and choose who you are: the head, the district admin, the teacher or a student. The picker answers on loopback only and stands on the development sign-in, which production refuses. `--keep` keeps the schema.

**The journey.** `npm run test:school-ui` (in `test:ui:2b`, so CI's browser lanes run it) walks the launch story in Chromium at 1280 and WebKit at 390, in accounts mode with the real local PDF renderer: we issue a school, and its head opens the setup link, signs up, sets it up, imports 24 students and invites a teacher; the teacher accepts and prints cards; one student signs in by pictures and one by card, and both answer problems; Today counts them; the gradebook suggests a range and the teacher sets a grade; the CSV export parses and the report cards render; a stack of 24 workbooks merges; a family link opens; removing a student frees the seat and deletes their learning.

**Load.** `npm run school:load` runs 30, then 300 students answering in lockstep through the practice API, against a server process built like production's, with the per-pod pool `databasePoolMax` gives production by default. It reports p50 and p95 per answer and per batch, and errors. `--pool <n>` compares another pool size.

On a development laptop (2026-11-07, pool 6, local PostgreSQL), 30 students answered at p50 24 ms and p95 39 ms; 300 at p50 281 ms and p95 413 ms, with no errors. A batch of problems took p95 345 ms at 30 and 2.5 s at 300. A pool of 20 changed neither, and no event-loop stall over 500 ms was logged: the limit is one process's CPU working through 300 requests that arrive in the same instant (about 1.5 ms of server time per answer and 8 ms per batch), not database connections. Real classes don't answer in lockstep, the app fetches the next batch while a student works, and the API autoscales across pods.

**Gallery.** `npm run school:capture -- [--size 10000] [--product-shots]` drives the seeded demo and captures every school screen, both overviews, every class tab, the student record, the evidence sheet, sign-in and Start class, standings, the family page and the public pages, at 1280 and 390 in light and dark at device scale 2, with geometry at 320 and 768 too. Every screen must have no overflow, no clipped cells, 44 px targets and CLS at or below 0.01. It writes PNGs and an `index.html` contact sheet to `artifacts/schools-gallery/<date>/`, or wherever `--out` says, outside version control. `docs/reviews/schools-launch/` keeps the 1280 and 390 light captures at device scale 1. `--product-shots` refreshes the `/schools` page's screens, as WebP, from the same data.
