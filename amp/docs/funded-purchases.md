# Education-fund purchase requests

AMP can prepare a PDF quote or a program-payment invoice for a parent, or for an adult student paying for herself, and track its submission separately from verified approval, payment and learning access. An authenticated account holder selects an owned learner (a child, or their own adult learner) and an explicitly configured program/product offer. The server fixes the price, tax, term (a year or one month) and approved route in an immutable request snapshot. Preparing or reporting a request never grants access.

No program offers are enabled by default. The national funding registry records research; it does not establish Corpora's vendor approval or a product's eligibility. Deployment requires documentary evidence for the merchant, purchasing route, payment method and document requirements, plus the seller's actual product, price and term. Marketplace and direct-pay routes are supported. Reimbursement and app-store purchasing are outside this request workflow.

## Operator configuration

`AMP_FUNDING_OFFERS_FILE` identifies a private, bounded JSON array validated by `loadFundingOffers`. Each entry follows `fundingOfferSchema` in `src/funding-purchase/contracts.ts`. It includes a revision, program/year, SKU, merchant details, price and tax in integer cents, verified HTTPS destination, parent instructions, validity window and private evidence references. Use a new revision when an offer changes. The price a program sells at is declared one line per program in `src/funding-purchase/program-prices.ts`: Arkansas, Arizona and New Hampshire sell at the public list prices, $599 a year (`programAnnualPriceMinor`) and $59 a month (`programMonthlyPriceMinor`). A listed program sells at that price whatever `subtotalMinor` the file says (the loader logs the difference), and an unlisted program keeps the file's price. An issued invoice's snapshot is never rewritten; as with any offer change, a new price withdraws an invoice not yet converted, which the family replaces with a new one.

### Monthly offers

A program with a monthly price also sells one month at a time. `withMonthlyOffers` (`src/funding-purchase/monthly-offers.ts`) derives, from each of its annual named invoices under individual purchase review, a sibling with `termMonths: 1`, `termPolicy: "one-month-from-verified-provisioning"`, the monthly price, and `annual` changed to `monthly` in its id, SKU and title ("one year" to "one month" in its description). Everything else, including merchant, route, instructions, refund terms and validity, is the program's own offer. The derivation is deterministic, so an issued invoice still matches after a restart. It is skipped for an offer approved product by product (its term evidence names a year), one with tax, text that still names a year, and a program whose offers file already sells a month (`termMonths: 1` may also be configured directly). The annual offer stays first: the pitch quotes it, and the invoice form offers **Yearly** or **Monthly** as a segmented choice. Optional `statusLabel` (at most 100 characters) is the one public status line for the program, copied exactly from the parent site's funding presentation (`presentation.statusLabel` in the site configuration), for example its vendor status and payment route. The signed-out pitch shows it under the funded Buy line; without it, the pitch makes no status claim. Each offer states its program's `refundPolicy`: `full-to-account` (the full amount back to the child's program account), `purchase-to-account` (the purchase as received; a payment platform's fee may not return) or `none` (the program allows no refunds or cancellations). A refunding policy also needs `refundSummary` (at most 200 characters), the terms in one line. The invoice form shows that line above "Create invoice PDF"; for `none` it says the program doesn't allow refunds or cancellations, so the money-back guarantee can't apply. Researched terms with their official sources are in `src/funding-purchase/refund-terms.ts` (`ar-efa`, `nh-efa`: full-to-account; `az-esa`: purchase-to-account); copy them into the offers file. For one release an offer without `refundPolicy` still loads and logs a warning; its pages then make no program-specific refund promise. After that the field becomes required. The parent pages show the 30-day money-back guarantee on every route with one footnote: refunds go back the way you paid, and a program that allows none is disclosed before payment. Synthetic fixtures in tests are not product prices or available offers.

There are two purchase-review modes:

- Without `purchaseReview`, the approved-offer configuration requires references for the exact SKU and term (`annualTermReference`, whatever the term) as well as vendor, route and payment evidence.
- `purchaseReview: "individual-approval-required"` lets an approved DirectPay vendor prepare a named invoice for the program to review. The exact SKU and annual-term approval references may be absent because the invoice starts that review. The seller must accurately describe its product, price and annual term. This mode requires `route: "direct-pay"`, `documentPolicy: "named-student-invoice"` and `activationPolicy: "after-settlement"`. It cannot enable marketplace checkout or credit-based access.

Preparing an invoice is not program approval. The family submits the invoice through its program's ordinary purchase process; no separate blanket product ruling is needed merely to generate that document. Fulfillment requires independently verified approval and full settlement for the matching invoice, correct account and learner, active consent, and the quoted price and annual term. A rejected or differently approved purchase must be reconciled; it cannot be provisioned as if the original invoice had been accepted.

An offer selects one reviewed document policy:

- `opaque-student-reference-accepted` produces a quote using the learner reference. No student billing name is accepted.
- `named-student-invoice` produces an invoice with a name explicitly entered by the parent as it appears in the program account. It includes a unique invoice number, issue and submission dates, merchant details, product/SKU, quantity, subtotal, tax, total and the one-learner term. The form asks for one email, "Email for the AMP account (parent, or the student if 18+)": the account that later claims the invoice. It is not a receipt or proof of program approval, payment or access. Submit it through the configured program route only once; do not also pay by card.

Enable an offer only when the specific program accepts its document format. Additional tax forms or a program-specific format require a separate supported workflow. The supplied PDF fonts support Western Latin text, including accents. Unsupported names are rejected before persistence with an instruction to contact the merchant for an invoice; names are never transliterated or discarded. Configuration rejects unsupported merchant/product text before showing offers.

A request expires after at most 30 days, bounded by the offer's validity. Disabling an offer or changing its configuration blocks document download, submission reporting and conversion of an unconverted request. An original invoice linked to a verified order remains available to its parent even after offer withdrawal or expiry; it does not purport to show current payment status. Its history displays an unavailable-offer notice. It does not revoke existing licenses. An exact replay of a completed conversion remains a no-op even if its offer has since been disabled.

## Parent document and status

`FundingPurchaseCenter` is a focused workspace with learner selection, request history, an authenticated PDF download, the verified program destination and explicit submission reporting. Its stable account identity is separate from its renewable access token. Active verified consent is required for a new request for a child; existing records can be viewed without creating new access.

### An adult buying for herself

An adult account holder (a college student with her own education-funds account, say) buys for her own adult learner. She acts for herself, so no parental consent applies: claiming an invoice, preparing a request, provisioning, the activation email and gift activation check `purchaseConsentSatisfied` (`src/server/account-access.ts`), which accepts the account's own live adult learner (never a practice profile) and otherwise requires the account's verified parental consent. An unnamed child's restricted learning permission never stands in for that consent. On the program page (`/purchase/<program>`), the invoice is connected to **Me** or to a child; "Me" starts her own learner if she has none. A child chosen without consent leads to the consent step instead of a claim that would fail. An account with children or a parental consent record follows the child or consent steps; an adult with her own learner can buy for herself. A consent request in progress resumes consent even before a verified record exists. Pending invoices for the verified account email select a recipient only when their billing names uniquely match the same existing learner or the signed-in adult’s name. Names are compared without case or extra whitespace; an email address alone does not identify who is learning. With no learner or unambiguous invoice recipient, the page asks who's learning: **For me**, or **For my child** (consent, then the child's details). Matching a name never grants consent or claims an invoice automatically; a child's name and birth year still need consent first.

The Letter PDF includes merchant/contact details, quote and student references, program/year, SKU, description, subtotal, tax, total, UTC expiry and the activation-based term: twelve months, or one month, which starts at activation or when the learner's current access ends if that is later (`fundedTermText`). The quote policy produces an unpaid quote, not an invoice, receipt, approval or promise of reimbursement. The invoice policy produces the separate payment-request document described above. Downloading it does not open a popup or send it to an agency. Parents must complete the program's process themselves. Cancelling a quote in AMP does not cancel an order submitted to the program.

Payment, approval, fulfillment, refund and access are separate status fields. A parent's “submitted” report is unverified. Private evidence references are excluded from parent purchase-status responses.

## Verified provisioning

`npm run funding:import -- /trusted/path/events.json` requires an explicit `AMP_DATABASE_URL` and the current offer configuration. This is an operator-only import, not a public webhook or marketplace integration. The bounded JSON input contains `requestId` and an explicit commerce-event bundle: order creation, approval or settlement as required by policy, fulfillment and license activation. Verify the program transaction and its documents before importing anything.

The bounded operator import takes an exclusive parent-account advisory lock before request, order and learner locks, checks consent (for a child) and learner ownership, and binds account, learner, SKU, channel, amount, currency and activation policy to the quote snapshot. It applies the commerce events and links the request in one transaction. Invalid or incomplete bundles roll back. An identical replay is idempotent; changed replay payloads are rejected. A second active or scheduled grant overlapping the proposed term is rejected with `overlapping_learning_access`, including pilot access. A term paid early is not an overlap: it starts exactly when the learner's current access ends (`fundedTermStart` follows back-to-back terms, up to a year ahead), and provisioning accepts a later start only when an active grant of that learner ends at that moment (`funded_term_start_unsupported` otherwise). Beyond that the operator must reconcile the existing entitlement before provisioning; this importer does not silently replace it. Exact replay of an already converted request remains a no-op. This overlap check covers recorded entitlements; it does not detect an unpaid checkout session or an unissued future renewal. Operators must reconcile pending purchases across payment channels before accepting another funded order.

The default activation policy requires settlement. Activation after approval requires an explicit approved-credit agreement reference in the private offer. An operator cannot change that policy in the order. The license starts at the activation event, or when the learner's current access ends for a term paid early, and ends one calendar year or one calendar month later (`fundedTermEnd`), clamped to the month's last day (February 29 + 12 months and January 31 + 1 month both end on February 28). A term must match its invoice's (`funded_activation_term_required`). The activation email is sent only when access is active at delivery, so a month scheduled after current access sends none. Events preserve approval, fulfillment, settlement and activation as distinct facts. Refunds and revocations use the commerce ledger; disabling a purchasing offer does not silently alter a paid license.

## Verification

The tests cover account isolation, server-bound prices, concurrent idempotency, consent/deletion checks, rollback, exact replay, policy mismatch, disabled/revised offers and financial-record retention. `npm run test:funding-ui` exercises three phone/landscape widths, consent gating, token renewal, submission without activation and PDF downloads. `tsx scripts/verify-funding-quote.ts` additionally requires Poppler tools (`pdftotext`, `pdfinfo`, `pdftoppm`) to inspect text, arithmetic, dimensions and rendered pages. Rendering checks do not establish acceptance by any funding program.

## Records and deletion

A student billing name belongs only to the immutable financial request and authenticated document; it is not copied into the learner profile, campaign aggregates or ad-platform exports. Exact retries must preserve that name; correcting it requires cancelling an unconverted request and preparing a new one.

Learning-data deletion removes unconverted requests, including drafts for a learner whose billing history requires a minimized retained record. Converted requests retain the financial snapshot and order link for reconciliation. Parent export includes an account-scoped funding-request dataset, including a supplied student billing name, with private approval, credit and document-policy evidence removed. The privacy-retention command removes expired unconverted requests; it does not purge linked financial transactions.


## Deployment and program review

Apply migration `0029-funding-invoices.sql` and deploy the compatible API and parent interface before enabling a named-invoice offer. Review the deployed parent notice for the student billing-name purpose and financial retention before collecting it. Existing opaque-reference offers and records keep their quote behavior. Do not change an issued offer snapshot or edit an applied migration. Use a new offer revision when document requirements change.

The Arkansas [vendor invoicing guide](https://efavendoronboardingchecklist.my.canva.site/efa-vendor-invoicing-and-payment-guide) requires a student name for services, vendor identification, itemized charges and a total matching the payment request. Rendering these fields does not establish approval for a specific SKU or successful ClassWallet submission. Verify the actual vendor/product route and payment/refund process before activation. The workflow continues to require verified parent consent; it does not modify the deployed consent policy.
