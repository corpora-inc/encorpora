# Parent consent enrollment

AMP supports purchase-backed consent and a bounded email-plus workflow. Purchase-backed consent requires an explicit parent acknowledgement of the direct notice and a verified initial subscription payment using a reviewed card or Link payment system. No learner profile is collected before consent. The purchase first creates an account license; assignment to an owned learner is a separate atomic operation.

Email-plus is available for deployments whose reviewed practices qualify for that method. Enrollment is disabled without an enabled, current database policy, matching server configuration, a private cryptographic secret and an explicitly supplied mail adapter. The Resend adapter is opt-in; no live credential, verified sender domain, approved deployment policy or legal determination is supplied. Automated tests intercept provider requests and send no real email.

A parent signs in, reviews the notice and supplies their own email address. The first message contains the complete notice and a link. An authenticated affirmative response schedules a second complete notice at least 24 hours later. A second affirmative response activates consent. Opening a link, provider acceptance, elapsed time and an unverified payment notification do not activate email-plus consent. No child profile is collected during this process. Creating learners and purchasing access remain separate actions.

The 24-hour delay is a product safeguard, not a statutory minimum or safe harbor. The additional affirmative response is also a product safeguard. Applicable law and the actual deployment's practices require independent assessment before enabling this method.

## Confirmed parent email

Both enrollment methods require an AMP parent account whose email address the parent has confirmed through the emailed link (`amp_auth_users.emailVerified`). `POST /api/v1/consent/requests` and `POST /api/v1/billing/enrollment/checkout` check it inside the account transaction and refuse with `403 email_unconfirmed`, before any consent request or Stripe checkout exists. This is an account-ownership safeguard, not consent evidence: email-plus still verifies its own recipient address and purchase consent still rests on the verified payment.

Accounts created with a password in production, or through a provider, are confirmed when they first sign in. A passkey sign-up starts unconfirmed: the parent can sign in, use the free trial and open Family. `/api/v1/me` reports `account.confirmationRequired`; the first run shows **Confirm your email** in place on the way into the plan, with resend, and continues to the plan once the link is opened.

## Supported legal boundary

Research checked October 17, 2026 against the amended rule; eCFR displayed Title 16 through October 15. Email-plus is listed only for operators that do not disclose children's personal information within the rule's definition. An ordinary login, checkbox or email-verification flag is not the evidence implemented here. Material changes require renewed notice and consent. [16 CFR 312.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.5)

A qualifying internal-operations provider is different from a recipient with independent uses. Calling a vendor a processor does not establish eligibility. Public profiles, advertising, independent-use analytics and other unsupported disclosure are outside this workflow. Separate consent for disclosure does not make email-plus an eligible method for that disclosure. [16 CFR 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.2)

The FTC describes a delayed confirming message and a revocation notice but does not specify a numerical delay. The current rule controls if older FAQ wording differs. [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)

## Notice and deployment review

This notice contract supports a single operator; joint-operator deployments require a separately reviewed expanded notice contract. The strict policy contract requires actual operator name, postal address, telephone and email; public privacy and parent-account URLs; child-data categories, purposes and retention; service-provider identities, data, purposes and retention; review/deletion instructions; pending-contact and evidence periods. Both emails repeat that notice and explain withdrawal. The public privacy notice must also be published prominently at the relevant collection points; a configured URL alone does not prove publication. [16 CFR 312.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.4)

Deployment review must inventory all operational flows, not just the application source:

| Flow | Application behavior | Required operational review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parent authentication | OIDC authorization-code login; access tokens in session storage; API verifies issuer/audience and remote JWKS | Actual identity provider, account/contact retention, terms, subprocessors and separation from child use |
| Learning service | First-party API stores account-linked learner nickname, generated problems, attempts and derived skill evidence | Hosting/database/backups, access logs, IP/device identifiers, staff access, deletion and security safeguards |
| Math and books | Local deterministic math generation and first-party book delivery; no per-question inference in this service | Hosting/CDN logs and any deployment-added scripts or telemetry |
| Consent email | Only parent recipient, complete notice and short-lived challenge; no child name or learning data | Selected provider's purpose restrictions, retention, security assurances, idempotency and delivery behavior |
| Stripe | Checkout sends an opaque checkout ID as metadata and client reference; AMP internally links it to a learner; provider collects adult billing data | Whether linked identifiers and provider uses meet the applicable disclosure boundary; do not assume eligibility from an opaque ID |
| Funded purchase | Parent downloads a quote with opaque learner reference and chooses how to submit it; no agency submission API | Actual program workflow and recipient uses; distinguish parent-directed submission from operator disclosure |
| Funnel analytics | First-party only: the web app posts a fixed event vocabulary (`src/analytics/events.ts`) to `POST /api/v1/events` under a random browser visitor ID kept in local storage. Props are closed enums and counts, never free text. Visitor rows hold first-touch UTM values, referrer category, device class, placed grade band and a coarse age band (read from a live trial, never its birth year or ID). All funnel times are whole UTC days. Checkout requests carry the visitor ID so the verified webhook can write `purchase`; that link is erased when the purchase is recorded or 90 days after the checkout, leaving only idempotency flags. No account, learner, trial, email, checkout or invoice ID is stored with an event. A purchase's day still matches that day's billing records, which identifies the buyer on a single-purchase day, so the 90-day retention below is the limiting control | Disclose first-party funnel measurement and its 90-day retention in the notice and site privacy summary |
| Problem reports | First-party only: after a problem resolves, "Report a problem" posts reasons from a fixed list to `POST /api/v1/learners/:id/problem-flags`. The server stores them with the learner link, the problem's template, version, seed, prompt and figure kind, the recorded answer and outcome, the web and API build commits, a client kind and the viewport size. A free-text note (280 characters) is offered only to a learner in a consented parent account whose birth year shows they are 13 or older; anonymous trials and younger learners choose reasons only, and the server rejects a note from them. Parent exports include reports. Never sent to a third party | Disclose problem reports, the note rule and their retention in the notice |
| Analytics/support | Reviewed web source has no external analytics request | Confirm deployed tags, error capture, support tools and log exports; unreviewed additions invalidate the inventory |

A reviewer must substantiate the contract assertions with private evidence. Code does not verify those assertions. Written security practices, designated responsibility, periodic risk assessment and service-provider assurances are operational prerequisites beyond these tests. [16 CFR 312.8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.8)

## Configuration and adapter integration

Apply all database migrations, including `0012-consent-delivery.sql`. A private policy file can be validated and installed with `AMP_DATABASE_URL=… npx tsx scripts/consent-policy.ts <private-file>`. This administrative command does not send email. API startup never installs a policy: an obsolete server cannot re-enable a replaced database policy.

The API and worker use matching private `AMP_CONSENT_POLICY_FILE` and `AMP_CONSENT_SECRET_BASE64` configuration. The latter must encode exactly 32 random bytes. Delivery requires `AMP_CONSENT_DELIVERY=resend`, `NODE_ENV=production`, `AMP_CONSENT_RESEND_API_KEY`, a plain verified sender address in `AMP_CONSENT_SENDER`, and `AMP_CONSENT_RESEND_TRACKING_DISABLED=true`. That tracking value is an operator assertion, not an automated provider-settings check. The reviewed notice must name Resend among its processors and accurately describe its data, purposes and retention. A sender or processor configuration does not establish that email-plus is appropriate for the deployment.

Use a dedicated verified sender domain with click and open tracking disabled. Confirm the exact fragment-bearing link survives actual delivery and opens in the same signed-in parent account. Click tracking rewrites links; do not use it for these private challenges. Sender authentication, delivery reputation, provider contracts, actual data practices, retention and incident response require operational review. [Resend tracking](https://resend.com/blog/open-and-click-tracking)

Schedule `npm run consent:deliver` with protected worker configuration. Each invocation processes at most eight messages sequentially; each external call has a ten-second abort deadline, and database connections/statements also have deadlines. No background sender runs inside the API. The SDK endpoint is fixed to `https://api.resend.com`, redirects are rejected, and the adapter ignores environment endpoint overrides. Production runtime is required because the SDK's development-mode error logging can include private provider messages. Logs contain safe job outcomes only.

Before external dispatch, the database records an immutable provider/sender/credential fingerprint and payload digest with the first-dispatch time. Identical retries reuse the original outbox key. Resend documents a 24-hour idempotency period; AMP permits retries for at most 23 hours from the recorded boundary, at most eight attempts, separated by five minutes. A credential rotation, sender or payload change stops ambiguous retries because a changed provider account or request may not share the original key's protection. No automatic replacement key is created. [Resend idempotency](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys)

An uncertain send is not a rejection. Exhaustion, expired protection, binding mismatch or provider conflict produces a stopped-unknown status. Migration also stops historical attempted rows that lack a trustworthy first-dispatch time. The parent can withdraw the request and begin a new one; withdrawal invalidates its old links. Accepted means provider acceptance, not inbox delivery or consent. Both explicit affirmative responses remain required. Delivery metadata follows its request's retention and is deleted with the outbox; it contains no duplicate recipient or message body.

The API routes are authenticated `GET /api/v1/consent`, `POST /api/v1/consent/requests` and `POST /api/v1/consent/acknowledge`. They use strict request schemas, account ownership, bounded bodies and no-store responses. GET cannot consume consent authority. Per-account request limits and route limits bound accidental resend and abuse; operational abuse monitoring is still required.

`ParentConsentCenter` is an isolated workspace with `token`, stable `identity`, `onClose`, optional `onChanged`, and an optional `ConsentChallenge`. Call `takeConsentChallenge` before authentication/telemetry initialization: it removes the fragment immediately and returns an in-memory challenge. Do not persist it or put it in query parameters. If an external sign-in discards the challenge, sign in first and reopen the email link. The component requires an explicit response; it never submits on arrival. App mounting and sign-in return behavior must be verified by the integrating application.

## Evidence, concurrency and withdrawal

Each request binds its account, recipient hash, random nonce, complete notice, notice digest and deployment-policy digest. Separate derived keys protect recipient ciphertext, address binding and challenge generation. Tokens are recoverable from a private HMAC key and stored nonce for reliable delivery; this is not a hash-only storage design. Database records alone cannot recover the token or recipient. For secret rotation, disable the installed policy before changing the key and explicitly reinstall it afterward, creating a fresh policy generation that permanently retires pending links; active evidence does not require the delivery key.

Account advisory locks serialize initial/final responses, request replacement, withdrawal and privacy deletion. Exact consumed-token retries cannot create another transition. Replacement or withdrawal invalidates older challenges. The worker checks request and policy both while claiming and immediately before sending. A network send already in flight cannot be recalled; a late email can arrive after withdrawal, but its link cannot activate consent. A stale lease cannot overwrite a newer delivery result.

Effective learning authorization joins email-plus evidence to a completed request and the exact enabled, unexpired policy and its installation generation. Re-enabling an earlier policy does not revive requests invalidated by an intervening change. Policy disablement or replacement blocks learning and new annual/funded purchases through this method, including servers with stale configuration. Existing separately verified manual-consent records retain their own path. Annual billing also requires `AMP_STRIPE_CONSENT_POLICY_VERSION` to match the accepted notice version; a mismatch rejects a new purchase. The staff consent importer cannot fabricate the reserved self-service method.

Withdrawal and account-wide learning deletion cancel pending enrollment within the same account transaction. Parents retain account access to export, review, withdraw and request deletion without granting fresh learning consent. Export omits challenge material, encrypted recipient, address hash and private deployment-review references. [16 CFR 312.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.6)

## Retention

`npm run privacy:retention` enforces these periods daily. The values are the named constants in `src/privacy/retention-periods.ts`; the deployment notice must state the same periods.

| Records | Deleted |
|---|---|
| Learner profile name, birth year, starting level, generated problems, answers, skill estimates, offline packs | 24 months after the latest of the learner's last practice activity, the family's last purchase activity (order, checkout, payment or funding request) and the scheduled end of the family's latest licence or entitlement. Uses the parent deletion path: the learner profile keeps only an opaque "Deleted learner" reference. Names on verified financial invoices follow the financial period below. |
| Unsaved anonymous trial and its answers | 14 days after it starts |
| Trial milestone events and trial records, saved or not | 90 days after creation; a saved trial's learner follows learner retention |
| Funnel analytics events, visitor records and checkout attribution | 90 days (`funnelEventDays`): events from when they occurred, visitor records from their last event, checkout links from creation. A converted visitor's events first add to monthly counts by event, source, device and grade band, with no visitor ID |
| Orders, payments, refunds, licences, entitlements, webhook receipts, linked funded invoices (including a required student billing name) and their learner references | 7 years after the later of the last transaction and the end of the licence period bought. A checkout that is still the evidence of a purchase consent first follows the consent-evidence period below. |
| Offline packs and offline answers | 30 days after the pack expires |
| "Report a problem" reports | Notes: 90 days after sending (`problemFlagNoteDays`). Reports: 365 days (`problemFlagDays`). Deleting a learner's learning data, by a parent or by retention, unlinks their reports and erases the notes first |
| Meta browser confirmation context | At most seven days after verified payment (`advertisingContextDays`), or earlier on permission expiry or withdrawal. No query strings or learner-page context are retained |
| Local purchase-measurement preparation records | 90 days after the payment evidence date (`advertisingPaymentDays`); withdrawal cancels unsent records |
| Privacy activity log | 90 days |
| Consent contact and evidence | Uncompleted requests: the policy's `pendingContactDays` (2–14; 7 in the reviewed policy). Completed evidence: `withdrawnEvidenceDays` (1–730; 180) after withdrawal or policy replacement, then a do-not-collect flag. |

Each category is deleted in bounded batches and logged as counts only. Learner, order, checkout and tombstone deletions re-check eligibility under the account lock; trial records and unreferenced financial rows are deleted by age. A unit blocked by a busy account is skipped until the next run. Learner deletions appear in the privacy log as `learner.retention-deleted`. Commerce events, payment observations and refund holds reject updates and deletes unless the transaction sets `amp.retention_purge='financial'`. This guards against accidental deletion; it is not a privilege boundary, because the API and the job share a database role.

The configured pending period is bounded to 2–14 days and must exceed the confirmation delay with room to respond. Recipient ciphertext is erased on completion, replacement or withdrawal. Expired uncompleted requests and their outbox records are deleted by `privacy:retention`. Completed evidence is kept while consent is active; withdrawal or policy invalidation starts its explicitly configured 1–730-day evidence period. After evidence deletion, the matching email-plus record becomes a minimal account-level do-not-collect preference: it retains no request pointer, consent notice version or verification proof. That preference lasts while the parent account exists so cleanup cannot accidentally restore collection. Existing manual evidence is not rewritten. These bounds are product limits, not legally mandated periods. The policy must state the concrete purpose and selected duration; schedule the cleanup job and review provider/back-up deletion too.

Policy invalidation is recognized by the retention job, which closes ineffective completed requests and starts their evidence period. The job processes bounded batches; operators must run it frequently enough to clear the backlog. A linked hash is not anonymous merely because it omits the address. The rule requires purpose-limited, documented retention, not indefinite storage. [16 CFR 312.10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.10)

## Purchase-backed transaction-notification method

The parent authenticates, reads the complete direct notice and explicitly acknowledges parental responsibility before Stripe Checkout. The request accepts only the selected offer, interval, request ID and acknowledgement: no child name, birth year or learning data. Checkout and payment records have a null learner reference. No placeholder learner is created.

The acknowledged notice, its digest, policy digest and installation generation are immutable. A purchase intent permits settlement within 24 hours of acknowledgement; this is an expiry window, not a waiting period. An invoice settled within that window can be processed after it expires, provided the snapshot survives and its policy is still current. Uncompleted snapshots are retained for the configured pending period after expiry to allow delayed delivery. Later payments or missing snapshots require reconciliation; the parent retains billing-portal access.

Only `invoice.paid` for the initial subscription can establish consent. The worker retrieves the paid invoice, its single paid InvoicePayment, successful PaymentIntent and captured charge, and validates amount, currency, selected plan, customer, subscription, checkout and refunds. A supported `card` or `link` method and the reviewed notification setting are required. Browser return parameters, Checkout completion, renewal, zero-dollar activity and failed payments cannot establish consent. The policy review must establish that the supported payment system notifies the primary account holder of each discrete transaction; a merchant receipt alone does not establish that fact. [16 CFR 312.5(b)(2)(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312/section-312.5)

Within one account transaction, verified settlement records method `monetary-transaction`, the minimal customer/invoice evidence and the paid account entitlement. Failure to create the entitlement rolls back consent. Immutable financial evidence is retained independently for reconciliation. Identity collisions quarantine both consent and assignment. Assignment rechecks consent, ownership and the provider's refund/dispute state and atomically projects the paid grant to exactly one learner. Its purchase-time receipt remains unassigned; assignment never rewrites financial history.

Refunds and disputes do not withdraw parental consent. A full refund prevents assignment or revokes its invoice grant. A dispute holds the entitlement and revokes assigned access pending reconciliation. Partial refunds preserve the paid term. Renewal and replay never reactivate consent after withdrawal, deletion or policy replacement. Parents can use the existing billing portal and payment-history endpoints before creating a learner and after withdrawing consent.

Purchase consent uses the same installed, enabled, current deployment policy as email-plus, without requiring email transport or its encryption secret. The API and Stripe worker need matching `AMP_CONSENT_POLICY_FILE`; its notice version must equal `AMP_STRIPE_CONSENT_POLICY_VERSION`. Apply migrations including `0020-purchase-consent.sql` and explicitly install the reviewed policy. `review.paymentNotificationsReviewed` must be true for purchase enrollment; its absence disables this method. `review.noDisclosureUnder312_2` is checked separately for email-plus. Provider-contract and deployment-flow review assertions remain required. These configuration assertions are not a legal certification. Review the direct notice and actual provider notification behavior before launch.

The authenticated endpoints are `GET /api/v1/billing/enrollment`, `POST /api/v1/billing/enrollment/checkout` and `POST /api/v1/billing/enrollment/claim`. Reads cannot consume an acknowledgement or create consent. Withdrawal invalidates pending intents under the account lock. The retention job removes completed consent snapshots after the configured withdrawal/policy-invalidation evidence period and replaces their active consent pointer with a do-not-collect preference. Financial records and opaque assignment references remain for billing reconciliation; late events cannot recreate deleted consent evidence. Parent exports include the notice and entitlement status, excluding private policy review references.

A definitively rejected first payment (withdrawn, expired or replaced notice, unsupported verification method, or an already refunded/disputed initial payment) queues a verified, retry-safe subscription cancellation with no proration or extra invoice. This prevents future renewal while retaining the settled payment record. It does not issue a refund; the parent can request review through support. Database/provider failures and stale server configuration alone do not cancel billing. The bounded cancellation queue backs off failed actions and continues processing other accounts.

An existing paid entitlement can be assigned after fresh effective consent through an independently verified method, including after the original snapshot was purged. The old snapshot is never reactivated. A purchase-only deployment needs a reviewed recovery process for renewed consent after withdrawal or policy changes; it cannot treat a prior payment or a new checkbox as fresh verification. Where email-plus is eligible, that workflow remains available; otherwise use the documented verified-consent administration process. Withdrawal of previously completed consent does not automatically cancel its subscription: parents manage renewal through the billing portal.


Policy replacement or expiry before initial reconciliation rejects present collection authority even when the charge settled under an earlier policy. That rejection stops future renewal and preserves the receipt for reviewed recovery or refund handling; a historical payment timestamp cannot enable collection under an invalidated notice. Before executing a queued cancellation, the worker rechecks that no paid entitlement has been established by recovery.


## Adult purchase measurement preference

The optional Google and Meta purchase-sharing preferences in Family privacy controls are independent of one another and of learning consent. Each requires an explicit adult/own-email attestation and a confirmed account email. They do not authorize child information disclosure or make email-plus eligible for unsupported disclosures. Google’s notice requests measurement without ad personalization; Meta’s notice includes measurement, ad delivery and audience creation.

A parent can withdraw either preference independently. Learning-consent withdrawal, learning-data deletion, and authenticated Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signals withdraw both. Each preference lasts up to 365 days and is retained for up to 90 days after withdrawal or expiry. Withdrawal removes its email binding digest. Family exports include both preferences without the digest. See [Google purchase measurement](google-purchase-measurement.md) and [Meta purchase measurement](meta-purchase-measurement.md) for delivery controls and integration requirements.

Local purchase-measurement preparations retain the financial source reference, adult account link, random export reference, destination, value/date and permission revision. They contain no email, learner details or learning events. Withdrawal cancels unsent preparations; cancelled entries cannot be restored by a later grant. The privacy worker deletes them 90 days after the payment evidence date, and family exports include their status and purchase/permission dates without internal routing identifiers.

For an explicitly permitted Meta purchase, the adult first-payment confirmation screen can retain its fixed page address and browser user agent after the server verifies the live initial payment. Retention ends no later than seven days after payment; withdrawal erases this context. Family exports include the retained page address and user agent without payment, checkout or account identifiers. This local preparation does not send an event to Meta.
