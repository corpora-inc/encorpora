# Instructional design

The workshop treats multiplication and division as relationships among a total, a group count, and a group size. Objects, pictures, words, symbols, and practical decisions reinforce those relationships. Fluency grows from useful facts and repeated retrieval across time; speed alone is not a definition of understanding.

## Progression

| Section    | Pages                                   | Instructional purpose                                                                                                                         |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Entry      | welcome, route, entry                   | Offer oral and tactile participation; identify equal-group addition and sharing readiness without a timed placement label.                    |
| Meaning    | equal-groups through meaning-workshop   | Establish equal groups, pairing, rows and columns, factor roles, commutativity, interval counting, zero and one.                              |
| Strategies | twos-fives-tens through fluency-routine | Connect anchors, doubling, distributive and associative structure, area, place value, and independent retrieval.                              |
| Division   | share through division-workshop         | Contrast equal sharing and group measurement; identify the missing quantity, use inverse facts, distinguish zero cases, and chunk a quotient. |
| Decisions  | remainder through patterns              | Interpret leftovers by context; contrast additive and multiplicative comparison; reason about factor pairs, primality, and parity.            |
| Transfer   | two-step through adult-guide            | Choose operations without cues, model an unknown, detect missing information, satisfy multiple constraints, revisit later, and reflect.       |

Entry arrays use at most five rows and five columns; parity examples stay within twenty. The later array and arithmetic examples deliberately extend beyond that entry range. Grade-four extensions are selected bridges, not claims of complete grade-four coverage. Sharing a divisible length introduces one fraction connection and is explicitly optional.

## Representation and language

Group count and group size are named separately. In the book's story convention, the first factor counts groups. Other orientations are accepted when labels preserve the roles. The 84 ÷ 7 array explicitly uses columns as groups: twelve columns of seven, split into ten columns and two columns. The 23 × 4 explanation explicitly turns the factors before discussing four groups of twenty-three.

Unit squares represent area, not just object count. Captions identify square units and disjoint regions. Number-line arrows count intervals rather than endpoints. Integer grouping diagrams identify complete groups and leftover original units. A fraction strip keeps the whole fixed as one meter. Double lines preserve exact proportional relationships.

Every diagram has a caption and meaningful alternate text. The models use exact dimensions and rational quantities, not generated bitmap estimates. Print and reader renderers must preserve those mathematical relationships and inspect multi-jump label bounds at their actual display sizes.

## Practice and feedback

Tasks vary the work: calculate, draw, describe, compare two interpretations, repair a misconception, supply missing information, test a universal claim, invent a story, and satisfy a design constraint. Several workshops omit operation cues. A delayed mixed page changes contexts after a break.

The same arithmetic supports different decisions: 29 ÷ 6 yields four complete kits with five beads left, but five vans when all twenty-nine visitors need seats. Whole counters can leave unused objects; ribbon can be subdivided into equal fractional lengths. These distinctions prevent a remainder rule from replacing attention to the question.

Reasoned keys give a result, its unit, and a mathematical explanation. Open responses receive acceptance criteria and an example, not a claim that one wording is required. A partner should inspect whether the learner's quantities, model, and equation agree. Related numeric templates offer bounded calculation practice; they do not evaluate written explanations, constructed diagrams, or complete standards mastery.

## Verification boundaries

Structural tests check unique anchors, one key per task, real curriculum/template references, and explicit limitations. Mathematical regression checks cover exact array regions, jump endpoints, proportional scales, remainder decisions, and all allowed inventory plans. Independent review must also read the task/key pairs and exposition. Rendered review checks every physical page for overflow and label bounds, then examines instructional density, workspace, and model meaning. Source tests cannot substitute for these editorial and visual judgments.
