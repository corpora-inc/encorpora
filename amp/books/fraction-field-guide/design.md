# The Fraction Field Guide

**Measure, Share, and Scale** is an original fractions unit for learners who can count equal groups, multiply small whole numbers, and read simple measurements. Typical use spans grades 3–6; the entry check and section choices matter more than age. The field-station setting provides recurring quantities—trail distances, water, seed beds, and supplies—whose units stay visible throughout an argument.

The authored source is `book.ts`, using `schema.ts`. Each source page is a deliberate learning composition. Rendering may split a page only when it preserves task/diagram proximity and adequate response space; a page count is not a quality target. Diagrams use exact rational inputs and identify the reference whole. Learner tasks have durable IDs, and the solution section references those IDs rather than relying on printed page numbers.

## Instructional route

Readers who need fraction meaning begin at page 3. Readers confident with meaning use pages 4, 8, and 12 as entry checks before choosing a section. An uncertain answer invites a related lesson; it does not bar access to later ideas. The multiplication and division chapters need equal-group understanding and multiplication facts, but diagrams can support learners whose fact recall is developing.

Solution compositions can span multiple physical pages. They must retain task IDs and enough reasoning to support a learner who made a plausible mistake. Open tasks need criteria and at least one valid example, not a single supposedly unique answer.

## Visual and language requirements

Use a quiet consistent page grid with an immediately visible question or idea. Reserve generous workspace on investigation pages. Distinguish instruction, learner work, and answer keys by labels as well as color. Large fraction symbols, well-spaced labels, and uncluttered tick marks matter more than ornament. A diagram must fit an ordinary printable page without tiny text. Avoid a long series of identical card layouts: a number-line investigation, worked comparison, error analysis, and design challenge have different spatial needs.

Words, pictures, and symbols must agree on quantity and units. Never compare shaded areas in unequal rectangles while implying equal wholes. Number lines mark the unit interval explicitly. An area model has equal rows and columns; overlap shading distinguishes each factor and their intersection. Grouping diagrams show a partial final group when appropriate. Sharing diagrams label the recipient count and the amount per recipient in the accompanying explanation.

The prose speaks directly to the learner without baby talk. It invites prediction, an attempted drawing, and revision. Every new rule receives a reason before independent practice. Contexts supply useful mathematical structure rather than paragraphs of decoration. There is no promise that completion establishes all fraction standards or guarantees a test score.

## Acceptance checks

Every task ID must be unique and have exactly one solution record. Every solution reference must resolve. Rational denominators must be positive, labels must match values, and diagram constraints must be validated. Check calculations independently of the authoring source. Inspect rendered instructional pages, dense answer pages, grayscale output, and page breaks. Mathematical review and instructional review require a reviewer other than the author. The publication's coverage statement lists the tasks and assessed meanings; it does not infer complete mastery from book completion.
