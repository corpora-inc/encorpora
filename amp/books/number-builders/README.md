# Number Builders

*Count, Compose, and Change* is an illustrated early-number book and workbook. It connects small collections, parts of five and ten, teen numbers, tens and ones, addition/subtraction stories and selected two-digit strategies. An adult can read the invitations aloud while the learner points, moves counters, draws or explains.

The instructional route begins with one-to-one counting and cardinality. Rearrangements preserve the count; a ten-strip preserves the value of ten loose units. Those ideas support missing parts, make-ten strategies and exchanging a ten before subtraction. Comparison and unknown-start stories prevent learners from relying on keywords. Mixed and later-return tasks invite a different representation or independent attempt.

Learner pages use brief invitations and generous drawing spaces. The adult guide and reasoned keys explain what a valid response must show and accept alternative strategies. Optional reusable mats and unit pieces support hands-on work; cutting is not a prerequisite. Open explanations, drawings and invented stories require human review.

## Scope and assessment

This is a connected selection of K–2 number-and-operations ideas, not a complete grade-level mathematics course. Hundreds, counting to 1,000, the full geometry and measurement curriculum, time, money and data require other instruction. Completing pages does not certify fluency or mastery.

`alignments.ts` links selected standards and skills to exact pages and tasks, with explicit exclusions. `practice.ts` binds 17 existing numeric problem families to relevant instruction and records their ranges. A numeric result cannot establish one-to-one pointing, unit reasoning, a learner-created model or an oral explanation. Intermediate-part questions in make-ten practice are distinguished from final-total questions.

The exact models derive quantities from occupied cells or unit counts. Ten-frames have two rows of five. Ten-strips contain ten unit squares, and noncanonical collections preserve the same units when a ten is unpacked. Conserved comparison panels must contain the same count. Color is supplemented by outlines, patterns and labels.

## Standards sources

The original prose, activities and diagrams address selected requirements from the official Common Core domains below. Identifiers are checked against the curriculum catalog; source links do not imply full coverage.

- [Kindergarten counting and cardinality](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/CC/): structured collections through 20 and scattered collections through 10 have distinct requirements. This book uses scattered entry collections of at most ten.
- [Kindergarten operations](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/OA/) and [teen numbers](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/NBT/): small-group operations, decompositions, partners of ten and ten-plus-ones structure.
- [Grade 1 operations](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/) and [base ten](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/): selected story structures, arithmetic strategies, equality, unknowns and two-digit units.
- [Grade 2 operations](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/OA/) and [base ten](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/): selected strategy and two-step transfer tasks, with no claim to the complete within-100 fluency or hundreds requirements.
